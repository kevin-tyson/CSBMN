These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on February 15, 2024. Verify against the recording and the primary sources before relying on any of them.

HIGH Nonpublic minutes sealed with no stated finding, and the minutes leave the seal motion blank

Minutes of a nonpublic session must be disclosed within 72 hours unless, “by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism” (RSA 91-A:3, III, statute text). On the recording (0:04:41 to 0:05:15) Crawford moves only “to seal the minutes for three years”, the chair seconds, and the clerk takes a roll call; no ground or determination is stated, and no tally is announced. The draft minutes record the vote as “Makes a motion to seal the minutes for , seconds” (minutes text), with the mover, the seconder and the duration left blank, and as “all present voting yes” (minutes text), while the transcript carries one No at 0:05:09. RSA 91-A:2, II separately requires the minutes to record who made and seconded each motion. The vote cleared two-thirds on either count, so the defect is the missing determination and the defective record of the vote, not the margin. The minutes give the session's grounds as RSA 91-A:3, II(a) and II(c) in substance (see flag 2), which could support a reputation finding, but the statute requires the determination itself, made in public.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (sealing requires a 2/3 recorded vote with a determination; text in force from Oct. 3, 2023); RSA 91-A:2, II (movers and seconders recorded in minutes); draft minutes 2.15.24

MEDIUM The nonpublic motion cites a chapter that does not exist and a paragraph with no exemptions

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires a motion to enter nonpublic session to “state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II” (statute text). The agenda heads the item “Non-Public Meeting Session RSA 92-A:3, I and II” (agenda text), the chair states it aloud as “RSA 92-3. No. 92-A colon 328 and see” at 0:02:37, and the draft minutes record the motion as “under RSA 92-A:3, I(a) and II(c)” (minutes text). There is no RSA chapter 92-A; chapter 92 concerns tenure and oath of office. Paragraph I of RSA 91-A:3 contains no exemptions; the text the agenda prints under I. (a) (agenda text) is the wording of II(a), employee dismissal, promotion, compensation or discipline. The intended grounds, II(a) and II(c), are recoverable from the agenda's quoted text, which is why this is MEDIUM rather than HIGH. This is the third SAU 6 agenda in a row to carry the phantom chapter (12/14/23, 1/11/24, 2/15/24); the April 11, 2024 paperwork corrects it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II (motion must state the paragraph II exemption); RSA Title VI table of contents (no chapter 92-A); agenda

OBSERVATION The HR director's pay was delegated to the superintendent

At 0:21:08 the board authorized the interim superintendent “to negotiate and enter into a contract with the HR human Resources director to be consistent with prevailing market rates”. The SAU board “shall fix the salaries of all school administrative unit personnel” (RSA 194-C:5, III, statute text). A motion that sets a market-rate standard and leaves the figure to the superintendent may or may not be the board fixing the salary; the statute does not address delegation, and the record does not show whether the resulting contract came back to the board. The same board delegated the interim superintendent's own contract terms to the chair and counsel on January 11. The contract, or a later vote ratifying it, would settle whether the board fixed the figure.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (SAU board fixes salaries; 1996, 298:3, never amended)

OBSERVATION The nursing-mothers policy cites a state law not yet in force

Policy ACN, adopted at 0:23:49, gives its legal reference as “RSA 275:78-83” (policy text). Those sections were enacted by 2023 N.H. Laws ch. 191 (HB 358) with an effective date of July 1, 2025; on February 15, 2024 they were not in force. The binding source then was the federal PUMP Act, 29 U.S.C. §218d, in force since December 29, 2022, which requires reasonable break time and a private place other than a bathroom for one year after birth. The policy meets those terms; only its citation is premature. Citing the federal provision would have given the policy a basis that was law on the day it was adopted.

Sources: RSA 275:78 (source note: 2023, 191:20, eff. July 1, 2025); 29 U.S.C. §218d (PUMP Act); policy ACN as presented

POSITIVE Paid nursing breaks for hourly staff, beyond the federal minimum

Policy ACN as adopted provides that “A nursing mother who is an hourly employee will be paid during nursing periods” (policy text), the change the board asked for in January (0:22:53). Federal law does not require it: an employer “shall not be required to compensate an employee receiving reasonable break time” unless other law requires it (29 U.S.C. §218d(b), statute text). The policy also guarantees at least three nursing periods a day and a private space other than a bathroom in each building.

Sources: 29 U.S.C. §218d(b) (break time need not be paid); policy ACN

POSITIVE The chair asked for board review of every budget transfer and encumbrance at year end

At 0:18:24 the chair asked that the June meeting receive “the record of transfers. The rationale for those transfers and for incumbent incumbent encumbered items, so that the board can approve and close them”, and Henry agreed. RSA 32:10's transfer rules are written for towns and districts; RSA 32:1 does not name school administrative units, so no statute requires an SAU board to see its transfers. Asking for the list, with reasons, before closing the year is a control beyond the minimum.

Sources: RSA 32:1 (application of the Municipal Budget Law); RSA 32:10 (transfers)
