SAU 6 Board, May 19, 2022

Short meeting of the SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity) chaired by Marjorie Erickson. The April minutes were still not ready, so their approval was postponed. On the superintendent's recommendation the board voted to remove the SAU merit pay plan, about $35,000, from the SAU budget and to freeze the money until the board approves its use; it asked for a staffing assessment by June, set a tentative joint retreat for July 24, and heard that the superintendent's evaluation committee would meet May 23. The superintendent said the administration had inherited "six years of audits that weren't done". MEDIUM The superintendent said the administration inherited "six years of audits that weren't done"

Answering a question about spending the merit money, the superintendent said: “we also inherited, you know, six years of audits that weren't done and all kinds of other work that we're working on” (0:19:51), and that the auditors could come to a later meeting “and talk about where we are and audits as well” (0:20:11). He did not say whose audits (the SAU's, Claremont's or Unity's) or which years. For a school district spending $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year, 2 CFR 200.501(a) requires a single audit, and 200.512(a)(1) requires it to be filed within nine months of year end; a backlog of that length would be a missed federal deadline. No located New Hampshire statute requires an annual audit of an SAU itself, though RSA 194-C:4, II(a) lists audits among the services an SAU must provide. The statement is a speaker's characterization with no figures, so this is MEDIUM, not HIGH. The Federal Audit Clearinghouse filings for Claremont, Unity and SAU 6 for fiscal years 2016 to 2021 would settle it.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2022 edition ($750,000 single-audit threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2022 edition (earlier of 30 days after the report or nine months after period end); RSA 194-C:4, II(a) (audits among the services an SAU provides)

OBSERVATION Merit pay removed from the SAU budget and frozen by voice vote, with the fiscal year and the vote count unrecorded

The board voted to “accept the recommendation to remove the mayor pay plan” [merit pay plan] from the SAU 6 budget (0:18:46), amended to “freeze those funds until board approves use of them” (0:21:04, 0:21:39, 0:22:05). The amount was put at “about 35,000” (0:19:18). RSA 194-C:5 gives the SAU board the power to fix the salaries of all SAU personnel, so the board was acting within its authority. Two things are not on the record: which year's SAU budget the line sits in (the superintendent speaks of “June”, and the chair of how “move money around in the line items”), and how members voted. Both votes were by voice; RSA 194-C:8's weighted votes apply only when a majority of members present demand them, so a voice vote is regular, but with a garbled roll and no minutes the tally and the mover's second cannot be checked.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5 (SAU board fixes salaries of SAU personnel); RSA 194-C:8 (weighted votes only on demand of a majority present and voting)

OBSERVATION The superintendent evaluation committee is a public body, and its meetings left no record found

The chair described a four-member committee of the board (herself, Heather Whitney, Joshua Lambert and Unity's Shannon Popescu) that met with the superintendent in September, December and February and would meet May 23 (0:35:49, 0:36:23, 0:34:06). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes “any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body” of a public body a public body itself, and RSA 91-A:2, I has no exception for evaluation sessions, so each meeting needed notice and minutes, with any nonpublic portion entered by motion under RSA 91-A:3. No notices or minutes of these sessions were found, but the SAU 6 archive does not reach before July 2022, so this records an absence in what can be inspected, not a finding that the sessions went unnoticed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2022 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, I-II (2022 codification)
