MEDIUM The incoming superintendent describes contracts signed and liabilities incurred outside the business office, including a $350,000-plus services contract with no known funding

Dr. Broadrick told the committee that schools "incur liabilities without necessarily, having purchase orders or contracts in place" (0:11:29), that one incurred last fall reached the business office "within the last four days" (0:11:56), and that pay is not always backed by a signed document in the personnel file: "that is absolutely not the case today" (0:12:57). Special-education staff could not say how "a 350 to $400,000 liability" with the behavioral-services provider Constellations would be paid (0:21:08), and administrators, coordinators and principals "have never been told that they're not allowed to sign contracts" (0:25:24). Lavalette recalled a purchasing policy whose list of permitted signers ended in etc. (0:27:29). The district's treasurer may pay "only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole" (RSA 197:23-a), so a commitment made by staff outside that chain reaches the board only as a bill already owed. Where the spending is charged to federal awards, such as special-education services, the district must keep effective internal control over those awards (2 CFR 200.303). The committee agreed with the remedy stated on tape and in the draft minutes ("No one but the Superintendent and/or the Business Administrator signs a contract", minutes text). MEDIUM: a control weakness reported by the district's own incoming chief executive, without a total.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (payments only on orders of the board or empowered members); 2 CFR 200.303 (internal controls over federal awards).

OBSERVATION A behavioral-services contract tied to students' IEPs held back for negotiation, with compensatory services named as the fallback

Dr. Broadrick said he declined to sign next year's Constellations contract ("$350,000 worth of work") until the provider met him to negotiate, after staff told him the services were needed "because they're in a kid's IEP" (0:26:00, 0:26:33). He added: "there's a special and complaint process. We can provide compensatory services in the future." (0:27:01). Federal rules require that, at the start of each school year, each public agency have an IEP in effect for each child with a disability, and that "special education and related services are made available to the child in accordance with the child's IEP" as soon as possible after it is developed (34 CFR 300.323(a), (c)(2)). Negotiating price before signing is ordinary contract practice, and the contract was for the following school year, so nothing on this record shows a lapse in services. The point to watch is that the negotiation finishes before services under those IEPs are due; compensatory services remedy a denial after the fact rather than satisfying the rule.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.323(a) and (c)(2) (IEP in effect; services as soon as possible).

OBSERVATION The May 31 figures restated here do not match the report given to the board two days earlier

Angell opened by recalling the figures he gave at "the last school board meeting": year-to-date expenditures through May 31 of 32,849,000, encumbrances of 2,582,000 and remaining appropriation of 4,283,000 (0:08:45), and said the remaining appropriation was unchanged in reports through June 30 (0:09:19). The expenditure report in the June 17 board packet (CSD 05.31.2026 Expenditures.xlsx), summed by this project across its 1,063 general-fund lines, shows a budget of $39,714,564.00, year-to-date expenditures of $30,020,932.89, encumbrances of $5,459,619.74 and a final balance of $4,234,011.37, the figures the June 17 approved minutes record. The two sets share the same total appropriation but split it differently, and the remaining balance differs by about $49,000. The recording does not show whether the June 19 numbers came from a later run of the ledger or were misread. The year-end deficit estimate ("less than 1 million") rests on the remaining-appropriation figure, so the committee should know which report it is using.

Sources: CSD 05.31.2026 Expenditures.xlsx (June 17 packet); 6.17.26 Approved CSB Meeting Minutes; see also the June 17, 2026 page.

OBSERVATION Proposed budget "roundtables" would split the board into sub-quorum teams, unrecorded

For the FY2028 budget Angell proposed "Two teams of two school board members and one team of three school board members" rotating through administrator teams in 20-minute discussions (0:53:27). Asked "So this is not recorded?" (0:54:04), the chair answered "That would be that would be a school board meeting. Kind of." and Angell "You're kind of ending the meeting and then you're having the decision." (0:54:14, 0:54:17). None of the teams would be a quorum of the seven-member board, but RSA 91-A:2-a, II provides that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter." Rotating every member through the same budget questions outside a noticed, minuted meeting is the pattern that provision addresses. This was a proposal only; holding the roundtables as a noticed, recorded board work session would avoid the question.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, II (no circumvention through communications outside a meeting); RSA 91-A:2, I (definition of a meeting).

OBSERVATION The business administrator warns the DOE-25 may be late; the audits remain behind

"It's due September 1st. I've never been late. Okay. Here. I may be late." (0:50:42). RSA 198:4-d, ¶III, as reenacted by 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586; approved July 2, 2026, effective July 1, 2026), requires the annual financial report "on or before September 1 of each year" with no extension provision. The same law's ¶VIII requires the audit report for the last completed fiscal year within nine months of year end (March 31, 2027 for FY2026) and directs the commissioner to withhold "state grant funding, not including RSA 198:40-a funding" from non-compliant districts. On this record the FY2023 to FY2025 audits are still in progress, the chair wants the auditor to explain the delay on July 21 (1:02:09), and the forensic audit awaits a $10,000 retainer, with the authorized ceiling stated only as "I'm pretty sure we authorized up to 50,000" (1:03:38).

Sources: 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), RSA 198:4-d, ¶III and ¶VIII.
