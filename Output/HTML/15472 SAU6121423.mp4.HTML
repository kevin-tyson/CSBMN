MEDIUM The nonpublic session was entered under a statute that does not exist

The agenda heads item VI "Non-Public Meeting Session RSA 92-A:3,II", and the chair announces it aloud the same way at 0:05:32 ("Rs 92 dash, a, three comma two A and C") before moving entry herself at 0:06:00. There is no RSA chapter 92-A: Title VI runs 91-A, 91-B, 91-C, 92 (tenure and oath of office), 93-A. RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires the motion to state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II. The paragraph letters, (a) compensation or discipline of an employee and (c) reputation, were stated and are the right letters for the text the agenda quotes, so the defect is in the chapter, not the ground. The draft minutes, written later, record the motion as made under "RSA 91-A:3,II(a) & (c)", which is what the body plainly meant; they do not record that the motion as spoken cited a different chapter. The same defective citation recurs on SAU 6 agendas through February 2024.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II(a), (c) (2023 codification; source note ends 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); RSA Title VI table of contents (no chapter 92-A); agenda, item VI

MEDIUM Minutes sealed for 99 years with no statutory determination on the record

RSA 91-A:3, III requires nonpublic minutes and decisions to be disclosed within 72 hours "unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism" (statute). The vote here was taken in public session, and the minuted 8 to 3 of eleven present clears two-thirds. What is missing is the determination: the motion at 0:08:03 is only "to seal the minutes for 99 years", nobody states which ground applies, and the chair's own response at 0:08:11 is to question the length ("It sounds like a long time. It is a long time."). The draft minutes record the motion, mover, seconder and named roll but no finding either. The exemptions cited for entry, (a) and (c), would make a reputation finding available; the record does not show that the board made one.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2023 codification); draft minutes, item VI

OBSERVATION A 99-year seal adopted ten weeks after the law began requiring review within ten years

Since October 3, 2023, RSA 91-A:3, IV has provided that a body may adopt procedures to review sealed nonpublic minutes and decide by majority vote whether the paragraph III circumstances still apply, with review no more than 10 years after the last vote to withhold them. A 99-year term does not displace that review. Nothing on the recording or in the minutes mentions it, and the chair's question at 0:08:11 ("Why do we do 99 minutes?") goes unanswered except by 99 minutes. I don't I mean, yeah, out years. This is noted for anyone tracking when these minutes fall due for review, not as a breach.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, IV (added by 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023)

POSITIVE A public hearing the statute does not require, a budget adopted inside the deadline, and the counsel session disclosed

RSA 194-C:9, I requires the SAU board to adopt its budget "At a meeting held before January 1" (statute) and requires no public hearing. The board noticed and held one, posted the line-item report with FY22 and FY23 actual and FY24 budget columns about 30 hours before the meeting (Drive createdTime December 13, 11:33 a.m. Eastern, with the agenda), invited comment from the floor (0:03:09), and adopted the budget eighteen days before January 1 (0:13:53). The agenda also disclosed the 6:00 p.m. consultation with legal counsel, which RSA 91-A:2, I(b) excludes from the definition of a meeting and so needed no notice at all.

Sources: RSA 194-C:9, I (2023 codification; source note 1996, 298:3; 2003, 279:1); RSA 91-A:2, I(b)
