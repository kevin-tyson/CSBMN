Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each rests on the rule it cites, read as it stood on the meeting date; where no rule is confirmed the item is an Observation.

HIGH A $15,000 private gift was added to the agenda and accepted the same night, with no public hearing on the record

At the start of the meeting the superintendent asked to add donations to the agenda (0:03:06). Near the end he presented the Class of 1968's gift: “a total of $15,000. They would like to donate $10,000 to the music program. And they would like to donate $5,000 to landscaping in the front of Stevens High School.” The board accepted it on a motion and second by voice vote (3:06:43 to 3:07:05). No one mentions a public hearing.

RSA 198:20-b, in the 2017 codification in force in 2018, lets a board accept unanticipated money from a private source only where the district has adopted the enabling article (¶I), and ¶III(a) provides: For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken, with newspaper notice of the time, place and subject at least 7 days before. Both parts of this gift reach $5,000. A gift first placed on the agenda that evening could not have had a hearing noticed seven days in advance.

What would mitigate it: a hearing held and noticed separately, or acceptance under some authority other than RSA 198:20-b. Neither appears in the record, and no minutes survive to show either. The $2,000 landscaping item was a plan approval for a student group, not a gift, and is not flagged.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2017 codification; prior public hearing at $5,000 or more)

MEDIUM Investment policy reviewed without the annual adoption vote the statute requires

The business administrator walked the board through policy DFA, saying “we have severely lax in doing that for the school board” and describing the review as a financial-statement disclosure item that needs no action (1:29:08). No motion followed.

RSA 197:23-a, IV (2017 codification): At least yearly, the school board shall review and adopt an investment policy for the investment of public funds in conformance with the provisions of applicable statutes. Review alone does not meet the duty to adopt. MEDIUM, not HIGH: the board could still adopt the policy at a later 2018 meeting, and no minutes are available to show whether it did.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a, IV (2017 codification; annual review and adoption of the investment policy)

MEDIUM Board subcommittees working between meetings, with no notice or minutes in the record

Zullo said the policy subcommittee would meet the next day to sort the superintendent's list of policies (2:33:29); this is the three-member subcommittee whose meeting dates were set on May 2 (May 2, 2018 page). Towle reported a subcommittee formed at the retreat “for this effort” on school funding (1:10:52), and when Pierce asked about the “crisis committee development”, Benware said one had been formed at the retreat: “it was you and I and Caroline” (2:43:10). Whether these are one body or two is not clear.

Under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) a board's committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body is a public body, so each subcommittee meeting needs posted notice and minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II. An announcement at a board meeting is not posted notice. MEDIUM: whether notices were posted and minutes kept cannot be checked from here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (subcommittees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; notice and minutes)

OBSERVATION Policy DBJ, as read, lets the superintendent move money between line items

The business administrator read policy DBJ: “the superintendent is authorized to transfer funds between items when necessary to achieve board policy goals”, with exceptions for the unemployment line and special warrant articles (2:32:42). The board's retreat decision to be notified of transfers of $5,000 or more (2:31:58) would require a policy change.

RSA 32:10, I (text in force from August 15, 2017) gives the transfer power to the governing body, limits total spending to the total appropriated, and requires records showing the purposes transferred to and from; ¶II defines a purpose to include line items. Whether a school board may delegate that power to its superintendent is not settled by the text; the notification threshold is a step toward board oversight.

Sources: RSA 32:10, I-II (2019 codification; source note ends 2017, 127:4, eff. Aug. 15, 2017)

POSITIVE Retreat commitments that go beyond what the Right-to-Know law requires

The board agreed to e-mail each agenda to about 2,300 families (2:49:48, 2:50:11), to publish detailed agendas in print rather than a bare notice that a meeting will be held (2:47:09, 2:48:51), to receive expense reports at least quarterly in public session (2:37:39, 2:38:24), and to raise members' finance questions at meetings so the public hears the answers (1:32:39, 1:34:43). RSA 91-A:2, II requires only posted notice of a meeting's time and place 24 hours ahead and minutes within five business days.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; notice and minutes)
