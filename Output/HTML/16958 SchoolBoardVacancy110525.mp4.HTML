Claremont School Board, November 5, 2025 (vacancy-discussion excerpt)

A 49-minute excerpt of the November 5, 2025 board meeting covering only agenda item III.6, the vacant school board seat. MEDIUM The seat was filled by three of the six remaining members; whether that is the "majority vote" RSA 671:33 contemplates was not addressed

The motion to return Sprague carried with Madden, Petrin and Whitney for, Crawford and Hawkins against, and Howard abstaining (draft minutes; on tape 0:43:46 to 0:46:24). The board counted it as passing under its by-law 2.07, which the adopted by-laws word "shall be adopted by a majority vote of members present and voting" and illustrate with "7 members present, 2 abstain , 2 vote no, 3 vote yes the motion passes." The statute speaks differently. RSA 671:33, II(a) says "The school board shall fill vacancies occurring on the school board" and that the selectmen appoint "if the remaining members are unable, by majority vote, to agree upon an appointment." Three is not a majority of the six remaining members. RSA 21:15, I, a general rule of construction, gives "joint authority to 3 or more public officers" to "a majority of them"; read that way, an appointment needs four of six. Read the other way, as a majority of those voting, three of five suffices. The project's anchor notes treat both readings as open, and this page does not choose. The by-laws themselves yield to statute (by-law 2.15: "All laws of the State of New Hampshire ... shall take precedence over these rules"). On tape no member or counsel addressed which measure the statute uses; the chair said "If I'm wrong, I'm wrong." (0:44:44) about the by-law, and first told the board that abstentions "can only really be made if you have an objection" (0:44:04), which the adopted by-law does not say. Graded MEDIUM because the validity of the appointment turns on a statutory question the record leaves unexamined; the board's own count followed its written by-law.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (board fills vacancies; selectmen if remaining members cannot agree by majority vote); RSA 21:15, I (joint authority goes to a majority of the officers); Claremont School Board By-Laws, adopted June 5, 2024, rules 2.07 and 2.15; 11.5.25 draft minutes (vote by name)

OBSERVATION The first motion to return Sprague was never voted, withdrawn or postponed while two other motions were taken

Petrin moved at 0:04:20 and the chair seconded at 0:04:32, then said she "deferred that" to hear from members and the public (0:16:06). Crawford and Hawkins pointed out that a motion was on the floor (0:16:14, 0:34:10); the chair and Petrin answered "We deferred." (0:34:14). By-law 2.07 provides "Another motion cannot be raised until all other motions are settled or adjourned" and lists postponing to a certain time among the motions that may be received while a motion is under debate. A by-law is the board's own rule, not statute, so this is an observation; the second motion's vote is the operative one.

Sources: Claremont School Board By-Laws, rule 2.07 (motions); 11.5.25 draft minutes

OBSERVATION "Reinstatement" is an appointment under RSA 671:33, and it runs only to the next district election

The letter, both motions and the draft minutes call the action a reinstatement ("Frank Sprague will be reinstated as the seventh Board member"). No statute cited on the record provides for reinstating a member who resigned; the board's power is to fill the vacancy by appointment, and RSA 671:33, II(a) states "Appointees of the school board shall serve until the next district election when the voters of the district shall elect a replacement for the unexpired term." Petrin said as much ("And then in March, the voters get to speak again", 0:04:20). The chair's description of the purpose, "to bring back a person to finish the term that he was elected to serve" (0:20:40), goes further than the statute: the appointment ends at the March 2026 election whatever remained of the original term.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (appointee serves until the next district election)

OBSERVATION Members described the statute as "guidance" and "not binding"; its text says "shall fill", and the deadlines discussed are not in it

Counsel's correspondence, as the chair read it, said the law "directs the board to fill the vacancy" and also that it "is not a controlling law" (0:00:35, 0:01:28). Members then called it "not binding" (0:13:50) and "guidance" (0:16:32, 0:16:43). RSA 671:33, II(a) is mandatory in form, "The school board shall fill vacancies", and supplies its own consequence: if the remaining members cannot agree, "the selectmen ... shall appoint". For a city, RSA 21:28, I lets "selectmen" mean the mayor and aldermen, which is how counsel's warning about the City Council fits. The statute sets no time limit: neither the 45 days mentioned by the chair (0:09:28) and by Crawford (0:13:58) nor counsel's "at the next meeting" (0:01:07) appears in it. Counsel's letter itself is not in the packet, so the reader has only the chair's reading of it. The October 15, 2025 draft minutes show no vacancy item at the meeting between the tie vote and this one.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a); RSA 21:28, I ("selectmen" includes a city's mayor and aldermen); 10.15.25 draft minutes

OBSERVATION The chair solicited the returning member, and the board adopted no written process for filling the seat

Asked whether Sprague volunteered, the chair said she had called him to ask for names and asked whether he would serve himself (0:12:13, 0:12:34); Petrin later described the letter as "Not of any sort of backroom deal or any sort of solicitation." (0:42:52). Commenters and Madden called the arrangement a back room (0:06:29, 0:28:10). The chair said the board has "never had a policy related to this" (0:08:55), and Petrin said "The process needs to be better in the future." (0:43:21). No statute requires an application process for a board appointment, so this records the facts rather than a breach. It matters for reading the result: the board had already taken applications and heard candidates, and the person appointed came forward by letter after the chair's call, writing (as the chair read it) that he was "not interested in a competitive process" (0:03:29).

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (no procedure prescribed); 11.5.25 draft minutes
