As it read on June 1, 2016, II(j) covered "Consideration of confidential, commercial, or financial information that is exempt from public disclosure under RSA 91-A:5, IV in an adjudicative proceeding pursuant to RSA 541 or RSA 541-A." Nothing on the recording identifies an adjudicative proceeding before the board, and the subject is never stated. II(d) (acquisition, sale or lease of property) would have fit the property search discussed earlier that evening, but no one cites it. The motion stated a letter on its face and the vote was by roll call, as I(b) requires; whether II(j) fit the matter cannot be judged without the minutes, which do not survive.

OBSERVATION The board was told the only cap on placement costs applies to court placements; the chapter cited governs insurance

On out-of-district special education costs (0:51:18 to 1:11:44), the director reported that four budgeted placements plus four students who moved in already placed put the district "well over $500,000 in costs", and that total out-of-district spending was $2.7 million (1:11:35, 1:11:38). The business administrator then said that for court placements "the court caps it at 50 grand, so that if it's more than that, the state takes over under under under RSA 402" and "that's the only cap on any of this stuff that exists" (0:57:30, 0:57:52).

Two problems for a reviewer. RSA chapter 402 is "Insurance Companies and Agents", so the citation is either a transcription error or a misstatement; this page did not identify the provision he meant. And the three-and-a-half-times figure he used matches RSA 186-C:18, III, which in 2016 made the state liable for 80 percent of any child's special education costs above 3 1/2 times the state average per-pupil expenditure, up to 10 times, prorated when the appropriation fell short. That catastrophic aid is not limited to court placements, and the superintendent had described it to the board two weeks earlier (May 18, 2016 page). The board left this discussion with an incomplete account of the state's share.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2016 codification; source note ends 2011, 224:227); RSA chapter 402 (Insurance Companies and Agents), table of contents

POSITIVE Meeting materials posted online before meetings, and district email accounts for members

The superintendent described agenda, minutes and backup material as normally posted on the website the Friday before each meeting (0:38:39), and members had just been given district email accounts at the chair's suggestion of May 18, so that "your personal email would not be a part of this process if, in fact, there was a right to know" (0:36:53). RSA 91-A:2, II in 2016 required minutes to be open to inspection, not posted online; the website-posting paragraph did not yet exist. Posting in advance went beyond the minimum.

One qualification. RSA 91-A:1-a, III defines governmental records as "any information created, accepted, or obtained by, or on behalf of, any public body", and IV reaches information "in whatever physical form kept or maintained". Board business conducted on a personal account is still a governmental record. District accounts make those records easier to find and produce; they keep personal email out of a request only if members stop using it for board business.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification); RSA 91-A:1-a, III and IV (2015 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1)
