Claremont School Board — June 1, 2016

Regular meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The board heard the Class of 2016's post-graduation survey, voted to buy Chromebooks for its own paperless meetings, set August 9 for a joint meeting with the City Council, and spent nearly an hour on out-of-district special education costs (about $2.7 million, by the figures given). It adopted the building rental, sexual harassment, school violence and summer activities policies on second reading after debating the $240 invoice sent to the Trump campaign, approved four new staff and a revised 2016-17 calendar, and went into a nonpublic session whose exemption the recording does not make clear. OBSERVATION The nonpublic session's exemption cannot be read from the recording: the chair's citation sounds like II(j) and the motion's like II(d)

At 0:01:04 the superintendent asked to drop one of two nonpublic items: "You're not going to need to go into nonpublic for RSA 91 A three, two C only three to J." At the end of the meeting the chair asked for "a quick nonpublic, RSA 91832 J." (2:27:33), and Brent Ferland moved "as pursuant to RSA 91 a three two day" (2:27:47); Pierce seconded and a roll call followed (2:27:56). The transcript therefore offers two readings. Paragraph II(j) as it read in 2016 covered "Consideration of confidential, commercial, or financial information that is exempt from public disclosure under RSA 91-A:5, IV in an adjudicative proceeding pursuant to RSA 541 or RSA 541-A.", which is an unusual fit for a school board. Paragraph II(d) covered "Consideration of the acquisition, sale, or lease of real or personal property which, if discussed in public, would likely benefit a party or parties whose interests are adverse to those of the general community.", which would fit the evening's long discussion of properties for an alternative program, though nobody on the recording says what the session was about.

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) required the motion to "state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II" and a roll-call vote; a motion was made, seconded and taken by roll call, and a citation was spoken, which is to the board's credit. What cannot be confirmed here is which exemption it was, whether the discussion stayed within it (I(c)), and whether the nonpublic minutes were disclosed within 72 hours or sealed by a recorded two-thirds vote (III). The minutes of this meeting and of the nonpublic session would settle all three; none are online.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(d), II(j), III (2015 codification, the text in force on June 1, 2016).

OBSERVATION About $2.7 million in out-of-district special education costs, including students who moved in already placed; several legal descriptions were the speakers' characterizations

The special education director reported at 0:51:18 that four students went to out-of-district placements on July 1 at a cost of about $350,000 (he speaks of a gap between budget development and the placements, though the transcript says they were "completely budgeted for", so whether they were in the budget is unclear), and four more moved in already placed, making the district "the district of liability" (0:52:26); together "well over $500,000". The business administrator and a member arrived at $2.7 million for all out-of-district placements (1:11:35). Out-of-district placements are the same pressure the business administrator had described two weeks earlier when he froze spending (see the May 18, 2016 page). The administration also reported a $750,000 vendor proposal for an in-district alternative program and a search for property.

Three statements about the law were not verified here and are recorded as the speakers' characterizations: that the receiving district becomes liable when a placed student moves in ("That is the law."); that for court placements "the court caps it at 50 grand" with the state taking over "under RSA 402", which does not match any provision this page verified; and a member's description of an "unfunded mandate". The 3 1/2 figure the business administrator mentioned does match New Hampshire's catastrophic special education aid threshold as it then read, RSA 186-C:18, III, under which the State paid 80 percent of a child's costs above 3 1/2 times the state average expenditure per pupil; that is a state reimbursement formula, not a cap on what a district pays, and whether he meant it is unclear.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2016 codification; catastrophic aid threshold and proration).

OBSERVATION A member's description of a state law defining transgender status could not be verified; federal guidance issued nineteen days earlier was not mentioned

After the policy votes, Brent Ferland said at 2:09:02: "They passed a law with the attorney general stating that a transgender is somebody that has been in counseling for six months". This page did not verify any New Hampshire statute in force on June 1, 2016 matching that description, and it is recorded as the speaker's characterization. The superintendent preferred the practice of handling cases individually and privately, and the principal proposed handbook language; no action was taken. The superintendent's statement that "legally a student handbook really is policy" is likewise his characterization.

The discussion did not mention the Departments of Education and Justice's Dear Colleague Letter on transgender students of May 13, 2016, which then stated the federal agencies' Title IX position on facilities access. It was guidance, not regulation, and was withdrawn on February 22, 2017. A transgender students policy reached the board as a first reading on August 17, 2016.

Sources: ED/DOJ Dear Colleague Letter on Transgender Students, May 13, 2016 (guidance); withdrawal letter of February 22, 2017.
