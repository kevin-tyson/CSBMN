MEDIUM The student school board seat required since January 2023 was unfilled at this meeting, and the student the administration identified was appointed rather than elected

The agenda prints a Student Board Members heading with no names under it. Presenting policy BBBF at 1:36:54, the Policy Subcommittee member said “under HB 1381 from 20 2022, districts which has high schools are required to have at least one” and that boards were previously “permitted but not required”. The interim superintendent explained at 1:38:23 that “We did have the student representative that we were hoping to bring in soon, so we did table it”, and the approved minutes record that the student was appointed because the district needed someone, and that the position could go back to election. The chair noted at 1:40:24 that “In the past, we've had our student representatives appointed by the principal or other individuals in the school”. The board sent BBBF to a second reading to settle when the term starts.

RSA 189:1-c, in force since January 1, 2023, provides that the school board "shall have at least one nonvoting student member from each public high school maintained by the local school board within its district" (statute text). RSA 194:23-f, II, in the text in force since August 13, 2024, provides that a student board member "shall be chosen by a simple majority vote of the high school student body" (statute text). The seat was not occupied on March 4, 2026, and the method the administration described (appointment) is not the method the statute prescribes. Mitigation on the record: a student had been identified, the 3/18/26 agenda lists two student members expected to attend from April, and the board was drafting a policy to bring its procedure into line. The record here does not show how long the seat has been empty.

Sources: RSA 189:1-c (at least one nonvoting student member from each public high school; 2022, 195:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2023); RSA 194:23-f (student member chosen by majority vote of the high school student body; one-year term; text as amended by 2024, 69:1, eff. Aug. 13, 2024); 3.4.26 approved minutes, Policy Subcommittee item; 3.4.26 agenda

MEDIUM The approved minutes leave the list of members present blank and give no start time

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to include "the names of members" and, since August 22, 2025 (2025, 112:1), "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes" (statute text). The approved minutes for this meeting print Present: with nothing after it, record only Absent: Arlene Hawkins, and give no call-to-order time; they do give the adjournment (9:00 p.m.) and name the clerk as author. Every vote in the minutes is recorded as all present voting in favor, so a reader of the minutes alone cannot tell which members cast those votes. The recording shows the chair calling the meeting to order at 0:00:07; the Drive record for the minutes document places its creation at 6:30 p.m. that evening, consistent with the agenda's 6:30 start.

The gap is one of documentation, not of conduct: the roll call was taken on tape at 0:00:39, and the presence of Michael Petrin, who has no attributed rows in the dialogue file, rests on the minutes' naming him in the homework exchange and listing only Hawkins as absent. The board approved these minutes on March 18, 2026 with a request to add the presentations, not the missing names.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (notice; minutes contents, including start and end time as amended by 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025; mover and seconder; 5 business days); 3.4.26 approved minutes; 3.18.26 approved minutes (approval of the 3.4.26 minutes)

MEDIUM Audit documentation going back to FY2023 is still being assembled by hand, and the payroll process depends on one departed employee

Asked for an audit update at 0:07:42, the interim business administrator said of the regular financial audits that “There's about 100 that they've asked, and. And it spans multiple years” and that “when you go back several years ago, trying to find the documentation because it wasn't scanned in”. Documents from 2025 on were scanned, “Although I'm finding that it's spotty”, and “I'm finding that sometimes it was stored with the wrong transaction”. At 0:09:49: “our payroll person has left the district. Okay. And that process is manual”; time-clock data “It's not imported into our accounting system”. At 0:10:53 he set the order of work: “my priority is, is making sure payroll is, is is being paid”. The forensic audit had not started; the firm was still running a conflict check at 0:08:10.

These are the official's own descriptions of control weaknesses: records that cannot be located, transactions filed against the wrong entry, and a manual payroll that rests on one position. For federal grant money, 2 CFR 200.303(a) requires the district to "Establish, document, and maintain effective internal control over the Federal award" (regulation text). The timing question is separate. Under 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) a single-audit reporting package is due no later than nine months after fiscal year end, which for FY2023 was March 31, 2024; the FY2023 audit was still at the document-request stage on this date. Whether the single-audit requirement applies turns on the district's federal expenditures in each year (2 CFR 200.501), which are not in this record, so the page grades this MEDIUM rather than HIGH. The administrator also reported active remediation (automating the payroll import).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.303(a) (effective internal control over federal awards); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (single-audit reporting package due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after fiscal year end); 2 CFR 200.501 (single audit at $750,000 in federal expenditures; $1,000,000 for fiscal years beginning on or after Oct. 1, 2024); 3.4.26 approved minutes, Finance Update

OBSERVATION The consent agenda, including approval of the manifest, passed by general consent with no motion recorded, and no manifest is in the packet

At 0:00:56 the chair listed the consent items and added “just acknowledgment that the board has to review and approve the manifest this evening as well”, then asked “Is there any objection to moving the consent agenda as written, or is there any alterations?” After the calendar was pulled at the interim superintendent's request (0:01:31), the meeting moved on with no motion, second or vote. The minutes record Consent approval. The agenda line reads Approval of Manifest: with nothing after it, and none of the nine documents in the packet folder is a manifest.

Approval by unanimous consent is a recognized parliamentary method, and no statute reviewed here forbids it. Two rules make the record of it matter: RSA 197:23-a lets the treasurer pay district money only on orders of the school board, which is what manifest approval supplies, and RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to name the members who made or seconded each motion. A reader cannot tell from the public record what payments the board authorized on March 4.

She said at 1:30:25 “I did note that the RSA specifies 24 hours for a regular meeting, but past practice is a week for”, and at 1:30:52 “we generally have a practice of one week in advance for agendas”.
