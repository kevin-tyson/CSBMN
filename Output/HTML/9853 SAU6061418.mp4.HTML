Some of what the minutes would fix is unclear on tape: the clerk's roll calls do not audibly call every member present (0:12:25, 0:15:33), the chair-election seconds are attributed inconsistently, and the motion on the separation costs was restated by the chair in different words from the mover's (1:39:17, 1:43:27).

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for 99 years with no statutory reason stated, on this night and on May 10

Horsky moved "to seal the nonpublic sessions minutes for 99 years." (0:15:00); Pierce seconded and the roll call followed (0:15:33-0:15:57). No one stated why the minutes should be withheld. The same was true, as read into the record, of the May 10 session: "a motion was made to seal the minutes for 99 years" (0:02:18), passed nine to none with one abstention (0:02:47). The nonpublic decision itself was announced in public (0:16:21), but the board's chair also described "the agreement that we made tonight with Middleton" as a cost the SAU must fund (1:27:12), and its terms are within what was sealed.

RSA 91-A:3, III, in the text in force in 2018, requires minutes and decisions of a nonpublic session to be disclosed within 72 hours unless the body, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", determines that divulging them would likely affect the reputation of a non-member, render a proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism; the information may then be withheld "until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply". The roll-call votes satisfy the two-thirds and public-session requirements. What the recording does not show is the determination itself, and a fixed 99-year term is not the statute's measure. The minutes of the session may record a determination that the recording does not; they do not survive (flag 1). This is a documentation gap, not a finding that the seal was unjustified.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(a) and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; 280:1)

OBSERVATION Three points of SAU law the transition touched: the superintendent office, the acting superintendent's pay, and the chair's mid-year replacement

The office. A member said "It's a legal requirement to have a superintendent." (0:31:59). RSA 194-C:5, II(a) says the opposite of the office: "School districts shall not be required to have a superintendent and may assign these services to one or more administrative personnel". What the law requires is that the SAU provide superintendent services, which is what the board arranged by naming an acting superintendent the same night.

The pay. The board named Cory LeClair acting superintendent with "details to be ironed out by the chairs of the Claremont and Unity School Board" (0:49:02), after the chair said compensation would need to be worked out (0:39:41). RSA 194-C:5, III provides that the SAU board "shall fix the salaries of all school administrative unit personnel". A member asked that the chairs bring the terms back for the board's agreement (0:47:02); the motion as passed does not say so. Whether the terms returned to the full board is a question for the June 28 and later records.

The chair. RSA 194-C:5, I has the SAU board meet between April 1 and June 1 each year and "organize by choosing a chairperson, a secretary, and a treasurer". Tonight's election replaced a chair who stepped down mid-term (2:11:11); the statute does not address that case, and nothing in the record suggests the election was improper. The 6-4 hand count (2:19:32, 2:19:39) was taken without a weighted vote, which RSA 194-C:8 reserves for “the demand of a majority of the members of any board present and voting”; none was made.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, I, II(a) and III (1996, 298:3; never amended); RSA 194-C:8 (weighted voting on demand; 1996, 298:3)
