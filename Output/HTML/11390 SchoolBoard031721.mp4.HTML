MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

This meeting swore in the clerk and two members, elected the chair and a second officer, and delegated the reopening decision to the superintendent, and the recording is now the only public account. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force since January 1, 2019, requires minutes that include the names of members, persons appearing and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions, requires the names of the members who moved and seconded each motion to be recorded, and requires the minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the board approves the March 3 minutes on tape (0:44:15), the clerk is present and calls every vote, and an agenda existed and was amended (0:42:54), so a minutes routine was operating. What is missing is any public copy now. The movers and seconders the minutes had to carry are, on this recording, Petrin and Whitney for the agenda and the March 3 minutes, Towle and Whitney for the chair, and Horsky, Whitney and Towle for the reopening motion; the second officer's nomination and the adjournment have no seconder that the transcript captures.

OBSERVATION A $450,000 federal equipment grant and a CARES-funded summer program are announced, with no acceptance step on this record; the grant was accepted on April 7

The superintendent reported that SAU 6, with Fall Mountain and Newport, had received a federal technology grant ("it's $1 million grant"), from which Claremont expects "approximately $450,000 worth of technology equipment" (0:21:38), and a six-week summer program paid for with CARES money (0:17:30, 0:27:38). He also said the grant carries "a 10% match" (0:23:21), which would be district money. RSA 198:20-b, I, as in force in 2021, lets a school board "apply for, accept and expend" unanticipated money "from a state, federal or other governmental unit or a private source" only where the district has adopted an enabling article, and ¶III(a) requires a prior public hearing, with seven days' newspaper notice, for $5,000 or more. Nothing on this recording shows a hearing or an acceptance vote for either. The next meeting answers part of this: on April 7 the superintendent told the board it should have been asked on March 17 to approve the grant, the grant was added on the night to that meeting's public hearing, and the board accepted $451,832 with a 15 percent match (see the April 7, 2021 page, flag 1, which examines that hearing's notice). Neither recording shows a hearing or acceptance for the CARES money. That is why this is an observation here. The documents that would settle the CARES question are the district's enabling article and any 2020 or 2021 hearing notice.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1; $5,000 hearing threshold until 2023, 38:1)

OBSERVATION The needle exchange program's "promised quarterly updates" never reached the board

Horsky asked whether the board had received "any of the promised quarterly updates" from the syringe service program, and the answer was No. (1:34:27, 1:34:40); he said the program "obviously haven't followed through on a promise they made three years ago" (1:34:42). Since August 11, 2018, RSA 318-B:45 has barred a syringe service program from a drug-free school zone (school property and 1,000 feet around it, RSA 193-B:1, II) unless "the applicable district school board" grants an exception at the program administrator's request. The statute sets no reporting condition, so the updates were a term of the board's own exception, and the board learned on this night that it had gone unenforced. No rule-based defect is shown; the point is that the board's oversight of an exception only it can grant depended on reports it never received. The exception vote itself predates every record this page relies on. At the next meeting the administration reported that the program had left the hospital site in March 2020, and the board revoked the permission (see the April 7, 2021 page).

Sources: RSA 318-B:45 (2018 codification; exception sentence added by 2018, 254:2, eff. Aug. 11, 2018); RSA 193-B:1, II (drug-free school zone: school property and within 1,000 feet)

OBSERVATION A daytime budget subcommittee and an April retreat: both are covered by the Right-to-Know Law

The chair described the budget subcommittee as meeting "during the actual school day" with directors and administrators (1:16:49), proposed regular subcommittee report-outs (1:15:53), and set a retreat of the full board for Sunday, April 18, at the community center (1:13:05, 1:13:47). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto" a public body, and a retreat at which a quorum discusses board business is a meeting under RSA 91-A:2, I. Each therefore needed 24 hours' posted notice and minutes. Nothing on this recording says those duties were skipped, and no share reaches back to 2021 to test it; the observation records what the duties were. Whitney's proposed accountability committee, with "a mechanism by which to easily look up every motion that was passed" (1:14:52), addresses the same need from the board's side. On May 5 the board approved minutes of the April 18 retreat (see the May 5, 2021 page).

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification)
