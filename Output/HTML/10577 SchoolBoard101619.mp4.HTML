MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes were sealed by a voice vote, with no count and no statutory reason stated on the record

Entry into nonpublic session followed the statute's form: the motion at 0:28:01 cited "RSA 91. Dash a colon three, sub paragraph two" and read out the words of the legal-advice exemption, and the chair called a roll-call vote. After the session, a member moved at 0:29:51 "to seal the minutes for 83 years." The chair put the question as "All those in favor?" the tape records one Aye., and the chair then asked for any opposed and moved on. In 2019, RSA 91-A:3, III required nonpublic minutes and decisions to be "publicly disclosed within 72 hours of the meeting, unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism." On the recording, no member states which of those determinations the board was making, and there is no roll call or tally to show a two-thirds vote.

Mitigating: a unanimous voice vote can be recorded as such in the minutes, and a determination may appear there; the minutes are not online to check (flag 3). Legal advice to the board is the kind of matter where disclosure could "render the proposed action ineffective", so a lawful seal was plausibly available. The duration, "83 years" as transcribed, is probably an ASR error and is not relied on here. The grade is MEDIUM because the record gap is in documentation, not in the power to seal.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(l) and III (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1 and 280:1).

The superintendent said "We also have our budget, workshop this Sunday." with the board (0:38:06), and at 1:10:38 members settled on 10:00 at the community center, in the big room. Sunday was October 20, 2019. In 2019 RSA 91-A:2, I defined a meeting as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body ... for the purpose of discussing or acting upon a matter or matters over which the public body has supervision, control, jurisdiction, or advisory power", and 91-A:2, II required 24 hours' posted notice and minutes. A board retreat on the budget fits that definition. Two weeks earlier the chair had said the retreat would be warned as "a regular board meeting open to the public, with citizens comments" (October 2 page, recording 10558, flag 4), which is the right approach; the venue then given was the tech center, while this recording settles on the community center, so a posted notice would have needed the final location. The recording is itself a public announcement of the date, time and place, and the chair scheduled a "report out on the retreat" for the next meeting (1:07:53). Whether a formal notice was posted, and whether minutes were kept, cannot be checked: no record from 2019 is online, and the project holds no recording of October 20. This is recorded as an observation.
