MEDIUM FY2017 audits still unfinished, FY2018 promised by year end

The superintendent reports that “the 17 audits should be completed by the end of October” and the 2018 audits “by the end of December” (0:23:01). Fiscal 2017 ended June 30, 2017. Where a district spends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year, the single-audit reporting package is due within nine months of year end: March 31, 2018 for FY2017 and March 31, 2019 for FY2018. This recording gives no federal-expenditure figure, so the page cannot confirm that the threshold applies, which keeps this at MEDIUM. Whitney's request for a public account of the safeguards that “would have protected the district from the losses that it” “Did and almost suffered” (1:25:23) and the superintendent's reference to coming “audit letter” recommendations (1:26:24) show the board treating the backlog as a control problem.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2019 edition (report due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2019 edition ($750,000 threshold)

OBSERVATION Catastrophic-aid threshold described as “three and a half times the tuition rate”; the statute measures it against state average expenditure per pupil

Nester told the board the district can claim special-education aid above “three and a half times the tuition rate”, “around 56,000”, and receives “62% of what the state promises” (1:14:40 to 1:15:16). In the statute as it then read, the threshold is three and one-half times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil, not a tuition rate; the state is liable for 80 percent of the cost above it; and when the appropriation falls short the payments are prorated proportionally based on entitlement, with no floor. A 62 percent payment is consistent with that proration, though the page cannot verify the figure. The 80-percent-of-entitlement floor some later pages discuss was added in 2025 and does not apply here.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III, 2019 codification (3½ times state average expenditure per pupil; 80 percent state share; proportional proration; source note ends 2017, 156)
