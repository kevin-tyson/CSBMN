For this meeting none of those records survives in the district's shares, so the exemption claimed, the roll call and the officer vote cannot be checked. Graded MEDIUM: the press report places the vote in public session, which the law permits, but the only public account of the board's most consequential act that night is a newspaper. OBSERVATION A $500 gift was added to the agenda at the table, and the "statute says 500" remark has no match in the gift statute

The superintendent brought "acceptance of a $500 grant" as one of "four brief amendments" (0:00:24), and the board accepted it the same night (1:46:04). For unanticipated money under $5,000, RSA 198:20-b, III(b) requires the board to "post notice of the funds in the agenda" and to "include notice in the minutes", with acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting". Acceptance in public session at a regular meeting is on the recording. Whether the posted agenda gave notice cannot be checked, because no agenda survives; the word "amendments" suggests it did not. The statute also presumes the voters adopted the enabling article under ¶I, which this record does not show. Later the superintendent told the board "The statute says 500." on the superintendent's own gift authority (1:57:12). RSA 198:20-b contains no $500 figure, and this page identified no statute he may have meant; the amount may come from the draft gifts policy then at first reading. The posted agenda, the minutes and the policy text would settle each point.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1; no $500 figure in the section, read 2026-10-02)

OBSERVATION A member's question about recording audio on school buses was left open, though the statute answers it

Rapp said it is "against state law to record audio without permission" (1:55:35), and the superintendent said he was not aware of that and would check (1:56:02, 1:56:46); the bus-surveillance policy moved to second reading with the other four (1:58:37). New Hampshire's wiretapping statute carries a specific exception: it is not unlawful for the owner or operator of a school bus to make "an audio recording in conjunction with a video recording of the interior of the school bus" if the school board authorizes it, and the recording may not be kept longer than 10 school days unless it is relevant to a disciplinary proceeding or a court orders it kept. That exception took effect in 2016 and 2017. The superintendent's own description of the policy, which "incorporates the statutory limitations on viewing and retaining audio recordings" (1:53:39), is consistent with it. The point for a reader is that board authorization is the condition the statute sets, so the policy vote itself carries legal weight.

Sources: RSA 570-A:2, II(k) (source note ends 2016, 169:1, eff. June 3, 2016; 322:2, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)
