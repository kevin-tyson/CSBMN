MEDIUM The annual organization chose a chair and a vice chair but no secretary, and the treasurer was continued without a vote

With the superintendent presiding, Michael Petrin and Frank Sprague were nominated for chair (0:02:15); Petrin declined, “I have the Claremont chair at this point” (0:02:55); Marjorie Erickson, absent, was nominated and Sprague withdrew in her favor (0:04:28); Erickson was elected by voice vote (0:04:38). Sprague was then elected vice chair after a nomination of Rocco Ruggeri was withdrawn on the point that “we've always split the power between the two” [Claremont and Unity] (0:05:33). On the treasurer, Steven Horsky asked whether the role is “a annual duty or is it for the life of my turn” and the superintendent answered “I guess for keeps” (0:07:43); no motion followed. No secretary was nominated.

RSA 194-C:5, I directs the SAU board to meet between April 1 and June 1 each year and to organize by choosing a chairperson, a secretary and a treasurer. Vice chair is not one of the three statutory offices. The window was still open on April 21, and the board met again on May 19, so this is a process gap on this record rather than a missed deadline. Whether the May 19 meeting chose a secretary or voted on the treasurer is a question for that meeting’s record.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, I (SAU board meets between April 1 and June 1 and organizes by choosing a chairperson, a secretary and a treasurer; 1996, 298:3, unamended)

MEDIUM Stipends and raises for SAU staff were set at the superintendent’s level outside the adopted budget, though the SAU board fixes SAU salaries

Reviewing the monthly financial report, Frank Sprague found cell-phone and mileage payments that “come to $16,000” with no budget line (0:22:58). The assistant superintendent for finance explained “there was a decision that was made, at the superintendent level, that this was a part of the contract” (0:23:26). Negative salary variances in the superintendent’s office, special education administration and the business office reflected raises the superintendent “negotiated with his direct reports” (0:29:57). On the merit pool, the superintendent said that in earlier years “We did the same, a similar thing last year where everyone got the same” without the board-approved evaluation (0:44:34), and that he checked first with “both board chairs” (0:58:11).

RSA 194-C:5, III provides that the SAU board shall fix the salaries of all SAU personnel. Pay set by individual negotiation within a budget the board adopted may be defensible, and the superintendent says the board chairs were consulted; but stipends with no budget line, raises above budgeted amounts and a uniform distribution of the merit pool are pay decisions the record does not show the full board making. Sprague drew the same connection on the floor ({T(320)}). What would settle it is any SAU board vote or delegation approving the stipends, the raises and the earlier merit distributions.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (the SAU board fixes the salaries of all SAU personnel; 1996, 298:3, unamended)

POSITIVE Both split merit-pay questions were decided by roll call, so each member’s vote is on the recording

Before the first merit-pay vote the vice chair said “I'm going to ask a roll call vote because it sounds like it's going to be split votes” (1:07:16). The motion to suspend merit pay failed 4–6 (1:13:34) and the amended motion to release it failed 5–5 (1:25:47), each with names called by the clerk. No statute required roll calls here: RSA 91-A requires them for entering nonpublic session and for meetings with remote participation, and RSA 194-C:8 weighted voting applies only on a majority’s demand, which no one made. Roll calls on contested pay questions give the public a record a voice vote would not.

Sources: RSA 194-C:8 (weighted votes used only on the demand of a majority of members present and voting); RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1: minutes with names, subject matter, final decisions, mover and seconder; open to inspection within 5 business days); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) (2022 codification: roll call required on a motion to enter nonpublic session)
