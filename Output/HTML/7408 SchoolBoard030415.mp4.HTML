OBSERVATION The superintendent opened tuition talks with Unity before raising the idea with the board

In his own words, the superintendent began "some initial discussions with the the Unity School District" about Unity students attending Stevens "without the Claremont School Board knowing" (0:40:43), and "brought it up actually without without talking to any of you" (0:58:39). He presented the rate change to the board only after those contacts, and said he could not claim Unity's support. No rule verified for this page governs informal contacts of this kind, and he was candid about them on the record. A tuition rate, and any tuition agreement, is the board's decision; the record shows the board taking up the question afterward.

Sources: Cablecast: CLAREMONT SCHOOL BOARD 3/4; Dialogue/7408 SchoolBoard030415.mp4.CSV (rows 200 and 336)

OBSERVATION Hold on a $289,000 contractor payment was directed by the chair, not voted

The owner's representative and architect recommended approving the contractor's last payment request of $289,000 while holding its release (1:14:27). The chair then said "I'd like to hold any payments until we get an update at our board meeting on the 18th of March" (1:17:25). No motion was made and no vote taken, so the record does not show a board decision either to approve or to withhold the payment. With $534,588.38 left on a contract of just under $11 million, the hold was a real lever, as the chair himself said ("our leverage", 1:18:26). Whether the payment was approved, and when, would be in the March 18 minutes, which are not available.

Sources: Cablecast: CLAREMONT SCHOOL BOARD 3/4; Dialogue/7408 SchoolBoard030415.mp4.CSV (rows 405 to 433)

OBSERVATION Board members were not receiving regular financial statements

Bob said he had seen a monthly financial statement only twice since joining the board (1:33:09); the business administrator said the statements are "normally" presented at the first meeting of the month, and the chair said they last arrived consistently under a previous business officer (1:35:33). A board that sets a $33 million budget without regular expenditure reports has limited means to see overspending early. No statute verified for this page sets a monthly reporting duty, so this is an observation about oversight practice. The business administrator agreed to resume the reports.

Sources: Cablecast: CLAREMONT SCHOOL BOARD 3/4
