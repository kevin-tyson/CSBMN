Claremont School Board, March 6, 2019

Last regular meeting before the March 12, 2019 election. The board accepted two donations, approved a summer carpentry course for Sullivan County inmates at the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center for $7,500, adopted a group of policies on second and final reading, heard the superintendent search was down to two finalists, and weighed whether to renew the food service contract. OBSERVATION The superintendent search committee's sessions are described, but their public record is not visible

The vice chair described a process run by a search committee with a hired consultant: five candidates interviewed, three in video interviews, two finalists in "3 to 4 hour storytelling interview" scored by board members, a public meet and greet, and a four-hour committee session the night before this meeting (0:14:17 to 0:15:46). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto" a public body, so a search committee of the SAU 6 board carries notice and minutes duties. Candidate interviews could lawfully be held in nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(b), "The hiring of any person as a public employee", but only on a motion naming the exemption and a roll-call vote under 91-A:3, I(b), with minutes kept. Whether the committee's sessions were noticed and minuted is outside this recording, and SAU 6 records from 2019 are not in any district share. One finalist, Cory, was the acting superintendent presenting at this meeting; the update was given by the vice chair, not by her. The SAU 6 board's decision was set for March 7 (0:15:59).

OBSERVATION Early intervention was framed as a way to reduce special education identification

The chair asked about the preschool as "a method by which we could reduce identification for special end in a beginning" (0:57:09). The director explained that early support might keep typical children from "being identified and needing an IEP" (0:57:32), and added "we're not so focused on identifying we're more focused on supports. But as we get closer to kindergarten, if somebody definitely needs ongoing supports, then we assess and then we identify." (0:58:21). Federal law requires that "All children with disabilities residing in the State" be identified, located and evaluated. Support that makes later identification unnecessary is lawful; delaying the evaluation of a child already suspected of a disability is not. Nothing on the recording shows a delayed evaluation, and the acting superintendent cautioned that "there are requirements by law" (0:58:07). The statement is recorded because the "reduce identification" framing is the kind a reviewer should test against the district's child-find practice.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §1412(a)(3)(A) (IDEA child find: all children with disabilities identified, located and evaluated)

POSITIVE State guidance obtained before the board changed its food service contract

Before the board debated changing a la carte sales, the acting superintendent asked the Department of Education's contracts manager what would count as a material change, and read the state's language to the board: a significant change "that would have caused the vendor to have issued a different proposal during the RFP process" means "you would have to go back out to bid" (1:24:24). She recommended running any change past the department first (1:15:24). The business administrator described the contract as "a five year agreement, but it's five individual years" (1:31:21), which matches the federal limit of a one-year term with up to four renewals for food service management company contracts. The provider was invited to answer questions in public at the next meeting, and the vice chair confirmed nothing about it needed to be discussed out of public view (1:32:11).

Sources: 7 CFR 210.16(a) and (d), 2019 annual edition (management company contracts: state oversight, procurement, one-year term with no more than four renewals)
