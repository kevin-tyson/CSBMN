OBSERVATION A planned student survey on adverse childhood experiences falls under a parental-consent rule that changed in 2017

The assistant superintendent said the district intends to learn "how many of our students, at least at the middle school and high school, have experienced adverse childhood experiences", "Working within the regulations around administering surveys" (2:08:14). RSA 186:11, IX-d governs non-academic surveys. The 2016 codification required notice, review "at least 10 days prior to distribution", and a way for a parent to "opt out of the non-academic survey or questionnaire". The 2017 codification, which reflects three 2017 chapters effective June 16, June 24 and September 16, 2017, provides that "no student shall be required to volunteer for or submit to a non-academic survey or questionnaire" without "written consent of a parent or legal guardian", with an exception for the CDC youth risk behavior survey. Which of those chapters made the change is not isolated here, so whether written consent applied on the meeting date is unverified; it applied to any survey given from September 16, 2017 at the latest. An adverse-childhood-experiences questionnaire is non-academic. This is flagged so a reader can check, when the survey was given, which rule governed it and how consent was obtained.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-d (2016 codification: opt-out); RSA 186:11, IX-d (2017 codification: written consent)

POSITIVE The district explained a lower homeless count by a change in the federal definition rather than claiming improvement

Reporting 156 students identified as homeless out of about 1,753, the assistant superintendent said the number was lower "primarily because in December of 2016, December 10th to be exact, and McKinney Vento law changed", so that students awaiting foster care are no longer counted (2:00:53 to 2:01:46). U.S. Department of Education guidance confirms that the Every Student Succeeds Act removed "awaiting foster care placement" from the McKinney-Vento definition effective December 10, 2016 for states without a covered statute. Telling a public meeting that a falling number reflects a definition, not progress, is the kind of data candor that lets a reader trust the next figure.

Sources: U.S. Department of Education, Education for Homeless Children and Youths Non-Regulatory Guidance (July 2016, updated Aug. 2018): ESSA §9105(b) effective dates
