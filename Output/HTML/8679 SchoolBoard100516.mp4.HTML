OBSERVATION A member's resignation treated as final by the chair, and the board kept from voting on her request to rescind

The chair announced that Rebecca Ferland's resignation “has been accepted” and said the board does not vote on resignations, while acknowledging “The only thing that we could do would be to vote on whether we wanted to accept our her reconsideration” (0:11:12). When Brent Ferland moved to reconsider, the chair ruled: “It's not on the agenda” (0:11:39). The chair had himself added the resignation to the agenda at the start of the meeting (0:01:35). At the end of the meeting Adrian asked to place her request on a future agenda, and the chair answered “No we're not we're going to move on” (1:35:57). Neither ruling was put to the board.

No rule verified for this page settles when a New Hampshire school board member's resignation takes effect or whether it may be withdrawn. RSA 91-A:2, II required notice of the meeting's time and place, not an agenda, so the agenda ground was the board's own practice rather than the statute. Brent Ferland cited district policy BBC as requiring a letter to the chair with a copy to the district clerk (0:09:41); the policy is not available to check. Irish told him he had a conflict of interest because the resigning member is his mother (0:21:06); no recusal was ruled on. The Valley News reported that she resigned on October 3 by walking out of the SAU 6 meeting and emailing her resignation, which it quoted as citing "bully tactics of some members of the board" (reporting). The documents that would settle this are the October 3 email, the two legal opinions the chair cited (0:11:09), policy BBC, and the board's adopted parliamentary rules.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification: notice of time and place); Valley News, Oct. 6, 2016, Patrick O'Grady (reporting)

OBSERVATION Condom policy adopted on second read the night its parental-consent rule changed

The September 21 first read made access depend on parental permission. On second read the superintendent reported “the adjustment is the the opt out” (0:56:52), a reversal of the default for students whose parents do nothing. The board then waived the district's usual third reading and adopted the policy with a further change made at the table (1:24:10). The superintendent described three readings as the norm at the next meeting (October 19, 2016 page). No statute governs the number of readings; the observation is that the adopted text, with both changes, was never read or posted in its final form before the vote, and no copy survives.

Sources: recording only; no rule cited

POSITIVE Vacancy to be advertised and filled by public interview

RSA 197:26 requires only that the board fill the vacancy until the next annual meeting, and RSA 671:33, II(a) that appointees serve until the next district election. The chair committed to an advertisement in the paper (0:16:29) and to interviews in which candidates “have them speak” before the board decides. An open, advertised process goes beyond what the statute requires. The process itself was set at the next meeting (October 19, 2016 page).

Sources: RSA 197:26 (2016 codification); RSA 671:33, II(a) (2016 codification; source note ends 2013, 114:1)
