Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer’s aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on, as it stood on November 30, 2023; an Observation rests on no confirmed rule. With no agenda or minutes, every flag rests on the recording.

HIGH A public body met for 84 minutes on the district budget and left no notice, agenda, packet or minutes anywhere the public can find them

“Public body” includes “any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto” (RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), statute text). All three members were present and deliberated on the operating budget, which is a meeting under RSA 91-A:2, I. RSA 91-A:2, II requires 24 hours’ notice and minutes open to inspection within five business days. MAP.md records a search of the 2023 Meeting Documents archive, both candidate year folders of the Meeting Minutes share, the next board packet and keyword searches (verified 2026-08-28), with nothing found. The district’s Claremont Finance Sub Committee folder (created October 27, 2022) returned no files to the earlier build’s check (2026-08-29) or to a Drive query on 2026-09-25; the project knows that query can under-report. This is the unmitigated limb of the project’s rule: no one on the recording says minutes are being kept or will follow. The December 6 board minutes report only “Sessions have been recorded on CCTV”, which points to a broadcast, not a record. Mitigation: no decision was taken; the session was deliberately televised; and absence from a share is not proof that no minutes were ever written. The board had a clerk from November 15, and no one was assigned to minute this.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2023, 188:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); approved board minutes, December 6, 2023.

MEDIUM The ESSER sunset was not costed, and three portable classrooms to be bought with ESSER money had not gone out to bid ten months before the obligation deadline

The superintendent: “I didn’t estimate the budget impact” (0:04:15), with the funded posts to “expire in September of 24” (0:04:47). ARP ESSER funds had to be obligated by September 30, 2024 (U.S. Department of Education). The portables are “funded in the, in the Esser [ESSER] monies” but not in the book the committee was reading (0:06:11), and “We still have to go out to bid. We have to start everything on that” (1:16:43). Federal procurement standards (2 CFR 200.318 to 200.327: competition, documented cost analysis, records) apply to that purchase. The board’s own November 15 minutes record that the district “Didn’t get to use about 300,000 of the ESSER II”. Mitigation: a sunset list exists, some posts are proposed for local funding, and vendor plans were said to be in hand. Nothing here is a violation; it is an uncosted cliff and an unstarted procurement, stated by the officers responsible.

Sources: U.S. Department of Education, ARP ESSER obligation and liquidation deadlines; 2 CFR 200.318 (2023 edition), with 200.319–200.327; approved board minutes, November 15, 2023.

MEDIUM Three errors in the current adopted budget surfaced in one sitting, found by conversation rather than reconciliation

Disnard’s secretary line “was incorrectly entered last year” (0:25:39); Disnard’s special-education salaries fell about $71,000 because of “a budgeting error from last year” (0:36:06), which Crawford immediately used to fund the new social worker; and Bluff’s special-education salaries were “a was a budgeting error” (0:42:50). These lines were appropriated by the voters in March 2023. An entry error found and corrected breaks no statute; RSA 32:10 governs moving money between appropriations during the year. The pattern is a control weakness, and a correction in one line becoming the funding for a new position in another deserves a reconciliation on paper. The board’s December 6 minutes note “Discussion of anomalies that occurred last year.” Mitigation: each error was acknowledged at once, one had been rechecked the night before (0:42:58), and all released money rather than requiring more.

Sources: RSA 32:10 (2017, 127:4); approved board minutes, December 6, 2023.

OBSERVATION A recurring $250,000-a-year curriculum cost was identified, and every source floated for it was one-time money

Koski put the standing cost at “$250,000 a year each year for curriculum” (1:22:27); workbooks are consumable every year (1:14:54); grades 6–12 have “a curricula” but no program. The sources floated: a state extraordinary need grant the chair described as “One year, just last year” that he believed was paying for this year’s curriculum (1:14:08–1:14:32); ESSER, ending in 2024; and fund balance (“say, a $400,000 fund balance”, 1:22:49). Using retained year-end funds is lawful: RSA 198:4-b, II lets a district that has voted to do so retain up to 5 percent of its net assessment, and requires a public hearing before spending it. The observation is about sustainability, not legality: a known recurring cost was matched only with non-recurring money, and nobody said so. The board chair drove the question in the right direction (flag 11).

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, I and II (2020, 38:25); RSA 198:40-f (extraordinary need grants; 2023 codification).

OBSERVATION Every elementary behavioral specialist is out of the budget, and the sum placed in the paraprofessional lines to absorb them was not disclosed

“There are no behavioral support specialists or the Haverhill [behavioral] support in any of these budget lines” (1:08:01). The intent is to move staff now on letters of agreement into the paraprofessional contract (1:09:00). Asked whether money had been built into the para lines, the superintendent said “That’s what I can’t talk about” and then “But yes yes yes” (1:09:45–1:09:47). The elementary total of 0.66 percent (1:11:31) therefore rests on an undisclosed figure. Strategy for collective bargaining is excluded from the definition of a meeting (RSA 91-A:2, I(a)), so reticence about a bargaining position has a basis; two of the three members were the board’s own negotiators. The amounts must be disclosed or discussed at the budget hearing: RSA 32:5 requires “All purposes and amounts of appropriations” to be disclosed or discussed “at the final hearing” (statute text), held January 3, 2024.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I(a); RSA 32:5, II (2023 codification).

OBSERVATION A new state instructional mandate reaches the budget as a workbook line

Lewis: “the state of New Hampshire, as you know, now requires the teaching of cursive handwriting” (0:15:31). She is right. RSA 189:11-c requires districts to “Provide instruction in cursive writing by the end of fifth grade as a component of English language arts” (statute text), mandatory since 2023, 17:1, effective July 3, 2023. No dollar figure for the cursive materials was stated. The item shows where an unfunded mandate lands.

Sources: RSA 189:11-c (2015, 41:1; 2023, 17:1, eff. July 3, 2023; read 2026-09-25).

OBSERVATION A Stevens schedule change is being costed before any agreement with the union on its impact

The chair asked about impact bargaining and confirmed there is no side letter (0:07:17–0:08:14); the superintendent said the district knows it must bargain and wants to present the option financially first. RSA 273-A:3 obliges the parties to bargain in good faith, and a refusal to do so is an unfair labor practice under RSA 273-A:5, I(e). Nothing shows a refusal; costing first and bargaining next is a lawful order. The budget carries a contingency the document does not show.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3; RSA 273-A:5, I(e).

OBSERVATION The committee reviewed budgets without the staffing schedule it said it needed, and the privacy line was drawn around salaries as well as benefits

Sprague called the staffing detail “really critical” (1:10:06); Henry had run out of time, and no date was set. Henry said individual benefits could not lawfully be shown (1:10:52). New Hampshire has treated teacher salaries as public since Mans v. Lebanon School Board, 112 N.H. 160 (1972): “disclosure of salaries of schoolteachers is not a disclosure of those intimate details which ‘would constitute invasion of privacy’” (opinion text). Individual benefit elections sit closer to the exemption in RSA 91-A:5, IV, which permits withholding but does not require it. The agreed approach, positions with names blinded (1:11:22), works. Separately, Disnard budgets field trips and Bluff relies on its PTO (0:42:25); neither is improper, and the difference went unremarked.

Sources: Mans v. Lebanon School Bd., 112 N.H. 160 (1972) (read 2026-09-25); RSA 91-A:5, IV.
