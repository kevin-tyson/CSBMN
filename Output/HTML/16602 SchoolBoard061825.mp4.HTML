MEDIUM The recording announces the eight fiscal policies "adopted"; the minutes record them moved to second read, and DEA was not on the posted agenda

Sprague moved to adopt DA, DB, DEA, DFA, DGA, DIE and DK with the DAF procedures (2:10:33). After Crawford said the administration had not been at the committee meeting (2:11:10), he withdrew that and moved them "to second read" (2:11:31). The chair then called "All in favor of adopting the motions with a friendly amendment" and declared all those policies have been adopted (2:11:34). The approved minutes record the opposite outcome: "Original motion was to adopt, ... so a friendly amendment was made to move it to a second read". The August 14 packet carries these policies as second-read items and the board adopted them that night, so the minutes' reading is the one the board acted on, and the spoken announcement does not match it. Two further gaps: DB is in this motion although Crawford had just said DB we're going to postpone (2:05:49) and the minutes instead list DBJ as the policy sent back; and DEA (revenues from local tax sources) appears nowhere on the posted agenda's first-read, second-read or review lists. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to record the "final decisions"; where tape and minutes disagree on what was decided, the public record needs one clear answer. The minutes also name Hawkins as seconder of Sprague's motion, which the tape does not make audible.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes: subject matter, final decisions, mover and seconder; 2024 codification); approved minutes 6.18.25; agenda 6.18.25

MEDIUM The FY2022 audit was still unfinished three years after that fiscal year closed

Crawford had understood the FY2022 audit to be complete; Henry said it was not quite complete yet and that she had not spoken to the auditor since going on leave (0:14:06, 0:14:14). Fiscal 2022 ended June 30, 2022. If the district spent $750,000 or more in federal awards in FY2022 (the record here mentions title grants and ESSER money, 1:09:13 and 1:12:36, but gives no annual total), 2 CFR 200.501(a) required a single audit, and 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) required it to be submitted "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period", that is by March 31, 2023. State law does not by itself make the delay a missed deadline: RSA 21-J:19 permits rather than requires an audit within a year. The flag is graded MEDIUM because the federal threshold is not established in this record; the district's schedule of expenditures of federal awards for FY2022 would settle it. The unaudited years also mean the board was making FY2026 decisions without audited opening balances.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (single audit at $750,000; 2022 edition); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (nine-month deadline; 2022 edition); RSA 21-J:19 (independent audits; permissive as to timing)

OBSERVATION The preschool pilot: the federal duty is real, but the board heard no cost, staffing or determination figures it could check

McCosker's premise is sound in law: 34 CFR 300.101(a) makes a free appropriate public education available "to all children residing in the State between the ages of 3 and 21, inclusive", so cutting the district preschool did not end the duty to serve identified three- and four-year-olds. Sprague's objection is about process: the board cut the program, and a replacement arrived as a plan with no staffing count (1:41:25) and no paraprofessional numbers (1:48:26). Three points in the record need checking against documents: the LEA determination, which McCosker called needs intervention (1:21:54) and the minutes call "needs assistance" (two different determination levels; the report itself, which Sprague asked to see, would settle it); the out-of-district $22 million figure (1:43:49), given without a basis; and the net-zero claim, which rests on merging third-grade sections to classes of 19 (1:36:40). No vote was taken.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.101(a) (FAPE for ages 3 through 21); PreSchool Planning 2025-2026.pdf (packet)
