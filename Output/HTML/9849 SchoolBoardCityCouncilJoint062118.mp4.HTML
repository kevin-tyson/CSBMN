Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting, June 21, 2018

Joint meeting of the two governing bodies a week before the superintendent's departure: the city presented him with a State House clock for his legislative work, the bodies agreed to meet again on August 9 on a shared communications (public information officer) position, named education funding their top legislative priority for 2019 and asked their state representatives to refile HB 413 on the state's retirement contribution, and heard updates on the superintendent transition and the city's $17.3 million budget. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the school district's online shares, and the city's records were not inspected. OBSERVATION A new duty for the district: test school drinking water for lead from July 1, 2019

The mayor told the board that SB 247 requires schools and licensed child care facilities to test their drinking water for lead from July 1, 2019 and every five years after, and suggested the board consider a testing policy (0:02:39, 0:03:05). Her description matches the statute. SB 247 was enacted as 2018 N.H. Laws ch. 4, approved February 8, 2018; its section 18, effective July 1, 2019, created RSA 485:17-a, which as first codified provided that "Upon the effective date of this section, and within every 5-year period thereafter", schools and licensed child care facilities "that have not sampled in the prior 2 years shall test". No board action followed on tape. Whether the district tested, and when, is a question for its later records. The section was rewritten by 2022, 325:1, so later meetings need the later text.

Sources: RSA 485:17-a (2019 codification; source 2018, 4:18, eff. July 1, 2019); SB 247 (2018) status: chapter 4, signed Feb. 8, 2018 (LegiScan status page; chaptered text not read); RSA 485:17-a, current text (amended 2022, 325:1)
