SAU 6 Board, January 11, 2024

Regular meeting of the joint Claremont and Unity SAU 6 board. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on, or says that none was verified. The statutes are cited as codified in 2023, the text in force on January 11, 2024.

HIGH Voice votes were taken while a member took part by telephone; the minutes give no reason for her remote attendance, and she could not hear the termination motion when first called

RSA 91-A:2, III governs a meeting at which a member participates other than in person: the reason in-person attendance "is not reasonably practical shall be stated in the minutes" (¶III(a)); each member "must be able to simultaneously hear each other and speak to each other" (¶III(c)); and "All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote" (¶III(e)). The minutes record Marjorie Erickson as "Present via phone", with no reason. She is on the line from the first recorded roll (0:00:39) to the adjournment she seconds (0:10:41). Yet the BGAA adoption (0:02:54), the ACN first read (0:05:45) and the adjournment (0:10:43) are voice votes ("All in favor? Opposed. Abstention."), and the minutes record the earlier agenda and minutes approvals as "unanimously approved" without a roll. On the termination roll she said "No idea what's being voted on. I can't hear you." (0:06:58); the chair had the motion read into the handset before she voted (0:07:04, 0:07:41), which cured that vote on the record. The roll-call requirement for the other votes was not met on the recording. A policy adopted by a voice vote of this kind is the item most worth a second look.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a), (c), (e) (2023 codification; remote participation); draft minutes, January 11, 2024

MEDIUM The only record of the motion to enter nonpublic session cites a chapter that does not exist and states no lettered exemption

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II which is relied upon". The entry is not on the recording. The draft minutes record it as Hawkins "motioned to move into non-public under RSA 92-A:3,II", seconded by Sprague, roll call by Koski. There is no RSA chapter 92-A, and II alone names no exemption. The agenda heads the item "Non-Public Session RSA 92-A:3,II" and prints the text of exemptions (a) and (c) beneath it, so the board may have intended II(a) and II(c); whether the spoken motion named them cannot be checked. This board's paperwork carried the same phantom chapter on December 14, 2023 and again on February 15, 2024. Graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the motion itself is not in the record and the agenda supplies the lettered text.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II(a), (c) (2023 codification); RSA Title VI table of contents (no chapter 92-A); agenda, January 11, 2024

MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes were sealed for 30 years without the determination the statute requires

RSA 91-A:3, III requires nonpublic minutes and decisions to be disclosed within 72 hours unless, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism". Withheld information is to be released when, in a majority's opinion, those circumstances "no longer apply". The seal vote here was a recorded roll in public (0:01:12), so the two-thirds requirement can be checked, but neither the motion ("We feel [seal] the minutes for 30 years", 0:00:41) nor the minutes state any of the three determinations, and a fixed 30-year term is not a statutory measure. The minutes record "all members voting yes"; the transcript of Erickson's answer reads "No. No. Yes, it is now. Yes." and Popescu's answer is not audible, so the tally rests on the minutes. The decisions reached after the session, the termination and the appointment, were made and recorded in public, which limits the practical effect.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2023 codification; 72-hour disclosure and sealing)

POSITIVE The policy committee recommended paid lactation breaks with no make-up time, above the federal floor

The federal PUMP Act requires reasonable break time to express milk for a year after a child's birth, but provides that "an employer shall not be required to compensate an employee" for that time unless other law requires it or the employee is not fully relieved from duty. The SAU's policy committee recommended option two of the NHSBA sample, under which "the mother will be paid during the nursing periods, and they will not be required to make up the time" (0:05:20), and the board advanced it on first read. This page did not verify whether New Hampshire law in January 2024 independently required paid breaks, so the credit is measured against the federal rule only. The policy was a first read and was not yet adopted.

Sources: 29 U.S.C. §218d(a)(1), (b) (break time; compensation); NHSBA Sample Priority Policy ACN (packet)
