Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — April 24, 2018

Joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares, and the city's records were not inspected. The heading keeps the project map's date, but the recording itself places the meeting on Tuesday, April 17, 2018 (see the Date entry and flag 1). The bodies heard presentations from the Boy Scouts and Turning Points Network, debated a draft job description for a shared communications coordinator and sent it back to the superintendent and city manager without a vote, reviewed the warrant-funded school security upgrades, and set a June 21 joint meeting with legislators. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION Two public bodies met with quorums; the district posts no record of the meeting, and the city's records were not inspected

A joint meeting is two bodies, each with its own quorum and its own duty to keep minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II as it read in 2018: minutes that include "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", open to public inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (2017 codification, in force for all of 2018). Six council members answered the roll against a nine-member council with a majority quorum, and six of seven school board members speak on the tape (see Board composition). The bodies approved the January 30 joint minutes here (0:01:27), which shows joint minutes were being kept. The district's Drive shares hold nothing older than 2022 (MAP.md section 65), and the city's notice, agenda and minutes live with the city clerk, so this page makes no city-side negative and no finding that minutes were never produced. The project's missing-records rule is applied on its online-availability limb, as an observation.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I-II (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 165:1 and 234:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2018, so in force throughout 2018); Claremont City Charter secs. 16 and 21 (copy of undetermined date; nine councilors, majority quorum).

OBSERVATION Both bodies' names were offered for a legislative letter on Medicaid expansion without a motion or vote by either body

The mayor asked whether, once the members had seen her draft, they would "sign off as both a school board and superintendent, as well as the city council and city manager" on a letter to the House Finance Committee supporting Medicaid expansion, to be drafted the next day because the committee would meet "on Thursday" (2:05:31, 2:06:55, 2:07:16). The only response on the record is an unidentified "I think that's okay." (2:07:34), and the meeting moved on. No verified rule requires a formal vote before a board's name goes on a letter, and the plan was for members to see the text first. But a letter written in the name of a public body is a decision of that body; if one was sent in the school board's name, the record of who authorized it would be in minutes that are not available online (flag 2). RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to describe "final decisions".

Sources: the recording at the times cited; RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification).
