Claremont School Board Finance Committee, December 13, 2023

The board's three-member Finance Committee questions the superintendent, the special education director and the business administrator on the proposed FY2025 budget: a middle-school schedule change that could free eight academic positions, the preschool's move to modular classrooms, elementary staffing, and why the city's tax rate had not been set. HIGH No minutes of this Finance Committee meeting exist in any district share

A committee of a school board is itself a public body: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) reaches any board of a school district "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto". RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes of every meeting, including "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting." All three committee members met here with the superintendent, the special education director and the business administrator for more than an hour and a half on the FY2025 budget (0:00:10 onward). MAP.md records that no packet, notice or minutes for this meeting are in the main packets share, the 2023 Meeting Documents archive, either candidate year folder of the Meeting Minutes share or the next board meeting's packet, checked three ways on 2026-08-28; a title search for the date on 2026-10-03 found nothing from a district share either. The same is true of every 2023–24 Finance Committee session. Nothing on the record says minutes were kept elsewhere. Under the project's rule for missing records this is the unmitigated limb: a public body met and no minutes exist in any district share, so the five-business-day deadline was missed. The recording is a public account of the meeting, but it is not minutes and the statute does not treat it as one.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d); RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification; source note ends 2023, 188:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023)

HIGH The business administrator says the documents the state needed to set the tax rate were filed eight months late

Asked why the tax rate had not been set, Henry said: "I turned in the stuff yesterday. I was waiting on getting a signed copy of the certified ballot, and also a signed copy of minutes from the deliberative that I did not have." (0:38:38), and "a lot of these things should have been uploaded back in March and April. So. And they weren't uploaded." (0:39:46); "I understand we're late." (0:40:13). RSA 21-J:34, II requires the governing body of each school district to file a report that shall "certify the appropriations voted by the meeting of the appropriate legislative body", and "This report shall be filed within 20 days of the close of the meeting." For official-ballot districts, RSA 21-J:34, IV-a requires "the minutes of any meeting or any deliberative session ... at which any appropriations may be amended, voted, or rescinded" to be certified by the clerk and "filed within 20 days after the date of the close of the meeting". The 2023 deliberative session was February 8 and the ballot vote March 14, 2023, so both 20-day periods ended in early 2023; Henry places the filing on December 12. Mitigation stated on tape: Henry arrived in July 2023 and says the failure "goes on, both of us because I didn't follow up" (0:39:02); the March and April deadlines fell before her tenure, during the business-office changeover. The consequence described on tape is a delayed city tax bill; Crawford notes tax bills normally arrive December 1 (0:39:07). The page relies on Henry's account; the Department of Revenue Administration's filing record would confirm the dates.

Sources: RSA 21-J:34, II and IV-a (2023 codification; source note ends 2007, 182:2, eff. April 1, 2007)

OBSERVATION Whether the district must run its own preschool was asked and left open

Sprague asked whether, "truly, truly bare bones", the district is "only obligated to provide services" to preschool children with disabilities rather than run a program (1:09:53); Nester answered "legally, you might be able to get away with that" (1:10:25) and that outside programs "don't exist" locally (1:10:48). Under IDEA a district must make a free appropriate public education available to "all children residing in the State between the ages of 3 and 21, inclusive", which for about 35 preschool children (1:24:51) is a duty the district carries however it delivers the services. The regulation does not require any particular program model, so the members' question is a placement and cost question, not one of legal minimums. The record also shows the special education director both proposing to replace the preschool director with a coordinator (1:08:22) and saying "We should have a director for pre-K 100%." (1:07:16).

Sources: 34 CFR 300.101(a) (FAPE for children aged 3 through 21)
