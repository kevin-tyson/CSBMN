Claremont School Board, March 4, 2020

Regular meeting held in person six days before the March 10, 2020 school district election, opening with a public hearing on two donations ($11,947.39 for Chromebooks and $3,000 for a fourth-grade trip). Business includes a Claremont Early Childhood Program presentation, the superintendent's entry-plan findings and a first coronavirus update, a food-service discussion, a Medicaid-to-Schools update ($290,000 budgeted for next year), provisional approval of the 2020-21 program of studies, and members urging a yes vote on the budget and negotiated agreements. MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material: neither district share holds a year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The recording is now the only public account of the donation acceptance, the provisional program-of-studies approval and the Medicaid figures. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force from January 1, 2019, requires that minutes "include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", that "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion shall be recorded in the minutes", and that minutes be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute text).

This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the chair works from an agenda (0:02:11), the curriculum specialist refers to "your board packet" (1:33:00), and the board approved this meeting's minutes on May 6, 2020 (May 6, 2020 page), so minutes were made. What is missing is their public copy, not their existence.

The program of studies was put to a vote, "All in favor?" (1:45:00), after the chair said "We're going to provisionally approve it tonight."; no member moves or seconds it on the recording. The donations were moved by Benware (1:45:25) and voted on (1:46:09) with no second heard. RSA 91-A:2, II requires the minutes to record "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion" (statute text). A motion may have been made off microphone, and the May 6 minutes approval suggests the clerk recorded something; with no minutes surviving, the recording cannot show who moved or seconded either item.

No statute requires a second as such; the requirement is to record the mover and seconder of each motion. This is labeled an observation because the defect, if any, would be in the minutes, which cannot be inspected.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification): mover and seconder recorded in minutes

OBSERVATION Board members urged a yes vote at a public meeting six days before the election; staff remarks and district signs raise a question the record cannot settle

The chair told viewers "It's important that people get out and vote. Support your schools. Support our kids." (1:51:50) and answered press criticism of the budget (1:50:52); Benware made the case for spending one-time state money on capital needs (1:52:11). RSA 659:44-a, I forbids a "public employee, as defined in RSA 273-A:1, IX" to "electioneer while in the performance of his or her official duties", and ¶II forbids using "government property or equipment" for electioneering (statute text). RSA 273-A:1, IX(a) excludes "Persons elected by popular vote", so board members are outside the section.

District employees also spoke to the vote. The superintendent tied the negotiated contracts to "the up coming vote" (0:35:28); the business administrator, after the chair's appeal, called it "an important budget that will materially change the future of the financial makeup of Claremont School District." (1:57:32); and the chair reported that "Mrs. Magoon is going to be getting the big, lighted signed by the police station activated" and that banners were up at schools (1:51:25). Whether any of this was "specifically designed to influence the vote" (¶III) depends on facts the recording lacks: what the sign and banners said (a reminder of the date is not advocacy), and whether each speaker is a "public employee" under RSA 273-A:1, IX, which also excludes appointees of the public employer's chief executive or legislative body and persons whose duties imply a confidential relationship. The remarks about contracts and the tax rate were largely factual. This is an observation, not a finding.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I-III (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 273-A:1, IX (2019 codification): "public employee" and its exclusions

POSITIVE The board held a public hearing before accepting the donations, including one below the hearing threshold

RSA 198:20-b, III(a), as in force in 2020, provides that "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with newspaper notice at least seven days before; for smaller amounts ¶III(b) requires notice in the agenda and the minutes and acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting" (statute text). The board opened the meeting with a hearing on both gifts, the $11,947.39 Chromebook fund and the $3,000 trip gift (0:00:12), invited public comment, and accepted them in public session afterward, with the chair noting "this is what we had the public hearing on here." (1:46:01).

Whether the newspaper notice was published, and whether the district had adopted the enabling article that ¶I requires, cannot be checked from the recording.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)
