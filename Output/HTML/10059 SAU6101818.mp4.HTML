SAU 6 Board, October 18, 2018

Regular meeting of the joint Claremont and Unity board, held without the interim superintendent or the assistant superintendent: the board approved a $1,930,809 FY2019-20 SAU budget for the November 8 public hearing, created a part-time food service compliance position at $12,000 to $15,000 with one vote against, adopted a superintendent evaluation tool, and approved the school funding letter to legislators and candidates. A citizen asked about the Claremont lunch debt, which the chair called a Claremont board matter. OBSERVATION A new SAU position was created by the SAU board alone; the statute conditions new positions and services on the districts' votes

The board created a food service compliance position (1:05:34, 1:19:19) and added it to the unit's budget (0:21:58, 0:23:26), funded about 89% by Claremont (0:30:03). RSA 194-C:5, II(c) provides: "Other administrative positions may be established, but only after 50 percent or more of the school districts in the school administrative unit representing 60 percent of the total pupils in the school administrative unit has voted favorably upon the establishment of the position." RSA 194-C:9, I separately provides that the SAU board "shall not add any new service to the school administrative unit budget unless a majority of the school districts", "representing not less than 60 percent of the total pupils in the school administrative unit", have voted favorably. Whether a part-time compliance post is an "administrative position", and whether moving food service paperwork to the SAU is a "new service", are open questions; staff described the duties as district responsibilities that the SAU business office had already been carrying (1:00:29, 1:12:50). No vote by the Claremont or Unity district is in this record. A Claremont vote alone would meet both thresholds (one of two districts, about 94% of pupils by the membership figure given at 0:29:46), so the gap, if there is one, would be easy to close. The district records would settle it.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, II(c) (2017 codification; 1996, 298:3, never amended); RSA 194-C:9, I (2017 codification; source 1996, 298:3; 2003, 279:1)

OBSERVATION Salary increases "given by the SA board" appear on no public record available to this project

Explaining the changes since September 13, the business administrator said "There was some salary increases given by the SA board in the staff totaling 10,000 186" (0:21:58), for the assistant superintendent, an administrative assistant and the receptionist (0:23:26). No vote on those raises is on this recording, and the September 13 recording ends as the board entered a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a), the exemption covering an employee's compensation (September 13, 2018 page). Fixing SAU salaries is the SAU board's power under RSA 194-C:5, III. If the raises were decided in that nonpublic session, RSA 91-A:3, III required "Minutes and decisions reached in nonpublic session" to be disclosed within 72 hours unless withheld by a recorded two-thirds vote in public session. Neither the decision nor any sealing vote can be checked, because the September 13 tape stops during the roll call and no minutes survive. This is an observation: the record shows raises approved somewhere, and does not show where.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (2017 codification); RSA 91-A:3, II(a) and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; 280:1)

POSITIVE The unit's budget goes to a public hearing that the statute does not require, with the apportionment explained in public

RSA 194-C:9, I requires the SAU board to adopt its budget "At a meeting held before January 1" and to apportion it half on average membership and half on equalized valuation; the section contains no public-hearing requirement. The board instead adopted the $1,930,809 budget for presentation at a November 8 public hearing (0:18:26, 1:22:48), and the business administrator walked through the apportionment on camera, step by step: valuation shares, membership shares and the resulting 89.47% and 10.53% (0:29:46, 0:30:03), which follows the statutory formula. Members also deferred adoption until the new position was decided, so the figure taken to the public matched the board's decisions (0:36:46).

Sources: RSA 194-C:9, I (2017 codification)

POSITIVE The chair would not schedule a nonpublic session for which no purpose had been given to her

Asked whether the board was going into nonpublic session, the chair said she "never got a request" and had received no information about what it pertained to (0:03:08): "I can't have one without information." RSA 91-A:3, I(b) allows a nonpublic session only on a motion that states "on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II", carried by roll call, and I(c) confines the discussion to the matters in the motion. Declining to put a nonpublic session on the evening's business without an identified exemption keeps the board inside that rule. Towle's remark that it "leaves a lot still up in the air" (0:03:58) shows the cost: whatever the session was meant to address is not on the record.
