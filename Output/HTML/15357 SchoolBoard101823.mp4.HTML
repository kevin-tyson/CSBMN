Claremont School Board, October 18, 2023

Regular meeting with five of seven members in the room. The board accepted a $6,000 Schwab Charitable donation after a public hearing, voted to retain up to $424,000 of year-end fund balance after the business administrator said the FY2022 audit had only just started, approved a two-year athletic-fields license with the City of Claremont, and adopted a letter of support for career and technical education. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on October 18, 2023, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The FY2022 audit had "just got started" more than six months after the federal single-audit deadline

Asked how many audits were outstanding, the business administrator said "Right now 21 is done" (1:15:11) and of FY2022: "We're working. We just got started on 22." (1:16:09). She described the filing as one "with the state in that or the federal clearinghouse" (1:15:34) and named grant reconciliation as her biggest obstacle (1:16:14). The superintendent had just said the district carried "$13 million of extra federal grant money" in its budgets (0:17:09). Under the Uniform Guidance in force for FY2022, an entity that spends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year must have a single audit, and the reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period." For the year ended June 30, 2022 that was March 31, 2023; on October 18, 2023 the audit had only begun. Mitigation on the record: the business office was new (Tempesta: "We have some new people in the business office", 0:05:14), FY2021 was finished, and the administrator described a standing Zoom call with "the auditors and his team" (1:17:30). State law does not fix a deadline: RSA 21-J:19 is permissive as to timing, so the deadline this flag rests on is the federal one. The district's FY2022 federal expenditure total is not in this record; the clearinghouse reference and the ESSER figures are the basis for treating the single-audit rule as applicable.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2023 edition: single audit at $750,000 or more in federal expenditures); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2023 edition: nine months after period end); RSA 21-J:19 (audits by independent accountants; permissive as to timing)

MEDIUM No roll call, and the approved minutes account for only five of seven members and were never corrected for Miles

The agenda lists "Secretary Roll Call of Attendance of Board Members", but no roll was called; the chair said only that a quorum was present (0:00:41). The approved minutes read "Present: Heather Whitney, Frank Sprague, Candace Crawford, Arlene Hawkins Absent: Jennifer Gallagher". Whitney Skillen, who speaks 21 times on the recording and reports for the policy subcommittee, is in neither column, and so is Bonnie Miles. At the next meeting the board approved this set of minutes with the correction "Bonnie Miles was not present at the last board meeting on 10.18.23" (November 1 minutes), yet the copy in the Meeting Minutes share, last modified November 7, 2023, still omits Miles and Skillen. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to "include the names of members", and an attendance record built without a roll call is the likeliest cause of the gap. Two seconders also differ between the tape and the minutes: the donation's second is heard but not identifiable on tape (0:02:24), and for the CTE letter both Hawkins (1:32:03) and Sprague (1:32:08) offer a second while the minutes name Hawkins alone. Mitigation: every vote was unopposed, and five members are a quorum of seven, so no outcome turns on the gap.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification, in force from Oct. 3, 2023: minutes name members and the mover and seconder of each motion)

OBSERVATION Fund-balance retention: within the voters' 2.5 percent, but the base was described as FY2022's

RSA 198:4-b, II lets the district, "by a vote of the legislative body", authorize retention of year-end unassigned funds up to "5 percent of the current fiscal year's net assessment under RSA 198:5", and requires a prior public hearing before the money is spent. The business administrator's account was consistent with that: the voters had authorized 2.5 percent ("Yours is only 2.5%, even though the RSA allows up to five", 1:09:13), the motion should say up to, and the money cannot be touched without "a, public hearing and a vote of the board". She described the base, though, as "2.5% of the amount of money you had to raise in fiscal year 22" (1:09:55), where the statute measures against the current fiscal year's net assessment. The district's warrant article and the DOE-25 worksheet would show which figure was used. The stated reason, that incomplete audits might show "we've given back too much to the taxpayers" (1:08:42), is a reasonable caution and ties this vote to flag 1.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2023 codification; 2020, 38:25: 5 percent cap, legislative-body vote, hearing before expending)

POSITIVE A public hearing for a $6,000 gift, and draft minutes posted on the fifth business day

The board noticed the gift on the agenda, held a public hearing anyway (0:01:33), and voted acceptance in open session. Separately, the draft minutes of this meeting were created in the November 1 packet folder on October 25, 2023 at 3:57 p.m., inside the fifth business day after October 18, meeting RSA 91-A:2, II.
