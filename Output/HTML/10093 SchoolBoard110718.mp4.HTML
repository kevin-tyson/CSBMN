Claremont School Board — November 7, 2018

Summary of a regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The board interviewed candidates for its vacant seat on camera, deliberated in nonpublic session, appointed Rob Lovett Jr. to serve until the March election and voted to seal the nonpublic minutes for 83 years; it also heard the regional technical center's goals, a draft in-district special education program, a budget-subcommittee progress report, and accepted $32,596.23 in donations that cleared the 2017-18 student lunch debt. MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes were sealed "for 83 years" by voice vote, with no statutory determination stated

After the deliberation on the vacancy, Benware moved "to seal the minutes of the nonpublic for 83 years", Pierce seconded, and the motion carried on a voice call (0:32:54 to 0:33:06). The law then in force required nonpublic minutes and decisions to be disclosed within 72 hours unless, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", the body determined that disclosure would likely harm the reputation of a non-member, render a proposed action ineffective, or bear on terrorism preparedness, and it allowed withholding only "until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply". The motion as heard states no determination, and a fixed term of 83 years is not the statute's measure. The vote was in public session and no dissent is heard, so the two-thirds margin was probably met; whether the minutes record a count or a determination cannot be checked, because they are not online. In the board's favor: the motion to enter nonpublic session did name its ground, paragraph II(c) (0:30:49 to 0:30:56), which fits discussion of non-member candidates, and the decision itself was announced in public at once (0:34:10), so the seal reaches the discussion rather than the outcome. MEDIUM: a probable documentation gap under a binding rule.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(c) and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017).

POSITIVE The vacancy was filled promptly, with candidate interviews held on camera and the same questions put to each

The seat left open by the resignation announced October 3 was filled about five weeks later. Two candidates were interviewed in public, each asked the same six questions by members in turn, and the absent candidate's written answers to the same questions were read into the record (0:26:45 to 0:30:27). The chair stated that the appointee serves "only till March" (0:33:09) and named the three seats and the filing window for that election (0:33:41). The law then in force directed the school board to fill vacancies, with appointees to "serve until the next district election", which is what the board announced.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2017, 3:2, so this text governed until the 2021 amendments): the school board "shall fill vacancies"; appointees serve "until the next district election".

POSITIVE Donations of $5,000 or more were accepted only after a public hearing, and grants not on the agenda were held over

The meeting opened with a public hearing on the food service donations because, as the chair and Pierce explained, a donation over the threshold "requires a public hearing" (0:01:19 to 0:02:08); the $29,066.23 and $3,530 gifts were then accepted by motion in public session, with the donors' names read (1:37:08 to 1:39:45). At the end, the assistant superintendent held two teacher grants over $500 to the December 5 meeting "because it wasn't warned as part of this meeting" (1:47:06). The law then in force required a prior public hearing for unanticipated funds of $5,000 or more and, for smaller amounts, notice "in the agenda" and acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting". Whether the newspaper notice the statute also requires was published cannot be checked from here.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) and (b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, so in force in 2018).
