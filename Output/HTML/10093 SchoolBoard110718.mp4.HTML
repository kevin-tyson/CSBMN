MEDIUM The vacancy was decided in nonpublic session and announced without a public vote, and the minutes that would show the decision were sealed for 83 years by voice vote with no stated reason

The board interviewed candidates in public, then entered nonpublic session on a roll-call vote under an exemption the chair read as RSA 91-A:3, II(c) (0:30:49 to 0:31:36). On its return it voted by voice to "seal the minutes of the nonpublic for 83 years" (0:32:54 to 0:33:02), and the chair announced: "The board has made a decision that we will be opting for Mr. Lovett" (0:34:10). No motion or vote on the appointment is heard in public session. RSA 671:33, II(a) required the board to fill the vacancy, with the appointee serving until the next district election, which the chair describes (0:33:09). RSA 91-A:3, as it read in 2018, allowed the body to discuss and decide only the matters set out in the motion (I(c)), and II(c) covers matters that would likely affect adversely the reputation of a person other than a member, which can reach a discussion of candidates. But ¶III required minutes and decisions reached in nonpublic session to be disclosed within 72 hours unless the body, by a recorded two-thirds vote in public session, determined that disclosure would likely harm a person's reputation, render a proposed action ineffective, or relate to terrorism, and then only until those circumstances no longer applied. The recording shows no such determination, no tally on the seal, and a fixed 83-year term the statute does not provide. If the appointment vote was taken in the nonpublic session, the seal as voted would keep the board's own count on filling a public office out of view for 83 years. The minutes, if they survive, may record a finding and a public tally that the recording does not.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(c), III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 671:33, II(a) (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 3:2, eff. May 30, 2017)

OBSERVATION The donations hearing and acceptance follow RSA 198:20-b's sequence; the hearing's newspaper notice cannot be checked

For unanticipated money of $5,000 or more, RSA 198:20-b, III(a), as it read in 2018, required a prior public hearing, with notice of its time, place and subject published in a newspaper of general circulation at least seven days before; below $5,000, III(b) required notice in the agenda and the minutes and acceptance in public session at a regular meeting. The board held a hearing before the meeting (0:01:19 to 0:02:08) and then accepted $29,066.23 from an anonymous donor and $3,530 from others by motion and vote (1:37:08 to 1:39:47). That sequence fits the statute. What the record cannot show is the notice: a hearing on November 7 needed newspaper notice by October 31, and at the October 30 meeting the administration had $280 in hand and no anonymous gift had been mentioned. A notice that described the hearing in general terms would suffice; the published notice, or the district's acceptance resolution, would settle it. The administration's holding of two teacher grants over $500 for December 5 "because it wasn't warned as part of this meeting" (1:47:06) is consistent with III(b)'s agenda-notice rule. The $500 board-approval threshold the chair describes (0:01:19) is not in the statute and presumably comes from district policy, which was not available.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a)-(b) (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION The budget pressures the superintendent named are written into state law

Pfeifer said the district "will be challenged to produce a level funded budget" because of "the loss of stabilization funds and catastrophic aid" (0:05:36). Both are statutory. RSA 198:41, IV(d), as codified in 2018, paid each municipality, from fiscal year 2017, its education grant plus a share of its fiscal year 2012 stabilization grant that started at 96 percent and fell by 4 percent each year after, so the FY2020 budget the board was building faced a further scheduled cut. Catastrophic special education aid under RSA 186-C:18, III, as codified in 2017, paid 80 percent of a child's costs above 3.5 times the state average expenditure per pupil, but was prorated proportionally when the appropriation fell short, with no floor at that date. The record gives no dollar figure for either loss; the district's revenue estimates would supply it.

Sources: RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2018 codification; source note ends 2016, 85:9, eff. July 18, 2016); RSA 186-C:18, III (2017 codification; source note 2017, 156:96-100, eff. July 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION The cost case for the in-district special education program rests on handouts that are not public

The board was told the draft program would cost about $665,000 a year in staffing against about $540,000 in avoided placements, a net of about $125,000 (1:30:20), with out-of-district tuition at "roughly $90,000 per student per placement" (1:24:38); a correction to "That last figure" was given orally and "I did not correct that on your copy" (1:30:59). The figures come from "two pieces of paper" in members' folders, a Venn diagram and a spreadsheet (1:13:27), which are not in any public share, so a viewer cannot see which line the correction changed. The program's aim of keeping children in district is consistent with federal law's preference for educating children with disabilities with nondisabled children to the maximum extent appropriate; the projections themselves are the speakers', and the December budget materials, if found, would test them.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. 1412(a)(5)(A) (least restrictive environment)

POSITIVE Candidates for the vacancy were interviewed in public with the same questions, and the absent candidate's written answers were read aloud

No statute required the board to interview vacancy candidates in public; RSA 671:33, II(a) says only that the board shall fill the vacancy. The board heard each candidate in public session and each member put the same question to every candidate (0:12:55). When Benware pointed out that the third candidate had submitted written answers (0:18:41), the chair dropped his plan to review them privately: "we think it's important, my colleagues, that we also share that with the community" (0:19:55), and the answers were read into the record (0:26:45 to 0:30:08). The public therefore heard everything the candidates offered before the board deliberated, even though the deliberation itself was closed (see flag 1).
