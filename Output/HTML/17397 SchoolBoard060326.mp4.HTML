Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on and links to the primary source; where no verified rule supports a concern, the flag is an Observation.

HIGH Incoming superintendent reports that close to half of a 54-IEP sample lapsed before renewal

Dr. Broadrick told the board that the district's special-education consultant checked "the first 54" IEPs and found "close to 50%" with "a gap in approval from a day to a few months" (0:34:20); he stopped the review as "close enough to a representative sample" and estimated that 40 to 45 percent of roughly 450 to 460 IEPs "might not have been renewed on time" (0:33:30, 0:34:20). He did not know "for how many years this has been going on" (0:34:51). Federal regulation requires each public agency to ensure that the IEP team "Reviews the child's IEP periodically, but not less than annually" (34 CFR 300.324(b)(1)(i)). A lapse between expiration and renewal is the gap that rule forbids. Mitigation stated on tape: the figure is a sample estimate, not a state monitoring finding; he expects the Bureau of Special Education to require corrective action rather than punishment (0:35:09); and he names special-education compliance as a reason for a dedicated director (0:35:15). He also ties the lapses to lost Medicaid to Schools reimbursement (0:33:57), which the board should expect to see quantified.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.324(b)(1)(i) (IEP reviewed not less than annually); Claremont Board Report, June 3, 2026 (concerns about "consistency of IEP writing", report text).

MEDIUM Nonpublic session: the spoken motion does not state II(c), and the minuted vote to seal is not on the recording

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires that a motion to enter nonpublic session "shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II which is relied upon." On tape the chair cites "RSA 91, a colon, three comma, section two, parents number one" when amending the agenda (0:00:45) and "Section two sub part one" when calling for the motion (1:31:02); neither rendering is II(c), which is what the minutes record ("RSA 91-A:3,II(c)", minutes text). The transcript may have garbled the letter, so the recording does not settle what was said. The more concrete gap is the seal. The approved minutes record that "Heather Whitney made a motion to seal the minutes until September 1, 2026, Brian Rapp seconded the motion" with a roll call, all in favor (minutes text). RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" with a determination that disclosure would likely harm a person's reputation, render a proposed action ineffective, or relate to terrorism preparedness. The recording runs continuously from the return-to-public roll call (1:31:39) into subcommittee reports (1:31:45) with no seal motion, and the minutes state no statutory determination. Either the seal vote was taken outside public session or it was not taken as minuted. Also: the session followed a discussion in which the business administrator said a truant-officer matter "deals with personnel" (0:43:05); if that was the subject, II(a) (an employee's discipline or compensation) is the employee-specific exemption, and II(c) excludes members of the public body only. The topic is not stated in the public record, so exemption fit cannot be judged here. The seal date has passed; a search of the district's Unsealed Minutes share on 2026-10-03 did not locate released minutes for this session, but that search relied on Drive's search tool, which has missed files before, so the result is "not found", not "does not exist".

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(a), II(c), III (motion must state the exemption; seal by 2/3 recorded vote in public session with a finding); 6.3.26 Approved CSB Meeting Minutes.

MEDIUM Written report finds staff positions without an identified funding source and federal grants left unspent for several years

Dr. Broadrick's packet report states that two contracted social-work positions had no approved grant activity, that "there is no history of all contracts having to be submitted to the central office for a purchase order and signature", and that one contracted position was one "for which the district did not have an identified funding source"; it adds that "the central office has not fully expended federal grant funds for several consecutive years" (report text). On tape he frames the rule as "you can only spend each dollar once" (0:15:56) and says the district's Safe and Supportive Schools grant has been hard "to actually expend in full" (0:30:36). Federal awards require the recipient to maintain effective internal control over them (2 CFR 200.303), and federal education formula funds carry over only one additional fiscal year before they lapse (20 U.S.C. §1225(b)). The report's own mitigation: the encumbrances were found and the costs are already in the expenditure reports, and next year's positions will be tied to a funding source before hire. MEDIUM because these are process gaps described by the district's own incoming chief executive, without a dollar figure for lapsed funds.

Sources: Claremont Board Report, June 3, 2026; 2 CFR 200.303 (internal controls over federal awards); 20 U.S.C. §1225(b) (one additional year of availability).
