Claremont School Board, December 18, 2024

Regular board meeting held the evening after the Finance Committee's afternoon session. The board approved the 2025–26 Stevens High School Program of Studies, heard the principal's review of the new period schedule, heard the business administrator's tutorial on tax-rate setting and an oral default budget of $39,791,261, voted to send a school-funding letter to Senator Ward, and authorized an $857,592 seven-year lighting lease. It moved no FY26 budget figure: the chair said two collective bargaining agreements were still unratified and set a January 7 meeting. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on December 18, 2024, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The earliest datable public copy of these minutes appeared on the fifteenth business day

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting"; a business day is 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday to Friday, excluding national and state holidays. Counting from Wednesday, December 18, with December 25 and January 1 excluded, the fifth business day was Thursday, December 26, 2024. The draft minutes that reached the district's public share were filed in the 12. CSB Budget Public Hearing 1.15.25 packet folder, which was created January 10, 2025 at 3:25 p.m. Eastern; the file's createdTime is the same minute, and its modifiedTime is January 8, 2025, the thirteenth business day. They were not in the January 7 packet (11. CSB 1.7.25). The approved version was created January 21, 2025. Drive dates measure when a document reached the share, not when it was written, and the minutes may have been available on request earlier; nothing in the record says so. Graded MEDIUM under the project's severity rule, not HIGH: the meeting was noticed and packeted, minutes exist, and they record movers and seconders as RSA 91-A:2, II requires; only their timeliness is in question.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2024 codification; minutes within five business days; business day defined); 12.18.24 DRAFT CSB minutes (1).pdf; 12. CSB Budget Public Hearing 1.15.25 folder

MEDIUM The Policy subcommittee met on the day of this meeting; no notice or minutes of that session were found

Hawkins, under Other Business: "The policy committee did meet. Subcommittee did me today." (1:24:00). A subcommittee of a school board is a public body: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) reaches "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto", so the session needed 24 hours' posted notice and minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II. The board's agenda lists no Policy subcommittee date, and its Policy item carried no report. No notice or minutes were found in this meeting's packet folder, in the January 7 packet, or by Drive searches for the date; a negative from Drive search alone does not prove that none exists. Graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the record mitigates it: the subcommittee's output, four policies, was announced in public session and is the kind of business that returns to the full board for a public vote. The same packet carries written accounts of two other subcommittees' December sessions (flag 8), which makes the gap specific to Policy.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (subcommittees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II (2024 codification; notice and minutes); CSB Agenda 12.18.24

OBSERVATION A $39,791,261 default budget was given orally with no document, and the definition read aloud stopped before the eliminated-positions clause

The agenda item was "Default Budget & Proposed FY26 Budget Summary"; the chair said "We are not going to review the budget summary today" (0:37:05), and no default-budget worksheet or budget summary is among the ten packet documents. The only figure was Henry's: "it's $39,791,261" (0:54:50). She then read the statutory definition, ending "reduced by one time expenditures contained in the operating budget" (0:55:24). RSA 40:13, IX(b) continues: "and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget." The omission matters when a proposed budget eliminates positions, because the default must then shrink by their cost. The chair's later reason for discussing no budget total (unratified agreements, 1:21:03) explains the missing budget figure, but not the missing default-budget document. No rule cited here requires a default figure at this meeting. Recorded as an observation; the January 7 and January 15 pages are where the default-budget form, if any, should be checked.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) (definition of default budget; last amended 2019, 192:2); CSB Agenda 12.18.24 (item IV.3)

OBSERVATION An $857,592 seven-year lighting lease was added to the agenda the same night and approved without the agreement in the packet

The lease was not on the posted agenda; Henry added it under "Amendments and Final Approval of board agenda" (0:01:40), and the amount she gave is garbled in the transcript (0:02:00). The motion was "to enter into a lease agreement with MC l leasing in the amount of $857,592" (1:19:07), seconded by the chair and carried by voice vote (1:20:26). Henry said "it's a seven year lease." and that the district received a $262,995 Eversource grant (1:16:17); Sprague described it as "cost neutral" because energy savings pay the payments. RSA 33:7-e lets the governing body enter "leases of equipment", counts related building improvements as equipment, and provides that a lease with a nonappropriation clause "shall not be treated as debt"; RSA 33:1 extends "municipality" in that chapter to school districts. Whether this agreement carries a nonappropriation clause, which determines whether it is debt, cannot be checked: the agreement is not among the ten packet documents and was not read aloud. Henry said the board had earlier approved going forward with the project; that earlier vote is not reviewed on this page. Recorded as an observation because the statute gives the board the power and the document that would settle the debt question is not public in this record.

Sources: RSA 33:7-e (equipment leases; nonappropriation clauses; 1999, 35:1; 2014, 60:1); 11. Approved CSB minutes 12.18.24 (item 6)

OBSERVATION The minutes attribute the funding letter's basis to a Supreme Court ruling that did not yet exist, and the spoken figure differs from the letter

The approved minutes say the letter rests on "the NH Supreme Court’s ruling in the Conval lawsuit"; Crawford said "the state Supreme Court said." On December 18, 2024 the only merits ruling was the Superior Court's of November 2023, which found base adequacy aid too low and set $7,356.01 per pupil; the New Hampshire Supreme Court had stayed that order on March 13, 2024 and did not decide the appeal until July 1, 2025. The letter itself (Exhibit C) is accurate on this point: it says "the Superior Court ruled". It asks the senator to raise base adequacy "from $4,100 to $7,356.01 per pupil"; the spoken and minuted figure is 7,300. The letter also criticizes Education Freedom Account funding the senator "actively supported" and asks her to "honor your campaign promises", which the presentation did not mention; the board voted to send it as written. No rule governs a board's advocacy letter; recorded so readers can compare the minutes, the speech and the document.

Sources: Exhibit C- Dear Senator Ward.docx.pdf; New Hampshire Bulletin, March 13, 2024 (Superior Court ruling of November 2023; $7,356.01; Supreme Court stay), reporting; NH School Funding Fairness Project, July 1, 2025 (Supreme Court decision), reporting
