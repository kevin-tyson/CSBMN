MEDIUM Nonpublic session under the reputation exemption for a general briefing on school conditions and budget direction, with no student names to be used

The superintendent asked for "a few minutes in nonpublic with our building principals", saying "we're not going to be talking about children's names with you" and that the purpose was for the board to have "this sensitivity about the seriousness of this, and also the direction that we anticipate heading in the near future through the budget process" (1:15:40, 1:16:06). The chair stated the exemption as heard: "91, a two Roman numeral two." and C. (1:16:28, 1:16:31), then "91, a Roman numeral two. See?" when taking the motion (1:20:01); a second and a roll call followed (1:20:09 to 1:20:30). The exemptions sit in RSA 91-A:3, II, not 91-A:2, so either the chair misspoke or the transcript misheard three; the recording cannot settle it. The law in force required the motion to state "on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II", confined the session to the matters in the motion, and allowed exemption (c) for matters that "would likely affect adversely the reputation of any person, other than a member of the public body itself". A briefing on the direction of the budget and on classroom conditions described without names is not, on its face, a reputation matter; the other reading, stated fairly, is that in a small community principals describing specific incidents could identify students and families even without names, which is the reason the superintendent gave. The session was brief on the recording's evidence, no decision was announced on return (1:20:59), and the roll call itself was properly taken. MEDIUM: a probable fit problem between the stated exemption and the stated purpose, and the minutes that would show what was discussed are not online.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(c) and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017, and 280:1, so the text in force on May 17, 2017).
