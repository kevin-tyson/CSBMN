Claremont School Board — November 20, 2019

Regular meeting summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The district's auditor reported that the fiscal 2017 audit would be final only in December 2019 and called the district one of the firm's most delinquent clients; the board met in nonpublic session on legal advice and voted to seal the minutes; the superintendent detailed the special education programs behind the next night's $650,000 warrant article; and, on counsel's advice about a website-posting question, the board scheduled a second special district meeting for December 12 to cure any defect. MEDIUM By the auditor's own account, the fiscal 2017 and 2018 audits were far past the federal nine-month single-audit deadline

Federal rules in force in 2019 required a single audit to be completed and filed with the federal clearinghouse "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period." For a fiscal year ending June 30, nine months is March 31. On that measure the fiscal 2017 audit was due by March 31, 2018 and the fiscal 2018 audit by March 31, 2019. At 0:09:21 the auditor said the fiscal 2017 final report would issue "the first week of December" 2019, about twenty months after that date, and that fiscal 2018 fieldwork would be done "this year". He called the district "one of our most delinquent clients in the history of a 40, 45 year firm" (0:12:21). His target of March 31, 2020 for fiscal 2019 (0:10:32) matches the nine-month deadline.

Mitigation stated on the record: the delays arose under the previous administration, whose management was sometimes unavailable to the auditors, and the current staff and interim help had made "serious progress". The tape does not state the district's annual federal expenditures for fiscal 2017 or 2018; the auditor's reference to the "federal clearinghouse" concerns fiscal 2019. The single-audit duty and its nine-month deadline apply only in a year the district spent $750,000 or more in federal awards (2 CFR 200.501(a)), which the recording does not establish for those two years. For that reason this is graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH, although a district running federal school meal and education grant programs of Claremont's size would ordinarily pass the threshold. The audit delay itself is on the auditor's own statements.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2019 annual CFR edition): audit submitted within the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end; 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2018 annual CFR edition): single audit required at $750,000 in federal expenditures.

MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes were sealed on a voice vote, with no stated statutory reason and no audible count

Under RSA 91-A:3, III as it read in 2019, nonpublic minutes "shall be publicly disclosed within 72 hours of the meeting, unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that" disclosure would likely harm the reputation of a non-member, "render the proposed action ineffective", or pertain to terrorism. At 0:31:30 Benware moved "to seal the minutes for 93 years" (the period as transcribed; the statute sets no fixed term), it was seconded, and the chair called "All those in favor?" No roll call was taken, no count is audible, and no one stated which of the statutory determinations applied.

Mitigation: entry into and exit from the session were by roll call, the entry motion stated the exemption on its face by reading the text of paragraph II(l), and minutes of this meeting were kept and approved on December 4; they may record a tally and a reason that the tape does not. Consultation with counsel is a common reason to seal, but the statute's determination still has to be made. This is a documentation gap on the recording, not a finding that the seal was invalid.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(l) and III (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1 and 280:1): motion states the exemption; legal-advice exemption; 72-hour disclosure unless sealed by recorded 2/3 vote with a determination.

OBSERVATION Students were barred from a la carte items while a meal was guaranteed; the state meal-policy law then in force requires no different treatment

The business administrator said at 1:16:57 that "We are not letting students do what is called a la carte or specialty items. You can't get coffee or you can't get other items in there, but a meal is provided to every student". In context this appears to describe students with unpaid balances, though he does not say so in terms. RSA 189:11-a, VIII, added by 2019, 301:1 effective July 1, 2019, requires a meal payment policy to ensure "that no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures." Whether a restriction on items outside the standard meal is "different treatment" under that paragraph is a question the statute's text does not settle, which is why this is an observation. The district's stated practice of feeding every child regardless of balance fits the paragraph's core requirement that all students "have access to a healthy school lunch".

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2019 codification; 2019, 301:1, eff. July 1, 2019): meal payment policy, access to a healthy lunch, no different treatment.

POSITIVE Facing a disputed posting defect, the board chose the statutory cure rather than rely on its lawyer's argument that no defect existed

RSA 197:3-a, V required notice of the special meeting on "the school district's website, if such exists". Counsel told the board at 1:01:24 that there was "a strong argument" the SAU 6 site is not the district's website, but recommended a curative meeting anyway, and the board voted at 1:05:10 to hold one on December 12. RSA 40:16 allows an official-ballot district to "call a special meeting for the exclusive purpose of curing such defect according to RSA 31:5-b", and RSA 31:5-b, II requires that meeting to fall at least 21 days after the original vote and to be preceded by a public hearing at least 7 days before. December 12 is exactly 21 days after November 21. The chair also explained publicly what the money was and how it would be split before the next night's vote.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, V (2019 codification): meeting notice on the district website "if such exists" 7 days before; RSA 40:16 (2019 codification; source 1999, 86:4); RSA 31:5-b, II(b)-(c) (2019 codification; source 1989, 287:1): at least 21 days after the original vote; hearing at least 7 days before.
