HIGH The district's FY2017 and FY2018 single audits were more than a year past the federal deadline

The auditor told the board that the FY2017 audit (the year ended June 30, 2017) would be final “the first week of December” 2019 (0:09:21), that FY2018 fieldwork would be done “this year” (0:09:43), and that FY2019 was targeted “in time for the federal clearinghouse, which is March 31st of 2020” (0:10:32). Under the Uniform Guidance a single audit's reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period" (regulation text, 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1)). For a July to June district that is March 31 of the following year: March 31, 2018 for FY2017 and March 31, 2019 for FY2018. On his own timetable both were missed, by about 20 months and by more than 8 months. His reference to the clearinghouse deadline indicates the district was over the single-audit threshold, which was $750,000 of federal expenditures in a year (2 CFR 200.501(a)); the recording gives no federal-expenditure figure.

HIGH: a missed federal deadline, stated by the auditor himself, who called Claremont “one of our most delinquent clients in the history of a 40, 45 year firm” (0:12:21). In fairness to the current administration, he attributes the delay to records kept by people no longer there and to a prior management that would not meet him (0:11:36), and reports improvement. The management letters he describes are not in the record.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2019 annual edition; reporting package due nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2019 annual edition; $750,000 threshold)

OBSERVATION Counsel told the board every other posting requirement was met; three weeks later he named a second defect

On this recording counsel framed the problem as a single question, whether the notices had to go on the SAU 6 website, and said “we have complied with each and every other requirement of the statute.” (1:03:54). At the December 12 curing meeting he listed two defects: no website notice of the November 6 hearing, and a meeting notice “posted six days ahead of the Special District meeting, not seven” (counsel on the December 12 recording, show 10649; December 12, 2019 page). RSA 197:3-a, V requires the meeting notice, with the warrant, in at least two public places "at least 7 days prior" to the meeting (statute text), so the timing defect was independent of the website question. The board was asked to proceed on November 21 on the narrower account. The curing meeting covered both defects, so the consequence was limited; the point is the accuracy of what the board and viewers were told.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, IV and V (2019 codification; source 2013, 197:1)

OBSERVATION School-lunch debt passed $32,000, and outside groups have offered to pay it

The business administrator reported a negative balance of $25,900 at the end of FY2019, now “up over $32,000” (1:15:48), while the district serves every child a full meal “the same as any other child” (1:16:35). That policy is what state law requires: RSA 189:11-a, VIII, in force since July 2, 2016, provides that "no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures" (statute text). Federal guidance bars covering bad debt from the nonprofit school food service account; USDA's SP 23-2017 says "these losses must be restored using non-Federal funds" (guidance text). Offers from groups to pay down the debt (1:17:23) would be gifts to the district; RSA 198:20-b requires notice in the agenda and minutes for unanticipated private money under $5,000, and a prior public hearing for $5,000 or more, if the district has adopted the enabling article. Nothing was accepted at this meeting.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2018 codification; source note ends 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016); RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1); USDA FNS SP 23-2017 (Mar. 23, 2017), unpaid meal charges

POSITIVE A possible notice defect was explained in public and a cure scheduled before the vote

The night before the special district meeting, district counsel explained on camera that concerns had been raised about the posting of its notices, that he had consulted the Department of Revenue Administration, and that the legislature provides a way to cure such defects (1:01:24 to 1:04:25). The board voted at once to hold a curing meeting on December 12 (1:05:10). RSA 40:16 lets an official-ballot district call a special meeting "for the exclusive purpose of curing such defect according to RSA 31:5-b", and RSA 31:5-b, II sets the timing, not less than 21 calendar days after the original vote; December 12 is 21 days after November 21. Disclosing the problem before the vote, rather than waiting for a challenge, protected the result and told voters what was at stake. (Flag 4 notes that the account given here was incomplete.)

Sources: RSA 40:16 (2019 codification; source 1999, 86:4); RSA 31:5-b, I and II (2019 codification; source 1988, 284:1; 1989, 287:1)
