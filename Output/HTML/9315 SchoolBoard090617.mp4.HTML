Claremont School Board — September 6, 2017

Summary of the first regular meeting of the Claremont School Board in the 2017-18 school year, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The board heard the superintendent's opening report and a food service update, argued at length over whether to hold a separate board-only goals meeting and then set one for Saturday, September 30; moved parent-teacher conferences so that no school is held on the November 7 voting day; approved a late-arrival model for early release days and six staff nominations; and worked through a batch of policies revised for new legislation. OBSERVATION A member praised the draft child abuse reporting policy for routing reports to the principal; the statute puts the duty to report to the State on each individual

On first read, Sprague summarized the draft as saying an employee "shall immediately report his her suspicions to the building principal" and proposed adding or designee (2:08:24). He then said what he liked about it: "it does go to the principal because a lot of staff members are very nervous about calling DC" (2:11:51) and "it puts it on the administration." (2:12:06). The law then in force named teachers, school officials, school nurses and counselors among the persons who, having reason to suspect abuse or neglect, "shall report the same", and required that the oral report "be made immediately by telephone or otherwise" to the department. Telling a principal does not by itself discharge that individual duty. The draft policy text is not available, so whether it also directed staff to report to the department cannot be checked here; the concern is with the reading offered at the table. The policy moved to second read without a vote on its substance (2:12:55).

Sources: RSA 169-C:29 (2016 codification; source note 1979, 361:2): teachers, school officials, nurses and counselors "having reason to suspect that a child has been abused or neglected shall report the same"; RSA 169-C:30 (2016 codification; source note ends 1995, 310:175): "An oral report shall be made immediately by telephone or otherwise, and followed within 48 hours by a report in writing, if so requested, to the department."

OBSERVATION The health education opt-out was explained as available for any reason; the statute gives two different routes with different conditions

The chair read the health education exemption policy's religious-objection clause (2:02:42), which the discussion placed in the older part of the policy rather than the new language ("your issue is. With the old part of the policy.", 2:04:58). Asked whether parents can opt out of health education for any reason, the superintendent answered "Absolutely. They can. Yes they can." (2:06:45), and described the new law's theme as "notification two weeks in advance." (2:03:06). The statute in force on September 6 had two separate requirements: an exception to a unit of health or sex education "based on religious objections", with alternative learning sufficient to meet state requirements (IX-b), and a broader exception to specific course material a parent finds objectionable, which required written notice to the principal and "an alternative agreed upon by the school district and the parent, at the parent's expense" (IX-c). So the answer was right that the religious ground is not the only route, but it left out the conditions attached to the broader one. The two-week notice he described was enacted but not yet in force: it appears only in the 2017 codification, whose source note runs to 2017, 251:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2017, ten days after this meeting.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-b and IX-c (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 14:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017, and 84:2): the text in force on the meeting date; RSA 186:11 (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 251:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2017): adds "not less than 2 weeks advance notice" of human sexuality course material.

POSITIVE The nonacademic survey policy was being updated in step with a statutory change that took effect ten days later

The superintendent brought the survey policy (ILDA) to second read as part of "a ton of new policy due to legislative changes" (1:39:59), and said the law "specifies that youth risk behavior surveys will not be affected by this." (1:42:03). That matches the amended statute: the 2017 codification requires written parental consent before a student takes a non-academic survey, with an exception from consent for the CDC youth risk behavior survey and a parental opt-out from it. The text in force on September 6 (2016 codification) required notice, a 10-day review period and an opt-out, but no consent. The board moved the policy to third read (1:42:28) and adopted it on September 20, days after the amendment took effect. Which of the 2017 chapters in the source note made the change is not verified here.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-d (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 9:1; 22:2; 251:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2017): "no student shall be required to volunteer for or submit to a non-academic survey or questionnaire ... without written consent of a parent or legal guardian"; exception for "the youth risk behavior survey developed by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention"; RSA 186:11, IX-d (2016 codification): notice, review "at least 10 days prior to distribution", opt-out.
