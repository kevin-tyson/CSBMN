OBSERVATION The child-abuse reporting policy was praised for putting the report "on the administration", but the state duty to report falls on each staff member

The draft JLF policy, as read aloud, has staff "immediately report his her suspicions to the building principal" (2:08:24), and Sprague welcomed it because staff are "very nervous about calling DC [DCYF]" and "it puts it on the administration" (2:11:51). Under RSA 169-C:29, teachers and other school officials "having reason to suspect that a child has been abused or neglected shall report the same", and RSA 169-C:30 requires an oral report "immediately by telephone or otherwise" to the department. A policy that routes reports through the principal is common and lawful as an internal step, but it does not discharge the individual reporter's own duty. The full text of the draft is not on the record, so whether it says this is not known; the concern is that the board's discussion described the policy as relieving staff of the call. The superintendent's answer on training ("When in doubt, you move", 2:11:44) points the right way.

Sources: RSA 169-C:29 (2016 codification; who must report); RSA 169-C:30 (2016 codification; immediate oral report to the department)

OBSERVATION The board was told parents may opt a child out of health education "for any reason"; the statute is narrower

Asked whether parents can opt out of health education without a religious reason, the superintendent answered "Absolutely. They can. Yes they can." (2:06:45). RSA 186:11, IX-b, in the text in force that day, requires a policy allowing an exception to a unit of health or sex education "based on religious objections", with alternative learning; IX-c covers specific course material a parent finds "objectionable", on written notice to the principal, with an alternative "at the parent's expense". So a parent may object to particular material for any reason under IX-c, but the statute provides a wholesale exemption from a unit only on religious grounds. The superintendent was right that the new element in the draft is two weeks' advance notice: the 2017 codification, effective September 16, 2017, adds "not less than 2 weeks advance notice" of human-sexuality course material. Members asked why religion appears in the policy at all; the answer is that the statute puts it there.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-b and IX-c (2016 codification, in force to Sept. 15, 2017); RSA 186:11, IX-c (2017 codification, eff. Sept. 16, 2017: two weeks' notice)

OBSERVATION No one could say whether the district runs the drug-free awareness program its policy promises

The draft drug-free workplace policy commits the district to a drug-free awareness program for employees. Asked whether one exists, the superintendent described pre-employment drug screening (1:43:16) and the assistant superintendent said "Not that I've seen, but we can check." (1:43:54). Federal grantees must establish "a drug-free awareness program" informing employees of "the dangers of drug abuse in the workplace", the grantee's policy, "available drug counseling, rehabilitation, and employee assistance programs" and the penalties for violations. Screening applicants is a different thing. The district receives federal grants (about $3 million a year, per the business administrator on August 2), so the question Zullo asked is the right one; this record leaves it unanswered.

Sources: 41 U.S.C. §8103(a)(1)(B) (drug-free awareness program for grantees; Pub. L. 111-350, 2011, from the 1988 Act)
