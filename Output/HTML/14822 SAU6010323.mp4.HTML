These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. HIGH The SAU budget was adopted on January 3, after the statutory "before January 1" meeting

RSA 194-C:9, I reads: "At a meeting held before January 1, the school administrative unit board shall adopt a budget". The board adopted its $2,406,073 budget by voice vote at 0:37:06 on January 3, 2023. The December 1, 2022 minutes show the budget still under review, with six action items assigned and the note "Next SAU meeting is scheduled for 12.15.22"; the January 3 meeting approves only the December 1 minutes, and no December 15 packet folder, agenda or minutes was found in the SAU 6 share (folder listing of the 2022 Meeting Packets archive, a title search for "12.15.22" and a date-range search of SAU files created December 2, 2022 to January 3, 2023; Drive searches can miss files, so this is "not found", not "does not exist"). Paragraph III exempts districts that adopted RSA 194-C:9-a, but that procedure only brings in RSA 194-C:9-b, which "shall not apply to a school administrative unit that includes a city" (¶IV); Claremont is a city, and nothing on the record suggests either district voted to adopt it. Nobody at the meeting mentions the date. What would settle it: a record of a budget vote at a meeting held in December 2022.

Sources: RSA 194-C:9, I and III, 2022 codification (SAU budget adopted at a meeting held before January 1); RSA 194-C:9-b, IV (alternative procedure does not apply to an SAU that includes a city); SAU6 12.1.22 Meeting Minutes

MEDIUM The minutes record a different budget motion from the one adopted, and leave one member out of the attendance

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to include "the names of members" and "final decisions". On the recording Sprague first moved the budget "for three public hearing" (0:33:21), then restated: "We approve the six budget of $2,406,073." (0:34:39); the chair called the vote "of adopting this budget" (0:37:06) and later confirmed "Did we did approve it." (1:09:55). The minutes instead read: "Frank Sprague makes a motion to move 2 million 4 hundred and 6 thousand and 73 dollars to a public hearing. Seconded by Heather Whitney. All in favor via voice vote." A reader of the minutes alone would take the vote as sending the figure to a hearing rather than adopting it. The minutes also list Shannon Popescu as neither present nor absent, give the salary increase as 6.5% where Seaman says 6% (0:35:31), and credit Heather Whitney with the question on the increase that the recording gives to Sprague and the chair (0:10:43, 0:15:03). The minutes taker worked from the broadcast (0:00:38), which may explain some of this.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2022 codification (minutes: names of members, persons appearing, final decisions, mover and seconder); SAU6 1.3.23 Meeting Minutes_

MEDIUM A new SAU administrative position was to be posted the next day, before the district vote RSA 194-C:5, II(c) calls for

RSA 194-C:5, II(c): "Other administrative positions may be established, but only after 50 percent or more of the school districts in the school administrative unit representing 60 percent of the total pupils in the school administrative unit has voted favorably upon the establishment of the position." The Human Resources Director replaces an eliminated HR position (0:17:45) at about $130,000 (0:15:37). Sprague raised the point himself (0:50:51), suggesting the budget vote was "probably tacit approval"; Tempesta undertook to check (0:51:03) and said the job would be posted the next day (0:50:45), funded for the rest of the year from the unspent $36,000 merit pool, which Seaman said was "not a motion request" (0:53:10). Whether the statute's district vote means a vote of each district's school board or of its voters, and whether the director counts as a new position or a re-titled one, is not settled on this record; a recorded vote of the Claremont and Unity boards or meetings establishing the position would answer it.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, II(c) (new SAU administrative positions need a favorable vote of districts holding 60 percent of pupils)
