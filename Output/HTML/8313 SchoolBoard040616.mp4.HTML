Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on April 6, 2016, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The MS-22 certifying the March 8 appropriations was signed 29 days after the vote; the statute allows 20

The business administrator presented the MS-22, "the report of a appropriations actually voted", as "a document that we must approve and sign", noting that its signature page is the one filing the state still requires in hard copy (1:37:32), and members signed it at the table (1:39:39). RSA 21-J:34, II requires the report by which a school district's governing body "shall certify the appropriations voted by the meeting of the appropriate legislative body" and provides: "This report shall be filed within 20 days of the close of the meeting." Claremont's annual meeting closed with the official-ballot vote on March 8, 2016, so the report was due by March 28; it could not have been filed with these signatures before April 6, the twenty-ninth day. The 2015 text has no extension clause for this paragraph. Two points in mitigation: the business administrator said the rest of the filing is electronic, so other parts may have been submitted earlier, and the statute attaches no penalty to this paragraph. The certification itself, which is what the paragraph requires, was nine days late on this record.

Sources: RSA 21-J:34, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2007, 182:2, eff. April 1, 2007)

MEDIUM The motion to enter nonpublic session, as recorded, names no exemption

The chair asked for "a motion to go into nonpublic"; a member seconded and the clerk began a roll call (2:02:25 to 2:02:50). The superintendent asked for the session "just for a brief minute" (2:02:19). Nothing on the recording states which ground in RSA 91-A:3, II the board relied on. RSA 91-A:3, I(b), in the text in force in April 2016, provides: "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II", and requires a recorded roll-call vote. The roll call is consistent with the statute; the missing exemption is not. This page grades it MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the mover's words are not captured (only the chair's request and the second are), so an exemption may have been stated and lost from the transcript, and with no minutes surviving the motion's text cannot be checked. The subject of the session, and whether any decision was made in it, are not on the record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) (2015 codification; source note ends 2015, 19:1; 49:1; 105:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2016; 270:2)

MEDIUM A $5,000 gift was accepted at a regular meeting with no public hearing mentioned, on the night the business administrator described the hearing rule

The board accepted, by one motion and voice vote, $500 from TomTom, "a donation of $5,000" from the Children's Fund for a middle-school cross-country program, and the CLiF literacy award to Maple Avenue, which was added at the meeting because it "hasn't come forward to the board" (1:28:49 to 1:33:08). RSA 198:20-b, in the 2016 codification, lets a school board accept unanticipated money from a private source if the district has adopted the enabling article (¶I); for $5,000 or more, ¶III(a) requires a prior public hearing with newspaper notice of its time, place and subject at least 7 days ahead, and for smaller sums ¶III(b) requires notice in the agenda and the minutes. Earlier the same evening the business administrator described exactly that route for the $110,000 energy rebate: "we could have a public hearing and accept them under RSA 198 20 be" (0:52:04). No hearing on the $5,000 gift is mentioned. With no agenda, notice or minutes surviving, it cannot be shown whether a hearing was held or the gift was noticed, or whether Claremont had adopted the ¶I article, so this is a probable process gap rather than an established violation. The CLiF award is books and services rather than money, by LeClair's account (1:31:34), and may fall outside the section.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a)-(b) (2016 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

MEDIUM By consensus and without a vote, the board let the superintendent decide school reassignments that the statute required the board to vote on

The superintendent told the board that "This new RSA requires me to come to you and seek approval" when a child who moves within Claremont is allowed to stay at the old school, and asked for license to decide such requests himself and report afterward (1:22:31, 1:23:33). A member asked whether a motion was needed; the superintendent said "I just think it's our it's our agreement", and the chair asked the clerk to record the consensus (1:26:42 to 1:26:58). RSA 193:3, III, as amended by 2015, 125:1 (effective August 8, 2015) and in force on this date, requires each school board's change-of-school-assignment policy to require "a vote of the school board to reassign a pupil from the public school to which he or she is currently assigned", with the superintendent's role being to "recommend reassignment" to another school in the same district. An informal agreement that the superintendent will decide first and inform the board later does not supply that vote. The superintendent's purpose, avoiding a two-week wait that forces a mid-year move, is stated on the record, and no reassignment was decided at this meeting. The section was amended again effective July 2, 2016 (2016, 44:1), which this page does not evaluate.

Sources: RSA 193:3, III (2015 codification; 2015, 125:1, eff. Aug. 8, 2015)

POSITIVE Bargaining priorities and future policy work were taken into public session

RSA 91-A:2, I(a), in the 2016 text, excludes "Strategy or negotiations with respect to collective bargaining" from the definition of a meeting, so the board could have set its bargaining priorities in private. The superintendent instead asked for "a public discussion as to what are the priorities of the school board" while keeping strategy out of it (0:26:22, 0:26:47), and members debated salary gaps, plan costs and contribution splits on camera for more than forty minutes (0:27:23 to 1:08:08). The board also chose to review policies at its meetings rather than in a subcommittee, on the superintendent's assurance that "the public can weigh in as well" (1:45:50). Both choices go beyond what the law required in 2016.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I(a) (2015 codification; in force through 2016)
