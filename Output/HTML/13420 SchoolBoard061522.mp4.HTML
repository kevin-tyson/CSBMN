MEDIUM The nonpublic session opened off camera, and the only record of the motion to enter it cites “RSA only 91-A:3(a)” and names no mover or seconder

The recording begins after the nonpublic session, with the chair recognizing a motion to come out (Steven., 0:00:33; “The second, by Heather.”, 0:00:38) and a roll call (0:00:52). The motion to enter the session, its stated exemption and its roll call are therefore not on tape. The combined agenda and minutes document (CSB Agenda minutes 6.15.22 .pdf) is the only record. It reads, as the minutes’ own words: “Non Public RSA only 91-A:3(a)”, followed by a list of the seven members under “Roll call”. It gives no mover or seconder for entering the session.

RSA 91-A:3, I(b), as codified in 2022, requires that a motion to enter nonpublic session “state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II” and be decided by roll call. “91-A:3(a)” omits the paragraph; the likeliest reading is II(a) (dismissal, promotion, compensation or discipline of an employee), but the document does not say so and this page does not supply it. RSA 91-A:2, II (in force since January 1, 2019) requires minutes to record the names of the members who moved and seconded each motion. Graded MEDIUM: a roll call is listed and the exemption letter is plausible, so this is a documentation gap rather than an unexplained closed session.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), 2022 codification (motion states the paragraph II exemption; roll call); RSA 91-A:2, II, 2022 codification (minutes name movers and seconders); CSB Agenda minutes 6.15.22 .pdf

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for 99 years with no statutory determination stated on tape or in the minutes

Steven Horsky moved “to seal the minutes from the non public for 99 years.” (0:01:11); the chair called a roll-call vote (0:01:21) and the members answered Yes in turn (0:01:23). The roll call satisfies the “recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session” that RSA 91-A:3, III requires. What is missing is the determination the same paragraph makes a condition of withholding: that disclosure would likely affect adversely the reputation of a person other than a board member, render the proposed action ineffective, or concern terrorism. No reason is stated on the recording, and the minutes record only that the motion was made, seconded by Heather Whitney and passed (the minutes say “all in favor via voice vote” and then list a roll call).

The statute also sets no fixed term: information is withheld “until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply.” A 99-year term is the board’s choice, not a statutory period. The seal may well be justified (the exemption cited for the session concerns employees), but a reader cannot tell which ground the board relied on. Graded MEDIUM as a documentation gap.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III, 2022 codification (2/3 recorded vote with a determination; withheld until the circumstances no longer apply); CSB Agenda minutes 6.15.22 .pdf

OBSERVATION The guaranteed food-service surplus was described as available to address unpaid meal debt; federal guidance says that debt must be restored from non-federal funds

Asked whether the vendor’s guaranteed $25,000 annual surplus solves the district’s unpaid meal balances, the assistant superintendent for finance said it “gives the board the ability to address that balance should the board decide to.” (1:30:23) and that “we have the ability within the food service process to be able to address any of those delinquent accounts.” (1:30:33). He also said that unpaid meal debt is the district’s, not the vendor’s: “If that debt cannot be covered, it’s our bad debt.” (1:33:10). The chair put past food-service debt at “$30,000 plus.” (1:32:51).

USDA’s memo SP 23-2017 (March 23, 2017) states that nonprofit school food service account resources “may not be used to cover costs related to the bad debt” and that “these losses must be restored using non-Federal funds.” If the guaranteed surplus is paid into the food service account, using it to write off delinquent meal charges would run against that guidance. No decision was taken at this meeting, and the guarantee could be structured or used differently; this is noted so a reader can watch how the surplus is applied. The memo is guidance, not a regulation, so the item is an observation.

Sources: USDA FNS SP 23-2017, Unpaid Meal Charges (bad debt restored from non-federal funds)
