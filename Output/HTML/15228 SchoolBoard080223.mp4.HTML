Claremont School Board, August 2, 2023

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, six of seven members present, Candace Crawford's first as a member. The board extended the Claremont Middle School transportation waiver for the fall season, moved immunization policy JLCB to a second reading, sent the head-lice policy back for a first reading, accepted $4,000 in gifts, heard the business administrator's adequacy-aid presentation and a technology update, and set a special meeting for August 9 on an alternative-education program proposed for the Tech Center building. OBSERVATION An alternative-education program was being planned for the Tech Center building, which state law dedicates to career and technical education

Crawford reported that a new alternative-education program was to be placed at the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center and said “It's not an allowed use” (2:12:27). The chair reported advice that there are “RSA that indicate or require that we use this facility for CTE related activities” but, as she relayed it, “no, teeth in those RSA or sanctions” (2:13:46); both attribute the advice to a state official the transcript calls Doctor Beard and Jeff Beard, whose surname this project has not settled. The statute in force on August 2, 2023, RSA 188-E:3, II (2023 codification), provided that a CTE facility built or renovated with state CTE construction money “shall become the property of the school district or public academy, for use by the career and technical education center exclusively” (statute; source note ends 2021, 210:2). The chair said “although monies were given by the state” (2:14:27) but the record does not show which state grant funded which part of the building. Ben Nester said he hoped to start the program in September (2:22:53), and the chair said the board had already “gave him the the go ahead in that meeting” on the program but had not known the location (2:32:37, 2:32:53). Nothing was decided; the board set a meeting for August 9 to hear options (2:35:50; see the August 9, 2023 page). The enforcement consequences the speakers described are their characterizations; no sanction provision was verified for this page.

Sources: RSA 188-E:3, II (2023 codification).

POSITIVE Gifts were noticed on the agenda, recorded in the minutes and accepted in public session

The consent agenda listed $2,000 from Claremont Savings Bank and $1,000 from the Jack and Dorothy Byrne Foundation for Bluff Elementary and $1,000 from Claremont Savings Bank for the Back to School Festival (agenda; the agenda prints Byrnes), and the chair read them aloud before the consent agenda was taken (0:01:46). Under RSA 198:20-b, III(b), as amended by 2023, 38:1, effective July 18, 2023, for funds under $20,000 “the school board shall post notice of the funds in the agenda and shall include notice in the minutes of the school board meeting in which such funds are discussed”, and acceptance “shall be made in public session of any regular school board meeting” (statute). All three steps are in the record two weeks after the amendment took effect. Whether the district has adopted the enabling article that ¶I requires is not shown in this record.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(b) (source note ends 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023); agenda Exhibits A and B.

POSITIVE The adequacy-aid presentation was posted in advance with its sources, and its key rates match the statute

The business administrator's slide deck (Exhibit H) was in the public folder the day before the meeting, and she walked the board and viewers through the formula from FY2020 on and pointed to the NHDOE and DRA sources (1:15:09, 1:31:54).
