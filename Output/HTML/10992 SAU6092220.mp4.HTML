HIGH Nonpublic session entered on the employee exemption, then described as a tuition-contract session

The motion read at 0:25:38 relied on RSA 91-A:3, II(a): the dismissal, promotion, compensation or discipline of a public employee. After the session the chair said he had cited the wrong paragraph and that the correct one was II(k), “Contracts to the regarding the Goshen School District” (0:30:41); the superintendent then referred to a pending tuition agreement (0:31:26).

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires the motion to state on its face the specific exemption relied on, and I(c) confines everything discussed and decided in the session to the matters set out in that motion. On the chair's own account the board discussed a tuition contract after voting to discuss an employee matter. A correction announced after the doors reopen cannot change what the public was told when it was excluded. In the chair's favor, he made the correction on the record, addressing someone transcribed as "Mr. binder", which is the only reason the discrepancy can be seen at all.

A further question for a reviewer: II(k) covers consideration by a school board of a pupil tuition contract authorized by RSA 194 or RSA 195-A. Tuition contracts are made by school districts, and this body is the SAU board. Whether II(k) was available to it is not settled by the text and is not decided here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(a) and II(k) (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1 and 280:1)

MEDIUM Minutes sealed on a roll call with no statutory finding stated, in a session the law says must later be disclosed

On return the chair announced that the board had “sailed in the meeting minutes of the nonpublic” [sealed] for a period transcribed as 76 years, and called a roll (0:29:50 to 0:30:41). RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld beyond 72 hours only by a recorded two-thirds vote of the members present, taken in public session, determining that disclosure would likely affect a person's reputation, render the proposed action ineffective, or concern terrorism. No determination of any kind is heard on the recording, and the statute sets no fixed term: information may be withheld only until a majority finds the circumstances no longer apply.

If the session concerned a tuition contract, II(k) adds its own rule: a contract negotiated by a school board must be made public before it is considered for approval, together with the minutes of all nonpublic sessions about it. A long seal sits uneasily with that duty. The chair calls Rob, Rocco, John, Heather, Rebecca, Jason, Marjorie and Frank, with Yes answers heard for most; whether Towle and Popescu answered is not audible, and the two-thirds count cannot be confirmed without the minutes.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, II(k) and III (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION The marijuana clause debate rested partly on a wrong statement of federal law

An unidentified speaker said medical marijuana is not “actually considered a controlled substance” and is “Class three.” [Schedule III] ({R(88)}, {R(90)}). In September 2020 marijuana was a Schedule I controlled substance: 21 U.S.C. 812(c), Schedule I(c)(10), lists "Marihuana", and no rescheduling had occurred. The board's choice to drop the card-holder clause is a policy decision the record explains on other grounds (drug-test timing, Vermont hiring competition, and the superintendent's statement that impairment at work is handled anyway), and the amended text, as the chair stated it, still bars unlawful manufacture, distribution and possession at work. No rule is shown to be broken; the point is that one premise offered in the room was incorrect.

Sources: 21 U.S.C. 812(c), Schedule I(c)(10)
