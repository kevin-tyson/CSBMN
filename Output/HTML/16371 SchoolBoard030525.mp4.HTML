Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on March 5, 2025 (or, for the audit, in the fiscal year audited), or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The FY2020 audit report was issued in July 2023 and presented to the board in March 2025; the FY2021 audit was still in fieldwork

The agenda noticed "Results of FY 2021 Audit"; Arlene Hawkins corrected it on the floor because "Our packet is FY 20." (0:01:20). The auditor, Michael Campo of Plodzik & Sanderson, attending virtually, said of fiscal year 2021: "We are in the 21 audits." (0:33:30). Candace Crawford read the FY2020 report's date: "the report is dated July 21st, 2023. This is March of 25." (0:35:43). Campo explained that the date is when the signed management representation letter arrived, "We don't need two years to ship a report." (0:36:43), and that "from management side we can't control when it's district." (0:37:08). He told the board "as a school district, receive a substantial amount of federal funds" (0:39:25).

Under the Uniform Guidance in force for fiscal 2020, an entity that "expends $750,000 or more during the non-Federal entity's fiscal year in Federal awards must have a single or program-specific audit conducted for that year" (2 CFR 200.501(a)), and the reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period" (2 CFR 200.512(a)(1)). For the year ended June 30, 2020 that was March 31, 2021; OMB's COVID-19 relief (M-20-26) added six months for year ends from March 30 to June 30, 2020, making it September 30, 2021. A report issued July 21, 2023 is about 22 months past even the extended date, and the FY2021 audit, due March 31, 2022, was not finished three years later. HIGH rests on one premise this page cannot close: that federal expenditures reached $750,000 in each year. The auditor's statement makes that likely; the FY2020 report's schedule of federal awards would settle it, and that report is not in the public packet (flag 2). State law (RSA 21-J:19) sets no comparable deadline for a district's own audit.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2020 annual edition ($750,000 single-audit threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2020 annual edition (reporting package due nine months after year end); OMB M-20-26 (June 18, 2020): six-month extension for fiscal years ending March 30 to June 30, 2020, as summarized by the Washington State Auditor (secondary source); CSB Agenda 3.5.25 (2).pdf; 18. OBSERVATION The chair proposed taking the design of the safety position into nonpublic session; the exemptions are about specific people

Explaining why she would not support the safety specialist that night, the chair said the board would be "talking about positions and people within that are currently employees here." and "That's a nonpublic, how we would be using resources, yada yada." (2:28:18); she then said "we'll do a non public for April 2nd." (2:33:47). Bonnie Miles disagreed in part: the resource analysis she wanted "is not for nonpublic" (2:31:36).

RSA 91-A:3, II(a) reaches "the dismissal, promotion, or compensation of any public employee or the disciplining of such employee"; II(b) the hiring of a person; II(c) matters likely to adversely affect a named person's reputation.
