SAU 6 Board, July 25, 2019

Summer meeting of the joint Claremont and Unity board, chaired by the vice chair: the board confirmed Jeff Small as director of technology and Donna Magoon as assistant superintendent, cut two central-office positions so that $205,416 could go back to Claremont and $24,125 to Unity, accepted a $3,602.09 backpack donation for the Back to School festival, and left the hiring of two middle-school teachers to a Claremont board meeting set for July 30. MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this SAU 6 meeting: neither share holds a year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The meeting confirmed two administrators, cut two positions and accepted a donation, and the recording is now the only public account. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the 2019 codification (in force from January 1, 2019), required that minutes "shall include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", that "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion shall be recorded in the minutes", and that minutes be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: minutes were written and approved. The SAU 6 board approved the July 25, 2019 minutes on Sprague's motion on October 10, 2019 (show 10567), and on this recording members correct and approve the June 4 minutes in the same way (0:02:56 to 0:06:03). What is missing is any public copy. Whether the minutes were open for inspection within five business days cannot be tested from here; the approval came eleven weeks later. The project's rule that SAU 6 minutes are often filed with later packets cannot be tested for 2019, because no SAU 6 folder from that year survives in the share.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2019); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (school administrative units are public bodies)

MEDIUM Staffing decisions held for audit findings still "almost complete", with fines pending abatement

The superintendent said he would not fill the assistant business administrator post until "the conclusion of the audit findings" (0:10:30), that the office needs "a significant accountant" as "the preliminary audit findings come in" (0:10:58), and that the auditors were "almost complete with the audit findings", with hope for "the abatement of the fines" (0:11:33). He did not say which audit, which entity or which agency imposed the fines. Six weeks later, on September 5, 2019 (show 10522), he told the SAU 6 board that the auditors were then closing the Claremont fiscal 2017 audit and would start fiscal 2018 the following week, and the board discussed an IRS closing letter. If the audit in question covers fiscal 2017 (July 2016 to June 2017) and the district spent $750,000 or more in federal awards that year, the federal single-audit reporting package was due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period", that is, by March 31, 2018. Neither condition is established on this recording, so the page does not grade this HIGH. It is graded MEDIUM as a control weakness stated by the superintendent himself: audits running well behind, penalties outstanding, a grants manager gone, and central-office staffing set by what the audit will show. The audit reports and the federal expenditure totals would settle the extent. New Hampshire's own audit statute, RSA 21-J:19, is permissive as to timing and is not the basis of this flag.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2019 annual edition; single-audit reporting package due date); 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2019 annual edition; $750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.303(a) (2019 annual edition; effective internal control over federal awards)

OBSERVATION A donation for a Claremont School District event was accepted by the SAU 6 board, with no agenda or minutes to show the notice the gift statute asks for

The SAU 6 board voted to accept "$3,602.09" from Wheelan and NFR for the Back to School festival (0:34:20, 0:34:38). The festival is run by a Claremont School District employee for Claremont families (0:23:38). New Hampshire's unanticipated-funds statute, RSA 198:20-b, lets a school board accept gifts "without further action by the school district" only where the district has adopted an enabling article, and for sums under $5,000 it provides (2019 codification, III(b)): "the school board shall post notice of the funds in the agenda and shall include notice in the minutes of the school board meeting in which such funds are discussed. The acceptance of unanticipated funds under this subparagraph shall be made in public session of any regular school board meeting." The acceptance here was made in public session, which the statute requires. Three things cannot be confirmed: whether the money was the SAU's or the district's to accept, since the statute speaks of a school district's school board; whether it appeared on the agenda, which does not survive; and whether the minutes noted it, which are not public. The Claremont board itself accepted two further gifts for the same festival on August 21, 2019. This is an observation: no rule verified for this page settles which board should have acted.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

POSITIVE The teacher-hiring decision was sent to the board the law gives it to

At the start of the meeting the presiding officer, a Unity member, asked that the part of the item that would reallocate money for middle-school teachers be struck, because it "is a Claremont issue" that "needs to be done at a Claremont school board and not here" (0:00:47, 0:01:25). The board agreed (0:01:47, 0:02:28), cut the SAU positions and returned the money to both districts (0:17:28), and scheduled a Claremont board meeting for July 30 to decide on the teachers (0:37:18). When Sprague suggested instead letting "the superintendent exercise his right to hire", she answered that "It's moving money around. That's the issue." (0:36:16, 0:36:25). RSA 189:39 provides that "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers employed in the schools in their school administrative unit", so the election of Claremont's teachers belongs to the Claremont board. Keeping the SAU board's action to SAU positions and leaving the district's money and hires to the district board is the allocation the statutes make. See the July 30, 2019 Claremont page for the follow-up.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (2019 codification; source note ends 1971, 371:2); RSA 194-C:5 (SAU board organization and SAU personnel; 1996, 298:3, never amended)
