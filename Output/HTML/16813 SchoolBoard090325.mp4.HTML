Claremont School Board, September 3, 2025

Board meeting opening with a public hearing on borrowing up to $4,000,000 from Claremont Savings Bank against the district's FY2026 adequacy grant. District counsel, the acting superintendent and the business administrator presented the district's financial condition; the comment period ran about an hour and 49 minutes; the board then adopted the borrowing resolution 6–0 by roll call, accepted three donated hospital beds, announced Frank Sprague's resignation and heard its policy subcommittee's report. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 3, 2025, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The district cited RSA 33:8-a, I for its hearing, then voted the note the same evening; that paragraph sets a 15-day minimum between hearing and vote

The agenda announced the hearing "Pursuant to RSA 198:20-d and RSA 33:8-a, I" to consider "incurring debt in excess of $100,000" (agenda text). RSA 33:8-a, I requires a public hearing on any note issue over $100,000 and adds that the hearing "shall be held at least 15 days, but not more than 60 days prior to the meeting, or adjourned session thereof, at which the bond or note issued is to be voted upon" (statute text). Angell told the room the board would vote "at the meeting immediately following the public hearing" (0:45:27), and it did, by roll call at 2:59:39. If ¶I governs this note, the vote came about 15 days early. Two readings cut the other way. RSA 198:20-d, under which the note was issued, opens "Notwithstanding any other provision of law to the contrary" and itself requires only "notice and public hearing" with no interval; its only express exemption from RSA 33 is "relative to debt limits" (statute text). And ¶I's interval is written for a meeting at which a note is "voted upon", language that fits a legislative-body vote more naturally than a board resolution. The notice itself met ¶I's seven-day publication rule on the resolution's own recital, "no later than August 27th, 2025" (2:56:18). Graded MEDIUM because the district invoked ¶I as its authority and did not follow its timing; whether ¶I applied is a question for bond counsel, whose opinion is not in the record.

Sources: RSA 33:8-a, I and III (hearing 15 to 60 days before the vote; 7 days' published notice; exemptions for cities and RSA 33:7 tax anticipation notes); RSA 198:20-d (reimbursement anticipation notes; notice and public hearing); CSB Agenda 9.3.25, item III

MEDIUM The draft minutes omit the call-to-order time and the name of the person who produced them, and do not show the attested resolution they were to include

Since August 22, 2025, RSA 91-A:2, II has required minutes to include "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes" (statute text). The draft gives an end time, "Consent adjournment at 10:00pm", and the hearing's opening, 6:36 p.m., but not when the meeting was called to order, and its text carries no "submitted by" line or other name of its author, unlike the same clerk's minutes for August 25. The resolution Hawkins moved directs "that an attested copy of said resolution be included in the minutes of this meeting" (2:55:17); the readable text of the posted draft (which also carries the policy slides and the financial presentation) contains no copy of it. The minutes also record Howard's motion "to approve the RAN loan as presented" (minutes text) with no vote on it. Mitigation: the minutes reached the public share quickly and name every mover and seconder (flag 8), and the times they do give reconcile with the recording to the minute.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes contents; 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025); draft minutes 9.3.25

OBSERVATION Several decisions were taken by "no objection" rather than by vote, including approval of four sets of minutes

The chair describes the consent agenda as a way to "accept the agenda as written without motion, and to also approve the minutes" (0:00:18) and approves the amended agenda and the minutes of August 14, 20, 21 and 25 on no objection (0:01:53), though the agenda labels the item "(vote required)" (agenda text). The Finance Subcommittee chair was filled by consensus (3:05:17), and the board agreed without a motion that the chair could seek a law-enforcement investigation (3:20:12, 3:26:44). Unanimous consent is a recognized way to take uncontested action, and the minutes record each outcome, which is what RSA 91-A:2, II requires. The observation is that the board's own agenda calls for a vote, and that a request to involve the county attorney is the kind of decision a recorded vote documents best. The agenda's separate citizens' comment item (II.b) was also not held as such; public comment was taken inside the hearing.

Sources: CSB Agenda 9.3.25, item II.a; RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes record final decisions)

OBSERVATION A $12,000 gift listed for a vote to accept was not voted on, and the threshold statement on the beds is garbled

The agenda lists both gifts "Per RSA 198:20-b,III and Policy KCD" for a "(Vote to Accept)" (agenda text). RSA 198:20-b, III requires a prior public hearing for unanticipated funds of $20,000 or more and provides that "The acceptance of unanticipated funds under this subparagraph shall be made in public session of any regular school board meeting" (statute text). The chair described a "letter of commitment" from North Country Smokehouse for $12,000, then "a letter of not accepting that donation" (3:01:06); the minutes say "No vote needed to accept" (minutes text). If the money is paid to vendors for the culinary program rather than to the district, no acceptance may be needed; if it comes to the district, ¶III's public-session acceptance applies. The record does not say which. For the beds the chair said their value is "estimated less than $20,000, which would require a public hearing" (3:01:36): below $20,000 no hearing is required, so the sentence as transcribed reverses the rule, though the board's action (acceptance by vote without a hearing) is consistent with it.
