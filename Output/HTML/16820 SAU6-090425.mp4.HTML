SAU 6 Board, September 4, 2025

Meeting of the joint SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity) nine days after it placed the superintendent on paid leave. The board went into an 84-minute nonpublic session on personnel and reputation grounds, then heard a staffing update from the temporary acting superintendent, voted unanimously to eliminate the vacant curriculum director position, heard an SAU audit update, and accepted a state-funded cybersecurity grant. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 4, 2025, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH No minutes of the 84-minute nonpublic session were located, and no vote to seal them was taken in public session

RSA 91-A:3, III requires that "Minutes and decisions reached in nonpublic session shall be publicly disclosed within 72 hours of the meeting, unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" (statute text), the body finds that disclosure would harm one of the interests the paragraph lists. The board's own policy BBBH-S, §I.5.f, says SAU minutes "shall be prepared and disclosed or sealed in accordance with RSA 91-A:2, II & III, and 91-A:3, III" (policy text). The board met in nonpublic session from 6:34 to 7:58 p.m. on employee and reputation grounds (0:01:27 to 0:02:33), nine days after the SAU 6 board placed the superintendent on paid leave. On return no decision was announced and no vote to seal was taken: the recording moves straight to the staffing update (0:03:02), and the public minutes record none. Because a sealing vote must be taken in public session, the nonpublic minutes were due to be public by September 7, 2025. A two-stage search found none: the meeting's own packet folder holds the agenda and the grant agreement; MAP.md's inventories of every later SAU 6 packet folder through May 2026 list none; and the Meeting Minutes share's 2025 Non-Public folder holds no SAU 6 file (the share has never held one). Graded HIGH under the project's rule for an unmitigated absence: nothing on the record explains it. The negative is "not found", not "does not exist"; the grade should fall if the district produces nonpublic minutes or a recorded public sealing vote.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III (motion and roll call; nonpublic minutes and decisions disclosed within 72 hours unless sealed by recorded 2/3 vote taken in public session); Policy BBBH-S, §I.5.f (Exhibit C copy in the September 12, 2024 packet); 2025 Non-Public folder

MEDIUM The public minutes reached the district's share 69 days after the meeting, and omit the time the meeting was called to order

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute text), which for a Thursday meeting is the following Thursday, September 11, 2025. Both copies of the draft were created in the SAU 6 packet share on November 12, 2025 (Drive creation times 3:16 and 3:17 p.m. Eastern), one in the 10. SAU6 9.11.25 folder, which itself was created on September 11. Since August 22, 2025, the same paragraph has also required "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes" (statute text). The draft gives the end time (8:16 p.m.) and its author, but its only start time is the 6:00 PM in its heading, which conflicts with the agenda's 6:30 call to order; the arithmetic in the facts panel puts the actual call to order at about 6:32 p.m. The same meeting postponed approval of the June 12 minutes, by then twelve weeks old, because members had not received them (0:01:07). Mitigation: the statute requires that minutes be open to inspection, not posted online, so a Drive date proves when the public could see them online, not when they first existed; nothing in the record shows whether they were available at the SAU office sooner.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (five business days; minutes contents as amended by 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025); 11. DRAFT SAU6 Meeting Minutes 9.4.25

OBSERVATION The board accepted a grant whose value it did not know and authorized signing an agreement with a blank license-cost line and a $1,000,000 indemnity cap

The motion authorized the administration "to sign any and all documents related to this grant" (0:15:00). The chair described it as costing nothing (0:15:00), and the minutes record that "It is unknown how much the grant is for" (minutes text). The agreement in the packet (posted a week after the vote; flag 3) runs three years from its effective date, carries readiness-audit forms with a line for "Estimated yearly ongoing cost to Municipality for licenses" left blank, and in its CrowdStrike acknowledgement provides that the end user's "defense and indemnification of Overwatch shall not exceed $1,000,000 in total" (agreement text). None of this was discussed. The chair cited RSA 198:20-b as the reason grants come to the board (0:13:20). That statute is written for school districts: its ¶I lets "any school district at an annual meeting" authorize its school board to accept unanticipated money (statute text). Whether and how it reaches an SAU board was not explained. The grant is state-administered and the services are delivered in kind, so the immediate cash risk Crawford raised about reimbursement (0:17:06) may not arise; the cost after year three is the open question.

Sources: SAU 6 CrowdStrike Municipal Cybersecurity ... Agreement (packet); RSA 198:20-b, I and III (school district acceptance of unanticipated funds); draft minutes 9.4.25, item 6
