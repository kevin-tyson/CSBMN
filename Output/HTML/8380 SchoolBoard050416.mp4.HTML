Claremont School Board, May 4, 2016

Regular meeting dominated by the Stevens High School A/B schedule: student and parent comment, the superintendent's account of two arbitration rulings against the district and his statement that the board will not return to the 4x4 schedule, and the principal's data. Also a Youth Risk Behavior Survey study group report, a 4-H underwater robotics team, adoption of a 2016-17 calendar with two parent conferences that the teachers' union says requires $54,000 in pay, and approval of an $8,000 strategic-planning consultant. OBSERVATION The superintendent announced that the board would not follow the arbitrator's schedule ruling, resting on a statute he misquoted, and no board vote on that decision is on the record

The superintendent reported two arbitration awards finding that the A/B schedule violated the teachers' agreement, the second (April 2016) ordering 8% pay and a return to the 4x4 schedule for 2016-17, and said "The board is not returning to the form of 4x4 block schedule" (1:47:52 to 1:53:08). His legal basis was RSA 189:1-a, II, which he read as making the board responsible for "the structure, accountability, efficacy, and delivery of instruction" (1:44:07). The statute, in the 2015 codification, reads: "Elected school boards shall be responsible for establishing the structure, accountability, advocacy, and delivery of instruction in each school operated and governed in its district." The word is advocacy, not efficacy. The question whether an award intrudes on management's authority turns on RSA 273-A:1, XI, which excludes from bargainable "terms and conditions of employment" any "managerial policy within the exclusive prerogative of the public employer", including "the functions, programs and methods of the public employer"; that question belongs to the Public Employee Labor Relations Board, which the superintendent said the dispute might reach (1:53:21). Whether the award is "actually unenforceable" (1:45:04) is his characterization. No motion or vote on refusing the award is heard at this meeting, and the April 6 recording ends as the board enters a nonpublic session whose subject is unstated (see the April 6 page). With no minutes, where the board decided this cannot be established from the record.

Sources: RSA 189:1-a, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2013, 243:1, eff. Sept. 22, 2013); RSA 273-A:1, XI (2015 codification; source note ends 2014, 13:1, 2)

OBSERVATION The board adopted a calendar with a second parent conference over the union's objection that its agreement provides for one

Option 1 keeps the contract's total hours (1,209, equal to 186 days under the agreement, by the superintendent's account) but redistributes them into 175 instructional days, workdays and two parent conferences (3:26:50). The assistant superintendent confirmed the teachers' agreement "does specifically outline that there would be one parent teacher conference" (3:31:30), and the association asked $54,000 to hold two (3:22:45). The superintendent said the board "has the authority under state statute" to set the calendar (3:33:57). Under RSA 273-A:1, XI, "wages, hours and other conditions of employment" are bargainable terms, while "the functions, programs and methods of the public employer" are managerial policy; a calendar that keeps total hours but changes how they are used sits between the two, and the record does not show counsel's view. A member proposed converting the second conference to an instructional day to avoid a new dispute (3:33:06); the board adopted Option 1 by voice vote with no audible opposition (3:37:52). Separately, the 175-day calendar rests on the statute's hours alternative: RSA 189:1 requires "at least 180 days in each year, or the equivalent number of hours as required in the rules of the department of education". This page did not verify the department's hour minimums as they stood in 2016, so the superintendent's statement of compliance (3:29:28) is reported as his.

Sources: RSA 273-A:1, XI (2015 codification); RSA 189:1 (2015 codification; source note ends 2011, 42:1, eff. July 8, 2011)

POSITIVE The risk-behavior survey ran on parental consent and was reported in aggregate, beyond the state's opt-out rule

The study group's presenter said the survey "requires parental consent" and "it is an opt in", that "It is completely anonymous. There are no names on students, surveys.", and that results are seen only in aggregate (0:33:47, 0:36:04, 1:00:04). RSA 186:11, IX-d, in the 2015 codification in force on this date, required districts to "notify a parent or legal guardian of a non-academic survey or questionnaire and its purpose" and to "allow a parent or legal guardian to opt out", with the survey available for review "at least 10 days prior to distribution to students". Active consent is more protective than the opt-out the statute required. The group presented its methods and findings at a televised meeting, recruited members with an open invitation to about 1,900 parents (1:15:24), and the superintendent reported a call from the state commending the effort (1:10:58). Whether the consent procedure was the district's own choice or the survey's standard protocol is not stated.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-d (2015 codification; source note ends 2015, 161:6, eff. Aug. 25, 2015)
