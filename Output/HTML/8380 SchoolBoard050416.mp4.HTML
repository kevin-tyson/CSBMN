Claremont School Board — May 4, 2016

Regular meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. Two students and a parent opened citizens' comments with complaints about the new Stevens High School A/B schedule; the superintendent then set out the history of the teachers' union's arbitration challenge to that schedule and said the board would not return to the 4x4 block despite an April 2016 award. The board also heard the Youth Risk Behavior Survey study group, adopted a 175-day school calendar with two parent-teacher conferences over the union's demand for $54,000, and approved an $8,000 strategic-plan consultant. OBSERVATION The superintendent announced that the board would not comply with the arbitrator's order to restore the 4x4 schedule, citing RSA 189:1-a

At 1:43:41 the superintendent said an April 2015 arbitration found that "the school board violated the collective bargaining agreement. But the school board did not accept this ruling.", and that a second award in April 2016 ordered an 8% payment ("the 8% increase represents $153,000", 1:52:40) and a return to the 4x4 block. He stated at 1:53:08 that "The board is not returning to the form of 4x4 block schedule", and at 1:54:15 that "The arbitrator did not acknowledge state statute." The statute he read is RSA 189:1-a, II, which in 2016 provided that "Elected school boards shall be responsible for establishing the structure, accountability, advocacy, and delivery of instruction in each school operated and governed in its district." (the transcript renders "advocacy" as "efficacy").

Whether that paragraph overrides a bargained agreement and an arbitration award is a contested legal question this page cannot resolve; it depends on the agreement's terms and the awards, none of which is in any available record. What the recording does show: the account was the administration's alone ("the other side of the coin", 1:25:37); no board vote to reject either award is on this recording; and the superintendent expected the dispute to reach the Public Employee Labor Relations Board. Whether the $153,000 was paid, and from which appropriation, is not stated. A reader should treat the history given here as one party's characterization and look for the awards and any PELRB decision.

Sources: RSA 189:1-a, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2013, 243:1, eff. Sept. 22, 2013).

OBSERVATION The board adopted a calendar with a second parent-teacher conference that the union said the contract does not provide for

The superintendent reported at 3:22:45 that the union's executive board wanted $54,000 if a second conference were added, and argued the total of 1209 hours matched the contract. The assistant superintendent confirmed at 3:31:30 that the existing agreement "does specifically outline that there would be one parent teacher conference". Brent Ferland suggested avoiding the dispute by making the day instructional; the board instead approved option 1 by voice vote (3:37:52). Under the 2016 text, "terms and conditions of employment" in RSA 273-A:1, XI means "wages, hours and other conditions of employment other than managerial policy", and refusing to negotiate in good faith is an unfair labor practice under RSA 273-A:5, I(e). Whether a second conference within unchanged total hours is a term of employment or managerial policy is exactly the kind of question the PELRB decides; this page does not. It is flagged because the board acted knowing the union's objection, on the same night the superintendent predicted PELRB litigation over the schedule.

On the other side: the calendar's 175 instructional days rest on the hours alternative that RSA 189:1 then allowed ("180 days in each year, or the equivalent number of hours as required in the rules of the department of education"), and the administration explained the hours arithmetic in public before the vote. The superintendent also disclosed that he had added his own footnotes to the union's email, which let members tell his annotations from the union's text.

Sources: RSA 273-A:1, XI (2014, 13:1, 2; text in force in 2016); RSA 273-A:5, I(e) (1979, 374:4; unamended); RSA 189:1 (2015 codification; source note ends 2011, 42:1).
