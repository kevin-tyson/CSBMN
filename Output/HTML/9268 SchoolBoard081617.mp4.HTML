On August 2 a Ward 1 resident asked in citizens' comments why Claremont's per-pupil cost exceeded the state average, and members asked for the taxpayer share and the feasibility of an outside review; the superintendent promised a presentation (August 2, 2017 page). Tonight the business administrator opened by listing those questions (0:38:35) and answered each with state data: revenue shares, spending by function, nine-year trends, valuation per pupil, salaries, ratios and adjusted cost per pupil. POSITIVE A member declined to deliberate with colleagues on social media

Responding to criticism posted online, Pierce said board members "cannot go on their 2 or 3 at a time and communicate back our thoughts or feelings, because we will be in a, I believe a quorum", and that she would answer at the meeting instead (1:01:00). RSA 91-A:2-a, in force since 2008, requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings" and bars communications outside a meeting, "including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members", from being used to circumvent the chapter. Her reasoning tracks that rule, and stating it on the record helps the public understand why members do not answer online.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification; source 2008, 303:4)

POSITIVE Free and reduced-price eligibility confined to one SAU employee, with no visible distinction at the register

The superintendent described the new process: "one person in the SA, one person having access to that information" for screening and approval (0:28:56), the application "it's not going to be common knowledge among principals of secretaries or myself", and at the register "they're not going to be a different colored ticket, a different line. All children will be the same and there'll be no stigma." (0:34:16). Federal rules bar "overt identification" of children receiving free or reduced-price meals "by the use of special tokens or tickets or by any other means" and separate lines or entrances; limiting access to eligibility files to a single reviewer goes beyond that floor. Members and the assistant superintendent also urged every eligible family to apply, since Title I, E-Rate and state adequacy aid turn on the counts (0:34:38).
