MEDIUM A policy subcommittee formed on the advice that three members need no notice, two hours after counsel said otherwise

Proposing a school board policy subcommittee, the superintendent said: "Can't be more than three unless we warn the meeting." (2:45:43). Horsky, Benware and Zullo volunteered, and he planned a Saturday-morning session (2:45:54, 2:46:08). Earlier the same evening the association's attorney had told the board that a policy committee "is governed by the right to know law in exactly the same way as your school board is", with 24-hour notice and minutes (0:18:56), and that two members of a three-member policy committee discussing policy are a meeting (0:21:27). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), unchanged since 2008, makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body" of a school board a public body in its own right, so the subcommittee's meetings needed notice and minutes whatever its size; three is simply below a quorum of the full board. The same advice was given to the SAU 6 board on March 29 (see the March 29, 2018 SAU 6 page). The record does not show whether the subcommittee's meetings were later noticed or minuted, which is why this is MEDIUM rather than HIGH.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1, so the text in force in 2018); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification)

OBSERVATION Two details of the training measured against the statutes

On sealing nonpublic minutes, the attorney said "There's only two reasons you can seal public minutes. I mean nonpublic minutes." (1:23:14). RSA 91-A:3, III, as in force in 2018, allows minutes to be withheld only by a "recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" that determines divulgence would likely affect adversely the reputation of a person other than a board member, would render the proposed action ineffective, or pertains to terrorism: three grounds, not two, though the terrorism ground rarely arises for a school board. His statements that the vote is taken in public with the reason stated and that unsealed minutes are due within 72 hours ("within three days", 1:23:33) match the statute. Separately, his forecast of a bill requiring a district data privacy plan by 2019 (0:46:34) was borne out: RSA 189:66, V, added by 2018, 252:1 (eff. Aug. 11, 2018), required a data and privacy governance plan "presented to the school board for review and approval by June 30, 2019". Neither point is a finding against the board.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; 280:1); RSA 189:66, V (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 252:1, 2, eff. Aug. 11, 2018)

POSITIVE Right-to-Know training held in public, on camera, for a new board

The board gave about 75 minutes of a regular meeting to Right-to-Know and digital-communications training by the association's counsel (0:14:24 to 1:31:30), and the chair asked that it be presented in full because "there are people watching at home as well" (0:15:40). The presentation covered the statutory definition of a meeting, committees as public bodies, sequential communications, non-meetings, email and social media, and members questioned the attorney on their own practices (1:10:20, 1:12:42, 1:26:49). RSA 91-A:2-a requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings"; nothing requires a board to train its members on the chapter, and doing it in public lets residents learn the same rules the board is held to.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4, never amended)

POSITIVE A roll-call vote so the administrators would know each member's answer

On the administrators' request to apply a second $1,000 of contract course money to student loans, Zullo asked for a roll call, and the chair agreed that it gives "the exact" count (3:00:17, 3:01:29). The clerk called each member and all seven answered no (3:02:49 to 3:03:10). In 2018 RSA 91-A required a roll call for a motion to enter nonpublic session and for votes taken while a member participates remotely, not for an ordinary open-session vote. Recording each member's vote on a request touching a ratified bargaining agreement is a practice above that minimum.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(e) (2017 codification); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) (2017 codification)
