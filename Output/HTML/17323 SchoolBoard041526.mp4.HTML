OBSERVATION Minutes of the 17-minute nonpublic session were not located, and no vote to seal them was taken

The board entered nonpublic session on a roll-call vote (1:09:58) and returned on another (1:10:31); the approved minutes record 7:40 to 7:57 p.m. under RSA 91-A:3, II(b), the hiring exemption, which fits the rehire vote that followed in public. On tape the chair's motion request is transcribed "part two sets a C" (1:09:27); the agenda and minutes both say II(b), and this page follows them. Neither the recording nor the minutes shows a vote to withhold the nonpublic minutes, so RSA 91-A:3, III required them to be "publicly disclosed within 72 hours of the meeting". This run did not find them: the approved public minutes summarize only the two motions, and a Drive search of the district's "2026 Non-Public" folder returned nothing. Drive searches in this project are known to miss files that exist, so this is reported as an observation, not a missed deadline. The roll-call requirement of RSA 91-A:3, I(b) was met on tape.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III (roll call; nonpublic minutes disclosed within 72 hours unless sealed by a 2/3 recorded vote); 4.15.26 approved minutes

OBSERVATION An unpaid prior bill "wasn't in the ledger", and the board still did not know its July 1, 2025 opening balance

Explaining why the deficit estimate rose from zero to $1 million to $1.6 million, the chair described a sizable surprise she could not detail publicly: "a past due bill that wasn't paid. Right? Right. That wasn't in the ledger." (0:34:00); Whitney recalled a "1.2 million surprise last year right at the end of the year" (0:34:27). Governmental funds recognize expenditures on the modified accrual basis when the liability is incurred (GASB 34, ¶79); a bill for goods or services received in an earlier year that was never recorded is the kind of error GASB 100 requires to be corrected by restating beginning fund balance, with note disclosure. The chair also said the auditors would produce a "reliable, verifiable figure" for the July 1, 2025 starting balance only by July 2026 (0:34:52), nine and a half months into the year. The record does not give the bill's amount, period or vendor, so no GASB departure can be tied to a figure here; the FY2025 audit report and any restatement note would settle it. The audit-filing deadline of 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (nine months after year end) took effect July 1, 2026 and did not apply on this date; RSA 21-J:19's audit timing is permissive.

Sources: GASB 34, ¶79 (modified accrual in governmental funds); GASB 100, ¶12, ¶25-28 (error corrections restate beginning balances); N.H. Admin. Rules, Rev 1102.03 (districts follow GASB GAAP); RSA 21-J:19 (audits; permissive timing)

OBSERVATION Right-to-Know compliance costs cannot be passed to requesters beyond copying; a public cost log is lawful but not required

The interim superintendent reported 61 completed and 11 pending requests, some involving 8,000 emails, and more than $10,000 in legal fees (0:52:01, 0:53:54). Her account of the timing rule matches RSA 91-A:4, IV(b): within five business days the body must make the record available, deny the request, or give a written statement of the time reasonably needed. Lavalette's comparison with state agencies that "may charge fees to recover actual costs" (0:55:17) needs a limit stated: RSA 91-A:4, IV(d) allows only "the actual cost of providing the copy" and provides that "No cost or fee shall be charged for the inspection or delivery, without copying, of governmental records". Staff search, review and redaction time and legal fees therefore fall on the district. Publishing a log of requests and their costs, as he proposed, is not required by RSA 91-A and not barred by it; requesters' identities would need the same review as any other record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:4, IV(b) and IV(d) (five business days; copy cost only; no fee for inspection; note ends 2024, 49:1, 2, eff. Aug. 13, 2024)
