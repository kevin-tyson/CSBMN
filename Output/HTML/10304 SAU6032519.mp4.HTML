MEDIUM Lunch program application filed late for a second year; reimbursement lost from the start of the school year

The acting superintendent reports that the SAU-level National School Lunch Program application "was not filed till January 30th" and that reimbursement was lost from the start of the school year to that date (0:32:42, 0:33:12). The prior year's application went in at the beginning of February with the same result. The business administrator's figures are partly garbled on the transcript, but the Claremont amount for one year is read as $150,722.12 (0:35:16) and members put the loss between "almost $200,000" (0:44:17) and "over $300,000" (1:18:23). For a federal program, the Uniform Guidance requires the recipient to "Establish and maintain effective internal control over the Federal award". The same failure in two consecutive years points to a control weakness rather than a one-off. Severity is MEDIUM because the missed date is an administrative program deadline, not a statutory one, and because the record shows remedies already in place: a dedicated program assistant hired in November, split system roles, internal spot checks and training (0:36:23 to 0:42:53). Whether the loss reached the financial statements as a reduction in federal revenue, and how it was funded (the business administrator expected health insurance savings to cover it, 0:44:40), would be settled by the FY2019 audit.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.303(a), 2019 annual edition (internal control over federal awards)

MEDIUM The March 7 superintendent decision was made in nonpublic session; the roll call reached the public record three weeks later

The chair states that, on counsel's advice, "we actually should have come out of our nonpublic on March 7th to take a vote" (1:41:56). Because two members had changed since March 7, the board did not retake the vote; Petrin read the March 7 roll call into the record instead: 8 to 3 to offer the post to Michael Tempesta, with Sprague, Erickson and Sara Lowe voting no (1:46:02 to 1:46:47). The March 7 draft minutes described the nomination item as removed from the agenda rather than moved into nonpublic session, and were corrected tonight (0:08:07, 0:08:51). RSA 91-A:3, III requires that "Minutes and decisions reached in nonpublic session shall be publicly disclosed within 72 hours" unless withheld by a recorded two-thirds vote taken in public session with a statutory determination. The choice itself was announced within that window: the board announced it on Saturday night, March 9 (Valley News, March 9, 2019; Zullo describes the weekend press release at 0:22:55). What is not shown is any public release of the roll call before tonight, 21 days after the meeting. Valley News reported on March 29 that the board's attorney required the hiring vote to be made public. The cure was partial by the chair's own account: reading a nonpublic roll call aloud is not a vote taken in public session.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III, 2019 codification (nonpublic motion and roll call; 72-hour disclosure of minutes and decisions); Valley News, March 9, 2019 (reporting); Valley News, March 29, 2019 (reporting)

MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting can be located

RSA 91-A:2, II, as in force from January 1, 2019, requires minutes that name members and persons appearing, describe the subjects discussed and final decisions, name the mover and seconder of each motion, and are open to public inspection within five business days. No minutes, agenda or packet for this meeting are in any district share; the Drive archives start in 2022. Under the project's severity rule this is the mitigated limb, MEDIUM, not HIGH: the record shows minutes were produced, because on the April 11, 2019 SAU 6 recording (show 10321) the chair brings the minutes of the March 28 meeting before the board, and the chair here refers both to agenda item 3H (0:02:48) and to a packet (0:36:23). What is missing is the public copy today. Two things on this recording depend on the minutes: the efficiency-study vote, where no opposed count is spoken (1:27:45), and the abstention counts on the November 8 minutes (0:10:34).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2019 codification (minutes content, mover and seconder, five business days)

OBSERVATION Nonpublic minutes of February 19 and 21 sealed "for 99 years"

Amending the February 19 minutes, the chair says she has "the roll call vote for sealing the minutes for 99 years" and the same for February 21 (0:06:09, 0:06:39). RSA 91-A:3, III permits withholding nonpublic minutes only by a recorded two-thirds vote taken in public session that determines one of the statutory grounds, and then only "until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply". The statute sets no fixed term; a 99-year term substitutes a date for that test. Whether those votes were taken in public session with a stated determination is in the February minutes, which are not available. Valley News reported that Dr. Pfeifer was placed on paid leave in February, which may be the subject; the recording does not say.
