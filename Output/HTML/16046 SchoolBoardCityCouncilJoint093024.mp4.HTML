HIGH A two-hour meeting of the school board with no public comment period on the agenda

RSA 189:74, I (quoted from the statute): school boards “shall provide the opportunity for the public to comment on school district matters at a meeting of the school board held under RSA 91-A:2. The public comment period shall be for no less than 30 minutes.” The only exceptions are emergency meetings and meetings held solely for nonpublic session; this was neither. The agenda carries no citizens’ comment item and no member of the public spoke in more than two hours. By-law 2.04 requires public comment “at each regular meeting”, but by-law 2.15 gives state law precedence, and the board’s own policy BEDH commits it to the opportunity “at all Board meetings” with the same two exceptions. The October 2 agenda carries the item. The usual doubt about whether the thirty minutes binds elapsed time does not arise: no opportunity was offered. Graded HIGH on the unmitigated limb; the record does not show anyone being refused.

Sources: RSA 189:74, I–III (2022, 333:1); policy BEDH, section B; By-Laws 2.01, 2.04, 2.15; approved minutes, September 30, 2024.

HIGH Five presentations, several from slides, and none published, against a by-law requiring every administration presentation to be posted

By-law 2.09 (the agenda rule): “Any and all presentations by the administration to the board or any committee shall be contained in the agenda and made available to the public in its original format online so the public may download or access the information prior to or during board meetings.” The curriculum and reading-data slides (0:30:42, 0:37:54) and the business administrator’s budget deck (from 0:59:14; “I know I’m kind of reading this word for word” at 1:02:46) carried the evening’s figures. No packet folder, exhibit or posted agenda for this meeting was found; the sources slide was promised only to the councillors (1:29:56). Which RSA chapters she cited, the reading percentages by band, and the district’s free-and-reduced rate cannot be checked by the public. This is a board rule, not a statute. Graded HIGH on the unmitigated limb: the documents exist, the duty is express, and nothing on the record says they will be posted. Drive search under-reports, so the decks are “not found”.

Sources: By-Laws 1.09 and 2.09 (agenda rule); 2024 Meeting Documents; approved minutes.

MEDIUM Two public bodies met, and the district’s record treats it as one: one roll, one call to order, one set of minutes

RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes both the school board and the City Council public bodies, and RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as the convening of a quorum of a public body. Five of seven board members and seven of nine councillors were present, so each body met and each owed notice and minutes. The school board’s minutes list the council under the heading “Secretary Roll Call of Attendance of Board Members”; the chair called both bodies to order in one sentence and adjourned both by no objection. This page inspected the district’s shares only. Whether the city posted notice or kept its own minutes is a question for the city clerk’s file, which a request under RSA 91-A:4 would answer; no city-side negative is asserted.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2023 codification); RSA 91-A:4; RSA 194:2; approved minutes; Valley News, October 27, 2025 (nine-member council, reporting).

MEDIUM The council was told the district owes full special education services to every resident child in a private or charter school; the federal rule for parentally-placed children is narrower

At 1:05:51–1:06:15 the business administrator described a duty to serve resident children in private and charter schools “on our dime”; the minutes say the cost of a free appropriate public education “falls on the school to provide services”. 34 CFR 300.137(a): “No parentally-placed private school child with a disability has an individual right to receive some or all of the special education and related services that the child would receive if enrolled in a public school.” Such children receive equitable services from a proportionate share of IDEA funds. Full services at no cost to parents apply to children the district itself places (34 CFR 300.146), the out-of-district category discussed earlier. The answer to Greenrose on who receives adequacy aid for a charter pupil (1:09:27) was correct.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.137(a); 34 CFR 300.146; approved minutes, item 5.

MEDIUM Shared arrangements between two separate corporations were reported as under way, including a city position the district would help pay for, with no agreement, figure or vote

RSA 194:2 makes the school district a corporation distinct from the city. The city manager described shared parking, fire, public works, HR, library and housing work and a proposed seasonal sports field specialist the district “has expressed interest in subsidizing” (0:14:12). RSA 53-A:3 lets two or more public agencies, which RSA 53-A:2 defines to include school districts, enter written agreements for joint action that specify duration and terms. No sum, term or instrument was put to either body, and no board member spoke to the subsidy, which would need an appropriation by the district’s own legislative body. Nothing was decided and no agreement is alleged; the flag records a cost commitment described without the mechanism the law provides. A councillor’s offer to work with students (1:53:30) involved no payment and is noted without a finding.

Sources: RSA 194:2; RSA 53-A:2; RSA 53-A:3; RSA 40:13; approved minutes, item 2.
