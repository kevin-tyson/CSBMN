Claremont School Board and City Council, Joint Meeting, September 30, 2024

The first joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council "in several years", by the mayor's account. District administrators briefed the council on poverty and special-education costs (about $4 million for 33 out-of-district placements), the new elementary curricula and early reading data, the state-aid formula and the FY25 budget, and the Tech Center. No motions or votes were taken, and no public comment period was offered. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript and the draft and approved minutes; no agenda or packet was posted to the district's Drive. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

HIGH A school board meeting with no public comment period

The board called itself to order, took its roll (0:00:37) and met for over two hours. The agenda reproduced in the minutes has no citizens' comment item, the chair invited only council members to speak, and the meeting ended on "Is there any objection to adjournment?" (2:09:30) with no invitation to the audience. RSA 189:74 requires a school board to "provide the opportunity for the public to comment on school district matters at a meeting of the school board held under RSA 91-A:2", for "no less than 30 minutes", and excepts only emergency meetings and meetings held solely for a nonpublic session. Neither exception applies. The board's own Policy BEDH, adopted September 6, 2023, requires public comment at "all Board meetings" with the same two exceptions. The question the project's anchor file leaves open, whether 30 minutes is a floor on time or on opportunity, is not reached: no opportunity was offered. Mitigation: no motion or vote was taken, and the session was informational. Graded HIGH because the duty is binding and the record shows it was not met.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (public comment at every school board meeting; 2022, 333:1); Policy BEDH (public comment at board meetings); approved minutes 9.30.24 (agenda as reproduced)

OBSERVATION The approved minutes depart from the recording on several figures

The minutes say Ms. McLaughlin "noted that significant change takes about 5 years"; on tape she says "it takes about three years to really begin seeing significant change" (0:35:19). They say Ms. Henry "shared that the state says it costs $4,182 to educate a student, but the actual estimate is closer to $10,000"; on tape she attributes "closer to ten" to "the lawsuits that are going on" and adds "they settled I think at 7 or 8" (1:11:30, 1:11:57). The $4,182 figure itself is correct for FY25: RSA 198:40-a, II sets $4,100 and RSA 198:40-d adds 2 percent a year from July 1, 2024. The minutes credit Mr. Pratt with saying turnover "was about half of what it was last year"; the tape has 41 departures against hires of 2323 [23] (0:44:58, 0:45:18). The minutes call Michelle Herrington the "new assistant director"; Crawford and Manale call her the director (0:10:29, 1:43:43). Henry's statute numbers as transcribed (RSA 193-C, 186:11, 189:1 by the most likely reading) are not checked here. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to describe the subject matter discussed but sets no accuracy test, so this is an observation; the recording is the better record of what was said.

Sources: approved minutes 9.30.24, items III.4 and III.5; RSA 198:40-a, II ($4,100 base; 2023, 79:150); RSA 198:40-d (2 percent annual adjustment from July 1, 2024; 2023, 79:151)

OBSERVATION The $800,000 catastrophic-aid figure was presented without the proration that applied to it

Henry told the council the district had "about $800,000 coming to us in cat aid" for FY24 costs, payable in FY25 (1:10:24). RSA 186-C:18, III as it read in 2024 set the state's share above the cost thresholds and ended "the appropriation shall be prorated proportionally based on entitlement"; there was no minimum floor until 2025. The New Hampshire Bulletin reported on November 15, 2024, after this meeting, that FY2025 catastrophic aid was funded at 67.5 percent of entitlement ($33.9 million against $50.3 million). On that rate an $800,000 entitlement would pay about $540,000. The board could not have known the rate on September 30, but the figure was presented as money coming in, and it bears on the FY24 surplus and FY26 budget the board was about to set.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (proration based on entitlement; 2023, 79:141, 142); New Hampshire Bulletin, November 15, 2024 (reporting; 67.5 percent for FY2025)
