MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this meeting: the district's shares hold no year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. This meeting elected a principal, accepted two donations and adopted at least eight policies; the recording is now the only public account. Some of what minutes would fix is not recoverable from tape: the roll call is mostly inaudible (0:01:01), the text of the amendment to policy BEDH was never read back when a member asked "So the policy is amended has what in it" (1:56:48), and no vote on BEDH is audible (1:57:35).

RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force throughout 2018, required minutes with "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", open to public inspection not more than five business days after the meeting. This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record shows minutes were kept: the board approved its May 16 minutes at this meeting (0:02:23) and approved these June 6 minutes, with a correction, on June 20 (about four minutes into that recording, Dialogue/9850 SchoolBoard062018.mp4.CSV). What is missing is a surviving public copy. The 2018 text did not yet require minutes to name movers and seconders (that sentence took effect January 1, 2019), so no such defect is charged here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; text in force Jan. 1 to Dec. 31, 2018)

MEDIUM District audits "essentially three years behind": fiscal 2014 just final, fiscal 2015 not yet issued in June 2018

Asked how the audit went, the business administrator said: "we were essentially three years behind. So we finished 14. The field work for 15 is done. So they're they're going to be issuing 15 shortly. We're we're going to go right under 16. And then 17." (1:14:00). The fiscal 2014 audit had findings: "they had some trouble locating some invoices." The board had not yet seen the final 2014 report (1:14:30).

Two rules bear on this. RSA 21-J:19 lets a school board hire a certified public accountant "to conduct such an audit within one year after the close of the municipality's fiscal year"; the section is permissive ("may hire"), so a late district audit is a control weakness rather than a missed state deadline by itself. The federal single-audit rules are mandatory for any district that spends federal awards above the threshold: the reporting package is due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after the fiscal year ends. For fiscal 2015 (begun July 1, 2014) that rule was OMB Circular A-133, section 320(a), with a $500,000 threshold; for fiscal 2016 and 2017 it was 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), with a $750,000 threshold under 2 CFR 200.501(a). On those terms the fiscal 2015, 2016 and 2017 packages would have been due by March 31, 2016, 2017 and 2018. The recording does not state the district's federal expenditures, so this page cannot say the threshold was met; that is why the flag is MEDIUM rather than HIGH. The district's federal award totals for those years would settle it.

Sources: RSA 21-J:19 (2017 codification; source 1985, 204:1; 2008, 174:1); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2018 annual edition (nine-month deadline); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2018 annual edition ($750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.110(b) (Subpart F applies to fiscal years beginning on or after Dec. 26, 2014); OMB Circular A-133 (2007 revision), sections 200(a) and 320(a)

OBSERVATION Lines left overspent instead of transferred; the original-to-final budget trail depends on tracking every move

The business administrator described leaving accounts overdrawn rather than moving money each time: "every time somebody overspend by $10 in supplies, I move $10 there. What do I say? I'll move it when it gets to a thousand" (0:48:48), and said leaving a line over is "okay to do that. You can do it either way." (0:49:22). He added that next year's budget is compared "to the budget that I adjusted this year, not the budget that the board that was approved last year." (0:49:22).

Since August 15, 2017, RSA 32:10 has defined an appropriation's purpose to include “individual line items” in the district's detailed budget, lets the governing body transfer unexpended balances between purposes when spending must exceed a purpose's appropriation, and requires records that let a citizen trace transfers to and from each purpose. GASB Statement 34, paragraph 130, requires the audited budgetary comparison to show the original budget, the final appropriated budget and actual results. The practice described, with transfers made by staff and comparisons drawn against the adjusted figures, makes both trails harder to follow. Who authorizes transfers is not stated on the recording; the board adopted a policy on transfer of appropriations (DBJ) at this meeting (1:51:23). No figure in the record shows a misstatement, so this is an observation.

Sources: RSA 32:10, I-II (source note ends 2017, 127:4, eff. Aug. 15, 2017); GASB Statement 34, paragraph 130 (budgetary comparison: original, final, actual)

OBSERVATION A board member's $105 gift was handed over during citizens' comments and never taken up for acceptance, unlike the $600 gift later the same night

Member Steven Horsky, speaking as a parent, announced "my family has a donation of $105, to support the CMS Band fund" and handed it to the music teacher at the podium (0:07:10). The board later took up "acceptance of donations" as an action item and voted on a $600 gift to Bluff Elementary and an in-kind gift of shoes (1:46:37, 1:47:24, 1:49:34). The $105 gift was not part of that item. For gifts under $5,000, RSA 198:20-b, III(b) requires the board to post notice of the funds in the agenda, include notice in the minutes, and make the acceptance in public session at a regular meeting. Whether the band fund is district money subject to that section, or a separate student or booster account, is not stated, and the agenda that would show what was noticed does not survive. The page notes the difference in handling; it does not find a violation.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)
