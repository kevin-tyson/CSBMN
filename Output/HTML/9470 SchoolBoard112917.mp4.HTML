HIGH Nonpublic session entered "for personnel", with no exemption cited and no vote heard

RSA 91-A:3, I(b), in the text in force for 2017, requires that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II", and that the vote be a recorded roll call. The motion at 2:47:31 is “I'd like to make a motion to go into nonpublic.”, completed by the chair's “For personnel.” and the mover's “Personnel hearing for personnel.” "Personnel" is not a paragraph II ground; the statute's employee-related grounds are II(a) (dismissal, promotion, compensation or discipline of an employee) and II(b) (hiring), and reputation is II(c). The chair then notes “So we have a motion who was seconded.” and the recording is shut off (2:47:51) with no roll call heard. Mitigation: when the session was added to the agenda, the mover cited “RSA 9183 personnel.” (0:01:23), which reads as RSA 91-A:3 but still names no paragraph. If a roll call was taken after the camera stopped, it was not public in any surviving form, and no minutes exist (flag 2) to show the vote, the ground or any decision; RSA 91-A:3, III required nonpublic minutes and decisions to be disclosed within 72 hours unless sealed by a recorded two-thirds vote.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(a)-(c), III (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION Members' sign-off on the question list was to be gathered by email

The vice chair proposed to circulate the spreadsheet, take each member's additions by email, and send it to the business administrator once he had “everybody's blessing by like tomorrow night” (2:46:25). He framed it as information only: “I'll copy everybody in because it's informational. Just don't go back and forth.” (2:46:50). RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings", and ¶II provides that communications outside a meeting, "including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members", shall not be used to circumvent the chapter. Exchanging information is permitted; collecting every member's approval of a list the board will put to the administration is closer to the line, because the approval is a collective decision. The instruction against back-and-forth shows awareness of the rule. Whether any approval was in fact exchanged by email is not on the record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I-II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4)

OBSERVATION Members say positions were created or changed without the board's knowledge

Reviewing the payroll lines, members questioned a truant officer position now full time, a curriculum director's role split into three specialist positions (0:35:10), a nurse relisted as a program assistant (0:37:49) and an added high school secretary (0:46:54). Zullo: “we didn't get told about another secretary to the high school.” (0:49:46). Irish said of restructured positions “we don't have any say in that”, and “we legally we don't have any” [say beyond the total]; Rapp disputed that the board had been kept uninformed (0:50:36). The statute that does give the board a say is RSA 189:39: "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers". It covers teachers, not secretaries, nurses or coordinators, so the positions named here are not, on the record, a breach. The board's other lever is the budget it adopts, which is what this session was using.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (source note ends 1971, 371:2; the same text governs 2017)
