Claremont School Board, January 18, 2022

The public hearing on the fiscal 2023 school budget, held at the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center, followed by a short regular meeting. About eighteen residents spoke over some 80 minutes, most against halving the technical center director's position and eliminating the district's autism specialist position. After a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a), the board sent the budget unchanged at $37,243,825 to the February 9 deliberative session, added the four collective bargaining agreements as warrant articles, and set Article 1's offices. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

MEDIUM For the second meeting running, no minutes were available for approval

Lovett: "We don't have previous minutes. No." (1:41:14). On January 5 the chair had reported no minutes "due to some technical difficulties", so by this meeting neither the December 1 nor the January 5 minutes had come before the board. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". Draft minutes can meet the statute without board approval, which is why this is graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH; but a public body with two meetings' minutes outstanding, six weeks after the earlier one, is showing a records problem. The SAU 6 board, served by the same clerk, reported the same on December 16.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1)

OBSERVATION The nonpublic session came between public testimony on two eliminated positions and the vote that kept them eliminated

The hearing's testimony centered on the half-time technical center director and the eliminated autism specialist position; the chair cut off public discussion of the latter as "specific personnel" (1:37:45). The board then entered nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a), "the dismissal, promotion or compensation of any public employee" (1:41:22), and on returning voted the budget unchanged (1:49:28). RSA 91-A:3, I(c) provides that "All discussions held and decisions made during nonpublic session shall be confined to the matters set out in the motion." A discussion of an individual employee's dismissal or compensation fits II(a); a discussion of whether the budget should fund a position, which is a budget question, does not obviously fit any paragraph II ground. The recording cannot show which took place, and the nonpublic minutes that would show it are missing (flag 2). Members' public remarks afterwards discuss the director reduction openly (1:46:21), which suggests the position question itself was treated as public.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(c) and II(a) (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION The default budget was disclosed only as equal to the proposed budget

RSA 40:13, XI(a): "The default budget shall be disclosed and presented for questions and discussion at the first budget hearing held pursuant to RSA 32:5 or RSA 197:6." The assistant superintendent for finance said the default budget "will be the same as the proposed budget" (0:13:54) because there is no one-time spending or growth to remove, and that eliminated positions are reflected in it (as he also said on January 5). That is a disclosure, and no question followed. The default budget form, which would show the arithmetic from the current year's appropriation, is not in the record, so a reader cannot check that a budget $541,581 below the current year produces an identical default.

Sources: RSA 40:13, XI(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 192:2, the text in force through this date)

OBSERVATION The position cuts reached the public, and at least one board member, only at the hearing

The presentation described reductions as "a couple of positions that have been determined to be removed" (0:06:24) without naming them; residents learned of the autism specialist cut from "the person directly impacted" (0:29:43). A resident said the budget was hard to find before the hearing (0:36:29). And Lambert, who had voted on January 5 to send the budget to this hearing, said "I was not aware that we were cutting such an important position." (1:38:02). On the January 5 recording (show 12115) a former member had asked the board not to "say, oh, well, we're going to have to drop eight positions and then not say what those eight positions are". No statute required position-level detail at the hearing; RSA 40:13, II-a requires the budget to be posted by the last Monday in January, January 31 this year, after this hearing. The observation is that the hearing worked as the first notice of the budget's main changes.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION The board and the center's director disagree on whether the required Perkins needs assessment was ever delivered

Whitney: the board was told in December 2019 that a local community needs assessment was coming, "We did not get a report on that." (1:22:03), and "it's required that we have that data to get our Perkins Grants" (1:32:45). Herzog: "We do have the comprehensive needs assessment" and "we got our Perkins funding because we did that" (1:33:26, 1:33:44). Federal law, 20 U.S.C. §2354(c), requires a recipient of Perkins V local funds to conduct a comprehensive local needs assessment and to update it at least once every two years. The statute does not require presentation to the school board, so both statements can be true. The assessment is not in the public record; its existence and date would settle the dispute and inform the "right size" question residents raised.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §2354(c) (Perkins V, Pub. L. 115-224 §133, July 31, 2018)

POSITIVE The hearing was held on time, presented the whole budget, default budget and contract costs, and heard everyone before the board finalized

RSA 40:13, II-a(c) requires budget hearings "on or before the third Tuesday in January"; January 18, 2022 was the third Tuesday. The administration presented the budget by location, revenue, tax rate, default budget and each warrant article's cost (0:02:06 to 0:17:02); about eighteen residents spoke over some 80 minutes, several twice; and members responded on the record. Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a(c) (2019 codification); RSA 32:5, I (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 246:1)
