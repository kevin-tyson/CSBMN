SAU 6 Board, March 29, 2018

Organizational meeting of the joint Claremont and Unity board: Sara Lowe was re-elected chair, Michael Petrin elected vice chair and Michelle Pierce elected treasurer after the business administrator said the unit had gone without one "for the past few years". The superintendent said he had not been evaluated in two years, a five-member subcommittee was named to draft an evaluation instrument, and a requested nonpublic session on his evaluation was held in public at his request. MEDIUM Advice that a subcommittee needs notice only if it holds a quorum of a board

Asked what rules govern the new evaluation subcommittee's meetings and notes, the superintendent answered "Board has a quorum. It has to be warned." (1:32:14) and "If if there are four of the seven board members in Claremont, that's a quorum." (1:32:26). The members then sized the group to avoid a Claremont quorum (1:33:14). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), unchanged since 2008, defines a public body to include a school board or SAU board "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto". A subcommittee therefore carries its own notice and minutes duties under RSA 91-A:2 whether or not its members form a quorum of either parent board; the quorum question decides only whether the parent board itself is meeting. Three weeks later the New Hampshire School Boards Association's attorney told the Claremont board that a policy committee "is governed by the right to know law in exactly the same way as your school board is" (show 9740 transcript; see the April 18, 2018 meeting page). The record does not show how the subcommittee then met, and the chair's question shows she was trying to get the rules right. The severity is MEDIUM because the advice, as given, would lead a subcommittee to meet without notice.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1, so the text in force in 2018); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification)

POSITIVE A nonpublic request named its exemption, and the employee's request for an open meeting was honored

The agenda item for a nonpublic session was identified by its statutory ground: Sprague gave "91-832 see" [RSA 91-A:3, II(c)] and the chair read its text aloud (1:47:16, 1:47:24). The superintendent then asked that the discussion be public (1:47:35), and the board did not go into nonpublic session; Pierce's motion had drawn no second (1:49:04). Earlier the chair had explained the employee's right to an open meeting correctly (1:19:04). RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires a nonpublic motion to "state on its face the specific exemption", and both II(a) (an employee's compensation, discipline or the investigation of charges, "unless the employee affected (1) has a right to a meeting and (2) requests that the meeting be open") and II(c) (reputation, "unless such person requests an open meeting") give way to the affected person's request. The board did what the statute requires. Members' unease about what they could say on camera (1:53:54) is the cost of that choice, and the superintendent pointed them to counsel (1:56:37).

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(a) and II(c) (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; 280:1)
