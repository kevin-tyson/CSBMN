Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on August 19, 2026, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies. Counts: 1 HIGH, 2 MEDIUM, 4 OBSERVATION, 2 POSITIVE.

HIGH Two August 6 payments went out with no board or treasurer signature, the day after the board heard the treasurer's July report

The treasurer's August report (CSB Treas - August2026.pdf) lists a $2,246.51 payroll EFT and a $444.93 IRS payment on August 6 with "Not Signed" in both the signature and date-signed columns, under the legend "Payment issued without board or treasurer signature" (report text). On tape: "Two payments went in that were not signed. I found the vouchers. They were, but nobody signed them, so they were just put in." (0:15:45); "Neither of those were signed okay by anyone." (0:16:22). The same report shows further EFTs (an Amazon payment, a postage payment and a $20,648.17 health-insurance payment) with a ? in the signature column and lists checks still awaiting a manifest (report text). Diamond: "There shouldn't be such entries. The treasurer should always sign for every payment that goes out." (0:18:31).

RSA 197:23-a lets the treasurer pay out district money "only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of the 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole to authorize payments" (statute text). A payment no board member and no treasurer signed is outside that rule on its face. The amounts are small and payroll and tax payments are time-critical, and the superintendent and treasurer both say procedures are improving (0:19:28, 0:19:34); but this followed the August 5 report of the same class of problem (August 5 page, flag 1). Graded HIGH as a recurring departure from a binding rule.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (treasurer pays out "only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of the 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole"); treasurer's August 2026 report

MEDIUM The board decided in nonpublic session who sits on the teachers' negotiating team, a matter outside the grounds its motion cited, and the minutes surfaced two weeks later

The board entered nonpublic session "under RSA 91 a three part two A, C and E" (3:16:15); the agenda listed "Personnel" and "Negotiations Planning - SREA, Custodians, Secretaries, CAA" under the same three grounds (agenda text). Just before, Whitney had raised the March assignment of officers and roles and the chair said it would go to nonpublic (3:15:10, 3:15:24). The nonpublic minutes (CSB Non-public minutes - Aug 19 2026.pdf) record: "the Board voted unanimously by roll call to add Don Lavalette and Candace Crawford to the SREA negotiation team" (minutes text). That question had been argued in public at the August 12 Finance Subcommittee.

RSA 91-A:3, I(c) requires that "decisions made during nonpublic session shall be confined to the matters set out in the motion" (statute text). Grounds II(a) (dismissal, promotion or compensation of an employee), II(c) (reputation) and II(e) (pending claims or litigation) do not describe choosing which board members negotiate. Collective-bargaining strategy is excluded from the definition of a meeting by RSA 91-A:2, I(a), but that exclusion was not invoked and does not obviously reach a vote assigning members. RSA 91-A:3, III requires nonpublic minutes and decisions to be "publicly disclosed within 72 hours" unless sealed; no sealing vote appears in either set of minutes. The earliest public copy found was filed in the September 2 packet folder (Drive creation time September 2, 9:46 a.m. Eastern), fourteen days after the meeting; MAP.md records that the Meeting Minutes share does not hold it. Drive timing shows when a copy reached the share, not whether the minutes were available on request. Graded MEDIUM as a probable process gap; the decision itself was recorded and disclosed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b)-(c) and III (nonpublic motion and scope; minutes and decisions disclosed within 72 hours unless sealed); RSA 91-A:2, I(a) and II (collective-bargaining strategy excluded from meeting; minutes contents); nonpublic minutes; agenda

OBSERVATION Special education aid was described at the table in terms the statute does not use

Dr. Broadrick put the reimbursement threshold at "three times the previous year's average per pupil cost. Statewide, roughly $70,000" (1:45:00) and the state share at "at least 90 and hopefully 100% of the rest" (1:45:33). Madden said a 2026 bill "which is now statute" means "It can't be prorated less than 90%." (1:41:32, 1:41:38). RSA 186-C:18, III as it read on August 19, 2026 sets the threshold at "3 and 1/2 times" the state average expenditure per pupil, has the State pay "80 percent of the cost above" that up to 10 times and all cost above 10 times, and requires the department to distribute "not less than 80 percent of the district's entitlement" (statute text). HB 1563 (2026) changes the formula, but LegiScan reports its effective date as July 1, 2028; its chaptered text was not read for this page. The district's figures (13 students and about $2.6 million in FY26) are the superintendent's and the packet's claims summary. Recorded as an observation: the speakers' figures do not match the statute in force, and the gap matters for budgeting.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (3 1/2 times threshold; 80 percent above it; not less than 80 percent of entitlement); HB 1563 (2026), reported chaptered as ch. 232, approved July 2, 2026, eff. July 1, 2028 (LegiScan status page; chaptered text not read)

POSITIVE Special education cost data were put before the public in aggregate, with care for student privacy

The superintendent presented five years of special education aid and Medicaid claims, saying "this is the kind of data I think school boards should receive" (1:36:00), and warned that "It's very important when we have these conversations, that we keep this general enough that there's no way to identify the students" (1:39:28). The packet sheet gives only yearly student counts (11, 16, 14, 17 and 13) and totals. FERPA treats as personally identifiable any information that would let a person in the school community identify a student with reasonable certainty; aggregate reporting of this kind avoids that while informing the budget discussion.

Sources: 34 CFR 99.3 (FERPA: personally identifiable information includes information that would allow identification with reasonable certainty); SpEd Aid & Medicaid Claims, FY22-26
