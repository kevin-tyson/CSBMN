Claremont School Board, September 23, 2020

Single-item special meeting on whether to bring hybrid students back full time sooner than November 2, chaired by the vice chair because the chair could attend only by video. District counsel briefed the board on liability, the administration presented the costs of an October 5 return, a motion for October 5 went to a roll call whose result was never announced, and a substitute motion passed six to one: cohorts A and B back full time on October 19 once safety equipment is installed, the high school hybrid to the end of the first quarter, no high school busing. MEDIUM A roll-call vote on the October 5 return whose result was never announced

The acting chair put the October 5 motion to a roll call at 2:04:19. The recording catches some answers (an unidentified No no., Benware Yes., the clerk's “Rob Lovett no.”, Whitney Yes.) and a dispute over whether someone had voted (2:05:29), then moves straight to Zullo's substitute (2:05:50). No one states the tally or whether the motion failed. The substitute's passage implies it did, but the record does not say so.

RSA 91-A:2, II requires the minutes to record final decisions, and III(e) requires a roll call when members participate remotely so that each vote is known. The roll call was taken; what is missing on the public record is the decision. With the minutes not public, a reader cannot learn how each member voted on the question the meeting was called to decide.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II and III(e) (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION Ending high school busing: the statute supports the superintendent, with limits worth stating

Tempesta said “it's not required that we we have to transport to the high school” (0:18:53), and the adopted motion provides that “I want to add that no bussing will be provided.” for the high school (2:11:41). RSA 189:6, in the text in force from July 1, 2020, requires a district to transport pupils in kindergarten through grade 8 who live more than two miles from their assigned school, and makes transport for grades 9 through 12 discretionary unless the commissioner directs it. The superintendent's statement is consistent with that.

Two limits the meeting did not discuss: federal law still requires transportation to the school of origin for students experiencing homelessness (42 U.S.C. 11432(g)(1)(J)(iii)), and transportation written into a student's special education plan is a separate obligation. The October 7 citizens' comment from a Stevens counselor shows the decision's effect on families who cannot drive their children.

Sources: RSA 189:6 (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 181:1, eff. July 1, 2020); 42 U.S.C. 11432(g)(1)(J)(iii) (McKinney-Vento transportation)

OBSERVATION Public attendance at board meetings closed and reopened without a vote

Petrin: “We didn't take a vote or anything to close the meeting” (2:17:33). The board agreed by consensus to readmit the public in person from October 7, with registration and assigned seating, and Benware confirmed no vote was needed ({R(718)}).

No statute required a vote either way. What the law required during the emergency is narrower: Emergency Order #12 let a public body meet without a physical quorum only if it gave the public telephone access, published how to use it, provided a way to report problems and adjourned if the public could not get in; ordinarily RSA 91-A:2, III(c) requires remote participants to be audible to the public at the noticed meeting location. The meeting notices that would show which rules the board was meeting under are not in any reachable record. This meeting itself took telephone comment (flag 6).

Sources: Emergency Order #12 (March 23, 2020); RSA 91-A:2, III(c) (2019 codification)
