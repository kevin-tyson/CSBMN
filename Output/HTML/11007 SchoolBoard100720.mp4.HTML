Claremont School Board, October 7, 2020

Regular meeting with the public back in the room after the September 23 decision, two members on Google Meet: a Stevens counselor's plea to keep high school buses, the superintendent's report on the October 19 full return, an FY20 surplus of a little over $2 million, a roll-call vote to retain 5 percent of the net assessment (about $845,000) under the newly amended RSA 198:4-b, and up to $247,000 from the transportation expendable trust for two buses and one van after members objected to using it for special education vans. RSA 91-A:2, III(c) required that members participating remotely be able to hear each other and speak simultaneously and be audible to the public at the meeting location; III(e) required roll-call votes. The board did use roll calls on every substantive question (flag 6); the gap is audibility. The motion passed with at least five clear yes votes, so the outcome does not turn on Lovett's answer.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(c) and (e) (2019 codification)

MEDIUM Up to $247,000 committed from an expendable trust with no public hearing shown on the record

Seaman described the fund as the $500,000 transportation reserve created at “the vote that we had in March of this year” and an “expendable trust that we have set up for transportation” (1:43:48); the board named the use and the ceiling by roll call (2:02:30). RSA 198:20-c, II provides that, to expend a school district expendable trust fund, the school board shall hold a public hearing before the expenditure, with notice of time, place and subject published in a newspaper at least seven days before. Nothing on the recording refers to a hearing on this withdrawal; the public hearing the chair recalls (1:44:54) was the one held when the trust was proposed.

Mitigation: the vote authorized a ceiling so the district could seek bids (Seaman: “we can't go out for a bid or even get some solid pricing without at least having an agreement from the board”), and a hearing could still have preceded the purchases. The recording also does not say which statute the March article used; RSA 198:20-c, V subjects these funds to the same rules as reserve funds under RSA 35:1 or 35:1-c. A reviewer should look for a hearing notice between October 2020 and the bus purchases, and for the March 2020 warrant article.

Sources: RSA 198:20-c, I, II and V (2019 codification; source note ends 2012, 219:2, 3)

OBSERVATION The 5 percent retention: right cap, right hearing rule, but described as a board-created contingency fund

The motion was to “set aside 5% of current assessment per RSA 198.5” [RSA 198:4-b] to establish a contingency fund (1:37:24), and Seaman said the amount “needs to be re approved each year”. Ten days earlier, 2020 N.H. Laws 38:25 (effective September 27, 2020) had raised the cap in RSA 198:4-b, II from 2.5 to 5 percent of the net assessment and added a public hearing before retained funds are spent, which Seaman described accurately (1:28:52).

Two points a reader should keep separate. First, paragraph II lets the district's legislative body (the voters) authorize retention for an indefinite period until specifically rescinded; it does not itself provide for a board vote or an annual re-approval, so the board's vote reads as implementing a voter authorization whose text is not in any reachable record. Second, a "contingency fund" is the paragraph I device, created annually by a separate warrant article for unanticipated expenses; retained year-end funds under paragraph II carry no purpose limitation. The labels were mixed on the record, but the mechanism described (retain up to 5 percent, hold a hearing before spending) is paragraph II.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, I and II (2020 codification; 2020, 38:25, eff. Sept. 27, 2020)

POSITIVE Members tested the withdrawal against the trust's purpose and narrowed it before voting
