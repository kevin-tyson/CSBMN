Claremont School District, Deliberative Session — February 9, 2022

First (deliberative) session of the 2022 Claremont School District annual meeting under the SB 2 official-ballot system, summarized from the recording and the district's two sets of minutes. The body here is the voters, not the board. The operating budget came to the floor at $37,243,825 with an identical default budget. Residents objected to cutting the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center director to half time, and on two secret ballots the voters added $61,973 to restore the director (53–6) and $87,290 for a full-time technical-center guidance counselor (48–9 per the minutes). Four collective-bargaining articles went to the ballot unamended. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the session against the state law in force on February 9, 2022. A deliberative session is a meeting of the voters, so school-board rules (public-comment periods, nonpublic sessions, roll calls) do not apply to it; RSA chapter 40 as extended to school districts does. They are not findings of violation and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Five flags follow, ordered by severity.

MEDIUM The session was presided over by an "acting" moderator, and neither the recording nor either set of minutes shows how she came to hold the office

The board chair introduced "our acting tonight, moderator Charlene Lovett" (0:01:56); both sets of minutes style her "Charlene Lovett (moderator)". The district moderator presides at district meetings (RSA 197:19). If the moderator is absent, RSA 197:20 provides that the district clerk "shall act as moderator of any meeting until a moderator pro tempore shall be chosen", and if the office is vacant RSA 671:33, III provides that "until a replacement is chosen, the school district clerk shall serve as moderator or shall appoint a moderator pro tempore." The school board has no power to fill a moderator vacancy (RSA 197:26). The clerk was present and introduced; nothing on the recording or in the minutes records the clerk presiding, the meeting choosing a moderator pro tempore, or the clerk appointing one, and the elected moderator is not named anywhere in the record.

Severity. MEDIUM rather than the HIGH given on the February 3, 2024 page, and the divergence is deliberate: here the dialogue file begins at 0:01:23, after the moderator is already presiding, so an appointment or choice made in the opening minute cannot be excluded; and the only source for "acting" is the chair's passing word, whereas in 2024 the district's own agenda, rules and minutes all used a title the law does not recognize. If no statutory route was followed, the defect is the same one.

Sources: RSA 197:19 (1979, 321:6); RSA 197:20 (1963, 120:5, unamended); RSA 671:33, III (source note ends 2021, 91:318, eff. July 1, 2021, in force on this date); RSA 197:26.

OBSERVATION Two sets of minutes, filed four months apart, both prepared by the SAU's administrative assistant; they differ from the recording on a date and a tally, and name speakers the recording cannot

RSA 197:20 makes the district clerk responsible: "The clerk shall keep a true record of all the doings of each meeting". Both documents are "submitted by Melissa Small", the SAU administrative assistant; nothing in either shows the clerk's review. They were filed in two different packet folders (July 20 and November 3, 2022) with small textual differences. Against the recording: the minutes approve "previous minutes of 2/4/2021" where the moderator says "February 1st" (0:05:48); and the second ballot is "48 yes, 9 no" in the minutes against the moderator's "48 to none" (2:15:28), most likely an ASR rendering of "nine". The minutes' great value is that they name floor speakers and wards the recording loses, which is how several voters on this page can be named at all. Recorded as an observation: the statute does not forbid the clerk from using a drafter, and the date and tally differences are small.

Sources: RSA 197:20; minutes (July packet); minutes (November packet).

OBSERVATION A default budget equal to the proposed budget left voters no cheaper alternative, and the money the voters added cannot follow into the default

Article 2 set both figures at $37,243,825. Under RSA 40:13, IX(b) the default is last year's operating budget adjusted for debt service, contracts and legal obligations, "reduced by one-time expenditures" "and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget." The business administrator's account at 0:48:32, that the proposed budget's reductions "have to carry into the default budget", is consistent with that eliminated-positions clause: a budget that only cuts and meets contracts will produce a default at or near its own level. Residents did not find this intuitive (Crawford at 0:26:28: "Why should voters go to the effort to go and vote if it doesn't mean anything one way or the other?"), and he conceded "There's confusion across the state". The two amendments raised the operating budget by $149,263; RSA 40:13, XI(b) provides of the default that "This amount shall not be amended by the legislative body," so if voters defeated the amended budget in March the added positions would fall away. Consistent with the statute; recorded because the equality of the two figures was the session's most-asked question.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI(b) (last amended 2019, 192:2, eff. July 10, 2019; in force on this date).

OBSERVATION Voters added money for two named positions, but counsel told them the amendment does not bind how it is spent and that he would have to research the point

Both amendments were argued as restoring specific posts, but the article is a bottom-line appropriation. The answer from the floor at 1:02:17 was "ultimately, that's a decision for the school board"; district counsel at 2:00:25 described "a line of cases" on money moved into special warrant articles and said "I'd have to do the research". Benware put the consequence plainly at 1:05:59: "if they choose not to spend it, they don't have to spend it." No provision was cited on the night and none is verified here, so this is the speakers' characterization. What happened next is on the record: on April 6, 2022 a resident asked whether the director's post would be fully funded, and a board member asked whether counselor positions in the ESSER plan were the money voters had put back at this session.

Sources: the recording at the times linked above. No verified rule applies.

POSITIVE Both contested amendments were decided by secret ballot on a written five-voter request, with the procedure explained, and reconsideration was restricted as the statute allows

RSA 40:4-a (incorporated at a deliberative session by RSA 40:13, IV) lets five voters present request "in writing prior to a vote" that a vote be taken by secret written ballot, and then "the moderator shall conduct the vote by secret 'yes-no' ballot". On the recording the request is made and signed before each vote (1:27:08, 2:02:39), the moderator explains the yes/no question and the colored cards, counters are named, and each result is declared. RSA 40:10 allows the meeting "at any time during a meeting, and without notice in the warrant" to restrict reconsideration, and applies to "school district meetings under RSA 197"; the meeting did so for Article 2 at 2:16:18. Both amendments changed only a dollar amount, which RSA 40:13, IV(c) expressly permits.

Sources: RSA 40:4-a (2006, 117:1); RSA 40:10, I and V (1996, 64:1); RSA 40:13, IV.
