Claremont School District, Deliberative Session, February 9, 2022

First (deliberative) session of the 2022 annual school district meeting under RSA 40:13, the official-ballot (SB 2) procedure. The body is the district's voters; the school board presents the warrant. Voters amended the $37,243,825 operating budget twice by secret ballot, adding $61,973 for a full-time tech-center director (53 to 6) and $87,290 for a full-time tech-center guidance counselor (48 to 9 per the minutes), and sent all six articles to the March 8 ballot. Built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript, the posted warrant and two copies of the district's minutes. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. A deliberative session is the first session of the annual school district meeting: the voters are the body, so school-board rules (public comment periods, nonpublic sessions, board quorum) do not apply. The governing law is RSA 40:13 and the provisions it incorporates, the statutes on district officers, and RSA 91-A as it reaches "any legislative body" of a school district. Each flag names the rule as it read on February 9, 2022, or is labeled an observation.

MEDIUM An acting moderator presided, and no record shows how she was chosen

The board chair introduces "our acting tonight, moderator Charlene Lovett" (0:01:56), and she presides throughout. The minutes call her "(moderator)" without saying how she came to preside. The district elects its moderator on the official ballot (Article 1 of this warrant). When the moderator is absent, RSA 197:20 has the district clerk "act as moderator of any meeting until a moderator pro tempore shall be chosen"; where the office is vacant, RSA 671:33, III has the clerk serve as moderator or appoint a moderator pro tempore. RSA 197:26 bars the school board from filling a moderator vacancy. The clerk, Mary Woodman, was present (0:01:56), but neither route appears in the minutes, and the recording begins after the call to order, so a choice made in the first minute would not be on tape. This page applies MEDIUM rather than the HIGH grade the project gave the same pattern at the 2024 session, because there the whole opening was on record and here it is not: the documents leave a gap rather than proving an irregularity. The district's Rules of Procedure sheet for this session, which might explain the arrangement, was not located.

Sources: RSA 197:20 (clerk acts as moderator until a moderator pro tempore is chosen; 1963, 120:5); RSA 671:33, III (moderator vacancy; source note ends 2021, 91:318, eff. July 1, 2021); RSA 197:26 (board fills vacancies "except that of moderator"); RSA 197:19 (district moderator has a town moderator's powers)

MEDIUM The minutes differ from the recording on three votes

(1) The recording shows two approvals of 2021 minutes, the February 1 session (seconded by a Ward 2 voter, 0:05:48) and the February 4 session (seconded by "Vice chairwoman", 0:06:22); both copies of the minutes record one motion, on the "2/4/2021" minutes, "seconded by Frank Sprague". (2) The minutes record a "Motion by Rob Lovett seconded by Steven Horsky to approve Article 2, as amended"; on the recording Lovett Jr. moves to "restrict reconsideration" of Article 2 (2:16:18), a different motion with a different effect under RSA 40:10, and it carried with audible opposition (2:16:39). The restriction itself is nowhere in the minutes. (3) The second ballot: the transcript renders the announcement "48 to none" (2:15:28) and the minutes give 48 yes, 9 no; the minutes are probably right (the announcement opens with a garbled reference to nine), but the record should not leave a reader to reconcile them. RSA 197:20 requires the clerk to "keep a true record of all the doings of each meeting", and RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to show the names of persons making and seconding motions. Both copies are "submitted by Melissa Small", the SAU administrative assistant, not the clerk.

Sources: RSA 197:20 (clerk keeps a true record); RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; mover and seconder in minutes from 2018, 244:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2019); RSA 40:10 (restriction of reconsideration; 1996, 64:1); minutes, July 2022 copy; minutes, November 2022 copy

OBSERVATION What the voters' amendments bind: the board keeps transfer authority, and no hearing is required to use it

Voters were told, accurately, that money added to a line "Gets added to the budget" and that "ultimately, that's a decision for the school board" (1:02:17). Several voters said an amendment is pointless unless the money is spent as intended (1:05:38, 1:59:04). RSA 32:10 lets the governing body transfer an unexpended balance from one appropriation to another during the year, provided the total is not exceeded and records let any citizen trace the transfers; only special warrant articles and capital reserve appropriations are locked to their purpose. So the two amendments, which change the operating budget's line items, do not oblige the board to fill a full-time director or counselor position. Counsel also said that for a transfer the board would "hold a public hearing" (2:00:19); RSA 32:10 contains no hearing requirement, so any hearing would come from district practice or policy, not the statute. Whether the added money was in fact used for the two positions is a question for the FY2023 records.

Sources: RSA 32:10, I(a)-(d) and II (source note ends 2017, 127:4, eff. Aug. 15, 2017; read 2026-10-03)

OBSERVATION The default budget equals the proposed budget to the dollar; the explanation given is broader than the statute, and the default budget form was not located

The warrant sets both the operating budget and the default budget at $37,243,825. The business administrator explained that "We include any reductions that are included in this year's budget" (0:22:17) and that "if you are making any reductions in the proposed budget, those reductions have to carry into the default budget" (0:48:32). RSA 40:13, IX(b) defines the default budget as the prior year's operating-budget appropriations, adjusted for debt service, contracts and obligations previously incurred or mandated by law, and reduced by one-time expenditures "and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget". Eliminated positions (such as the vacant nurse, guidance and accounting positions) therefore come out of the default; the statute does not say that every other reduction does. RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires a default budget form identifying each change by account code with reasons, which would show whether the two figures were built line by line. That form was not located. The warrant, which must be posted with the budgets "on or before the last Monday in January" (RSA 40:13, II-a), is signed January 31, 2022, the last Monday; its posting date is not in the record.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a, IX(b) and XI(a) (last amended 2019, 192:2, eff. July 10, 2019); 2022 CL Warrant.pdf

OBSERVATION Voice votes to move articles to the ballot have no legal effect, and the minutes present them as decisions

The moderator calls "All those in favor of the article as written" on Articles 1 and 3 to 6 (0:07:36, 2:18:25, 2:21:57, 2:24:27, 2:27:19), and the minutes record each as "Motion passed via voice vote". Under RSA 40:13, VI "All warrant articles shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote, including warrant articles as amended by the first session", so these votes neither advance nor block anything. The practice is common and harmless, but a reader of the minutes could take an article that passed on February 9 to have been adopted; the binding votes were on the March 8 ballot.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and VI (first session explains, debates and may amend; all articles go to the ballot)

POSITIVE Both amendments were decided by secret ballot on written requests, counted at the meeting and announced with tallies

Before each vote the meeting was told "You're going to need five voters to sign." (1:27:08), signatures were collected, the moderator explained the procedure (1:29:23), ballots were cast at the front, counted, and the result announced with the numbers (1:47:28, 2:15:28); the minutes name the people who assisted with the count. RSA 40:4-a requires the moderator to conduct a vote by secret yes-no ballot when five voters present request it in writing before the vote, and RSA 40:13, IV makes that section govern the first session. The meeting followed the statute and gave voters exact tallies rather than a declared voice vote.

Sources: RSA 40:4-a (secret ballot on written request of 5 voters; 2006, 117:1); RSA 40:13, IV
