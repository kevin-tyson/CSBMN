Claremont School Board, August 21, 2019

Regular meeting a week before school opened: the board received the Claremont School District's fiscal 2016 audit report, with fiscal 2017 and 2018 still under audit and an abatement of IRS penalties expected; heard that Richard Seaman would start as business administrator on October 2; elected 33 new teachers; approved the 2019-20 calendar; moved the student discipline policy JICD to second reading; appointed Alex Herzog to the CCTV board; and tentatively approved the student handbooks it had received that day. MEDIUM The fiscal 2016 audit reached the board three years and two months after the year ended, with 2017 and 2018 still open

The agenda item was the "2016 fiscal audit report for Claremont School District" (0:29:52). Fiscal 2016 ended June 30, 2016. The superintendent said the auditors were "still looking 17 and 18" (0:29:56), and that the district expected "an abatement of the IRS penalties" (0:30:44). Later in the meeting he tied the new student ID system to "the audit report and the findings" on "controls over the, you know, the lunch program" (1:01:16). The record does not say what the IRS penalties were for. Under the Uniform Guidance, a district that spends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a fiscal year must submit its single-audit reporting package "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period". For fiscal 2016 that is March 31, 2017. This page has not verified Claremont's fiscal 2016 federal expenditures, so it cannot say the threshold was crossed, and it grades the item MEDIUM as a control weakness rather than HIGH as a missed deadline. The schedule of expenditures of federal awards in the audit packet would settle it; the report reached the board about 29 months after that date. New Hampshire's audit statute, RSA 21-J:19, is permissive as to timing and is not the basis of this flag. In mitigation, the superintendent disclosed the backlog and the penalties publicly and twice (0:30:23, 0:33:50), the auditors had promised to report any sign that a forensic audit was needed (0:35:09), and a full-time business administrator was hired (0:31:17).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2019 annual edition); 2 CFR 200.501(a) ($750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.110(b) (Subpart F applies to fiscal years beginning on or after December 26, 2014); 2 CFR 200.303(a) (internal control over federal awards)

MEDIUM Members treated committee meetings as needing notice only when a board quorum attends

Lovett described the capital improvement committee, which "myself, Carolyn and Heather are heading", as holding meetings, with another "to be determined" (1:16:03), and Towle described its "last meeting" with the superintendent (1:18:39). Asked to put a capital improvement member on the budget committee, Benware objected: "if you have one extra person from our board sitting there, we have to warn everything that we do, and we can't just meet on a on a Wednesday morning." (1:20:36). The chair proposed that another member recuse from such meetings (1:21:01). Both answers rest on the idea that a committee's meetings need notice only when four of the seven board members attend. RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), unchanged since 2008, defines a public body to include a school district's board "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto". A board committee is therefore a public body in its own right, with the notice and minutes duties of RSA 91-A:2, II, whatever the number of members present. The quorum count decides only whether the full board is also meeting. No notice or minutes of any capital improvement or budget committee meeting from 2019 survive. The severity is MEDIUM: the remarks describe a practice that would leave committee meetings unnoticed, but the record does not show how any particular committee meeting was noticed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION The district's existing discipline policy fell short of the suspension statute, by the administration's own account

Presenting the draft JICD, the assistant superintendent said the existing policy "just stated that we could only do ten days" with "no superintendent" and "no school board" (0:09:26), and the superintendent said "the policy adopted by the board was actually less than the state." (0:09:53). RSA 193:13, I, in the 2019 codification, lets the superintendent or a written designee suspend for up to 10 school days, and lets the school board or its written designee, "following a hearing", continue a suspension beyond 10 days; the designee for the longer suspension may be the superintendent but not the person who imposed the first 10 days. The draft described on tape, with a principal's short-term suspension, referral to the superintendent's office and then to the board, and new due-process language (0:09:00, 0:10:06), tracks that structure, provided principals act as written designees. The policy was moved to second reading (0:12:10) and adopted on September 4, 2019. This is an observation: the old and new policy texts are not in the record, so the page cannot compare them line by line.

Sources: RSA 193:13, I(a)-(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2017, 12:1, eff. June 16, 2017)

OBSERVATION Student badges will carry a barcode of the state student identifier that links to the student information system

The technology director said the new badge "has their their Sassa number" [SASID], "which is only tied to them, which will correspond to our power school", that scanning it brings up the student's picture in the meals program, and that it may later be used for sign-in at the high school, printing and door locks (1:02:30, 1:02:52, 1:03:21). Benware worried about lost cards "floating around in the community" (1:03:42). Under the federal student-privacy rule, directory information "does not include" a student identification number, except that a student ID number may be treated as directory information "only if the identifier cannot be used to gain access to education records except when used in conjunction with one or more factors that authenticate the user's identity, such as a personal identification number (PIN), password or other factor known or possessed only by the authorized user". A badge worn in public displays whatever it carries. The record does not show whether the district designated badge numbers as directory information, what the barcode opens without a second factor, or what the meal-line scanners show to whom. The plan responds to an audit finding on lunch-program controls (1:01:16) and to students using other children's PINs (1:02:02), which are real problems. This is an observation, offered so the design can be checked against the rule.

Sources: 34 CFR 99.3, "Directory information" (2019 annual edition, revised July 1, 2019)
