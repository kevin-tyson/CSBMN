Claremont School Board (part 1 of 2), December 6, 2017

First part (about the first four hours) of the regular meeting at which the administration presented, line by line, the reductions needed to meet the board's directive of a fiscal year 2019 budget $1.2 million below fiscal year 2018. It covers the opening business, the superintendent's report, citizens' comments, the city manager's discussion and most of the budget review. The page is built from this recording's own dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION Senate Bill 193 was described as likely to pass; it was never enacted

The superintendent presented SB 193 from an advocacy group's analysis, including the figure of "about $3,600 per student" and the expectation that "it is expected to pass next year" (0:19:45 to 0:23:03); Sprague urged the audience to press their representatives (0:27:17), and Ron Perry raised it in citizens' comments (1:10:23). At the time the bill was in the House, where the Education Committee had issued majority and minority reports on November 15, 2017. LegiScan records its status as died in chamber; it was not enacted. The dollar and eligibility figures belong to amended versions of a bill that did not become law, and the page reports them as the superintendent's characterization. Budget planning around pending legislation is reasonable; readers should not take the figures as law.

Sources: LegiScan, NH SB193 (2017), status and history (secondary source)

POSITIVE Reduction list explained item by item in public

The administration gave the board and the audience the same three documents: the reduction list with impacts and tax effects, a staffing comparison with four other districts, and answers to the board's November 29 questions ("we're also going to pass them out to the residents here", 2:03:54). The board then chose to go through the list line by line in public (2:07:17), and the administrator responsible for each item explained what the cut would do. RSA 32:5, I requires a public hearing on the budget; it does not require this kind of itemized public explanation before the hearing. The practice exceeded the legal minimum and gave residents the administration's own account of each proposed cut.

Sources: RSA 32:5, I (2017 codification)
