MEDIUM Nonpublic motion stated its exemption only by reference, and a seal was mentioned with no audible statutory finding

The superintendent asked to go into nonpublic “under RSA 91 a three Roman numeral two see” (0:54:28). An unidentified member then moved “We go into nonpublic under” and the motion was completed and seconded as “what he said second” (0:54:47). A roll call followed (0:54:51). RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires that the motion "shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II" (statute text). A motion that adopts another person's words by reference does not state the exemption on its face, though the exemption was spoken aloud moments earlier.

After the session the chair says “This is a to seal” and an answer follows (0:59:35); the rest is garbled. RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" together with a determination that disclosure would likely affect a person's reputation, render the proposed action ineffective, or concern terrorism (statute text, paraphrased after the quoted words). No determination is audible. With no minutes surviving, there is no record of whether the minutes were sealed, on what finding, or what was discussed. MEDIUM: a documentation gap on the record, mitigated by the spoken exemption and the roll call.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(c) and III (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1 and 280:1, eff. June 21, 2016)

OBSERVATION Transgender policy adopted after the federal guidance behind its legal framing was enjoined

The superintendent told the board the policy rests on “some very clear law” and that without it the district risks violating “a person's constitutional rights”; on August 17 he pointed to the policy's legal references (August 17, 2016 page). The references are not read into the record and no copy of the policy survives. The federal Departments of Education and Justice guidance of May 13, 2016 on transgender students was the most recent federal statement on the subject; on August 21, 2016 the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas preliminarily enjoined federal enforcement of it nationwide (Education Week reports the ruling as August 22), and an October 2016 clarification limited the injunction to restrooms and locker rooms (reporting). Nothing on the recording shows the board was told of the injunction.

The injunction did not bar a district from adopting its own policy, so this is not a finding against the policy. It is an observation that the legal basis described to the board was not current on the date of the vote, and that the vote itself has no audible tally and no surviving minutes. The policy text and its legal references would settle what the board relied on.

Sources: Education Week, Aug. 30, 2016, "Judge Blocks Guidance on Transgender Rights" (reporting); Connecticut School Law blog, Oct. 2016, on the scope clarification (reporting)
