Claremont School Board — January 24, 2017

Budget session summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. Held the same evening, after the joint meeting with the City Council (see the joint meeting page). The administration presented a revised FY18 operating budget of $31,193,006 with no tax-rate impact; the board restored $90,000, dropped the $89,200 technology warrant article, voted support for the other warrant articles, and heard three members of the public on cuts to the elementary specials. Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the meeting against the law in force on January 24, 2017. MEDIUM The default budget figure first appears on the record here, thirteen days after the budget hearing that the statute says must disclose it

At 1:05:02 the vice chair asked "Mike, just out of curiosity, when will we know the default budget?" and the business administrator gave the figure, $31,207,288 (1:05:14). In 2017 RSA 40:13, XI(a) read: "The default budget shall be disclosed at the first budget hearing held pursuant to RSA 32:5 or RSA 197:6." The district's budget hearing was held on Wednesday, January 11, 2017 (Cablecast show 8873, which the superintendent opened by describing the session as a public hearing), inside the RSA 40:13, II-a(c) deadline of the third Tuesday in January (January 17). On that recording, about 81 minutes in, the superintendent says the default budget is yet to be announced (the transcript reads the fall budget to be announced), and no other mention of a default figure is found in that hearing's transcript.

Mitigation, and why this is not graded higher: a written default budget form may have been distributed at the hearing without being read aloud, and the business administrator's was in his answer at 1:05:14 implies a figure existed before this meeting. The hearing handouts and the district's MS-DSB form are not available online. A reviewer should check the posted default budget form and the January 11 minutes, if the district still holds them.

Sources: RSA 40:13, XI(a) and II-a(c) (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, 190, eff. Sept. 9, 2014): default budget disclosed at the first budget hearing; hearings on or before the third Tuesday in January.

MEDIUM The default budget comparison given to the board does not reconcile: after the $90,000 restoration the default is lower than the proposed budget, not higher

The business administrator said at 1:05:14: "Was $14,282 higher than the proposed budget. Now it's going to be $104,282 higher. So the default budgets 31,000,207 288." The first half checks: $31,207,288 less the presented operating budget of $31,193,006 is $14,282. The second half does not. The board had just raised the operating budget by $90,000 to $31,283,006 (1:03:26), which puts the default budget $75,718 below the proposed budget. The $90,000 appears to have been added to the gap instead of subtracted.

The direction matters. The superintendent had warned at the January 18 meeting that a default budget below the proposed one would endanger the collective bargaining agreements, and the chair argued at 0:41:17 that next year's default would be built from this year's budget. The board was told the default sat comfortably above its proposal when, on the figures stated, it did not. No member questioned the statement ("Awesome. Okay. Awesome job."). No statute is engaged by a spoken arithmetic slip, and the error may have been caught when the forms were prepared; the posted budget and default budget forms are not available online to check. Graded MEDIUM as a control weakness in the figures presented to the board.

Sources: the recording at 1:01:58, 1:03:26 and 1:05:14; RSA 40:13, XI(a) (2017 codification) for the default budget's role; the January 18, 2017 page for the superintendent's warning.

OBSERVATION A separate technology warrant article was dropped and its $89,200 folded into the operating budget for the administration to distribute, removing a separate voter choice

The administration had placed technology equipment of $89,200 on its own warrant article after cutting it from the operating budget (0:08:05). The board voted at 1:02:52 to "restore 90,000 cuts back to the school administration to, distribute into their lines as they see fit" and to take the technology article off. The business administrator said afterwards that "at least half of it will" go to technology. The Ward 3 commenter put the consequence plainly: "did it ever need to be a warrant article to begin with?" and "You don't really have that much of a say." A member asked for a report back on where the money goes (1:01:06).

This was within the board's authority. RSA 32:5, I (2017 codification) directs the governing body, "after the conclusion of public testimony", to "finalize the budget to be submitted to the legislative body", and ¶II's bar runs against inserting amounts or purposes not disclosed at the hearing. The operating budget stated at the January 11 hearing carried an increase of roughly $850,000, so the $31,283,006 finalized here sits well below it. The point for a reader is transparency, not legality: voters at the deliberative session and the polls saw one operating budget line rather than a separate technology question, and the members themselves disagreed on the record about whether that was right (Irish, 0:23:56; Rapp, 0:24:51).

Sources: RSA 32:5, I and II (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:7, eff. Sept. 9, 2014): finalize after public testimony; no insertion of undisclosed amounts.
