OBSERVATION Middle-school cuts approach the minimum-standards floor; no one tested them against it

Claremont Middle School was already “down one science, one social studies, one English, one math” (0:50:13); a guidance position and a PE vacancy were removed at this meeting, and the principal warned that deeper cuts could mean going “to half your science and socialism [social studies]”. Sprague raised the only compliance point: unified arts, PE and health “are all subject to minimum standards as well. At least they used to be.” The Ed 306 minimum standards for school approval were readopted effective December 13, 2024, three weeks before this meeting, and are the framework against which such cuts would be checked. No one in the meeting made that check, and this page does not assert that any standard was breached.

Source: N.H. Admin. R. Ed 306 (minimum standards for public school approval; Doc. #14150, eff. 12-13-24; no subsection asserted).

OBSERVATION Pre-K was removed as a gross $500,000 program cost; the continuing special-education cost was never quantified

The draft carried no money for the pre-K program (Henry: “That is correct”, 0:37:25), which served 23 identified children and about 20 typical peers. The committee accepted that services to identified children continue: McCosker, “one way or the other, we have to provide those services”, while cautioning “I don’t know that assuming is safe.” Sprague spent much of the second hour trying to isolate the program’s net cost and could not: the contracted-services line could not be found, and part of it “got zeroed”. The roughly $500,000 carried to the board the next evening is therefore a gross classroom-program figure; no net figure appears in any record. Federal rules make a free appropriate public education available to “all children residing in the State between the ages of 3 and 21, inclusive”, with each child’s placement decided by a group that includes the parents; they do not require a district to run its own classroom program, so no violation is suggested. Sprague also asked for a presentation on out-of-district placement protocols (“This is very foreign to me”); no date was set.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.101(a) (FAPE available to all children residing in the State between the ages of 3 and 21); 34 CFR 300.116(a)(1) (placement decided by a group including the parents).
