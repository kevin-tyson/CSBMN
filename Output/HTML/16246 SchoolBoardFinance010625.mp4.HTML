Claremont School Board Finance Committee, January 6, 2025

Televised working session of the board's three-member Finance Committee with the superintendent, business administrator, student services director and principals, the day before the board voted to send the FY26 budget to its public hearing. The committee worked from version six of the draft: $490,062 to be raised before the collective bargaining agreements, about $1,005,062 (5.78%) with the teachers' agreement, no money for preschool, $15,000 instead of $30,000 for high school summer school, and one middle school counselor position removed. No motion or vote was taken. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, notice, packet or minutes for this session were found. HIGH A quorum of the Finance Committee met to settle the FY26 budget; no notice, agenda or minutes of the session were found

All three committee members are heard, so a quorum was present, and the business was squarely within the committee's charge: the final version of the FY26 budget the board received the next night (flag 2). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes the committee a public body ("or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto"), so RSA 91-A:2, II required 24 hours' posted notice and minutes open to inspection within five business days. No notice, agenda or minutes were found: MAP.md records searches of the 2025 and FY 2025–26 meeting-document archives, both Meeting Minutes year folders and keyword searches (2026-08-23); this run added Drive searches for the date in four formats and for finance documents of that season (2026-10-03). The district's Claremont Finance Sub Committee folder could not be listed; its modifiedTime is December 1, 2025, eleven months later, so it cannot have held contemporaneous minutes even if it holds some now. The December 18, 2024 board agenda lists no January 6 session, and the only committee meeting announced on that board recording was a gathering over the Christmas period (see the December 18 page, flag 3). The recording itself starts mid-discussion, so it does not show a call to order, a start time or who was present when the session began. Graded HIGH under the project's severity rule: a public body met, no minutes exist in any district share searched, and nothing on the record says minutes exist elsewhere. Televising the session is a real mitigation of the secrecy concern, but it is not one of the rule's mitigating limbs, and RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes whether or not a meeting is recorded. A negative from Drive search is not proof of absence.

OBSERVATION Preschool was removed on cost and outcome claims not documented in the record, while the services owed to identified children remain

The draft carries "no money for preschool" (0:37:26), which serves "23 identified" children and 20 typically developing peers (0:37:35). The cost basis was not settled on tape: Pratt put the in-house program at about $500,000 (1:13:35), Sprague said "I'm trying to find out what pre-K costs" because related-service salaries sit in district-wide lines (1:12:20), and McCosker said those staff would remain busy because "one way or the other, we have to provide those services." (1:17:37). Whitney's outcome claim, that typical attenders "scored lower than non attenders" (1:38:50), rests on data that is not in any document reviewed for this page. Federal special education law requires a continuum of placements and a placement decided by a group that includes the parents, at least annually, as close as possible to home (34 CFR 300.115(a), 300.116); the committee's discussion is consistent with keeping IEP services, and the January 7 minutes record the chair's assurance of "no reduction in services embedded in IEPs". Separately, removing preschool positions engages the default-budget rule, which reduces the default "by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget" (RSA 40:13, IX(b)); Henry applied that logic to the counselor (1:27:35) but the preschool positions were not discussed in default-budget terms. Recorded as an observation: the decision is within the board's discretion, and the documents that would test its premises are not public in this record.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.115(a) (continuum of placements); 34 CFR 300.116 (placement decisions); RSA 40:13, IX(b) (default budget; eliminated positions); 12. Approved CSB minutes 1.7.25

OBSERVATION The default budget is described as tunable to the operating budget, three weeks after a different default was given to the board

On the December 18 board recording the default was given as $39,791,261 and expected to exceed the budget (see that page). Here Henry reports "the default is 90,000 more than the general budget right now." (1:23:20); Whitney's aim is "making it a wash between the default and all the Warren articles" (1:24:09); Henry says she would rather keep a difference "because it gives people a choice" (1:26:03). RSA 40:13, IX(b) fixes the default by formula from the prior year's operating budget, adjusted for debt service, contracts and obligations, one-time expenditures and eliminated positions; it is not a policy choice to be aligned with the proposed budget. Read in context, the remarks concern how the two totals will compare once the formula is applied, and Henry ties the change to a specific eliminated position (1:27:35). Recorded so the default-budget form presented at the January 15 hearing can be checked against these statements.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) (default budget defined; last amended 2019, 192:2); Claremont School Board, December 18, 2024, flag 4
