OBSERVATION The nonpublic session for negotiations was entered without a stated exemption

The chair called for “nonpublic for negotiations” (0:09:03) and the motion was moved and seconded with no statutory ground named; the roll call was taken (0:09:25 to 0:09:42). RSA 91-A:3, I(b) required that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II." Collective bargaining is not among the paragraph II exemptions. It is handled differently: RSA 91-A:2, I(a) excludes "Strategy or negotiations with respect to collective bargaining" from the definition of a meeting, so a closed bargaining-strategy session needed no nonpublic motion at all. Treating it as a nonpublic session, with a roll call, gave the public more notice than the law required. The point for a reviewer is that the superintendent described the purpose as discussing “some financials” of agreements already reached (0:01:26); whether that fell within strategy or negotiations cannot be told from the recording. The board then took its votes in public.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I(a) (2016 codification); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION Five agreements went to the ballot before their terms or costs were stated in public

The five motions (0:11:28 to 0:13:01) named only the bargaining unit; no term, raise or cost was read before any vote. The cost items came an hour later in O'Neill's comparison: teachers' first-year cost items of 268,000 for 68 as transcribed and an overall increase in appropriation he put at $1.39 on the tax rate (1:06:40, 1:08:12). He explained that he could not “go public” with the numbers before ratification (1:10:51, 1:11:17). The superintendent had also said that only the paraprofessionals and teachers had ratified (0:01:26, 0:01:47). Placing cost items before the voters is what the statute contemplates: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) provides "Only cost items shall be submitted to the legislative body of the public employer for approval at the next annual meeting". No rule required the board to read terms aloud before voting. The observation is that a viewer of the votes learned the cost only later in the meeting, and that the votes for three units preceded those units' ratification as described on tape.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (source note ends 2013, 244:1, eff. Sept. 22, 2013)

OBSERVATION The superintendent placed the constitutional duty on the school board; the aid figure he gave matches the statute

The superintendent told the board that ensuring an adequate education is “even a constitutional duty” of school boards (0:14:03). The New Hampshire Supreme Court's 1997 decision in this district's own case placed that duty on the State: "it is the State's duty to provide a constitutionally adequate public education and to guarantee adequate funding". Local boards have statutory duties to operate schools, but the constitutional obligation he described is the State's, which bears on his later point that a shortfall in state aid falls on local taxpayers. His figure for the aid reduction is accurate: RSA 198:41, IV(d), as it read in 2016-17, set the stabilization percentage at "96 percent for fiscal year 2017", reduced by 4 percent of the 2012 grant each fiscal year after that (0:18:35).

Sources: Claremont School District v. Governor (N.H. Supreme Court, Dec. 17, 1997); RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 85:9, eff. July 18, 2016)
