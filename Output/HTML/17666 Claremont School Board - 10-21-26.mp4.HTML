Claremont School Board — October 21, 2026

A public forum on the HB 1300 school tax-cap ballot question, followed by the regular meeting at the tech center. Eleven residents and officials spoke at the forum, which ran about twice its scheduled half hour. In the regular session, the new business administrator presented the first quarter's budget-to-actual report, the treasurer said electronic payments are still reaching neither him nor the board, and the superintendent reported more special education reassignments. The board authorized a 24-month electricity contract at a rate 26 percent above the last one, awarded the Disnard parking work, adopted five purchasing policies and elected two certified educators. OBSERVATION The forum is expressly allowed; the electioneering limits apply to employees, not to board members

On October 7, board members disagreed over whether the board could take part in the ballot question. The statute that created the question says nothing in it prohibits "a municipality, school board, or school administrative unit board from holding a public hearing on the question" (RSA 32:5-i, III, statute text). Separately, a public employee may not electioneer on duty or with government property, and "electioneering" means information a reasonable person would believe "explicitly advocates for or against" a measure (RSA 652:16-h, statute text). The superintendent framed his talk as "strictly factual", and his slides list what "Proponents say" and what "Opponents point out" (deck text). Board members who gave their own views at the forum and in the discussion item (0:11:09, 1:58:54 to 2:13:15) are elected officials, and the statute's definition of "public employee" excludes "Persons elected by popular vote" (RSA 273-A:1, IX(a), statute text). Whether any statement crossed into advocacy is a judgment this page does not make.

Sources: RSA 32:5-i (2026, 324:1, eff. Sept. 1, 2026), ¶ III; RSA 659:44-a, I and II (no electioneering by a public employee on duty or with government property); RSA 652:16-h ("electioneering" means information a reasonable person would believe "explicitly advocates for or against" a measure); RSA 273-A:1, IX(a) ("public employee" excludes persons elected by popular vote)
