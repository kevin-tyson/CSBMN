Claremont School Board, Public Hearing on Proposed Budgets, January 20, 2026

The board's statutory budget hearing for FY2026–27. The administration presented Option A, the four-school aligned budget ($44,844,373, default $44,449,704), and Option B, the current four-school configuration ($46,211,951, default $47,800,535), along with warrant articles for the administrators' agreement, the sale or lease of Bluff School and 52 Maple Avenue, and a citizens' petition to adopt a school district budget cap that the business administrator estimated would force a budget of $35,700,906. Fifteen residents spoke, several more than once; the board closed the hearing by voice vote, which bars any increase before the January 21 vote. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on January 20, 2026, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

OBSERVATION Option B's default budget is $1,588,584 higher than Option B itself, and the record does not show why

RSA 40:13, IX(b) builds the default from the prior year's operating budget, "reduced and increased, as the case may be, by debt service, contracts, and other obligations previously incurred or mandated by law", less one-time items and the salaries and benefits of positions eliminated in the proposed budget; vacant positions under recruitment are not "eliminated". Both defaults start from the same $43,179,564 and share every line except two: salaries and related benefits rise $876,001.63 under Option A (0:14:54) and $4,572,300.48 under Option B (0:20:00), while health insurance rises $1,053,316.60 under A and $707,849.60 under B. The lines sum as stated ($44,449,703.53 and $47,800,535.38). The result is that if voters defeat Option B they receive $47,800,535, more than the $46,211,951 the board would be asking for. A $4.57 million contractual salary increase on a budget of $43.2 million is large, and the hearing did not explain it. RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires the default to be disclosed at this hearing, which it was, and requires a default budget form identifying each change "by account code, and the reasons for each change". The hearing folder holds only the agenda, and no form was shown. The form would settle the question; the January 21 page should be checked for it.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) (default budget defined) and XI(a) (disclosure at the first budget hearing; default budget form)

OBSERVATION The budget-cap petition does not use the statute's ballot wording, and it needs a hearing of its own

RSA 32:5-f, IV prescribes the question: "...shall not submit a recommended budget that is higher than _____ dollars per pupil cost times the average daily membership in residence...plus a _____ percent annual increase for inflation." As read at 0:27:09, the petition fills the first blank with "the 2025 2026 fiscal year per pupil cost" and the second with CPI for the Boston area rather than a dollar figure and a percentage, and cites RSA 325 [32:5-e]. Angell's later explanation that a dollar amount could be amended but "You cannot change the language" (1:40:12) turns on that difference. RSA 32:5-f, III separately requires a school board hearing on the question "at least 15 days, but not more than 30 days, before the question is to be voted on", with newspaper notice 7 days ahead; for a March 10 vote that window is February 8 to 23, after the February 7 deliberative session. Kennedy said that hearing's date was still "to be determined" (0:30:12). Counsel's statement that overriding an adopted cap needs a supermajority matches RSA 32:5-e, III. Whether a nonconforming petition can appear on the ballot as written is a legal question this page does not answer.

Sources: RSA 32:5-f, II–IV (placement by petition; hearing window and notice; required ballot wording; 2024, 353:2; 2025, 183:6, 7); RSA 32:5-e (the cap; 3/5 override)

OBSERVATION The hearing notice is not in the public packet

RSA 40:13, II-a requires notice of budget hearings to be posted by the second Tuesday in January (January 13, 2026), and RSA 32:5, I requires seven days' notice of the hearing. The hearing folder 16. CSB Public Hearing 1.20.26 was created January 12 but holds one document, the agenda, uploaded at 1:43 p.m. on the day of the hearing. The date, time and place were public earlier: the January 7 agenda, on Drive since December 30, lists the public hearing as "1/20/26 Tuesday @ 7pm Stevens High School Auditorium" (agenda document), and the date was announced on the January 7 recording. That is not the posted notice the statutes describe, and this page could not locate one; a notice posted at the SAU office or in a newspaper would not appear on Drive. The January 21 folder does hold a drafted newspaper notice for that night's separate trust-fund hearing, which shows the district prepares such notices.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a (notice by the second Tuesday in January); RSA 32:5, I (budget hearing with seven days' notice); Drive metadata for the hearing folder and agenda, read 2026-10-03

OBSERVATION A resident's claims about the administrators' unit were partly checkable

Demars argued that a three-school model would have shrunk the administrators' unit below the size needed to bargain: "you have to have ten members" (0:44:33). RSA 273-A:8, I does say "In no case shall the board certify a bargaining unit of fewer than 10 employees"; whether the CAA would have fallen below ten, and what that would mean for an already certified unit, is his argument and is not settled here. His salary comparisons (a state average principal base of $103,000, Claremont about $19,700 above it, a Stevens principal costing $185,167 fully burdened) and his 97% health-insurance share differ from the 95% average Crawford gave on January 7; they are attributed to him and were not verified. The agreement had already been ratified on January 7, and its cost items are now on the warrant as RSA 273-A:3, II(b) requires: $0 for FY2026–27 and $25,871.82 to $103,487.25 for FY2027–28 (minutes).

Sources: RSA 273-A:8, I (no unit of fewer than 10 employees); RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (cost items to the legislative body)

POSITIVE A hearing on the statutory date, with the defaults disclosed, the new ballot data shown and the closing rule explained

The hearing was held on January 20, 2026, the third Tuesday in January, the date RSA 40:13, II-a(c) sets as the last day for SB 2 budget hearings. Both default budgets were presented line by line, as RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires at the first budget hearing. The administration showed the voters the prior year's cost per pupil ($26,012.84) and the ELA, math and science proficiency rates that RSA 671:20, II now requires immediately before the budget question. Both property articles carry the charter-school right of first refusal that RSA 194:61 requires in any contract to sell or lease unused district facilities. Counsel attended to answer procedural questions. Comment had no overall time limit, with second and third turns, and the chair and Angell told the public before the vote that closing the hearing bars any increase, consistent with RSA 32:5, I, under which the board finalizes the budget after testimony closes.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a and XI(a); RSA 671:20, II (cost per pupil and proficiency on the ballot; 2025, 281:1, eff. Sept. 30, 2025); RSA 189:75, I (cost per pupil); RSA 194:61 (unused district facilities; charter-school right of first refusal); RSA 32:5, I
