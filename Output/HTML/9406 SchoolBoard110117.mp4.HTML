Regular meeting at which the board interviewed four candidates for the vacant seat, appointed Dr. Alex Herzog after a nonpublic session, heard the Claremont Middle School administration on bullying, and heard the superintendent argue against the board's $1.2 million reduction target for the FY2019 budget. HIGH The motion to enter nonpublic session states no exemption

RSA 91-A:3, I(b), in the text in force for 2017, provides that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II," and requires a recorded roll call. The motion heard at 1:00:18 is “Please make a motion run in on public.” No exemption is named before or after it, and the chair's correction at 1:00:26 concerns only the roll call. The roll call was held, which partly meets the statute, but only unattributed Yes. answers can be heard, so how each member voted is not on the record. The purpose, stated in advance by the superintendent, was to deliberate on the four vacancy candidates (0:17:46). Which paragraph II ground would fit is itself unclear: II(b) covers "The hiring of any person as a public employee", and an appointee to an elected board is an officer rather than an employee; II(c) covers matters likely to affect a person's reputation. The minutes, if any existed, might record an exemption, but the statute requires the motion itself to state it. Mitigation on the record: the appointment vote was taken in public (1:02:24), and RSA 91-A:3, III's 72-hour disclosure duty for nonpublic minutes cannot be tested because no minutes of either kind survive.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(b)-(c), III (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 29:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

MEDIUM Board signatures on vouchers had "not been done for some time"

The superintendent asked for two members to come weekly to sign vouchers, a requirement he attributed to the district's auditors, and said “But this has not been done for some time, and so we have some catching up to do.” (0:13:02). RSA 197:23-a provides that the district treasurer "shall pay out the same only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of the 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole to authorize payments." The two-member signing the superintendent describes is that mechanism. His statement that it had lapsed, with signatures to be caught up afterwards, suggests a period in which payments were made before board orders were signed. The recording does not say how long the lapse ran, whether the board approved manifests in some other form during it, or whether the board as a whole had empowered particular members. A probable control gap, not a confirmed breach; the treasurer's records for 2017 would settle it.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (2017 codification; source note ends 2015, 272:45, eff. Oct. 1, 2015)

OBSERVATION "The law ... is 21 days" does not match the bullying statute

The assistant principal told the board that “in New Hampshire, the law and this district's policy is 21 days to do an investigation” (1:41:17), and a member repeated it (1:42:49). RSA 193-F:4, II, as codified in 2017, requires the district policy to provide for an investigation "to be initiated within 5 school days of the reported incident", with the completion period set by the policy and a superintendent's extension "for up to an additional 7 school days"; the number 21 does not appear in the section. A 21-day completion period may well be the district's own policy choice, which the statute permits, but it is not a state-law requirement. The middle school's practice as described (starting within five days, same-day calls to both families, followed by a letter, 1:43:29) is consistent with the statute's initiation and parent-notification terms. The section was later amended (2025, 57:1, eff. Aug. 1, 2025); the 2017 text governs this meeting.

Sources: RSA 193-F:4, II(h), II(j) (2017 codification; source note ends 2010, 155:2)
