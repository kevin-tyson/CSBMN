Claremont School Board, May 4, 2022

Regular meeting chaired by Michael Petrin, with the two members elected in March 2022 seated. The superintendent laid out a position-by-position plan for about $3.3 million of ESSER money with every new post sunsetting in FY25; the special education director reported that children who attended the district's pre-K scored lower in kindergarten than children who did not; the board joined an amicus brief on adult disability services, authorized the chair to sign the FY2023 federal general assurances, heard the business administrator explain two NH Department of Education Title monitoring findings, adopted two overdue policies, tabled BEDH, and went into nonpublic session. MEDIUM State Title monitoring found required policies unadopted for six months and no time-and-effort certifications

The business administrator reported two findings from the NH Department of Education's monitoring of the Title programs. First, two policies the board gave first reading on November 3, 2021, employee references (aiding and abetting sexual abuse) and the drug-free workplace, “never put it on the agenda for second read” (2:09:55), and the district had no written procedures for federal purchasing, time and effort, disposal of assets or record retention (2:10:43, 2:11:06). Federal law requires an LEA receiving ESEA funds to have a policy prohibiting aiding and abetting sexual abuse (20 U.S.C. 7926). Second, staff paid partly from federal grants had not signed time-and-effort certifications (2:12:59, 2:13:25); 2 CFR 200.430(i)(1) requires salary charges to rest on records that accurately reflect the work performed, supported by internal control. He took responsibility: “I thought we had the policies and we never brought them in for second read.” (2:25:19). Mitigation on the record: a corrective action plan was filed and accepted (2:15:21), corrections are due August 30 (2:14:49), and the board adopted both policies the same night (2:30:52, 2:31:22). MEDIUM, a control weakness identified by the state and being corrected; the monitoring letter itself was not found in the district's shares.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. 7926 (ESEA sec. 8546: LEA policy prohibiting aiding and abetting sexual abuse); 2 CFR 200.430(i)(1), 2022 edition (salary charges supported by records of work performed); 2 CFR 200.303(a), 2022 edition (effective internal control over federal awards)

OBSERVATION Federal grant reimbursements were behind, and earlier grant money may have lapsed

The business administrator said reimbursement requests were behind on several grants, adult education among them (2:19:36), that before his arrival in October 2019 “we may have left behind some of the grant dollars” without saying how much (2:20:22), and that the district was “working through a few different years of reconciling three years ago and two years ago” (2:22:01).
