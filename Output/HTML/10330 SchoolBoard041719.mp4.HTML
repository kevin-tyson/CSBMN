Claremont School Board, April 17, 2019

Regular meeting. After a short nonpublic session whose minutes a member moved to seal for 76 years, the board approved the Bluff fourth-grade White Mountains trip, scheduled May 1 hearings on a $25,000 alumni donation and E-Rate money, heard that lunch debt stood near $21,000 to $22,000 with $15,555 owed on 67 accounts, approved a Goshen tuition agreement, presented its three goals from the April 14 retreat, and cancelled its June 5 meeting. The acting superintendent added to the agenda a board questionnaire for the audits of fiscal years 2016, 2017 and 2018, which were still being brought up to date; members answered it off camera after adjourning. HIGH The audits for fiscal years 2016, 2017 and 2018 were still being completed in April 2019

The acting superintendent added a "board questionnaire for audit for the fiscal year 16, 17 and 18" to the agenda (0:01:18) and explained that "we're working to clean up" and "Audits and get them up to date. And we are making some progress" (0:40:47, 0:40:54); some current members were not on the board in the years being audited. The same night she presented the general assurances by which the board acknowledges "the rules for accepting federal money" (0:25:46).

Under the Uniform Guidance, a non-federal entity that expends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year must have a single audit, and the reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period" (2 CFR 200.501(a) and 200.512(a)(1), 2019 edition; regulation's wording). Subpart F applies to audits of fiscal years beginning on or after December 26, 2014, which covers a district fiscal year 2016 beginning July 1, 2015. For a July to June district the nine-month limits fell on March 31, 2017, March 31, 2018 and March 31, 2019. An audit still in progress in April 2019 for all three years means the first two were a year or more past due, if the threshold applied.

The tape does not state the district's federal expenditures, so the threshold is inferred, not shown; the district received federal program money (the general assurances, the school lunch program discussed at the April 11 SAU 6 meeting), and a district whose lunch accounts a member counted at "67 out of 1875 kids" (0:19:37) would ordinarily expend more than $750,000 a year in federal awards. The audit reports themselves, and the Federal Audit Clearinghouse record, would settle the dates. HIGH because the rule is binding and the record points to all three deadlines having passed.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2019 edition; earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2019 edition; $750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.110(b) (2019 edition; Subpart F applies to fiscal years beginning on or after December 26, 2014)

RSA 91-A:3, III, in the 2019 codification, requires nonpublic minutes to be disclosed within 72 hours unless the body, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", determines that disclosure would likely affect a person's reputation adversely, render the proposed action ineffective, or relate to terrorism, and it sets no fixed term. On April 3 the same board sealed its minutes for 99 years on a voice vote (see the April 3, 2019 page). The motion here cited II(a) and II(c), so a reputation determination may have been intended. MEDIUM After adjourning, the board stayed to answer the auditor's questionnaire as a body, off camera

The questionnaire was an agenda item, added at the start of the meeting (0:01:18). When it was reached the chair proposed doing it "right after the meeting" because "I don't think it's a matter of public necessarily" (0:41:25, 0:41:43); the acting superintendent offered to read the questions so the board could "answer them for each of the, each of the different fiscal years" (0:41:29). After the vote to adjourn, the chair said "Hang in for a second, though. We're going to do these questionnaire." (0:48:04).

Under RSA 91-A:2, I, a meeting is the convening of a quorum to discuss or act on a matter within the body's jurisdiction, and RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires a public body to deliberate on such matters only in meetings held under RSA 91-A:2. A quorum answering, as the board, an auditor's questions about three years of district finances is that kind of business. If the room stayed open, the public could in principle have stayed; but the session was after adjournment, off the recording, and so outside the minutes of the noticed meeting. The board's answers to an auditor's governance questions are ordinarily part of the audit file, not a public record by themselves, and nothing on the tape suggests anything was concealed. MEDIUM as a process gap.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2019 codification; meeting is the convening of a quorum); RSA 91-A:2-a, I (2008, 303:4, never amended; deliberation only in meetings)

OBSERVATION The chair asked counsel whether graduation or a diploma could be withheld over lunch debt

The chair recalled that seniors once had to clear their debts before graduation practice and asked that counsel advise on "the leverage that we really have around making sure that students have no debt before they can participate or in graduation or get a diploma" (0:22:59); the vice chair recalled that "there was panic in the community" over the idea (0:23:17). Recorded as an observation so a reader can follow whether the question returns.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2018 codification; added 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016)
