MEDIUM Minutes first appear in the district’s public share 17 business days after the meeting

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be “open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting” (2019 codification, in force on this date). For a Wednesday, October 19 meeting that is Wednesday, October 26. The only copy located, CSB Hearing & Meeting Minutes 10.19.22, has a Drive createdTime of 15 November 2022, inside the packet folder for the November 17 meeting. Counting from October 20 and excluding the November 8 biennial election day and November 11, both legal holidays under RSA 288:1, that is the 17th business day.

Limb applied (html_briefing §5): MEDIUM, because the meeting was noticed and packeted and minutes exist; only their timing is in question. Drive createdTime shows when the file reached this share, not when it was written or whether a paper copy was available at the SAU office earlier. The same pattern touches the October 5 minutes, which this meeting tabled at 0:02:16 because a member had not had time to review them.

The minutes otherwise meet the content sentence of RSA 91-A:2, II: they name members, persons appearing, movers and seconders.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; minutes within 5 business days; mover and seconder); RSA 288:1 (legal holidays, incl. biennial election day and Veterans Day)

OBSERVATION The hearing notice gave a different meeting date, and the agenda cites a different statute from the warrant

The posted CSB Meeting Notice 10.19.22 announces a hearing “regarding the scheduling of a Special District Meeting to be held on October 27, 2022”. The warrant read at 0:06:09 and posted the same day (Claremont District Meeting Warrant 10.19.22, Drive createdTime 19 October) sets the meeting for Thursday, November 3. The agenda heads the hearing “Special Town Meeting (RSA 197:3)”, while the warrant calls the meeting under RSA 197:3-a, the section for a single-session special meeting on a change in RSA 198:41 aid.

The dates the district used fit RSA 197:3-a, IV: a hearing “at least 14 days prior to the meeting” (October 19 to November 3 is 15 days), with notice posted “at least 7 days prior to the hearing” (the Drive notice was created October 12, seven days before). An October 27 meeting, as the notice stated, would have been 8 days after the hearing. No rule found makes the stale date in the notice a defect in itself, so this is an Observation; it does bear on whether the public notice described the meeting accurately. Whether the notice was also posted on the district website, as ¶IV requires where a website exists, could not be checked.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, IV (hearing 14 days before; notice 7 days before hearing, incl. website); CSB Meeting Notice 10.19.22; AGENDA CSB 10.19.22

OBSERVATION The board sent the warrant forward with its central legal question answered three different ways

Members asked what happens to the money if voters reject the article. On tape the chair says rejection means “they're voting to not accept this money. So the money doesn't even come in.” (1:25:07); Skillen and Whitney say it reduces the tax rate; Lambert says that without a warrant “we don't get anything, tax rate or otherwise” (1:31:14); the superintendent calls counsel’s view “And it is vague.”. The minutes record a single answer: the funds “would then go back to the taxpayers as a tax reduction”.

The codified grant section created by SB 420, RSA 198:40-f (2022, 318:1, eff. July 1, 2022), requires a district plan with an accountability component and an annual progress report; it says nothing about what follows if the legislative body declines to appropriate. Other sections of 2022 ch. 318 were not read this run (the NH General Court bill page refused the fetcher), so this page does not state the answer. The motion to table for financial and legal answers failed, and a written opinion from counsel was requested after the vote (1:34:08).

Sources: RSA 198:40-f, II (2022 codification; plan and accountability component); 2022 N.H. Laws ch. 318 (SB 420), status page (approved and effective July 1, 2022); minutes 10.19.22

OBSERVATION The record of the vote on the motion to table is internally inconsistent

On tape the chair calls the question at 1:33:10; one aye is audible, the noes are called together, and the chair declares the motion defeated at 1:33:24. The minutes read: “All in favor via voice vote with Frank Sprague, Joshua Lambert, Whitney Skillen, Bonnie Miles opposing. Motion was rejected.”

That sentence begins “all in favor” and ends “rejected”, and it lists Frank Sprague, who seconded the motion, among the opponents. Four named opponents of seven implies a 3–4 defeat, which fits the outcome. No rule found requires a tally on a voice vote, so this is an Observation; the wording makes the vote hard for a reader to reconstruct.

Sources: CSB Hearing & Meeting Minutes 10.19.22; recording 1:33:10

POSITIVE Minutes name the dissenters on split voice votes and each public speaker by ward

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to name members, persons appearing, and the mover and seconder of each motion. These minutes go further: on both split voice votes they name who voted no (Whitney and Horsky on the warrant motion), and each citizen speaker appears with a ward. The superintendent also agreed at the table to bring accountability-plan updates to future board meetings (1:12:19), a reporting step beyond the annual progress report RSA 198:40-f, II requires.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification); RSA 198:40-f, II (2022 codification)
