MEDIUM Four weeks after the November 7 meeting, its minutes were still "a truncated version" that the board could not approve

The chair deferred the November 7 minutes because "what you have in front of you is sort of a truncated version. It's incomplete" (0:03:29), and the assistant superintendent said she would "get those back" for December 19 (2:10:57). RSA 91-A:2, II, as it read in 2018, required minutes to be promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than five business days after the meeting; a draft can satisfy that, but it must carry the names of members, persons appearing, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions. November 7 was the meeting at which the board appointed a member after a nonpublic session and accepted $32,596.23 in gifts. The record does not say what the truncated draft lacked, so whether the five-day requirement was met cannot be decided here; a draft missing the appointment or the final decisions four weeks on would not meet it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; text in force for all of 2018)

MEDIUM The budget subcommittee did the line-by-line budget work in more than twenty hours of meetings that left no public record found

The budget subcommittee (Sprague, Petrin and Benware) put in "20 plus hours in meetings" with administrators and department heads (1:21:43), held its "next to last meeting yesterday" (1:36:12), and settled reductions it was not yet ready to make public (1:39:55). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), in the text in force throughout this period, makes any committee or subcommittee of a school board a public body, so its meetings needed notice and minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II. No notice, minutes or recording of any subcommittee session was found in the district's shares or the Cablecast gallery mapped in MAP.md. The record mitigates the notice question in part, since at least one session was announced at the November 7 board meeting, which is why this page grades it MEDIUM rather than HIGH under the project's severity rule; it does not show that minutes were kept. Petrin's statement that material becomes "public information" only "once it's here in the packet" (1:39:55) describes the board packet, not the subcommittee's own records, which were public records of a public body in their own right. Holding personnel reductions back from the full board until the next meeting (1:24:33) is not itself a violation; RSA 91-A:3, II(a) would have allowed the board to discuss specific employees' dismissal in nonpublic session, as Pierce suggested (1:40:50).

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1, so in force in 2018); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification); RSA 91-A:3, II(a) (2017 codification)

OBSERVATION The 2018 deliberative session's secret ballots had been kept at the moderator's home for ten months

The moderator told the board she and the clerk tallied the secret ballots from the February 2018 deliberative session and that she still had them "in a Ziploc bag at my home because I didn't know where they went" (0:26:28); after a brief exchange the ballots were to go to the school district office (0:27:10, 0:27:18). She kept them because "I didn't think they should just be tossed out", and the board settled custody in public, which is to its credit. No statute verified for this project fixes where or for how long a school district meeting's secret ballots must be kept; RSA 197:20 makes the district clerk responsible for keeping a true record of each district meeting, which is the natural home for them. A written custody rule for future sessions would settle the question.

Sources: RSA 197:20 (district clerk keeps a true record of each meeting; source note ends 1963, 120:5)

OBSERVATION Gifts described as given to pay down lunch debt were redirected to school food pantries

The assistant superintendent presented four gifts "to pay down school lunch debt", the largest "from the GoFundMe for Claremont School lunches" (0:40:05). Because the 2017-18 debt had already been cleared on November 7, the board voted to send the $2,311.93 to the school food pantries rather than hold it against new debt (0:46:37 to 0:47:36), on the reasoning that donors "were going on the assumption that it was going to be used to feed children" (0:42:19). The chair asked whether the board could direct the use of a gift at all (0:44:44). RSA 198:20-b, III(b) governs acceptance of gifts under $5,000 (agenda and minutes notice, acceptance in public session at a regular meeting), and this item was on the amended agenda; the statute does not address restricted purposes. The record does not say whether the donors or the GoFundMe organizer were asked. Members later described the GoFundMe as "specifically set up for food banks" (2:04:39), which differs from how it was presented earlier in the meeting. The donors' own descriptions of their gifts would settle which purpose they chose.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(b) (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION The federal funding analysis is the presenter's; its legal citations are garbled in the transcript, and its central conclusion cites no rule

The presentation put federal allocations at $3,881,000 and state aid at about $12.5 million, together "about $16.4 million of a $32 million budget" (1:11:18 to 1:12:51). Two of its legal points can be checked. The chair's observation that the attendance requirement runs "through age 18 instead of age 21" (1:15:29) is right: RSA 193:1, I, as codified in 2018, requires attendance for children at least 6 and under 18, while federal special education law makes a free appropriate public education available from 3 through 21. The citations as transcribed (RSA 21-9N, RSA 1931, 8306) are speech-recognition renderings and should not be relied on. The conclusion that "if we were to say no thank you to federal funding, you're also saying no thank you to state funding" (1:12:51) is not tied to any cited provision on the record, so this page treats it as the presenter's characterization. The 47-slide deck itself is not in any public share.

Sources: RSA 193:1, I (2018 codification; source note ends 2017, 182:2, eff. Aug. 28, 2017); 20 U.S.C. 1412(a)(1)(A) (FAPE for children aged 3 through 21)

POSITIVE The moderator debriefed the deliberative session in public, and the 2019 dates were set inside the statutory windows with snow dates

The moderator brought a written debrief of the 2018 deliberative session to the board and walked through what worked and what did not (0:25:17 to 0:36:52). No statute requires that. The 2019 dates she was told of fit RSA 40:13 as codified in 2018: the budget hearing on January 2 (snow date January 3) falls before the third Tuesday in January, January 15, as II-a(c) requires, and the deliberative session on February 6 (snow date February 7) falls within III's window, from the first through the second Saturday after the last Monday in January (February 2 to February 9, 2019). Publishing snow dates in advance, which the moderator asked for after the 2018 session had to be moved (0:28:03), goes beyond what the statute requires.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a(c) and III (2018 codification; source note ends 2018, 313:1, 2, eff. Aug. 24, 2018)
