Claremont School Board — December 5, 2018

Summary of a regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The moderator debriefed the 2018 deliberative session; the board accepted $4,311.93 in small donations, heard a staff analysis of what refusing federal funds would cost, received the budget subcommittee's 64-page draft FY2020 budget without its personnel reductions (drawing a sharp objection from one member), adopted two policies, and set aside a stipend request pending a job description. MEDIUM Minutes of the November 7 meeting were still incomplete four weeks later

The chair deferred approval of the November 7, 2018 minutes because "what you have in front of you is sort of a truncated version. It's incomplete." (0:03:29), and the assistant superintendent undertook to "get those back" (2:10:57). Four weeks had passed. The law then in force required minutes to be "promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting"; a draft satisfies that, but an incomplete draft may not show the members present, the persons appearing, the subjects and the final decisions that the same paragraph requires. The November 7 meeting included an appointment to a board seat and a vote to seal nonpublic minutes, so completeness mattered. Graded MEDIUM under the run's rule for missing records rather than HIGH: the record itself shows a partial draft existed and a complete version was in hand, which mitigates, and whether a draft was open to the public within five business days cannot be established from here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 165:1 and 234:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2018): minutes contents and five-business-day availability.

OBSERVATION A three-member budget subcommittee held back the personnel pages from the full board, and its own meetings have no public record online

The board chose to have a subcommittee of Sprague, Petrin and Benware build the draft budget with the administration (1:20:42, 1:41:54). The draft handed out tonight omitted "the reductions that are being made to any staff or personnel" (1:24:33), which members of the subcommittee had discussed. Petrin explained that giving it to the board would make it public: "if we go to the board, it is then public information." (1:39:55). Pierce objected that the rest of the board was asked for its "100% support" (1:31:30) without it, and suggested it could have been shared in a nonpublic session (1:40:50); the administration said the remaining pages would come a week before the next meeting under board policy (1:37:33). A subcommittee of the board is itself a public body with notice and minutes duties; no notices or minutes of its sessions (by the chair's account the latest was the day before, 1:36:12) are online. Petrin also mentioned an email to board members asking for questions midway through the process (1:41:24); the chapter then in force barred deliberation outside meetings, and nothing on the record shows that the email was used for deliberation. Recorded as an observation about process and online availability, not a finding.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (source note ends 2008, 354:1): a public body includes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto"; RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (source note 2008, 303:4; unamended, so the same text governed in 2018).

OBSERVATION Secret ballots from the 2018 deliberative session were being kept at the moderator's home

The moderator told the board she had kept every secret ballot from the 2018 deliberative session "in a Ziploc bag at my home because I didn't know where they went" (0:26:28), and asked what to do with them. A voice at the table said they "would go to the school district office" (0:27:10), and she agreed to deliver them. Her instinct to preserve them was sound, and she raised the question in public. The episode shows that the district had no settled custody rule for deliberative-session ballots ten months after the vote. No statute on the retention of school district meeting ballots was verified for this page, so this is recorded as an observation only.

Sources: No verified rule; recorded as an observation.

POSITIVE Small donations were accepted by motion in public session, their use debated openly, and donors named

Each of three donations under $5,000 was put to a motion at this regular meeting under an agenda item for donations, with the donors named (0:40:05, 0:47:53, 0:49:48). The board debated in the open whether the lunch money should build an escrow for future arrears or stock school food pantries, heard both ideas (Pierce's split proposal drew no second, 0:46:28), and adopted Benware's pantry motion (0:46:44). The law then in force required unanticipated funds under $5,000 to be noticed "in the agenda" and in the minutes, with acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting"; the two $1,000 teacher grants appear to be the ones held over from November 7 because they were not on that night's agenda.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, so in force in 2018).
