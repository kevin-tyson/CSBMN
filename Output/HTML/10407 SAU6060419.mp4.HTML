HIGH The SAU's FY2018 audit carried an adverse opinion, and the SAU's entities were still five audits behind, Claremont's FY2018 among them

Presenting the audit of SAU 6 for the year ended June 30, 2018, the acting superintendent read a description of an adverse opinion (“either statements did not fairly represent the entities accounts, or the audited statements do not comply with the generally acceptable the counting principles”, 0:31:08) and confirmed “it's an adverse opinion” with significant deficiencies and material weaknesses listed at the back (0:24:16, 0:32:10). An adverse opinion is the auditor's finding that the financial statements as a whole are not fairly presented under generally accepted accounting principles, which is a material departure by definition. New Hampshire's rule on school-district financial reporting, Rev 1102.03, sets the GAAP hierarchy with GASB statements first; this page has not confirmed whether that rule reaches an SAU as a separate entity, but the opinion itself measures the statements against GAAP. The audit report itself is not in the record, so the specific departures cannot be listed here.

She also reported “17 outstanding audits” when she began, “five left”, and that Claremont's and possibly Unity's FY2018 audits may still be unfinished when she leaves at the end of June (0:25:51, 0:26:16), a full year after that fiscal year closed. Where an entity spends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year, the Uniform Guidance required its single audit to be submitted within the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or “nine months after the end of the audit period”, which for FY2018 was March 31, 2019. The record does not state Claremont's FY2018 federal expenditures, so whether that deadline applied is not established here; the backlog is graded with the adverse opinion because the administration itself described it. Mitigation stated on the record: the SAU's own audits were now current, and corrective work was under way (0:32:27).
