Claremont School Board Finance Committee, December 13, 2024

Friday-morning working session of the school board’s three-member Finance Committee, televised by CCTV: a school-by-school public walkthrough of the first draft of the FY2025–26 operating budget with the superintendent, the business administrator and the director of student services. No motions and no votes; the committee sent questions and a pre-K proposal back to the administration. This page is built from the dialogue transcript of the recording and the board’s own agendas and minutes; no notice, agenda or minutes of this meeting were located. HIGH No minutes of this meeting exist in any district share, more than 21 months later

The Finance Committee is a public body: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) reaches any “committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body” of a school district. RSA 91-A:2, II (2024 text) required minutes including “the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions”, open to inspection “not more than 5 business days after the meeting”, here by Friday, December 20, 2024. The board’s own by-law 1.05(d) (adopted text) says subcommittee minutes “must be recorded and available for public inspection on the SAU 6 website within 5 days of the meeting”, and by-law 1.11 makes the subcommittee chair responsible for assigning a minutes recorder.

None were located: not in the 2024 Meeting Documents packets, the Meeting Minutes share, the district’s Claremont Finance Sub Committee folder (a parentId search returned nothing; a negative from that search alone is not proof), or the next board packet. The comparison is the December 18 board packet: two other subcommittees minuted their December meetings and the district filed them there (Visioning, December 9; Capital Improvements, December 10). The Finance Committee filed nothing for any of its televised sessions.

Severity: the HIGH limb of the project’s rule applies. A public body met, no minutes exist, and nothing in the record explains the absence. No notice was located either (flag 2). The board’s approved December 18 minutes answer the Finance item by recommending that people “watch the CCTV recordings of the committee meetings”. A recording is not the minutes the statute requires. The December 18 session is graded MEDIUM on its own page because it was noticed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (subcommittees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II, 2024 codification (minutes within 5 business days); Claremont School Board By-Laws, adopted June 5, 2024, rules 1.05(d) and 1.11; Approved CSB minutes 12.18.24

MEDIUM No posted notice was located, and every published list of committee dates omits December 13

RSA 91-A:2, II requires notice of the time and place posted in two appropriate places at least 24 hours ahead, excluding Sundays and legal holidays; by-law 1.05(c) requires subcommittee notice “at least 24 hours in advance on the SAU 6 website”. The November 20 agenda listed Finance meetings for December 4 and December 18, 1–3 p.m.; the December 4 agenda listed only December 18. December 13 is on neither, and this was a morning meeting.

The mitigating fact is in the approved December 4 minutes: “Ms. Henry had emailed Frank Sprague to ask to meet with the finance committee on Thursday next week and then a second meeting on Friday to be televised”. So the meeting was announced at a public board meeting nine days ahead. Those minutes reached Drive only on January 7, 2025 (file metadata), and an announcement in another body’s meeting is not the posted notice the statute describes. A notice may have been posted on the SAU 6 website; the site as it stood in December 2024 cannot be inspected from here, so this is “not found”, not “never posted”.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (24-hour notice of time and place); By-law 1.05(c) (notice on the SAU 6 website); CSB Agenda 11.20.24; CSB Agenda 12.4.24; Approved CSB minutes 12.4.24

MEDIUM The committee worked from page-numbered budget drafts that were never published

Members cite a draft by page throughout: Crawford on “the budget copy that we had last Friday” (0:20:11), “page 23” (1:01:04), “page 47” (2:06:00), “page 52” (2:11:33); Sprague on “page 43” (1:35:28); Whitney on “A packet that I have here” (2:14:49). The board’s by-laws require it. The second rule numbered 2.09 in the adopted text says “Any and all presentations by the administration to the board or any committee shall be contained in the agenda and made available to the public in its original format online so the public may download or access the information prior to or during board meetings.” Rule 4.03(d) delegates to the superintendent’s executive assistant posting “any and all agenda materials not-exempt from public dissemination by RSA 91-A in conjunction with the posting of any board or committee agendas.” A departure from the board’s own rule is a process gap, graded MEDIUM. Draft budget documents are governmental records that anyone may request under RSA 91-A:4; that right does not cure the posting rule. The by-laws contain two rules numbered 2.09; the text is quoted so the reference is unambiguous.

Sources: Claremont School Board By-Laws, adopted June 5, 2024: the second rule 2.09 and rule 4.03(d)

OBSERVATION The “95% by law” reimbursement rate does not appear in the statute

Crawford told viewers that state reimbursement for special education “is set, I think, at a 95% by law and over costs of $70,000 per student” (0:06:56) and that payment would be “like 63%” (0:07:23). RSA 186-C:18, III as it read in 2024 makes the district liable up to 3½ times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil, the state liable for 80 percent of the cost above that up to 10 times, and for all costs above 10 times, with appropriations prorated when insufficient. The numeral 95 does not appear in the section. The 80-percent-of-entitlement floor in today’s text came later (2025) and does not apply to this budget year. The speaker hedged (“I think”), and the point she drew from it, that a shortfall in state aid lands on local taxpayers, does not depend on the figure. The 63% payment figure could not be checked from here.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III, 2024 codification (source note ends 2023, 79:141, 142)

OBSERVATION The pre-K proposal rested on a statute that says nothing about preschool, and went to the administration without a motion

Whitney said the district is “not required to provide this standalone service in Claremont per RSA one six C semicolon 3-A” (1:46:05). RSA 186-C:3-a is headed “Duties” and assigns duties to the state’s special-education division (“The division shall help school districts meet their responsibilities”). It does not mention preschool, pre-kindergarten or children aged three to five. Whether the district must run its own program is a separate question that this citation does not answer. Federal rules do reach preschool children: 34 CFR 300.116 governs the placement of “a preschool child with a disability” and requires the placement to be decided by a group including the parents, at least annually, as close as possible to the child’s home.

Whitney said repeatedly that required services would continue (“We will continue to provide all the necessary services that we are required for these kids”, 1:55:43). The committee took no vote. Crawford framed it as a request for the administration to report back (2:02:04, 2:02:16). Five days later the business administrator had taken pre-K out of the draft (December 18 page), and the board moved the budget to hearing on January 7, 2025, after Arlene Hawkins read a statement dissenting from the pre-K cut and Whitney Skillen agreed with her; the vote is recorded as all present in favor (approved January 7, 2025 minutes). Under by-law 1.05 the committee is advisory and the board decides, which is what happened. The record contains no description of where the identified children would be served.

Sources: RSA 186-C:3-a (duties of the division; source note ends 2024, 351:2, eff. Oct. 1, 2024); 34 CFR 300.116 (placements, including preschool children); By-law 1.05 (subcommittees are advisory)
