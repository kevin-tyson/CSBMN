Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on, checked as it read on May 7, 2025, or is labelled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The motion to enter nonpublic session stated no exemption, and the ground later recorded does not fit a hiring

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires the motion to "state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II". On tape the motion is "under RSA 91-A colon three" (1:45:38); the clerk asks The rest of it? and is told No., and the vote proceeds (1:45:48). The agenda named 91-A:3 (c); the approved minutes write RSA 91-A:3(c), omitting paragraph II; the nonpublic form ticks II(c). The matter, per the nonpublic minutes, was "presentation of personnel recommendation & discussion and other personnel matters", followed in public by the vote on an assistant principal. Hiring is RSA 91-A:3, II(b), and discipline or compensation of an employee is II(a); II(c) protects the reputation of a person other than a member of the public body. Mitigation: the agenda gave notice of a nonpublic session under (c), a roll-call vote was taken, the session lasted eleven minutes, and the hiring decision was made in public. The nonpublic form also prints (c) as "other than a member of this board"; the statute reads "other than a member of the public body itself".

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(a)–(c) (motion must state the specific exemption; roll call; grounds); CSB Agenda 5.7.25; approved and nonpublic minutes of 5.7.25.

HIGH The Youth Risk Behavior Survey was given at the high school without the parent opt-out notice, as the superintendent acknowledged

Pratt told the board the opt-out "did not get sent out" and that "the requirements say we have to send it out" (1:52:18); the minutes record "the opt out option was unintentionally left out". Two rules bear on it. RSA 186:11, IX-d, as it read in May 2025 (2024 codification), requires every district's survey policy to notify parents of a non-academic survey and its purpose, to "allow a parent or legal guardian to opt-out of the youth risk behavior survey", and to make such surveys available for parents' review at least 10 days before distribution. The federal Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment requires a district to notify parents directly of the dates of any survey containing items on protected topics such as sex behavior, illegal behavior or mental health, which the YRBS covers. Mitigation stated on tape: students were told verbally it was optional, the full survey was posted for parents afterward, and the superintendent said it will not recur. The 2025 amendments to IX-d (2025, 22:1 and 64:1) took effect after this meeting and are not applied.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-d, 2024 codification (source note 2024, 207:1; 235:1, 2) (notice, YRBS opt-out, 10-day review); 20 U.S.C. §1232h(b) and (c)(2)(B)–(C) (protected survey topics; direct parent notification); approved minutes 5.7.25, item IV.2.

MEDIUM Unsealed nonpublic minutes first reached the public share three months later

The nonpublic minutes record "minutes not sealed". Under RSA 91-A:3, III, minutes and decisions of a nonpublic session "shall be publicly disclosed within 72 hours of the meeting" unless sealed by a two-thirds recorded vote. The only public copy found sits in the district's Unsealed Minutes share, where Drive dates its creation to August 15, 2025, 100 days after the meeting, with a later modification in January 2026. A copy may have been available on request sooner; nothing in the record shows that. Graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the minutes exist, were never sealed, and the decision they concern was taken in public the same night.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (disclosure within 72 hours unless sealed); Drive metadata for file 1Iz9wUQ0w84pNiGIzCLTCmMRAwzXWtdUX, read 2026-10-03.

OBSERVATION $888,998.42 drawn from five trust and reserve funds to pay bills already incurred, leaving $91,796.69

The business administrator's May 6 memo lists the balances: building repair and renovation capital reserve $169,763.42 to $19,763.42; school bus capital reserve $298,694.47 to $14,834.47; special education expendable trust $479,539.39 to $54,539.39; Claremont School District fund $31,447.42 to $2,659.41; capital improvements $1,350.41 to zero. Every withdrawal reimburses money already spent: buses ordered "over a year ago", $30,138.42 of FY25 repairs itemized in the memo, $425,000 of out-of-district tuition, and $150,000 of FY24 roof work. Two questions the record does not answer. First, authority: RSA 35:15, I has trustees hold capital reserve funds until the district votes to withdraw or has named agents to carry out the fund's objects; Henry says the funds are written so the board may withdraw, but the warrant articles naming the board as agents are in neither packet. Second, timing: the $150,000 reimburses a prior fiscal year's spending into FY25 cash to "pay our bills"; how that was recorded is a question for the audited FY25 statements. Also, the special education trust's remaining $54,539.39 sits against the up-to-$50,000 Maple Avenue fit-up the board authorized on April 16 and had not yet drawn. Mitigation: each motion was read with its fund and figure, the memo itemizes the smaller withdrawals, and Henry says counsel and the auditors approved the approach.

Sources: RSA 35:15, I (capital reserve funds held until the district votes to withdraw or names agents; read 2026-10-03, source note ends 2021, 105:2); Trust Fund Withdrawal 5-6-25_0001.pdf; Claremont School Board, April 16, 2025, flag 4.

OBSERVATION Policy IJOC was adopted with a "discriminatory concepts" clause no member could explain

Asked what a discriminatory concept is, the policy chair answered "I don't know, but it's in the Fair Labor Standards Act" (1:38:29). It is not: the revised policy's own drafting note (the NHSBA sample in the packet) traces the clause to "the State human rights laws (RSA 354-A:31 & 32)" and to RSA 193:40, which New Hampshire enacted in 2021. The policy's FLSA reference is to its definition of a volunteer. On May 28, 2024 a federal district judge held those 2021 amendments unconstitutionally vague, as reported by InDepthNH; the status of the appeal on May 7, 2025 was not checked for this page. The board adopted a policy requiring volunteer training on a prohibition whose meaning it could not state and whose enforceability was in litigation.

Sources: i. Revision Policy IJOC_ Volunteers (packet); RSA 193:40; InDepthNH, May 28, 2024, "Federal Judge Strikes Down NH's 'Divisive Concepts' Law" (reporting, not law).
