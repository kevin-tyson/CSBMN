Claremont School Board, FY25 Budget Public Hearing, January 3, 2024

The board's public hearing on the fiscal year 2025 budget, followed by a short regular meeting. Business Administrator Mary Henry presents a proposed operating budget of $36,313,408 and a default budget she states as $406,634 lower; Superintendent Michael Tempesta presents the Claremont Middle School schedule change behind most of the staffing cuts; no member of the public speaks; the board takes no vote on the budget and sends its questions to the Finance Committee meeting of January 5. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on, or says that none was verified.

MEDIUM A $688,426.17 reduction sat unallocated in one district-wide salary account, and no one could trace it

The business administrator's memo for this hearing states that account 100.40.1100.113.5.00000, "Board Voted Expense Reductions for FY24 ($688,426.17)", "should have been allocated out to the salary and benefit lines that it belonged" (memo, quoted as written). On tape she says the amount was "uploaded before my time" (0:08:29), that she does not know where it belongs (0:09:26), and that David Jack of MRI, who had covered the business office, "Didn't know either" (1:06:40). The board's own questions show the effect: a member reads the special-education line as up $212,000 when the summary page shows it down $212,168.56 (0:59:39), and the vice chair warns that "there could be a quarter of a million over here that that's in both places" (1:03:06). The fiscal 2024 bottom line was not affected, as Henry says (0:09:49), but the function totals in the fiscal 2024 column the board was comparing against were skewed by it, as she also says ("it's really skewing some of our district wide numbers"). The NH Financial Accounting Handbook's chart of accounts is guidance, which Rev 1102.03 ranks as "other guidance", so this is a control and presentation weakness rather than a breach of a binding rule. The board responded on the record: it reached consensus that the amount be reallocated by journal entry before the January 5 Finance Committee meeting (1:04:03, 1:06:31).

Sources: Claremont School Board Budget Memo 1.3.24; NH Financial Accounting Handbook for Local Education Agencies (chart of accounts; guidance); N.H. Admin. Rules, Rev 1102.03 (accounting hierarchy)

OBSERVATION The default budget was presented from a handout that is not in the posted packet, and the spoken figure is transposed

Henry presents the default budget from a sheet "which I didn't pass out to you" (0:27:30); no default budget form is among the seven documents in the hearing's packet folder. RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires the governing body to complete a default budget form that identifies changes "by account code" with reasons. The form may exist elsewhere (the deliberative-session packet would be the place to check), so this records only that the hearing's public folder does not carry it. The spoken default, "35,000,960 774" (0:29:01), does not reconcile with the stated $406,634 gap: $36,313,408 less $406,634 is $35,906,774, the figure the deliberative session later used. The transcript's 960 is a transposition of 906, whether by the speaker or the ASR.

Sources: RSA 40:13, XI(a) (default budget disclosed at the first hearing; default budget form contents); packet folder 1. CSB FY25 Budget Public Hearing 1.3.24

OBSERVATION Two different tax-rate figures for the $406,634 gap

Asked the tax impact of the gap between the proposed and default budgets, Henry says "$0.08 per thousand" (0:31:06) and then "$32 on 100,000" (0:31:13). Those are inconsistent: 8 cents per thousand is $8 on a $100,000 property, while $32 on $100,000 is 32 cents per thousand. The approved minutes record "approximately 32 cents per thousand", which matches the second figure. A viewer of the broadcast heard both; the minutes resolve it only in one direction and do not mention the correction. No rule is engaged; this is noted for accuracy of the public record.

Sources: approved minutes, January 3, 2024 (section III)

OBSERVATION The draft minutes reached the public share on the eighth business day

RSA 91-A:2, II (as in force in January 2024) requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after" the meeting; the fifth business day after Wednesday, January 3 was Wednesday, January 10. The draft minutes were created in the 2. CSB 1.17.24 packet folder on January 16, 2024, 15 seconds after the folder itself, which is the eighth business day (January 15 was Martin Luther King, Jr. Day). The file's modifiedTime is the same morning, so it does not show an earlier existence. The statute requires availability for inspection, not online posting; the minutes may have been available on request at the SAU office earlier, and nothing here shows otherwise. The Drive date is the only evidence available from here, so this is an observation, not a finding.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification; minutes open to inspection within 5 business days); draft minutes 1.3.24 (Drive metadata)

OBSERVATION No notice of the hearing was found; the statutory calendar was otherwise met

RSA 32:5, I requires public notice of the budget hearing at least 7 days in advance, and RSA 40:13, II-a sets the second Tuesday in January (January 9, 2024) as the last day to post notice of budget hearings. No notice is in the hearing's packet folder, which was created on January 2. The hearing was announced as a future date at the December 20 meeting (December 20 draft minutes, "Budget Public Hearing 1.3.24"), but that is an announcement, not the posted or published notice the statute contemplates. A newspaper or posted notice may exist outside the district's Drive shares. The other calendar requirements are met on the record: the hearing was held before the third Tuesday in January (January 16) as RSA 40:13, II-a requires, and 31 days before the February 3 deliberative session, more than the 25 days of RSA 32:5, I.

Sources: RSA 32:5, I (2023 codification; hearing at least 25 days before the meeting, 7 days' notice); RSA 40:13, II-a (January calendar for budget hearings); draft CSB meeting minutes 12.20.23

POSITIVE The default budget was disclosed and questioned at the first hearing, and a line-item budget with prior-year columns was posted beforehand

RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires the default budget to be "disclosed and presented for questions and discussion at the first budget hearing". The agenda gave it its own item and Henry presented it with its components (0:28:22–0:29:34), followed by questions on its tax effect and its purpose (0:29:51, 0:45:09). The packet folder, created the day before, carries a 56-page line-item budget (Updated Claremont School District Budget 12.20.23.pdf) whose columns include fiscal 2022 and 2023 actual expenditures, the fiscal 2024 budget and the fiscal 2025 proposal, and a one-page summary with the same comparisons, which is what RSA 32:5, I (sub-account detail available at the hearing) and IV (comparative columns) ask for. The chair also notes that every Finance Committee meeting this cycle was televised (0:43:40).

Sources: RSA 40:13, XI(a); RSA 32:5, I and IV (2023 codification); Updated Claremont School District Budget 12.20.23.pdf
