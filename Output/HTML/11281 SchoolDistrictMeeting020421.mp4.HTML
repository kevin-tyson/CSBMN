Claremont School District Meeting — February 4, 2021

Summary of the second virtual session of the 2021 Claremont School District annual meeting, held under the optional procedure of 2020 N.H. Laws ch. 8 (HB 1129) in place of an in-person deliberative session, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The moderator reported no voter submissions on either warrant article, no board member proposed an amendment, and both articles (the election of officers and the $37,785,406 operating budget) went to the March 9 ballot as posted. The only debate was a board member's statement that the budget was "not at all a rubber stamp budget". Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the session against the law in force on February 4, 2021, including the 2020 session law under which the district chose to hold its annual meeting. They are not findings of violation, and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Four flags follow, ordered by severity.

OBSERVATION The first-session debate the voters normally hold was replaced by a board-only session at which no voter spoke and nothing was amended

In an official-ballot district, RSA 40:13, IV provides that the first session "shall consist of explanation, discussion, and debate of each warrant article", at which voters may move amendments. For 2021 the board chose instead, by a 6–1 roll call on January 13 (see the January 13, 2021 page), the optional procedure of 2020 N.H. Laws ch. 8 (HB 1129). As NHMA's guidance summarizes the enacted law, the governing body holds a second live virtual meeting within seven days of an information session "to consider and address comments" and "shall discuss, debate, and be permitted to amend the posted warrant"; whether the sessions take live public comment is left to the district. Claremont chose none: "No live comments or questions from registered voters were, or will be allowed" (0:03:02). The result on the record is thin. The assistant superintendent reported no submissions of any kind on either article (0:07:29, 0:09:06), no board member proposed an amendment, and the $37,785,406 budget went to the ballot as posted after twelve minutes. The safeguard the procedure kept is the first ballot question, whether voters approve the process at all (0:05:19); its outcome on March 9 is not in this record. No rule was breached on the tape; the observation is how little public deliberation the chosen design produced.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 192:2, the text in force in 2021): first session of explanation, discussion and debate; 2020 N.H. Laws ch. 8 (HB 1129), approved July 10, 2020 (bill status); NHMA, Guidance on 2021 Annual Meeting (December 4, 2020), summarizing the enacted law. The chaptered text of ch. 8 was not read for this page.

OBSERVATION Five budget subcommittee meetings are cited as the budget's vetting, but no notice or minutes of them are available

Petrin's defense of the budget at 0:09:43 rests on a board subcommittee that "met and went line by line through this budget. Spending probably at least ten hours, five different meetings", with the administration and the principals. On February 1 the superintendent named its members as the chair, Benware and Petrin (see the February 1 page). A subcommittee of a school board is itself a public body under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), whose definition reaches "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof", so its meetings carried the notice and minutes duties of RSA 91-A:2, II. None of that paper is in the district's online shares, which begin in July 2022, so whether the meetings were noticed and minuted cannot be tested here. The accounts of the work also differ: the district's February 1 minutes describe members who "worked mornings for fifteen days", while Petrin said five meetings and at least ten hours. This is recorded under the project's online-availability limb, not as a finding that the meetings were unnoticed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; in force until 2023): 24-hour notice; minutes open to inspection within 5 business days; 2.1.21 Minutes from Virtual District Meeting.

OBSERVATION The minutes of this session were approved a year later but are not posted, while the February 1 set surfaced only in a November 2022 packet

The district's February 9, 2022 deliberative session minutes record approval of "previous minutes of 2/4/2021", moved by Benware and seconded by Sprague. At the November 3, 2022 special district meeting the moderator explained that both 2021 sets "should have been approved at the February 9th, 2022 meeting and only the February 4th minute were approved" (show 14693; see the November 3, 2022 page), and the February 1 minutes were then approved from that meeting's packet. The February 4 minutes themselves have not been found in any district share searched for MAP.md. RSA 197:20 requires the clerk to "keep a true record of all the doings of each meeting" and to supply attested copies on request; it sets no posting rule. The record shows minutes were kept and approved, so this is an online-availability observation, not a finding that none exist.

Sources: RSA 197:20 (source note ends 1963, 120:5): the clerk's true record of each meeting; 2.9.22 District Meeting Minutes; Input/SupportingDocuments/MAP.md section 140.

POSITIVE The session ran on the statutory calendar, and every procedural and voting detail was read into the record twice

The two virtual sessions (Monday February 1 and Thursday February 4) fall inside RSA 40:13, III's first-session window for 2021, the first and second Saturdays after the last Monday in January (January 30 to February 6), and inside the seven days after the information session that NHMA's guidance gives for the second virtual meeting. The vote was set for March 9, 2021, the second Tuesday in March that RSA 40:13, VII names. The moderator read the whole procedure, the consequence of disapproving the process, the three ward polling places, identification at the car window, walk-through and absentee voting, and the checklist session, first at 0:02:00 and again at 0:10:40. The public could follow online, on CCTV, by live stream in the Stevens auditorium; at the February 1 session the moderator added that a seat in the auditorium could be reserved by telephone.
