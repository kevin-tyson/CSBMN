Claremont School Board, February 20, 2019

Regular meeting three weeks before the March 2019 election, with the assistant superintendent standing in for the superintendent. The board approved four student trips, accepted three donations including $7,500 from the Jack and Dorothy Byrne Foundation, authorized an Integrated Delivery Network partnership, heard that the district's audits were years behind (the fiscal 2015 audit had only just been finalized), and sent the nepotism policy back to subcommittee. No agenda or minutes survive in the district's online shares; the page is built from the recording's dialogue transcript. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

HIGH The district's federally required audits were years past their filing deadlines

The board was told that the final fiscal 2015 audit had "just recently" arrived (1:15:14), that the fiscal 2016 audit was close to a draft (1:15:40), and that the auditors hoped to reach fiscal 2018 in March (1:16:28). The business administrator explained why the district needs more than a financial audit: because of "the government dollars, we have to have two orders basically. So they do a regular audit and then they have to do a governmental audit as well of the grant funds" (1:20:12; orders is probably audits). That second audit is the federal single audit. For a fiscal year ending June 30, the reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period": March 31, 2017 for fiscal 2016 and March 31, 2018 for fiscal 2017, under 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1). Fiscal 2015 began July 1, 2014, before Subpart F applied, so its deadline came from OMB Circular A-133 §320(a), with the same nine-month rule: March 31, 2016. On the record, fiscal 2015 was nearly three years late on February 20, 2019, fiscal 2016 nearly two years late, and fiscal 2017 nearly a year late. In mitigation, staff described the backlog openly, attributed it to the years before the business administrator arrived in 2015, and set a goal of being current by June (1:18:40). The record gives no federal expenditure total; the finding rests on the business administrator's statement that the grant audit is required. RSA 21-J:19, the state audit statute, is permissive and is not relied on here.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2019 annual edition (single-audit report due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2019 edition ($750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.110(b), 2019 edition (Subpart F applies to fiscal years beginning on or after December 26, 2014); OMB Circular A-133 (2007 revision), §200(a) and §320(a) (earlier fiscal years)

HIGH A $7,500 private gift was accepted with no sign of the prior public hearing the statute requires

RSA 198:20-b lets a school board accept "unanticipated money from a state, federal or other governmental unit or a private source" once the voters have adopted the enabling article, and ¶III(a) adds: "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with newspaper notice of the time, place and subject at least 7 days ahead. The Byrne Foundation's gift for the eighth-grade Boston trip was $7,500 (1:05:15), so the threshold is met. The board accepted it with two smaller gifts on a single motion (1:07:46 to 1:08:13). Nothing on the recording refers to a hearing, and the recording begins at the call to order with no hearing before it. The finding would fall if the district holds a hearing notice and record for this gift. The two smaller gifts were under the threshold, where ¶III(b) requires notice "in the agenda" and in the minutes and acceptance in public session at a regular meeting. Acceptance in public session is on the recording. The $2,000 Byrne check, though, arrived after "the agenda has gone out for this meeting" (1:06:38), which suggests it was not on the posted agenda; with no agenda or minutes surviving, that cannot be checked.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a)-(b), 2019 codification (source note ends 2005, 188:1, so the same text was in force in February 2019; the $5,000 threshold was raised only in 2023)
