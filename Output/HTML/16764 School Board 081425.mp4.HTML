Claremont School Board, August 14, 2025

The meeting at which the board told the public the district was in a cash crisis: a FY2025 deficit the chair put "anywhere between 1 and $5 million", unpaid vendor bills, the state's Commissioner of Education watching, and no reliable numbers two weeks before school opened. Before that, the board approved the three school handbooks, adopted nine policies (including the Finance Committee's fiscal-control package) and spent 78 minutes in a nonpublic session whose minutes it sealed for five years. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid: points where the record raises a question under a statute, rule or accounting standard, or where the board did something worth noting. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on.

HIGH The district's own FY2024–25 budget report shows general-fund spending $1.53 million over budget, and nothing in the record invokes the statutory exception

The packet for this meeting contains Claremont FY24-25 Financials 8-8-25 (1).pdf, a general-ledger budget report for fund 100 covering July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025, printed August 8, 2025. Its grand total reads: budget $37,644,714.81; spent $39,170,152.24; balance ($1,525,437.43), or ($1,588,738.07) after $63,300.64 of encumbrances, minus 4.22% (budget report). Board members read the same report aloud as overages everywhere (1:00:52, 1:04:40), and Crawford said FY2026 money had already gone to pay FY2025 bills (0:57:00). RSA 32:8 bars a school board from paying or agreeing to pay money, or incurring liability, "in excess of the amount appropriated by the legislative body for that purpose ... except as provided in RSA 32:9-11". The emergency route in RSA 32:11, I requires the school board to apply to the commissioner of education "prior to the making of such expenditure", with budget-committee approval or a public hearing; no such application appears in this record. Mitigation the record supplies: Pratt said the figures were still being reconciled (1:05:41); the report's budget column may not equal the voted appropriation; part of the shortfall is revenue (special-education aid paid at 67%, 1:12:05), which RSA 32:8 does not reach. The audited FY2025 budgetary comparison, still years away on this record, is the document that would confirm or clear this.

Sources: RSA 32:8 (no expenditure beyond appropriation; 1993, 332:1); RSA 32:11, I (emergency expenditures only on prior application to the commissioner of education); FY24-25 budget report, printed 8/8/2025 (packet)

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for five years with no statutory reason stated

The nonpublic motion named its grounds, RSA 91-A:3, II(a), (c) and (l), and was taken by roll call (0:20:03), as RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires. The seal motion was put as seal the minutes of the meeting for five years (0:21:25) and carried on a roll call (0:21:40); the minutes record only that it passed and the date, 8/14/2030. RSA 91-A:3, III allows minutes to be withheld beyond 72 hours only when, by recorded two-thirds vote in public session, "it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism". The vote count satisfies the statute; the determination is not on the record. Given counsel's later statements that the business administrator was no longer in charge of the district's finances (August 20), a reputation ground may well have applied, but the board did not say so.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III (motion and roll call; sealing requires a stated determination); approved minutes 8.14.25

MEDIUM The FY2022 audit was still being completed in August 2025, and FY2023 to FY2025 had not been audited

Crawford: the FY2022 audit is still not done, because the district was extremely slow, very slow in providing that information to the auditors (0:56:24); Koski acknowledged 22 audits in 24, 25 (0:57:35); the agenda's future items list a "Possible Audit Presentation on FY 2022". If federal expenditures reached $750,000 in a year, 2 CFR 200.501(a) required a single audit and 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) set its deadline at nine months after year end (March 31, 2023 for FY2022). This record does not state annual federal expenditures, so the flag is MEDIUM. RSA 21-J:19 permits rather than requires an audit within a year, so state law alone sets no missed deadline. The practical consequence was on display: the board was asked to act on unaudited, unreconciled numbers with no audited opening balance.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2022 edition); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2022 edition); RSA 21-J:19 (permissive)

OBSERVATION Where did the board deliberate about going public?

Crawford: the board members of the board, have had a lot of discussion of whether to, bring this tonight to the public (1:34:25). No earlier public session in this record carries that discussion; the August 6 meeting the board had announced did not take place (see flag 6), and the only closed session on this record is the one held this evening under exemptions for personnel, reputation and legal advice, whose minutes are sealed. Legal advice about disclosure would fit II(l); a policy decision about informing the public would not fit any exemption. RSA 91-A:2-a, II provides that communications outside a meeting "shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". The record does not say where or how the discussion took place, and nothing here shows a violation; the unsealing of the nonpublic minutes in 2030, or the board's own account, would answer it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, II (communications outside a meeting); RSA 91-A:3, II(l)

OBSERVATION The approved minutes and the recording differ on movers, speakers and one of counsel's options

Four points. (1) The minutes credit Loren Howard with moving the first-read adoption of JFAA and GBCE, seconded by Whitney; on tape Hawkins asks Would anyone like to make a motion other than me? and then says I'll make a motion to accept or adopt. (0:17:30, 0:17:58). (2) The minutes give Petrin the second on the JED/JEDA withdrawal; the dialogue file gives I'll second. to Madden, marked uncertain (0:18:57). (3) The remark that the $3 million is adequacy aid due September 1 is Petrin's in the minutes and Sprague's (uncertain) in the dialogue file (0:37:57). (4) The minutes describe counsel's first option as an "emergency meeting with the county"; on tape it is a petition to Sullivan County Superior Court for permission to hold a special meeting (0:26:57). RSA 91-A:2, II requires the minutes to record who moved and seconded each motion; the mover/seconder discrepancies are worth a correction motion, and the description of the court route matters because it is the only lawful path to new money that counsel named. The dialogue file's diarization merges voices, so points (2) and (3) may be label errors rather than minutes errors.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (mover and seconder recorded; 2024 codification); approved minutes 8.14.25

OBSERVATION The published August 6 meeting did not happen, and this August 14 meeting was not on the published schedule

MAP.md records no August 6 meeting, recording, packet or minutes, and this meeting's consent agenda approves the June 18 minutes, which fits no meeting in between. The nonpublic session was added by floor amendment (0:01:43), which RSA 91-A does not forbid.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (24-hour notice; 2024 codification); CSB Agenda 6.18.25 (future dates); the June 18, 2025 page
