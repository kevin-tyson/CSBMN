MEDIUM The motion to enter nonpublic session cited “RSA 91, dash a colon three a”, with no paragraph II exemption

Frank Sprague moved “to go into nonpublic RSA 91, dash a colon three a.” (0:02:04). The chair began a voice vote, corrected herself (“Oh. Roll call.”, 0:02:16), and the secretary called the roll; Steven Horsky answered No and the others present Yes (0:02:33). The roll call is what the statute requires. The citation is not: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires the motion to “state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II”, and RSA 91-A:3 has no free-standing subparagraph (a). The likeliest intended ground is II(a) (dismissal, promotion, compensation or discipline of an employee), but the motion does not say so and this page does not supply it.

The nonpublic session itself is not on the recording: the timecode moves from the roll call at 0:02:33 to the motion to come out at 0:02:53 in about twenty seconds, so the recording was paused. The same short form of the citation appears in the Claremont board’s minutes of the previous evening (Claremont School Board, June 15, 2022). Graded MEDIUM: the vote was properly taken by roll call, but the public could not tell from the motion which exemption was invoked.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), 2022 codification (motion states the paragraph II exemption; roll call)

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for 90 years with no statutory determination stated

After the board came out of nonpublic session by roll call (0:03:10), Heather Whitney moved “To seal the minutes for 90 years.” (0:03:28), Frank Sprague seconded (“I’ll second. 90 years. 90.”, 0:03:31), and the motion carried on a roll call (0:03:48 to 0:03:57). RSA 91-A:3, III permits withholding nonpublic minutes only on a “recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session” that determines disclosure would likely affect adversely a person’s reputation, render the proposed action ineffective, or concern terrorism, and then only “until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply.” The recorded vote is on tape; the determination is not, and the minutes that would show it have not been located (flag 3).

Graded MEDIUM as a documentation gap. The Claremont board sealed its own nonpublic minutes for 99 years the night before on the same pattern.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III, 2022 codification (2/3 recorded vote with a determination)

OBSERVATION A subcommittee meeting and a board retreat were planned for a restaurant and the chair’s home

The chair said the superintendent-evaluation “subcommittee” was all present and set its meeting with the superintendent for Friday, June 24 at 6:00 (0:29:41, 0:30:42, 0:31:44): “Now on a Friday that screams pizza and beer.” (0:31:33); a member added “You get the back room.” (0:32:01); the chair: “It’s a business meeting.” and “for the moment, we’ll we’ll leave that open.” (0:31:50, 0:31:56). She also set the board retreat for July 24: “That will be meeting at 1:00 at my house. Will Phillips will come at two.” (0:26:11).

A subcommittee of the SAU board is itself a public body under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (“any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof”), so its meetings need 24 hours’ posted notice of time and place and minutes, as the full board’s do. Nothing in RSA 91-A forbids meeting at a restaurant or a home, and an evaluation discussion may qualify for nonpublic session. On July 20 Joshua Lambert told the Claremont board the subcommittee had met (Claremont School Board, July 20, 2022, flag 4). No notice or minutes of that meeting have been located; the district’s archive does hold a folder named for the July 24 retreat, which could not be opened. Noted as an observation so a reader can check whether the public had notice and access.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), 2022 codification (subcommittees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II, 2022 codification (notice; minutes)

OBSERVATION Audit backlog: the SAU expects to be “caught up” only with the FY2022 audit in February 2023

The assistant superintendent for finance reported “we’re working through a backlog on, on, audits” and an action plan with “our partner from Plods and Sanderson [Plodzik & Sanderson]” to have “fiscal 22 audit completed, by February of 23, which is a somewhat more normal timeframe for completing audits and that everything will be caught up by that time.” (0:36:27). Asked about grants, he said the SAU itself “does not receive grants” (0:37:16), so the federal single-audit deadline would not reach the SAU’s own audit.

The member districts are different. The district’s draft SAU 6 School Board Meeting Minutes 9.29.22 report that the auditor asked to push the Claremont FY2020 audit “to the end of October” 2022. For any entity spending $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year, the single audit is due nine months after year end (for FY2020, March 31, 2021). Claremont’s federal expenditures are not stated in this record, so the deadline is noted, not applied.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2022 CFR edition (nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2022 CFR edition ($750,000 threshold); draft SAU 6 School Board Meeting Minutes 9.29.22
