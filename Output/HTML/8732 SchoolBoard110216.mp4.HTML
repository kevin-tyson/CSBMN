HIGH The fiscal 2016 DOE-25 and MS-25 were still being finished and signed two months after the September 1 filing date

On November 2 the assistant superintendent said the business administrator was "working currently on the annual, report, the Miss document, and needs to be working on finalizing that" (0:02:26). The business administrator then presented the DOE-25 and the MS-25 for 2015-16, said he had been "working on it for 12 hours today trying to get it done", and asked members to sign "signature forms for the school financial report, which is the 2025 and then the D 25" (2:20:51, 2:21:17). RSA 198:4-d, III, as it read in 2016, required each school district's financial report to the commissioner of education, with receipts, expenditures and a balance sheet, to be "submitted on or before September 1 of each year", with a certification signed by the board chair. RSA 21-J:34, V required the same financial report to the Department of Revenue Administration: "School districts shall submit financial reports on or before September 1 of each year." Neither text, as read for this page, provides an extension for school districts. November 2 is 62 days after September 1. The record does not show whether a preliminary version was filed earlier or whether either department agreed to a later date; the district's filing records would settle it.

Sources: RSA 198:4-d, III (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 8:12, eff. Mar. 16, 2016): financial report on or before September 1, chair's certification; RSA 21-J:34, V (2016 codification; source note ends 2007, 182:2): school district financial reports on or before September 1

MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material: the district's shares hold no year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The board appointed a member and sealed the minutes of a nonpublic session at this meeting, and the recording is now the only public account. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force in 2016, required minutes including "names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the board approved the October 19 minutes at this meeting (0:03:28), the clerk is present, and the superintendent refers to a written agenda that lists the nonpublic statute (0:34:55), so minutes and agendas were being kept. In 2016 the statute required inspection, not online posting; what is missing is any public copy now.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2008, 303:4, so in force through 2016)

MEDIUM The motion to enter nonpublic session is not heard to state a specific exemption, and none plainly covers choosing a board appointee

The chair asked for "a motion for RSA 91 a three two" (1:34:49); the next row reads only See second (1:34:54), which may be the letter (c) or may be noise. A roll call followed (1:35:32 to 1:35:48). RSA 91-A:3, I(b) required that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II", by roll call. Paragraph II lists the permitted subjects; the nearest are II(b), "The hiring of any person as a public employee", and II(c), matters that would likely affect adversely the reputation of a person "other than a member of the public body itself". An appointee to an elected board is not obviously a public employee, and II(c) protects reputation, not deliberation as such. Mitigation: the superintendent said the agenda lists the RSA (0:34:55), the applicants were not members, and the appointment itself was voted in public. Whether the agenda named a paragraph, and whether (c) was meant, cannot be settled without the agenda or minutes.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(b), II(c) (2016 codification; the project anchors record these passages as identical to the 2015 text in force from Jan. 1, 2016)

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed by voice vote with no statutory determination stated

After the session the chair asked "Would it be prudent to seal the minutes of nonpublic." and took a voice vote with no opposition heard (1:38:57, 1:39:08). RSA 91-A:3, III required minutes and decisions of a nonpublic session to be disclosed within 72 hours unless the body, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", determines that disclosure would likely affect adversely the reputation of a non-member, render the proposed action ineffective, or relate to terrorism. The vote was in public session and appears unanimous, but no member stated which determination the board was making. The decision the session produced, the appointment, was announced and voted in public at once, so the sealed minutes would at most record the deliberation about the applicants.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2016 codification; same wording as the 2015 text)

OBSERVATION Retained year-end funds: purposes described more narrowly than the statute, and no board vote on the amount

The business administrator first described the permitted uses of the retained fund as "unexpected, catastrophic special education expenses or unexpected catastrophic building failure expenses, or to help stabilize the tax rate" (2:01:26), and later in words closer to the statute (2:13:36). RSA 198:4-b, II, as it read in 2016, allowed voters to authorize retention of year-end unassigned funds "not to exceed, in any fiscal year, 2.5 percent of the current fiscal year's net assessment", for "emergency expenditures and overexpenditures under RSA 32:11, or to be used as a revenue source to reduce the tax rate". His statement that a building purchase does not qualify matches the statute. The board reached an apparent consensus on keeping about $325,000 of the $651,329 (2:18:17), but no motion was made and the business administrator said he needed nothing from the board (2:18:54). The 2016 text does not say who decides the amount retained, so no rule is engaged; the effect on the tax rate ($1.14 to $1.72 by his figures) was decided without a recorded vote.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:6; 2.5 percent until 2020, 38:25)

OBSERVATION "The RSA says they'll pay 100%": the catastrophic aid statute provides for proration

The business administrator said Claremont's catastrophic aid entitlement was $740,844 but the state was paying 69%, and "The RSA says they'll pay 100%. And they're basically going to pay 69%." (2:17:48). RSA 186-C:18, III, as it read in 2016, made the state liable for 80 percent of a child's special education costs above 3 1/2 times the state average expenditure per pupil and all costs above 10 times, and provided that if the appropriation is insufficient the payments are "prorated proportionally based on entitlement". Underpayment against entitlement was therefore lawful proration, not a breach of a 100% promise; the shortfall to the district is real either way. By contrast, his account of the stabilization grant, reduced by 4% a year from its 2012 level (2:03:03), matches RSA 198:41, IV(d) as amended in 2016.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2016 codification; source note ends 2011, 224:227); RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 85:9, eff. July 18, 2016)

POSITIVE Vacancy filled through public interviews on common questions and a public vote

RSA 671:33, II(a), as it read in 2016, provided that "The school board shall fill vacancies occurring on the school board" and that appointees serve until the next district election; it prescribed no process. The board went well beyond that minimum: the superintendent introduced all four applicants by name (0:33:56), each interview used the same question sheet with the other applicants outside the room (0:37:31), every interview was on camera, and the motion and vote to appoint were taken in public session immediately after the nonpublic deliberation (1:40:10). Voters watching can compare the applicants' answers for themselves.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (2016 codification; source note ends 2013, 114:1, eff. Aug. 24, 2013)
