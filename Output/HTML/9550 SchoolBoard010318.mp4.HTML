Claremont School Board (part 2 of 2), January 3, 2018

OBSERVATION The board set aside a provision of its ethics policy by consensus, without a motion or vote

Immediately after the 4-3 vote, the vice chair said the board's ethics policy provides that "once the board makes a decision, we all support that decision", and proposed that members be free to argue their own positions before the deliberative session: "So we are all good with suspending that rule that things are in this process." (0:02:41). No motion, second or vote follows on the recording, and the policy's text is not in the record, so whether it allows suspension, and by what vote, cannot be checked. State law does not stand in the way of the members' advocacy itself: RSA 659:44-a bars electioneering only by a "public employee" as defined in RSA 273-A:1, IX, and that definition excludes "Persons elected by popular vote". Herzog had already urged residents to vote the budget down (part 1). The question the flag raises is the board's own governance, not a statute.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I (source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 273-A:1, IX(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2014, 13:1, 2, so the same text governed in 2018)

OBSERVATION The five-minute proposal was deferred; the deliberative session's rules belong to the moderator

Pierce asked who had set the three-minute limit and proposed five minutes per speaker at the deliberative session (0:02:59, 0:03:38). The assistant superintendent answered that the "Deliberative session is run by the moderator" while the hearing "is run by you" (0:05:03), and the item was held for the next meeting (0:05:51). That answer matches the law then in force: RSA 40:13, IV made the first session subject to RSA 40:4, which provided that "The moderator shall preside in the town meetings, regulate the business thereof, decide questions of order" and "may prescribe rules of proceeding", subject to change by the meeting itself. The board could recommend a speaking time but not set one. At the January 31, 2018 meeting the moderator, Tracy Pope, presented the session's rules of procedure to the board (Cablecast show 9586).

Sources: RSA 40:4 (2017 codification; source note ends 1998, 278:1); RSA 40:13, IV (2017 codification)

POSITIVE The board was reminded that district communications on warrant articles should give facts, not advocate a vote

Before the security article vote, a speaker who appears to be an administrator told the board that historically it would "be inappropriate to say, vote for it and not vote for it. But it is appropriate to give them the facts" (0:13:51), and the vice chair asked that article descriptions go on the website or into a flier like the one prepared for the hearing (0:10:47). That line tracks RSA 659:44-a, which provides that "No public employee, as defined in RSA 273-A:1, IX, shall electioneer while in the performance of his or her official duties" and defines electioneering as acting "in any way specifically designed to influence the vote of a voter on any question or office". Stating the rule on the record, and proposing factual materials for voters, is good practice for the staff who prepare them.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I and III (source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

POSITIVE The clerk corrected the agenda's filing dates on the record, and the corrected dates match the statute

The clerk told the board the dates on its agenda were wrong (0:20:58) and gave the filing period as January 24 to 4 p.m. on February 2 (0:21:24, 0:21:39). RSA 669:19, applied to school district elections by RSA 671:19, opens filing on the "seventh Wednesday" before the election and closes it on the "Friday of the following week". For the March 13, 2018 election that is Wednesday, January 24 through Friday, February 2. The 4 p.m. closing hour is not in RSA 669:19 itself, and this page has not verified it. Correcting a printed error on camera, where prospective candidates could hear it, served the public.

Sources: RSA 669:19 (2017 codification; source note ends 2011, 40:1); RSA 671:19 (2017 codification; source 1979, 321:1)

POSITIVE The budget sent to the voters was adopted by roll call, so each member's vote is public

The vice chair asked for a roll call on the main budget motion (0:01:04) and the clerk called each member by name (0:01:20 to 0:01:40): yes Irish, Pierce, Sprague and Zullo; no Herzog, Petrin and Rapp. In 2018, RSA 91-A:2, III(e) required a roll call when a member participated remotely, and none is mentioned here, so a voice vote would have been lawful. One gap remains: the chair does not announce the result on the recording, and the 4-3 tally on this page is read from the members' answers.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(e) (2017 codification: roll call when a member participates remotely)
