MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this meeting: neither district share holds a year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The board approved the program of studies and adopted policies on final read at this meeting, and the recording is now the only public account. RSA 91-A:2, II, as in force throughout 2018, required minutes including "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the board corrects and approves its March 21 minutes on tape (0:05:30, 0:10:39), and when the clerk leaves the assistant superintendent takes over the minutes (0:32:59). The exchange in which a member asked why minutes are needed when meetings are recorded (1:00:53) is answered by the statute itself: the duty to keep minutes does not depend on whether a meeting is televised.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; the text for all of 2018)

MEDIUM The MS-22 appropriations report was signed 22 days after the March 13 vote

The business administrator presented the MS-22, the report certifying the appropriations the voters approved on March 13, and members signed it at the table (1:44:52, 1:48:34). RSA 21-J:34, II provides that the report certifying "the appropriations voted by the meeting of the appropriate legislative body" for a school district "shall be filed within 20 days of the close of the meeting." For an official-ballot district the meeting closes with the March 13 ballot, so the twentieth day was Monday, April 2, 2018. A report still awaiting board signatures on April 4 cannot have been filed in signed form by then. This is graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the filing date itself is not in the record and the Department of Revenue Administration's file would settle it; a two-day lateness on a routine certification also had no evident consequence.

Sources: RSA 21-J:34, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2007, 182:2, eff. April 1, 2007)

OBSERVATION The 28-day bus-recording amendment was held back pending legal advice, and the buses have no cameras

On March 21 the board amended policy ECAF to require "a minimum of a 28 day recording cycle" (show 9682 transcript; see the March 21, 2018 meeting page). Here the superintendent explained that the statute speaks of "No more than ten days", that he had asked legal counsel whether the board could exceed it, and that he had "moved forward with the policies as they had been approved" with the ten-day text in place (0:08:29, 0:08:36, 0:09:22). He also said "we do not have cameras on the busses" (1:01:49). RSA 570-A:2, II(k) allows school-bus audio recording with video only with school board authorization and provides that "In no event, however, shall the recording be retained for longer than 10 school days unless" the recording is relevant to a disciplinary proceeding or a court orders longer retention. The superintendent's caution matches the statute; the member's view that a longer period would be "stricter" (0:08:54) does not, because the statute's limit protects the people recorded. No rule was broken: nothing was recorded, and the printed policy kept the statutory limit. Zullo's request that the board be told when advice is being sought on a policy it has just adopted (0:09:02) is a fair one.

Sources: RSA 570-A:2, II(k) (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 322:2, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION Gathering members' edits by email through the chair

To finish the list of questions for the NHSBA attorney, the chair asked members to send changes to him so he could forward a final list to the superintendent by Monday (1:23:40, 1:23:51). The superintendent cautioned that a message passed "from one to the other" could be a problem and promised an NHSBA presentation on email (1:22:55, 1:24:04); Zullo's view was that one-way informational messages to the chair, with no reply, are "not a quorum" and "not a discussion" (1:23:26). RSA 91-A:2-a requires the board to deliberate "only in meetings" and provides that communications outside a meeting, "including, but not limited to, sequential communications", "shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". The test is not quorum but deliberation: collecting edits to a list the board had already discussed in public is low-risk, while an exchange of views by email would not be. Nothing on the record shows what the emails contained.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4, never amended)

POSITIVE Opt-out alternatives provided at no cost to families, beyond what the statute requires

On March 21 the administration had proposed that an alternative for an opted-out student be at the parent's expense. The revision adopted here gives elementary and middle-school students an alternative activity and high-school students an extended learning opportunity "sufficient to meet state requirements", and the superintendent confirmed "No payment at all" (1:57:57, 2:01:28, 2:04:24). RSA 186:11, IX-c, in force since September 16, 2017, requires a policy allowing an exception to objectionable course material with "an alternative agreed upon by the school district and the parent, at the parent's expense". The adopted policy meets that requirement and goes beyond it by removing the cost to families, which was the concern members raised on March 21. Statements on the record about what state rules require of elementary health education (1:56:03) are the speakers' characterizations and are not verified here.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-c (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 251:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2017)
