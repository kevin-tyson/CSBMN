SAU 6 Board, June 28, 2018

First SAU 6 meeting under the newly elected chair, Marjorie Erickson, days before Superintendent Middleton McGoodwin's departure at the end of June: the board approved a succession plan, heard the business administrator correct the unit's fund-balance projection, adopted criteria for an interim superintendent with a preference for current New Hampshire certification, set the interim search committee's membership, and approved a press release. MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this SAU 6 meeting: neither share holds a year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The board approved a succession plan, hiring criteria, a committee and a press release, and the recording is now the only public account. RSA 91-A:2, II, as in force throughout 2018, required minutes including "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". (The 2018 text has no mover-and-seconder requirement; that sentence took effect January 1, 2019.) This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the chair refers to an agenda in members' hands (0:04:17), the board approves the June 14 minutes with corrections on tape (0:05:36), and a recording secretary is present (0:01:39). Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; the text in force for all of 2018)

MEDIUM The interim search committee was formed without settling whether it must meet in noticed public meetings

The board voted the committee's membership (1:23:47 to 1:24:51): the SAU 6 chair and vice chair, the Unity and Claremont board chairs, and three SAU staff. Asked whether its meetings must be noticed, the chair said "we'll need to define whether or not we have to warn those." (1:26:26); Horsky answered "you don't have a quorum at those, so I don't know if you'd necessarily need to warn it." (1:26:44); the chair added that "it's a hiring committee" reviewing resumes (1:26:49). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), unchanged since 2008, defines a public body to include a school board or SAU board "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto". A committee whose members the board fixed by vote falls within that text whether or not its members form a quorum of a parent board, and RSA 91-A:2, II then requires notice and minutes. Reviewing applicants is not a reason to skip notice: RSA 91-A:3, II(b) lets a public body go into nonpublic session for "The hiring of any person as a public employee", but only on a motion that states the exemption and a roll-call vote under RSA 91-A:3, I(b), with minutes. The record does not show how the committee met. The chair's own instinct was toward openness ("I think that we want open meetings. We probably should.", 1:26:33), which is why this is MEDIUM rather than higher; the advice given on the floor, if followed, would have let the committee meet without notice.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1, so the text in force in 2018); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II(b) (2017 codification)

OBSERVATION A revenue entry missing from the books changed the fund-balance projection, and the 2016-17 audit arrived a year after year-end

The business administrator said the fund-balance projection given to the board on June 14 understated 2016-17 revenue because indirect-cost revenue from Claremont, paid July 17, 2017, was recorded in the 2017-18 database but not in the 2016-17 accounts (0:32:39, 0:32:59); he found it while "rolling forward balances from the 2016 audit and preparing for the 17 audit", and said "we don't reconcile every single balance sheet account month." The correction was put at $44,000 (0:24:08). The unit received the final audit for 2016-17 on the day of the meeting (0:28:34, 0:29:02), about twelve months after that fiscal year ended, and the assistant superintendent described the office as having been behind on audits (0:28:49). RSA 194-C:4, II(a) lists audits among the services each SAU must provide, but the project has located no New Hampshire statute that sets a deadline for an SAU audit (RSA 21-J:19 is permissive and does not name SAUs). Whether the federal single-audit deadline of nine months after year-end applied depends on whether the unit itself spent $750,000 or more in federal awards that year, which this record does not establish; the "5 million in grants" LeClair mentioned may be the member districts' awards that the SAU office manages. The point is recorded as an observation. The 2016-17 audit report and its management letter would show whether the auditors noted the reconciliation practice.

Sources: RSA 194-C:4, II(a) (audits among required SAU services; 1996, 298:3; 2010, 5:2); Cablecast: S.A.U. #6 SCHOOL BOARD MEETING 6-28

POSITIVE The chair insisted that Claremont choose its committee member in an open meeting, not by email

When Sprague offered to "get something out through the email" (1:16:28) and Pierce asked whether he meant to choose without a meeting, the chair said "you have to do that in an open meeting." (1:16:46) and reminded members they were "not in a warranted Claremont board meeting" (1:17:43). Her opening statement had committed the board to decisions "conducted fully in the public domain" (0:03:00). RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings", and 91-A:2-a, II bars communications outside a meeting, including sequential ones, from being "used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". The chair's direction applied that rule to the Claremont board's choice.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification; source 2008, 303:4, never amended)
