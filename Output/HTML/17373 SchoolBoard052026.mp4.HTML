Claremont School Board, May 20, 2026

Regular board meeting with all seven members present. The board kept student transportation in-house after a request for proposals ($1,971,619 in-house against $2,011,500 from Butler), learning in the process that special-education transportation had been booked as regular education; renewed the Fresh Picks food-service contract 6-1 as the only bidder; renewed the Lifetouch photo contract; approved a broker RFQ for selling Bluff School and the Masonic Temple, whose carrying cost the business administrator put at $438,354; met 23 minutes in nonpublic session on the property sale and sealed those minutes for six months; signed the federal grant assurances; and adopted four policies, three of them at their first presentation and one over a member's objection that the policy required a second reading. MEDIUM The food-service contract was approved for a two-year term; federal rules cap the term at one year with up to four renewals

Asked the term, the business administrator said "it's going to be a two year contract with three when you're, you know, all periods" (1:16:44; the ASR's three when you're is evidently three one-year), and the minutes record Howard's motion "to approve the contract with Fresh Picks for 2 years with 3 one year continuations", carried 6-1 (1:20:41). For a school food authority contracting with a food service management company, 7 CFR 210.16(d) provides that the contract "shall be of a duration of no longer than 1 year" and that renewal options "may not exceed 4 additional years". The five-year total matches; the two-year first term does not. The contract documents in the packet's sub-folder were not opened, so the signed instrument may use a compliant one-year term; the motion as minuted does not. The single proposal followed a solicitation of six providers and a statewide notice (1:15:18).

Sources: 7 CFR 210.16(d) (one-year term; renewals not exceeding four years; last amended 88 FR 57845, Aug. 23, 2023); 5.20.26 approved minutes

MEDIUM Property-sale nonpublic minutes sealed for six months with no statutory finding stated

The board met in nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(d) on the sale of property and, on return, voted by roll call "To seal for six months." (1:28:43, 1:28:54); the minutes record "Minutes sealed until November 20, 2026". RSA 91-A:3, III permits withholding beyond 72 hours only by a recorded two-thirds vote in public session and only where disclosure would likely affect a person's reputation, "render the proposed action ineffective", or pertain to terrorism. For a pending sale the second ground is the natural one, but neither the motion nor the minutes states it. The same gap appears in the May 6 seal. Graded MEDIUM as a documentation gap under a binding rule.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, II(d) and III (property exemption; sealing requires a 2/3 recorded vote and a stated basis)

MEDIUM Special-education transportation has been booked as regular education, so the district says its special-education cost reporting is wrong

The staff report states: "I investigated how we are budgeting and found that our accounting for student transportation is not accurate. The regular education line includes both regular education and special education." Its table shows $1,730,019.46 for regular and $241,600.00 for special education, while Butler priced the special-education work at $1,017,000. On tape Angell said "we need to be accurately reporting our special education costs and we're not." (1:08:37) and that segregating the costs in the general ledger is summer work (1:06:30). The NH Financial Accounting Handbook's chart of accounts separates these costs by function, and district special-education expenditures feed state reporting, including catastrophic-aid claims under RSA 186-C:18. Graded MEDIUM because the Handbook ranks as "other guidance" under Rev 1102.03; the total transportation figure is said to be right (1:09:32). The minutes also record the bus vote as unanimous although Lavalette announced a No (see flag 8).

Sources: Staff Report - Award of Student Transportation (May 15, 2026); NH Financial Accounting Handbook (chart of accounts); Rev 1102.03 (Handbook as other guidance); RSA 186-C:18 (special-education aid)

OBSERVATION Masonic Temple improvements ran to about three times what the chair says the board authorized, with $18,900 in architect fees unpaid

The chair said the board voted $50,000 for improvements and about $150,000 was spent: "how could more money be expended than we authorized?" (0:38:05); Angell put improvements at "just over $160,000" with "unpaid architectural fees for 18,900" whose payment is under review (0:37:13). Rapp relayed a contractor's statement that building-maintenance money was set aside (0:39:05). RSA 32:8 bars a school board from paying or agreeing to pay money "for any purpose for which no appropriation has been made"; if the work was charged to an appropriated maintenance line, that statute may not be breached even though the board's own authorization was exceeded. The record does not show which accounts paid, who approved the excess, or when. An unpaid bill for services received is a liability whether or not the architect presses it.

Sources: RSA 32:8 (no expenditure without an appropriation; 1993, 332:1), gc.nh.gov; GASB 34, ¶79 (modified accrual)
