Claremont School Board — February 16, 2022

An unscheduled one-item meeting of the Claremont School Board, broadcast by CCTV as an "Emergency Session", summarized from the recording and its dialogue file. No agenda, packet or minutes are posted online. The board revised its COVID-19 decision matrix to make masks optional at all times in school from Monday, February 21, 2022, while keeping masks on buses under the federal transportation order and the state's post-isolation masking rule. The amendment carried on a confused roll call announced as 4–3; the amended motion passed 5–2, one member voting remotely. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the meeting against the state and federal law in force on February 16, 2022. The Governor's COVID-19 state of emergency, under which the emergency orders relaxing in-person meeting rules were issued, ended on June 11, 2021 (New Hampshire Bulletin, June 10, 2021, reporting), so the ordinary remote-participation rules of RSA 91-A:2, III apply. Flags are not findings of violation and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Six flags follow, ordered by severity.

MEDIUM The board received the December 1, 2021 minutes eleven weeks after that meeting

At 0:02:02, tabling the December 1, 2021 minutes, the chair explained: "Just what we just got these. So, give people a chance to look them over before we vote on them." RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification, in force on this date) required that minutes "shall be promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting." Severity limb (run contract section 5): MEDIUM, because the record mitigates: the minutes existed by this date and reached the board, and the chair's words date only the board's receipt, not when a draft was first open to public inspection. Seventy-seven days is nonetheless far outside five business days if the board copy was the first. No minutes of either meeting are posted online to settle it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1).

OBSERVATION The broadcast calls this an emergency session, but nothing on the recording states an emergency, and the action it took was deferred five days

CCTV's show title is "School Board Meeting Emergency Session of 2/16/22", and the chair said at 0:02:36: "this was not a scheduled meeting. There was, I guess, what I would characterize some urgency over the board over the weekend." If a meeting is called without the usual notice, RSA 91-A:2, II allows it only "in an emergency", which it defines as "a situation where immediate undelayed action is deemed to be imperative by the chairman or presiding officer of the public body, who shall post a notice of the time and place of such meeting as soon as practicable", and it requires that "The minutes of the meeting shall clearly spell out the need for the emergency meeting." On the recording no one declares an emergency or states one; the board chose a start date of Monday, February 21; and a member noted at 0:21:31 that the board's own matrix would have permitted a change the following week in any case.

Whether this meeting was in fact posted on the ordinary 24 hours' notice (the "urgency" arose over the weekend and the meeting was on a Wednesday, which left time for it) cannot be checked: no notice, agenda or minutes are online. If ordinary notice was given, the emergency label is CCTV's and this flag falls away. Recorded as an observation for that reason.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification: notice, emergency exception, emergency minutes); Cablecast show title.

OBSERVATION A member voted remotely; the recording shows the required roll calls but not the stated reason or who was with him, and his final vote was relayed by the chair

RSA 91-A:2, III as then in force allowed a member to participate remotely "only when such attendance is not reasonably practical", required that "Any reason that such attendance is not reasonably practical shall be stated in the minutes of the meeting", required each remote member to "identify the persons present in the location from which the member is participating", required that remote members be "audible or otherwise discernable to the public in attendance at the meeting's location", and provided that "All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote." A quorum was physically present. The chair complied with the roll-call rule and said why (0:47:16). But nothing on the recording states why Lambert could not attend or who was with him, and the minutes that would record the reason are not available. On the final roll call (0:55:54), when the clerk called his name, the answer in the room is the chair's "No, he." and the clerk's "Said no." rather than Lambert's own voice; whether he was audible to those present at that moment cannot be told from the transcript.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a), (b), (c) and (e) (2019 codification, in force on this date).

OBSERVATION The amendment vote cannot be reconstructed from the record, and one member changed his vote after saying he did not understand the question

The first roll call (0:52:25) is fragmentary in the transcript: several answers are unlabeled or run together with the clerk's calls, and the attribution report records that the per-member tally "is not recoverable" from the audio. Lovett abstained because "I'm confused on what we're voting on", the chair announced a tie, and after being told a defeat would revert to the unamended motion he said "Then I'll change mind. Yes." The result was announced as "It's 4 to 3." A tie followed by one abstention becoming a yes can produce 4–3 only if the tie was 3–3, which is consistent with the announcement but cannot be checked member by member. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to describe the decisions taken and to name each motion's mover and seconder; the minutes are what would settle the tally, and they are not online. No rule was breached on the recording; the flag records a gap in the verifiable record of a decision that changed school policy.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification).

POSITIVE The board amended its own motion to keep bus masking under the federal order then in force and the state's post-isolation masking rule

The motion as first made treated transportation as open for discussion. A remote member read the federal requirement into the record at 0:29:12, the superintendent reported counsel's advice not to drop bus masks (0:18:45), and the nurse described the state's return-from-isolation masking rule. Petrin's amendment (0:43:50, restated at 0:45:08) kept both. On this date the CDC's order requiring masks on public conveyances, issued February 1, 2021, remained in effect; according to contemporaneous reporting, the CDC announced on February 25, 2022, nine days after this meeting, that it would no longer require masks on school buses. Heeding counsel and a member's reading of the operative requirement over the pressure to act at once is the practice worth recording.

Sources: 86 FR 8025 (Feb. 3, 2021), CDC order requiring masks on conveyances; reporting: School Transportation News, "CDC No Longer Requires Face Masks On School Buses" (reporting, not law).

POSITIVE Although the board's comment policy excluded the agenda item, the chair opened the floor to residents on it, for and against, before voting

At 0:02:02 the chair read the board's "Relatively new policy that citizens comments cannot, involve something that's on the agenda." He then opened the floor on the masking item itself at 0:31:29 ("I'm going to allow some of the public to to speak your name and ward, please"), heard four residents on both sides, and let one more speak just before the roll call (0:50:32). No statute then required a school board to take public comment: RSA 189:74, which requires a comment period of at least 30 minutes, was enacted by 2022, 333:1 and took effect September 6, 2022. The practice exceeded the legal minimum of the day.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022; not in force on this date).
