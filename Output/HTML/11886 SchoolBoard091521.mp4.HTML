Claremont School Board, September 15, 2021

Regular meeting with the chair participating remotely and the vice chair presiding. After a long statement by the presiding member, the board took a roll-call vote on a motion to remove the Fish Tank program and temporarily reinstate Reading Wonders in the elementary schools, then disputed whether that vote had decided the motion; it also adopted public comment on every action and discussion item and heard the new assistant superintendent’s curriculum-mapping plan. HIGH With the chair participating remotely, four of the five votes were taken by voice; RSA 91-A:2, III(e) required roll calls

The chair took part from outside the room: “I think you're muted Frank” (0:35:35), and the superintendent remarks how he sounds “in the studio to on that Bose speaker Sony system over there” (0:37:50) Petrin later mentions “I know we had some comments on the chat” and asks whether the chair was trying to join in (1:17:06). Under RSA 91-A:2, III, in the text in force from January 1, 2019 to October 2, 2023, remote participation is allowed "only when such attendance is not reasonably practical"; "Any reason that such attendance is not reasonably practical shall be stated in the minutes of the meeting"; the remote member "shall identify the persons present in the location from which the member is participating"; and "All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote." (statute text, each). The governor’s Emergency Order #12, which had relaxed in-person quorum rules, lasted only for the COVID-19 state of emergency, and that emergency ended at midnight on June 11, 2021 (NHPR reporting), so the ordinary statute governed this meeting. Only the Fish Tank vote was by roll call (1:25:11). The agenda (0:04:56), the September 1 minutes (0:05:34), the public-comment motion (1:44:56) and adjournment (1:48:55) were voice votes; the board discussed the choice and settled on a voice vote: “Can we agree to a voice vote on this?” (1:44:44), “Roll calls. Okay. Voice vote is okay.” (1:44:53). No statement of why the chair could not attend in person, and no identification of anyone with him, is heard; the chair mentions his throat in passing (0:38:13), and the minutes that would hold a stated reason do not survive. The presiding member asked “Frank, are you. Did you go away?” (1:17:34) during the debate, so at least once the link’s connection was in doubt. Severity is HIGH because paragraph III(e) is mandatory and the record shows the votes were not taken as it requires. Mitigation: the one contested decision was taken by roll call, the voice votes were unanimous as far as the recording shows, and members visibly weighed the point.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a), (c), (e) (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1; in force to Oct. 2, 2023); Emergency Order #12 (March 23, 2020), duration tied to the state of emergency; NHPR, June 10, 2021: state of emergency to expire (reporting)

Whitney’s motion would “remove fish tank and reinstate reading wonders” (0:44:41), on a temporary basis while the administration investigates other programs (0:44:14). After debate Petrin said “I'd like to call the question and take a vote.” (1:24:37), Stone said “Second call.” (1:24:59), and the presiding member called for a roll call (1:25:03). Five Yes. answers and one No. are heard, with Lovett apparently marked absent, but no tally or result is stated (1:25:11 to 1:25:55). Twenty minutes later the remote chair argued the vote had only closed debate (1:45:04, 1:45:32); Petrin answered that his call “was never seconded motion” (1:46:00), and the board treated the roll call as the vote on the motion (1:46:02 to 1:46:11). The recording shows Stone’s second at 1:24:59, so the board’s own explanation does not match it. Under ordinary parliamentary practice a seconded call of the question is voted on before the main motion; whether a single roll call can be read as both is for the minutes and the board to say, and the minutes do not survive. The program in use was being replaced two weeks into the school year on this vote. Horsky’s statement that “This board was lied to” (1:00:52) and the chair’s “subterfuge or incompetence”… (1:18:03) are members’ characterizations; Lambert disputed them (1:01:47), and the July 21 recording they rely on is not part of this page.

OBSERVATION The chair floated ending special education at 18 or 19; federal law sets the range and ties any exception to state law, not to a local board

The chair said that, if he were running things, “I would be looking at capping, special ed at age 18 or 19” (0:36:12), adding “I know there are federal regulations and such” (0:37:16). Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, "A free appropriate public education is available to all children with disabilities residing in the State between the ages of 3 and 21, inclusive" (statute text), and the obligation for ages 18 through 21 lifts only "to the extent that its application to those children would be inconsistent with State law or practice" (statute text). A cap at 18 or 19 is therefore a question of state law, not something a school board can adopt by policy. This page did not verify the New Hampshire provision. No motion was made; the remark is recorded because it was offered as a budget idea in a public meeting.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §1412(a)(1)(A)-(B) (FAPE ages 3 through 21; exception tied to state law or practice)
