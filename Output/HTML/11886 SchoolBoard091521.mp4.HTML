Claremont School Board — September 15, 2021

Regular meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. With the chair participating remotely while ill and Heather Whitney presiding, the board heard a resident ask for transparency on curriculum, received the superintendent's COVID-19, enrollment, restructuring and schedule report, voted by roll call on Whitney's motion to remove the Fish Tank program and temporarily reinstate Reading Wonders after members said they had been misled about its use in social studies, heard the new curriculum administrator's plan, and adopted a temporary rule opening every discussion and action item to public comment. HIGH With the chair participating remotely, the board took four of its five votes by voice, although the law then required every vote to be by roll call

Frank Sprague took part from home by audio link (0:35:31; "I think you're muted Frank"), speaking and voting throughout. Emergency Order #12's relaxation of in-person meeting rules had ended with the state of emergency on June 11, 2021, and a 2021 bill to extend remote meetings (SB 95) did not pass. On this date, therefore, RSA 91-A:2, III governed. It allowed remote participation "only when such attendance is not reasonably practical", required that "Any reason that such attendance is not reasonably practical shall be stated in the minutes of the meeting", required a quorum physically present, and provided: "All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote."

Only the Fish Tank motion was decided by roll call (1:25:03). The amended agenda and the September 1 minutes were approved by voice at 0:04:56 and 0:05:34. On the public comment procedure the presiding member asked "Can we agree to a voice vote on this?" and a voice suggested "Roll call, but" before members settled on a voice vote (1:44:44); adjournment followed by voice, with Stone saying "Will call. I don't know if we need." (1:49:02). The recording itself shows these four voice votes with a remote member, which is why this is graded HIGH under the project's rule. Mitigating facts are on the record too: the reason for remote attendance (illness; the chair described symptoms, and on October 20 said he had been at home with COVID-19) would satisfy the practicality test if the minutes stated it; a quorum was physically present; and no voice vote appears contested. Whether the minutes record the reason cannot be checked, because they are not online. The requirement that a remote member "identify the persons present in the location from which the member is participating" is not heard on the tape.

The one roll-call vote carried its own confusion: the chair later said his vote had been on calling the question, members concluded the call had never been seconded (although the transcript records "Second call." at 1:24:59), and on October 20 the chair put his intent on the record (October 20 page, flag 3). A roll call with the motion restated before it is the protection III(e) is meant to give.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a), (b), (c) and (e) (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1; in force until October 3, 2023); NHPR, June 10, 2021 (state of emergency to expire June 11, 2021; reporting); NH Municipal Association State House Report, July/August 2021 (SB 95 not passed; reporting).

OBSERVATION A motion reversing the elementary reading program was made without notice under the agenda item "curriculum update"

Before the item was reached, a resident told the board he did not know what the two words on the agenda meant and asked for "a much more public announcement" of significant curriculum changes (0:05:54). At 0:44:14 the presiding member took the curriculum update item from the curriculum administrator ("I'm going to hijack that from you doctor temporarily") and moved to remove Fish Tank and reinstate Reading Wonders, supported by research she had prepared. A member who opposed the motion said he had not had time to prepare (1:06:24), and at the end of the meeting the superintendent agreed that agenda items should be "as detailed as we can be". New Hampshire's Right-to-Know Law in 2021 required notice of the time and place of a meeting, not an itemized agenda, so no statute was breached; the board's own agenda policy was not available to this page. The point is that residents and teachers affected by a mid-year program change had no advance signal from the agenda that it would be voted on.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; notice of "the time and place of each such meeting").

OBSERVATION Four members said the board had been misled in July about Fish Tank's role in social studies

Whitney read from the district's 2021-22 elementary expectations, which told teachers "to use Fish Tank as a guide for what to teach during science and social studies" (0:45:23), against the July 21 presentation at which "the board was told that the Fish tank program was the reading program." Horsky, having re-watched the July 21 recording, said "This board was lied to" (1:00:52); Petrin and Stone said the same in other words, and Sprague put it as "subterfuge or incompetence" (1:18:03). Lambert disagreed: the expectations also said what teachers would not do, and "No one was lying to us." The new curriculum administrator said "many of the people that made those decisions are long gone". The July 21, 2021 presentation is recording 11776 in this project; members' specific claims about it (timestamps 1:20 and 1:38 into that tape) were not checked for this page. No rule is cited, because the district's curriculum-adoption policy was not available; the motion carried, and a month later the superintendent said "we've rectified it and we, we own it" (October 20 page, flag 4).

Sources: Recording only; members' characterizations, not findings.

OBSERVATION A member of the public said the agenda was hard to find, and the district's own website was called clunky

At 1:46:58 an audience member said "Finding the agenda was a real interesting puzzle." and "I had to call your office twice." The superintendent agreed the web page is "clunky, it's awkward" and said a new site was coming; Lambert said he posts every agenda to the board's Facebook page. RSA 91-A:2, II in 2021 required notice of the time and place "posted in 2 appropriate places one of which may be the public body's Internet website, if such exists", at least 24 hours ahead. Whether that notice was posted cannot be checked from the recording, and no notice or agenda survives online, so this is recorded as an observation about access rather than a notice finding.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification).

POSITIVE The board opened every discussion and action item to public comment, a year before state law required any comment period

On Stone's motion (1:42:59) the board adopted, temporarily and pending a policy, a three-minute comment opportunity for citizens on each discussion and action item, with a timekeeper named by the chair, while keeping the general citizens' comment period for other matters. Members debated how to make it durable, and the policy committee undertook to draft a policy within 30 days (1:47:38). New Hampshire's first statutory school-board public comment requirement, RSA 189:74, was enacted in 2022, effective September 6, 2022, so in September 2021 this exceeded the legal minimum. The October 20 page records that the procedure was not consistently applied at the next meeting.

Sources: Practice beyond statutory minimums; RSA 189:74 (2022, 333:1, eff. September 6, 2022) did not apply on this date.
