OBSERVATION Store revenue will go to a school activities account described as "not our account"

The business administrator described each school's activities account as one that "operates under us, but it's not our account" and "has nothing to do with our general fund" (0:09:04). The second half can be right: activity money need not run through the general fund. The first half is the loose part. Under GASB 84, activity is fiduciary only where the government does not control the assets; money a district employee approves and spends for a district program (here, a credit-bearing class's store, with expenses approved by the SRVRTC assistant director) is generally the district's own governmental activity. A board member had just asked whether such money is tracked, so the classification matters. Loose wording is not a GASB departure; the district's audited financial statements, which show how student activity funds are reported, would settle it.

Sources: GASB Statement 84 (fiduciary activities; control test), effective FY2021; N.H. Admin. Rules, Rev 1102.03 (NH districts follow GASB GAAP).

OBSERVATION A $20 million renovation figure produced in 24 hours, against a state funding line that "has $1 in it"

The architects told the board the state asked for a project number within 24 hours (0:47:57) and that the $14.2 million construction and roughly $20 million total figures are per-square-foot assumptions, "not really significantly cost estimated" (0:48:40); no phasing has been done (1:03:14). The $15 million state share assumes the former 75% split, while the state line "has $1 in it" (0:47:16). The presenters were candid about all of this. The point for readers is that the figure is a placeholder sent to the state, not an estimate the board adopted; any future bond article would need a real estimate and, for a local share, a warrant article voted by the district.

Sources: Dialogue/16477 SchoolBoard041625.mp4.CSV; approved minutes 4.16.25, item VI.1 ("it is a very loose estimate"). No rule is cited.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (enabling article; hearing and 7-day newspaper notice at $20,000; threshold as amended 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023).
