MEDIUM Nonpublic session entered without an exemption from RSA 91-A:3, II stated in the motion as heard, and no nonpublic minutes survive

The chair announced "a vote to enter nonpublic RSA 91, a two. See?" (0:48:32); the motion itself was "A motion to go to nonpublic." (0:48:44). RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II", followed by a roll call. RSA 91-A:2 contains no nonpublic exemptions, so if the chair said what the transcript shows, the stated ground was the wrong section; the transcription may also have dropped a word from RSA 91-A:3, II(c), which the attribution report assumes but the recording does not show. The roll call that the statute requires was taken (0:48:50). The board came back without announcing any decision (0:51:47), no vote to seal the minutes is heard, and under RSA 91-A:3, III nonpublic minutes are to be "publicly disclosed within 72 hours" unless withheld by a recorded two-thirds vote; none was located. MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the motion's wording rests on an uncertain transcription and the roll call was held.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II and III (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; 280:1)

OBSERVATION Food-service employees' questions on the privatization RFP go to rules the district keeps under any vendor

Two food-service workers asked who would pay for unpaid meals and for costs above the $650,000 line under a contractor (0:20:41, 0:25:22); the chair said the answers would be "in the RFP" and Adrian said that with one vendor "that goes to the parents" (0:29:39). Two federal rules bear on the answer whoever runs the kitchens. Under 7 CFR 210.16(a) a district that contracts with a food service management company must "Retain control of the quality, extent, and general nature of its food service, and the prices to be charged", and the contract runs no more than one year with up to four renewals, so the board's expectations (1:16:20) have to be written into the contract. And USDA's Q&A of March 23, 2017, eight days after this meeting, restated its 2016 memo SP 46-2016 requiring a written meal-charge policy and stated that unpaid-meal bad debt cannot be paid from the school food account: "these losses must be restored using non-Federal funds". So collection from families and absorption by the district are both possible, but unpaid debt cannot simply disappear into a vendor's budget. The page does not assess the RFP itself, which was not before the board.

Sources: 7 CFR 210.16(a), (d) (2017 annual edition); USDA FNS, SP 23-2017, Unpaid Meal Charges: Guidance and Q&A (Mar. 23, 2017)
