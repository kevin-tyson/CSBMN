Claremont School Board, October 4, 2023

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, Chair Heather Whitney presiding, Candace Crawford absent. The board holds a brief nonpublic session under the tuition-contract exemption and seals its minutes for 20 years; drops the scheduled budget-surplus vote; discusses how the FY2025 budget will be built and whether budget subcommittee meetings should be televised; votes one by one on the NHSBA's proposed resolutions; adopts policy GBE on second reading; and hears the Capital Improvement subcommittee's Stevens High School findings. MEDIUM Tuition-contract nonpublic minutes were sealed for 20 years with no statutory finding, though the exemption itself requires those minutes to be published before any contract is approved

The motion named the exemption, RSA 91-A:3, II(k) (0:02:36, transcribed "91-A colon 322 K"), and the roll calls to enter and leave were taken, which satisfies ¶I(b). Three points remain. First, the seal: ¶III permits withholding only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" that determines divulgence "likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism". The vote was unanimous among the six present, but no finding was stated on tape (0:03:48 to 0:04:09) and the minutes record only "a motion to seal the meeting minutes for 20 years". Second, II(k) carries its own disclosure rule: a negotiated tuition contract "shall be made public prior to its consideration for approval by a school district, together with minutes of all meetings held in nonpublic session". A 20-year seal on minutes the statute will require to be released before any contract is approved should at least say how the two fit. Third, ¶IV, in force from the day before this meeting, requires sealed minutes to be reviewed "no more than 10 years" after the vote to withhold them; a 20-year term does not reflect it. The chair's description of the session's scope, "rationales for item three and item six", matches no lettered exemption and no agenda item 6, and is left unexplained in the minutes. Neighbouring pages record the same pattern: a ten-year seal without a finding on September 20.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(k), III, IV (source note ends 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); minutes, item III; September 20, 2023 page, flag 2

MEDIUM The draft minutes first appear on the district's Drive on the seventh business day after the meeting

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting", a business day being "8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Monday through Friday, excluding national and state holidays". Counting from Thursday, October 5 and excluding Columbus Day (October 9, a federal holiday), the fifth business day ended at 5 p.m. on Thursday, October 12. The earliest copy of these minutes on the public Drive, draft CSB Meeting Minutes 10.4.23.docx.pdf, was created Monday, October 16, 2023 at 18:15 UTC, the seventh business day (the eighth if Columbus Day is counted); its file modification stamp is the same day, so it does not show an earlier version. The approved copy reached the Meeting Minutes share on October 17. Drive dates show when the public could see a document online, not whether a paper copy was available at the SAU office, which is why this is graded MEDIUM. OBSERVATION The FY2023 budget-surplus vote was pulled because the information was still being gathered

The agenda scheduled "Budget Surplus Allocation (Vote)", and the September 20 future-dates list had set "Vote on Budget Surplus ( if any)" for this meeting. The chair removed it: "we're continuing to gather information on that topic" (0:00:48). Two weeks earlier the business administrator had put the surplus at a little over $400,000 with the year not yet closed (September 20 page, flag 3). No rule sets a date for this vote; the observation is that by early October the board still had no closed FY2023 figure to act on.

Sources: agenda, item IV.2; September 20, 2023 minutes, future dates

OBSERVATION Stevens High School's building management system has been broken for three or four years, and its south classrooms overheat

Reporting the subcommittee's tour with maintenance director Steve Holt, Skillen said the building management system "broke 3 or 4 years ago and has never been properly replaced or repaired", forcing manual monitoring that "introduces a lot more chance for error" (0:52:54); that south-facing classrooms run hot enough that one teacher's paper window covering made a marked difference (0:45:29, 0:45:48); and that two roof sections and failed floors over improperly installed subfloor await replacement (0:45:29, 0:52:10). These match the principal's September 6 report of 90-degree classrooms. No rule was verified that the conditions breach; they are recorded as a capital-planning matter for the FY2025 budget the board was beginning that night.

Sources: minutes, item V.5(a); September 6, 2023 page, flag 5

POSITIVE The board voted openly on each NHSBA resolution, and agreed to look into televising its budget subcommittee

Instead of the delegate following NHSBA's recommendations, which Sprague said had been past practice (0:21:35), the board debated twelve proposed resolutions in public session and took eleven separate votes (one needed none), recorded with movers and seconders in the minutes, so residents can see how their delegate was instructed. In the same meeting the chair proposed having CCTV film the budget subcommittee's working sessions "just for transparency" (0:17:35), with no objection. Neither step is required by law.
