OBSERVATION Bus-recording policy amended to a 28-day minimum while its own text, and the statute, cap audio retention at ten school days

On third read the board amended the audio and video surveillance on school buses policy. Horsky proposed "a minimum of a 28 day recording cycle" so that a pattern of bullying could be shown (3:02:08, 3:03:32). Zullo then read the policy's existing sentence: "no event, however, shall the recording be retained for longer than ten school days unless the school district determines that the recording is relevant to a disciplinary proceeding" (3:03:12). The discussion ended with the superintendent's "All right. Minimum of 28 day reporting." (3:04:18) and a vote to adopt the policies with the amendments (3:04:44). RSA 570-A:2, II(k), as in force since January 1, 2017, permits a school bus operator to make "an audio recording in conjunction with a video recording" only if the school board authorizes it, and provides: "In no event, however, shall the recording be retained for longer than 10 school days unless the school district determines that the recording is relevant to a disciplinary proceeding, or a court orders that it be retained for a longer period of time." A blanket minimum of 28 days for audio, if that is what the adopted text says, would conflict with that limit; the ten-day limit in II(k) attaches to the audio recording, so video kept without audio is a separate question. The record of the next meeting shows the conflict was caught before it took effect: on April 4 the superintendent said the printed policy still carried the ten-day limit, that he had asked legal counsel whether the board could exceed the statute, and that the district had no cameras on its buses (see the April 4, 2018 meeting page). That is why this is graded an observation: the vote adopted language at odds with the statute, but nothing was recorded or kept under it. The board's other amendment, board access to recordings in closed session for appeals, raises no such issue.

Sources: RSA 570-A:2, II(k) (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 169:1, eff. June 3, 2016; 322:2, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION The proposed opt-out revision tracks the statute; the cost question went unanswered

The administration asked the board to change its health-education exemption policy so that an alternative for an opted-out student is agreed between district and parent "at the parents expense" (0:33:47, 0:34:21). RSA 186:11, IX-c, in the text in force since September 16, 2017, requires districts to adopt a policy allowing an exception to specific course material a parent finds objectionable, with "an alternative agreed upon by the school district and the parent, at the parent's expense", and "not less than 2 weeks advance notice of curriculum course material used for instruction of human" sexuality. The proposal therefore matches the statutory minimum; the existing policy, under which the school provides the alternative, goes beyond it and is not unlawful for doing so. The superintendent had no cost figure (0:45:11), members asked for one before deciding (0:49:20), and the item was held. The record also shows a parents' night and two electronic notices before the healthy relationships curriculum (0:45:37, 0:47:39); whether that curriculum is "human sexuality" material subject to the two-week notice is not settled on the recording, and the claims made in comment about what state law requires of the curriculum (0:59:50, 0:35:18) are the speakers' characterizations.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-c (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 251:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2017)
