Claremont School Board Finance Committee, December 18, 2023

A two-and-a-half-hour afternoon working session of the board's three-member Finance Committee on the FY2025 district budget, two days before the board sent a budget to public hearing. The administration brings revised budget sheets, the Claremont Middle School team presents a new schedule, the business administrator cannot allocate a $688,000 line carried in the current year's budget, and the recording ends as the chair says the members need to caucus. HIGH A public body met for two and a half hours on the district budget with no notice, agenda or minutes in any district share, two days before the board was told the minutes were online

The Finance Committee is a committee of the school board and so a public body: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) reaches any school district board "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof" (statute). Its three members are the committee's whole membership and they met, deliberated and set their next steps on the budget. RSA 91-A:2, II requires notice at least 24 hours ahead and minutes "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute), naming members, persons appearing, the subjects and decisions. MAP.md records no packet or minutes in either district share (checked 2026-08-28, including the next board meeting's folder and keyword searches), and a date search on 2026-10-02 found nothing either. On December 20 the board's approved minutes record, under this committee's report, "Subcommittee meetings and minutes are available online for people to review" (minutes' wording). On the December 20 recording (show 15483, not linked here) the board chair added: "There has been one brief meeting, that occurred this week, and the minutes of that meeting will be on the website." No such minutes have been found either. The recording is public, but a recording is not the minutes the statute requires. Graded HIGH under the project's rule for unmitigated absences: nothing on the recording or in any document explains where notice or minutes went, and the deadline passed years ago. This matches the grading of the committee's December 6 and December 13 sessions.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (committees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification; notice and five-business-day minutes); approved minutes, December 20, 2023, item IV.6(b)

MEDIUM The session was called a caucus, and the recording stops when the chair says the members need to caucus

Sprague opens by saying the committee is there to "have a caucus" (0:00:13) and closes with "I think we need to we need to caucus, I guess." followed by "Are we done with the admin?" (2:24:03); the recording ends seconds later. RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body" able to communicate contemporaneously (statute), and its caucus exclusion, I(c), covers only "A caucus consisting of elected members of a public body of the same political party who were elected on a partisan basis" (statute). School board members are elected on the non-partisan ballot RSA 671:30 requires, so no caucus exclusion is available to them. If the three members continued to deliberate after the administration left, that was a meeting of the committee, with the same notice and minutes duties as the televised part; nothing shows whether they did. The record has a hole at exactly this point: at 2:04:16 the chair says "we're looking at 1.783", and two days later he moved $36,313,407.97 to hearing (December 20 page). Read as $1.783 million over the FY24 operating appropriation of $34,880,312 used in the district's later budget documents, the televised figure implies about $36.66 million, roughly $350,000 above the motion. That gap is unexplained on the record; it is not evidence of what was decided off camera.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and I(c) (2023 codification); RSA 671:30 (non-partisan ballot for school district officers; 1979, 321:1); December 20, 2023 board page

MEDIUM A $688,000 line in the current budget that the business office cannot allocate, carried into the FY2025 comparison

The budget book's page 44 carries a district-wide line Sprague reads as "board voting, voted expense reduction" of about $688,000 (2:06:20). The business administrator says that she and Dave Jack, the interim business administrator before her, had the number when the budget was uploaded ("Dave told me about it"), that it "Had something to do with" the deliberative session's added million, and "I don't know what it's allocated to" (2:07:31). She has compared her predecessor's spreadsheets to the budget and "They don't match up" (2:08:52); the line is "the number that reconciled it" (2:10:39). Crawford had asked "a number of times" (0:07:22), and the guidance-line reconciliation at 0:28:10 shows the same problem at line level ("I don't know if it was supposed to be there or it wasn't."). Because the FY24 column is the comparison base for every FY2025 line, the increases the committee is trying to evaluate cannot be read line by line. RSA 32:5, IV requires the budget form to carry comparative columns of prior-year appropriations and expenditures; it does not require the lines to reconcile, so no breach is shown, but a comparison column the preparer cannot tie to the appropriation is a control weakness. Whitney's question about a forensic audit (2:11:35) is answered by the business administrator, not decided by the board. The budget report the board moved to hearing two days later carries the line as account 100.40.1100.113.5.00000, ($688,426.17) in the FY 23-24 column and $0.00 proposed, so its reversal supplies $688,426.17 of the $1,433,096.06 increase (December 20 page, flag 3). The committee returned to the figure on January 5, 2024 (see that page).

Sources: RSA 32:5, IV (2023 codification; comparative columns); December 20, 2023 board page, flag 3; Finance Committee, January 5, 2024 page

OBSERVATION Middle-school teacher salaries $85,000 over the current year's line, with positions still open

Henry volunteers that "in this year's budget so far we're $85,000 over budget and teacher salaries" at CMS (1:07:22) while science and math positions are unfilled, and offers higher hiring steps as a possible cause, "So I would have to go back to confirm that" (1:09:25). RSA 32:8 bars spending for a purpose with no appropriation, and RSA 32:10 lets the governing body transfer between lines within the total, so a line overrun is not by itself a breach. It is noted because the same session shows the line budgets are not reliable (flag 3) and the committee was setting a target from them.

Sources: RSA 32:8 (limitation on expenditures); RSA 32:10 (transfers)
