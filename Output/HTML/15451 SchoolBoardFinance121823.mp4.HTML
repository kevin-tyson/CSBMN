HIGH A public body sat for two hours and twenty-four minutes on the budget with no agenda, packet or minutes, and two days later approved board minutes said the minutes were online

Limb: unmitigated. All three members of a three-member committee were present throughout. No minutes exist in any district share, and nothing on the recording says minutes are being kept or are coming; the board clerk is neither present nor mentioned. A finance subcommittee is a public body in its own right: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) reaches any board or committee of a school district “or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto.” RSA 91-A:2, II (as amended by 2023, 188:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023) required minutes naming the members and persons appearing, describing the subject matter and final decisions, open to inspection within five business days. The committee took no vote, but it received the revised middle-school staffing model, the tax-rate estimate, the explanation of the $688,426.17 line and the refusal of a forensic audit, and decided not to be ready for Wednesday before reporting to the board on Wednesday. Without an attendance record this page cannot even supply first names for the two CMS staff introduced at 0:27:43.

The approved minutes of 20 December 2023 record, under the Finance Subcommittee item: “Subcommittee meetings and minutes are available online for people to review.” The meetings are online as video. The minutes are not. The 6 December minutes had put it accurately: “Sessions have been recorded on CCTV.” Whether 24-hour notice was posted cannot be established from the record either way; the date was announced on camera at the 1 and 13 December sessions.
