Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting, August 9, 2018

A quarterly joint meeting of the Claremont City Council and the Claremont School Board that tabled a shared public information officer position on a combined 7-6 roll call, set an August 25 forum for gubernatorial candidates, asked the mayor and chair to schedule a children's behavioral health training, and deferred the city's position on the mayors' letter urging an override of the governor's vetoes of SB 365 and SB 446. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares, and the city's records were not inspected. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

MEDIUM No district copy of the agenda, packet or minutes survives for this joint meeting

MAP.md records no Drive material: the district's shares hold no year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The recording shows that an agenda and a packet existed (the chair's "number six old business", 0:16:32; the mayor's "has anybody seen the packet", 0:17:28) and that minutes of joint meetings were kept: the bodies approve the June 21, 2018 joint minutes at 0:01:28, and the city clerk is heard recording this meeting (0:02:06).

RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force for all of 2018, required minutes that "shall include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", open to inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting". Paragraph II-b, in force from January 1, 2018, also required a body that maintains a website to post its approved minutes there or a notice of where they may be reviewed. The mover-and-seconder requirement did not yet apply (it took effect January 1, 2019). This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: minutes were kept, by the city clerk, and the city's files were not inspected. What is missing is any copy in the school district's shares.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II and II-b (2017 codification; the text in force for 2018)

OBSERVATION The PIO position was tabled on a single combined tally that the City Council side voted against

After Horsky's motion to table (0:50:46) drew objections on the voice vote (0:51:27), the clerk called the roll body by body and the result was announced as 7 to 6 (0:53:54, 0:54:22). The tally that settles it, from an unidentified speaker, is "two school boards that are. No." plus four council no votes, against "two yeses from your council to our five" (0:54:03): the school board voted 5-2 to table and the council 2-4 against. Counting took more than a minute and produced several wrong totals on tape (0:52:59 to 0:54:17). One school board vote is also mislabeled in the dialogue file: Pierce's Yes. (0:52:23) is attributed to the mayor.

A joint meeting is two public bodies, and the Claremont City Charter makes a majority of the nine-member council its quorum (sec. 21). No rule this page verified says how a joint session must count a vote, and tabling a discussion bound neither body to anything. For a reviewer: the combined count let the school board's majority table an item that a majority of the councilors present voted to keep, on a position the city manager had just described as mainly benefiting the school district (0:23:36). If the two bodies later share an employee, RSA 53-A:3 (agreements between public agencies, school districts included since 2016) is the statutory route, and its paragraph II lists what such an agreement "shall specify".

Sources: Claremont City Charter secs. 16 and 21 (nine councilors; majority is a quorum; copy of undetermined date); RSA 53-A:3 (joint or cooperative action; source note ends 2016, 46:2)

OBSERVATION The three-member school funding subcommittee planned to attend the candidate forum as a group

Settling on the August 25 forum, the chair said "if three of us and we happen to be the subcommittee, the three of us." (1:01:06), with the next row ("Funding committee. Okay.", labeled for the clerk) completing the name, and asked the other members to send questions for the three to put "as a unit" (0:59:19, 1:01:15). Benware urged sending "as many as we can" (1:01:27); the chair said the whole board need not attend (0:59:19).

RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof" a public body, and RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body" to discuss or act on matters within its jurisdiction. Three subcommittee members presenting the board's agreed questions on school funding would be a quorum of that subcommittee doing its business, which would need the 24-hour notice of RSA 91-A:2, II. A forum the public attends is not the same as a noticed meeting. Whether a notice was posted, and whether four or more board members attended, cannot be checked from this record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2017 codification; the text in force for 2018)

OBSERVATION The two bodies' views on the SB 365/446 letter were to be gathered by e-mailed feedback to the mayor

The mayor proposed to "continue sending you information on this, getting your feedback" and to settle the city's position "by our next council meeting on the 22nd" (1:26:36). Horsky's motion, passed by voice vote, put that in terms: information "presented to both the City Council and the school boards, with a feedback due back to City Council later than the 20th" (1:28:44, 1:29:42). The school board met once before that deadline, on August 15, and the letter does not come up on that recording (August 15, 2018 page).

RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings", and II says communications outside a meeting, "including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members", "shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". Individual replies to the mayor are not deliberation by themselves; a reviewer would want to know whether the replies circulated among members, and whether the school board ever took a position in a meeting. The decision itself was left to the council's public meeting of August 22.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4, unamended)

POSITIVE A citizen's comment was heard before the business it concerned, and the bodies acted on it

Citizens' comments were taken before any business (0:10:10 to 0:16:32). Cornelia Sergeant asked the mayor not to sign the veto-override letter on SB 365 and SB 446 without further study (0:10:44). When the item came up the mayor cited "some of the strong sentiments I'm receiving from members of our community" (1:25:05), and the joint session voted to gather information before deciding (1:28:44, 1:29:42). No public comment period was required of a school board in 2018: RSA 189:74's 30-minute comment requirement was enacted by 2022, 333:1, eff. September 6, 2022. Hearing the public before acting, and then responding to what was said, went beyond what the law required at the time.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (public comment; source note 2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022)
