Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — August 9, 2018

Joint meeting of two public bodies, the Claremont City Council and the Claremont School Board, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for it are posted in the school district's online shares, and the city clerk's records were not inspected. The bodies heard the city manager's report on a shared public information officer and tabled the idea by a pooled 7-6 roll call, set a gubernatorial candidate forum, asked the mayor and chair to schedule a children's behavioral health training, and deferred any city position on the vetoed energy bills SB 365 and SB 446 after a citizen urged caution. OBSERVATION The information officer item was tabled by pooling both bodies' votes, although the City Council voted against tabling

When the voice vote on Horsky's motion was contested, the clerk took a roll call (0:51:44) and, after a confused count (0:52:59 to 0:54:17), the tally was stated as the council's two yes votes plus the school board's five, against four council and two board no votes: "seven yeses" and six nos (0:54:03). The mayor declared the item "still tabled" (0:54:23). Counted by body, the School Board voted 5-2 to table and the City Council voted 4-2 not to. The City Council and the School Board are separate public bodies under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), each acting by its own members. No rule was verified for how a joint agenda item is to be decided, and tabling a discussion item committed neither body to anything, so this is recorded as an observation. The practical point is that the combined count reported a result that one of the two bodies, voting on its own, had rejected.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (source note ends 2008, 354:1, so in force in 2018): each "legislative body, governing body, board ... of any ... municipal corporation, school district" is a public body.

OBSERVATION Members of both bodies received the training materials on the afternoon of the meeting

Towle (1:07:49), Councilor Pope ("I got this at 145 this afternoon", 1:08:24) and Zullo (1:10:46) said they had not been able to read the New Futures training menu, and Towle closed the meeting by asking for materials in advance, noting the school board's own seven-day practice (1:32:46). RSA 91-A:2, II, as it read in 2018, required notice "of the time and place" at least 24 hours ahead and said nothing about distributing materials, so no rule was breached; the item was resolved by delegating the date to the two leaders. Recorded because late materials limited deliberation on the one item that needed a choice.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification, in force through 2018): 24-hour notice of time and place.
