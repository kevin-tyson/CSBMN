Claremont School Board, September 10, 2025

Board meeting called during the district's cash crisis, with Patrick O'Hearn presiding over the administration as temporary acting superintendent. The administration proposed closing Bluff Elementary in mid-October and making Disnard a K–2 school and Maple Avenue a grades 3–5 school; the principals of Bluff and Maple Avenue said a smaller plan of their own had been set aside; 24 people spoke against or about the proposal over about 64 minutes of public comment; and the board tabled the plan unanimously, then voted 4–1 to take up the principals' and administration's proposals on September 17. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript, the amended agenda, the two packet documents and the draft minutes. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 10, 2025, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The administration stated on the record that services written into students' IEPs cannot currently be delivered

This flag rests on the administration's own words, not on an outside finding. O'Hearn: "we cannot fulfill our educational obligations." (0:25:42); case managers with loads "in the 40s almost 50" make it "impossible to deliver those services that are in the IEPs" (0:41:02); at Bluff "We would need 25 paraprofessionals to meet, one on one IEP services that are currently in IEP." (0:47:58), restated as 24 district-wide, of whom 12 are in place (0:53:02, 1:18:09). Koski: the district must reform "the way IPS are written and services provided" (0:52:04). The project charter in the packet says "Severe staffing shortages prevent the District from meeting minimum standards." Federal rules require that special education be "provided in conformity with an individualized education program" (34 CFR 300.17(d)) and that services be "made available to the child in accordance with the child's IEP" (34 CFR 300.323(c)(2)); the State's minimum standards for school approval rest on RSA 186:8, II. Mitigation on the record: the Bluff principal says general education is being met (0:57:21), the administration says it is working with the Department of Education (0:34:57), and the board's tabling vote did not reject action. What this record does not show is any interim plan, adopted that night, for the services the administration says are not being delivered.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.17(d) (FAPE in conformity with the IEP); 34 CFR 300.323(c)(2) (services made available in accordance with the IEP); RSA 186:8, II (minimum standards for school approval); Project Charter.pdf, section 1

MEDIUM The draft minutes state no start time and do not name who produced them, both required since August 22, 2025

RSA 91-A:2, II, as amended by 2025, 112:1, effective August 22, 2025, requires minutes to include "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes." The draft minutes give an end time ("Consent adjournment at 9:18pm") but no start time beyond the agenda's printed 6:30 PM, and name no author; the board clerk is not heard on the recording and O'Hearn read the roll. The minutes do record the mover and seconder of both motions, as the separate sentence of the same paragraph requires, and the 4–1 tally, though not who voted no; the statute does not require that name on a show of hands. The board approved these minutes on September 17 with no amendment recorded, so the gaps carry into the approved record. Graded MEDIUM: a documentation gap, not a missing record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (source note ends 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025; minutes contents); 07. DRAFT CSB Meeting Minutes 9.10.25
