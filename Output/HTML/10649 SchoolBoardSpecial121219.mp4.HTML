Claremont School District, Special District Meeting, December 12, 2019

A single-session special district meeting held under RSA 40:16 to cure the posting defects in the November 21, 2019 vote that created a $650,000 Special Education Expendable Trust Fund. The voters ratified the November vote 70 to 12 on a standing count. MEDIUM No minutes, warrant or notice of this district meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this meeting. RSA 197:20 requires the school district clerk to “keep a true record of all the doings of each meeting”. For a meeting whose whole purpose was to repair a notice defect, the missing documents are the ones that would show whether the cure itself was properly noticed: the posted warrant and notice of this meeting, the date and places of posting (RSA 31:5-b, II(d) requires the cure to “comply with all statutory notice and procedural requirements for holding special town meetings”), the written ballot request if any, the clipboard of speakers' names and wards that the moderator introduced because the last meeting's names were lost (0:03:24), and the counters' tallies.

This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the school board approved the clerk's minutes of the November 21 special district meeting on December 4, so the clerk was keeping minutes of these meetings, and the result was announced on tape. Sources: RSA 197:20 (2019 codification; clerk keeps a true record of each meeting); RSA 31:5-b, II(d) (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION Future spending from the new trust fund requires its own public hearing

The article creates a Special Education Expendable Trust Fund “pursuant to RSA 198 20 C” (0:14:43). RSA 198:20-c, I lets a district create expendable trust funds and provides that expenditure “shall be made only for the purpose for which the trust fund was established”; ¶II adds that “In order to expend such funds, the school board shall hold a public hearing prior to the expenditure to be made”, with newspaper notice of the “time, place, and subject” at least seven days ahead. Tonight's vote establishes the fund and its $650,000; it does not authorize any particular program spending. Each draw for the autism program, the PRIDE classroom or the high school program needs a prior hearing. This is recorded so later pages can check for those hearings.

Sources: RSA 198:20-c, I-II (2019 codification; source note ends 2012, 219:2, 3, eff. July 1, 2012)

OBSERVATION The savings case rests on projections and a business plan that is not in the record

The superintendent projected about $270,000 avoided for three autism placements (0:08:00) and about $700,000 for the PRIDE program if its students would otherwise go out (0:09:07). Opponents said no business plan had been provided (0:19:36) and that an elementary alternative program had been placed in the main budget earlier in 2019 without a separate article (0:26:16); the superintendent said the business plan had been online for weeks (0:35:55). None of the plan, the program costs or the earlier budget line is in this record, and an unidentified board-side speaker conceded the savings may be absorbed by other increases rather than seen as a tax cut (0:45:07). No rule verified for this page requires a business plan for a trust-fund article, so this is an observation; the plan's posting date and the FY2020 budget detail would settle the dispute.

Sources: Dialogue/10649 SchoolBoardSpecial121219.mp4.CSV (rows at 0:07:36 to 0:10:53, 0:19:18 to 0:20:59, 0:25:47 to 0:27:17, 0:35:55)

OBSERVATION A voter's request for a written ballot was refused on a timing rule this page could not verify for a school district meeting

When a voter asked to vote by ballot, the moderator said the request had to be “in writing to me with five registered voters signatures prior to opening of the discussion” (0:21:37). The statute verified for this page, RSA 40:4-a, I, speaks of “any meeting of a town with a population of more than 500”, where 5 voters “present may make a request in writing prior to a vote by voice vote or division vote” that the vote be taken by secret written ballot. Whether RSA 40:4-a governs a school district's single-session cure meeting was not verified, and the request on tape was oral, so the outcome may be the same under either reading. It is recorded because the moderator's stated deadline (before discussion opens) is earlier than the town-meeting text (before the vote), and the November 21 meeting it ratified had voted by paper ballot.

Sources: RSA 40:4-a, I (2019 codification; source note ends 2006, 117:1; town meetings)

POSITIVE The cure was explained, timed and counted with care

RSA 31:5-b, II requires a cure meeting not less than 21 calendar days after the original vote (November 21 to December 12 is 21), a prior hearing (held December 4), and “the same as the majority as required for passage of the original article”; the 70-12 result clears any majority. Beyond that minimum, counsel explained the defect in plain terms and conceded it rather than arguing the website point (0:06:34); the moderator told voters twice exactly what a yes and a no vote would do (0:18:04, 0:45:59); she introduced a sign-in sheet so speakers' names would be captured after the last meeting's were lost (0:03:24); childcare was provided (0:00:53); and when a voice vote was questioned she moved straight to a standing count with two named counters and registration cards (0:46:51).

Sources: RSA 31:5-b, II(b), (c), (e) (2019 codification; source note 1988, 284:1; 1989, 287:1); RSA 40:16 (2019 codification)
