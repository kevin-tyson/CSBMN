Claremont School Board, October 3, 2018

Regular meeting preceded by a public hearing on the $15,000 Jack and Dorothy Byrne Foundation gift: the board accepted the gift, set 2019-20 high school tuition at $15,000, adopted the cell phone policy on third reading, approved write-offs of small and free or reduced lunch balances against a 2017-18 unpaid total of about $32,000, and chose Carolyn Towle by paper ballot for the superintendent search committee seat left by Steven Horsky's resignation. The business administrator reported the annual financial report due September 1 was not yet complete. HIGH The annual financial report due September 1 was still incomplete on October 3

The business administrator told the board "I do not have a complete" report (0:18:43), identified it as the DOE-25 filed with the Department of Education and the MS-25 filed with the Department of Revenue Administration (0:18:56), and said "The form is due by September 1st, but I don't know anybody who files it by September 1st." (0:19:14). RSA 21-J:34, V, in the codification in force in 2018, provides: "School districts shall submit financial reports on or before September 1 of each year." The paragraph contains no extension clause. On the business administrator's own account the district had missed that date by more than a month, and the board's review and signature were pushed to the following meeting (0:19:34). He offered context: the state had not released next year's adequacy grants, so "nobody can set their tax rate anyways" (0:19:34), and he described late filing as common. Neither changes the deadline. The DOE-25's own due date to the Department of Education is set by the department and was not verified for this page.

Sources: RSA 21-J:34, V (2017 codification; source note ends 2007, 182:2, eff. April 1, 2007)

HIGH A committee seat was filled by an unsigned paper ballot in open session

With Towle and Pierce both seeking the search committee seat left by Horsky, the members wrote their choices on paper, the chair collected them and the superintendent counted them (0:46:59), announcing only the result: "Carolyn has three votes." (0:48:00). Nothing on the tape shows members signing their ballots or announcing their votes, so the public cannot tell who voted for whom. RSA 91-A:2, II provides: "Except for town meetings, school district meetings, and elections, no vote while in open session may be taken by secret ballot." A school board meeting is none of the three. Two weeks earlier, when the chair proposed the same method, Zullo asked why the vote needed to be secret and the chair answered that it was about efficiency, not secrecy (September 19, 2018 page); the statute turns on how the vote is taken, not on its purpose. If the ballots were signed and the minutes record each member's choice, the defect would be largely cured, but no minutes survive to show it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; the text for all of 2018; the secret-ballot sentence predates 1992)

OBSERVATION Lunch debt: proposals to withhold diplomas or cut off a la carte meet a state limit, and written-off balances need non-federal money

RSA 189:11-a, VIII, in force since July 2, 2016, requires a school's meal payment policy to ensure that "no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures". Staff described exactly that constraint (0:51:41, 1:02:07). The chair then proposed putting diploma withholding in policy (1:08:43) and shutting students with balances off a la carte items (1:09:45), which the business administrator said could be done (1:09:47). Diplomas are outside the cafeteria language, and the statute does not address them; an a la carte cutoff for students with balances is a cafeteria procedure applied to some students and not others, which is the treatment paragraph VIII addresses. Neither proposal was adopted; the policy went to the October 14 retreat. Separately, the board authorized writing off small and free or reduced status balances (1:12:02, 1:14:52). USDA's guidance on unpaid meal charges states that nonprofit school food service account resources "may not be used to cover costs related to the bad debt" and that "these losses must be restored using non-Federal funds". The record does not say which fund will absorb the write-offs.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2017 codification; added by 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016); USDA FNS memo SP 23-2017, Unpaid Meal Charges guidance (March 23, 2017)
