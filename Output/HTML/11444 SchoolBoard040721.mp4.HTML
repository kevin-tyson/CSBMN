HIGH A $451,832 federal grant was given its required public hearing without the required notice

RSA 198:20-b, III(a), as in force in 2021, provides: "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken. Notice of the time, place, and subject of such hearing shall be published in a newspaper of general circulation in the relevant municipality at least 7 days before the meeting is held." The hearing that opened this meeting had been noticed for one item, the $11,160.92 donation ("the other item that we posted the meeting for", 0:02:52; "the original item on the public hearing", 0:04:18). The superintendent asked on the night to add the technology grant because "we need to show the records that the board is, is in on that" (0:02:23), and the chair then took comment on both and closed the hearing (0:04:40) before the board accepted $451,832 (0:57:41). A subject added at the hearing itself cannot have been in a notice published seven days earlier. The administration's own words treat the grant as within the rule ("obviously that's over that threshold as well", 0:02:52). Mitigation stated on the record: the superintendent reported that "Legal counsel has met up and has okayed it." (0:03:55), offered to re-post for the next meeting if the board was uncomfortable (0:03:26), no member of the public asked to speak, and the district's 15 percent match was described as already budgeted, which bears on ¶IV(a)'s bar on requiring other district funds beyond those "lawfully appropriated for the same purpose". The basis of counsel's view is not on the record. A notice or a 2020-21 enabling article could change this reading; neither survives in the district's shares.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I, III(a) and IV(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, eff. Aug. 29, 2005; in force until 2023, 38:1)

OBSERVATION The grant was in use before the board accepted it

On March 17 the superintendent told the board that SAU 6 had received the grant and that the district had already been installing the same boards under its technology plan (see the March 17, 2021 page). Tonight he said the money is being used for "the same boards that Jeff and his crew have already started", "We're just finishing the job with this" (0:57:02, 0:57:28), and said "it really is just accepting that, getting it in the minutes" (0:57:28). RSA 198:20-b, I lets the board "apply for, accept and expend" such money, and ¶III(a) makes the hearing "prior" to "the action to be taken". The record does not show that any grant money was spent before tonight's acceptance; boards bought with district funds before the award would raise no issue. This is an observation because the timing of the first grant-funded purchase is not on the record; the grant award and the district's purchase orders would settle it.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION The superintendent stated a "$10,000 threshold" for the hearing rule; the statute's was $5,000

The donation was described as "the $11,000 over the $10,000 threshold" (0:02:52). RSA 198:20-b, III(a) set the prior-hearing threshold at $5,000, and ¶III(b) allows a board to set a lower notice amount only: "A school board may establish the amount of unanticipated funds required for notice under this subparagraph, provided such amount is less than $5,000." The misstatement had no effect tonight, since $11,160.92 exceeds both figures and the donation was heard. It would matter for a gift between $5,000 and $10,000, which on this understanding would have gone to a vote without a hearing. Whether the figure came from a district policy is not on the record.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) and III(b) (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION About $3.7 million in CARES/ESSER money is described as received, with no acceptance step on this record

The business administrator described a first allocation of about $760,000 and "approximately $2.9 million" more, on a reimbursement basis (0:23:25, 0:27:32), and the superintendent projected "about $10 million over the next 2 or 3 years" (0:30:47), money "not not in the budget" (0:19:18). Money of this kind, federal funds passed through the state during the fiscal year, is the "unanticipated money from a state, federal or other governmental unit" that RSA 198:20-b, I addresses, with a prior hearing at $5,000 or more under ¶III(a). This recording shows no hearing or acceptance for either allocation. They may have been accepted at meetings before this one, and no minutes survive to check; that is why this is an observation. The planning discussion itself, including the proposal to reconvene the capital committee and to keep the district's expendable trust intact (0:25:06), was held in public.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2019 codification)

POSITIVE The board followed up on the needle exchange question within one meeting and closed an open-ended exception

On March 17 Horsky asked whether the program's promised quarterly updates had ever arrived and was told they had not. Three weeks later the assistant superintendent reported that the program had left the hospital site on March 24, 2020, put its quarterly reporting forms before the board, and said those forms covered Sullivan and Grafton counties rather than Claremont (0:58:19 to 0:59:09). Horsky said the earlier approval "allowed it pretty much kind of open ended" (1:00:21), and the board revoked "the current permissions to run a needle exchange program at Valley Regional Hospital until it's represented to the board" (1:01:40, 1:01:52). Under RSA 318-B:45 a syringe service program may operate in a drug-free school zone only by an exception "granted by the applicable district school board", so the exception was the board's to grant and to withdraw. The follow-up, the documents and a recorded decision exceed what the statute requires, which sets no reporting or review duty at all.

Sources: RSA 318-B:45 (2018 codification; exception sentence added by 2018, 254:2, eff. Aug. 11, 2018)
