Claremont School Board — March 1, 2017

Summary of a regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, the last before the March 14, 2017 district election, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The board approved two student trips, heard the district's first midyear i-Ready growth data from every school, reviewed a projected tax-rate sheet and agreed to publicize it before the vote, accepted the resignation of the Bluff Elementary principal, passed three policies on first reading and penciled in a joint meeting with the City Council. MEDIUM The district's Strategic Planning Committee was meeting publicly but without posted notice

The superintendent announced a Saturday meeting of the "Claremont School District Strategic Planning Committee" (1:57:42). The chair asked for a public posting (1:58:25); the superintendent answered that it is "a public meeting" but "it's not like a school board meeting. We haven't we haven't posted it", adding that anyone who arrives can watch (1:58:37, 1:58:47). The assistant superintendent described open volunteer membership and a facilitator (1:59:20), and the March 15 recording describes a school board representative among the members. The law then in force treats as a public body "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body" of a school board "or advisory committee thereto", and requires notice of each meeting at least 24 hours ahead in two appropriate places, with minutes. If this committee advises the board, as its planned presentation to the board suggests (1:57:42), its meetings needed notice and minutes. Graded MEDIUM: the recording shows meetings not posted, on the superintendent's own statement, but whether the committee is the board's advisory committee or the superintendent's is not settled on tape. In the district's favor: the chair himself asked that the meeting be posted.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (source note ends 2008, 354:1, so the text in force in 2017): public body includes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto"; RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 29:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017): notice "at least 24 hours, excluding Sundays and legal holidays", in 2 appropriate places; minutes within 5 business days.

OBSERVATION The board asked for the tax-rate sheet to go to the press and every parent before the March 14 vote, with no editorializing

The business administrator's sheet projected a lower tax rate if the budget passed (1:44:21, 1:44:47). Members asked that it be published in the newspapers, possibly as a paid full-page ad "a couple days before" the vote, posted online, and sent "to every parent. Here it is. This is what you're voting on." (1:46:24, 1:48:14, 1:49:58); the superintendent said it could go out the next day (1:50:44). Irish specified "I don't want any editorializing" (1:47:21). New Hampshire law barred public employees from electioneering in the performance of their duties and from using government property to do so, defining electioneering as acting "in any way specifically designed to influence the vote of a voter on any question or office". Distributing a factual, district-prepared projection is not by itself electioneering, and the instruction against editorializing points the right way; how the sheet was worded and sent is not on the record. Recorded as a reviewer's aid for anyone examining the pre-election mailing, not as a finding.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I-III (source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017, so the text in force in March 2017): no public employee "shall electioneer while in the performance of his or her official duties"; no use of government property; definition of electioneering.
