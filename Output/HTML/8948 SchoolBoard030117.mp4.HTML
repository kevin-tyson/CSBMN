MEDIUM Strategic planning committee meetings held without posted notice, by the superintendent's own account

The superintendent said the Claremont School District Strategic Planning Committee would meet that Saturday from 8 to 12 and would "make a presentation to the school board" (1:57:42). Asked by the chair to post it, he answered that it is a public meeting but "We haven't we haven't posted it because, it it's, it's, it's unlike a board meeting" (1:58:47). Two board members take part (the assistant superintendent says "they're part of it as well" of Adrian and Pierce, 2:00:42). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes a public body of any committee of a school district "or advisory committee thereto", and ¶I defines an advisory committee as a body "whose primary purpose is to consider an issue or issues designated by the appointing authority so as to provide such authority with advice or recommendations concerning the formulation of any public policy". RSA 91-A:2, II then requires notice of each meeting at least 24 hours ahead. A committee formed to draft a multi-year plan for the board fits that description on its face. The page grades this MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the record does not show who appointed the committee or on what terms, and the meetings were open to anyone who arrived; the chair's request to post was the right instinct.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, I and VI(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification)

OBSERVATION Plan to send the tax-rate sheet to every parent before the March 14 vote, with members insisting it stay factual

Members asked that the revised tax-rate sheet go in the newspapers, possibly as a paid full-page ad, and "to every parent" through the district's own channels, and the superintendent said it "Can be sent out tomorrow." (1:49:58, 1:50:44). From January 1, 2017, RSA 659:44-a barred a public employee from electioneering "while in the performance of his or her official duties" or with government property, and defines electioneering as acting "in any way specifically designed to influence the vote of a voter on any question". Irish said repeatedly that the sheet should carry no editorializing (1:47:21), which keeps it on the factual side of that line; Adrian's framing that people should know "you can you can approve the whole package" (1:50:08) is the kind of gloss that would not belong in a district mailing. No vote was taken on the mailing or on spending for an ad. Whether the statute reaches a school board's own decision to circulate is a question for counsel; board members, as elected officials, are outside RSA 273-A:1, IX's definition of public employee. Whatever went out is the document that would settle it.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I to III (source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 273-A:1, IX (2019 codification; source ends 2014, 13:1, 2): elected officials excluded

POSITIVE Unflattering growth data presented in public, school by school, with a correction of an earlier presentation

The assistant superintendent and all five principals presented mid-year growth results that mostly fell short of the district's own targets (0:39:10, 0:40:12, 0:42:49), explained what each building is doing about them, and took members' questions for over an hour. The assistant superintendent told the board that her first presentation had counted students up to a year behind as on grade level and that the data would be shown the same way from now on (0:50:19). The chair contrasted this with a time when "I had to file 91 A requests to get information" (1:42:06). No law requires a board to receive interim assessment data at all, let alone with a correction volunteered; this is practice above the minimum.
