HIGH A $13,320 donation and a $100,000 grant were accepted the night they were added to the agenda, with no prior public hearing in the record

At the start of the meeting the superintendent added to the action items “acceptance of a donation” and the “Acceptance of three grants” (0:01:05). Later that evening the board accepted, by voice votes, a donation from the Jack and Dorothy Byrne Foundation of $13,320 for the early childhood sensory room (2:12:19, vote 2:13:04) and three grants: a New Hampshire Intel Mathematics professional learning grant of Title II funds, which the assistant superintendent believed to be $100,000, and two robotics grants of $1,250 and $2,250 (2:17:29, 2:21:03, vote 2:25:32). The Intel award was known at least six days earlier: at the SAU 6 board on October 12 the assistant superintendent said it would be coming to the Claremont board soon (see the October 12, 2017 page).

RSA 198:20-b, as in force in 2017, lets a district authorize its board to accept unanticipated money from a governmental unit or a private source, and provides that for unanticipated funds of $5,000 or more the board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken, with notice in a newspaper at least seven days before; for amounts under $5,000 the board must post notice of the funds in the agenda and record them in the minutes. The donation and the Intel grant are each over $5,000. Nothing on the recording refers to a hearing or to a notice of one, and the items were added to the agenda only that night. The robotics grants, under $5,000, were likewise not on the agenda as printed.

This is graded HIGH because the binding rule is clear and the record points to it not being followed. Two things could change that and cannot be checked here: whether a hearing was held at the earlier meeting the chair mentions at the start (0:00:18), and whether the district had adopted the enabling article that RSA 198:20-b, I requires (without it, the board could not accept such funds on its own authority at all). The minutes, which are not available, would show both.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2017 codification; prior public hearing for unanticipated funds of $5,000 or more); Dialogue/9385 SchoolBoard101817.mp4.CSV (rows 6 to 8, 865 to 935)

OBSERVATION The board retained $197,765 of year-end fund balance; the record does not show the cap it must stay under

The board voted to use $326,329 of $524,094 in unassigned fund balance to reduce the tax rate and to retain $197,765 (2:05:57). O'Neill explained that “Ever since 2012, we're allowed to carry over some money.” RSA 198:4-b, II, in the text in force in 2017, allows a district's legislative body to authorize retention of year-end unassigned general funds not to exceed 2.5 percent of the current fiscal year's net assessment, for emergency expenditures and overexpenditures or to reduce the tax rate; the 5 percent ceiling and the hearing requirement came only with 2020, 38:25. The authorization O'Neill describes would be the voters' 2012 vote, which is not in the record, and the district's net assessment is not stated on the tape, so this page cannot check the $197,765 against the 2.5 percent ceiling. The prior year's retained $388,714 is the figure that most needs that check. O'Neill's own figure for the amount carried forward, about $194,000 (2:05:14), differs slightly from the motion; the motion's numbers add up ($326,329 plus $197,765 is $524,094).

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2017 codification; retention up to 2.5 percent of net assessment); Dialogue/9385 SchoolBoard101817.mp4.CSV (rows 703 to 790)

OBSERVATION The superintendent said the board would choose the new member in nonpublic session

Describing the process to fill Patrick Adrian's seat, the superintendent said that after the candidates introduce themselves on November 1 “the board will go into a nonpublic session and elect who that next man or woman will be” (0:57:14). RSA 671:33, II(a) requires the board to fill the vacancy, the appointee serving until the next district election. RSA 91-A:3 allows a nonpublic session only for a ground listed in paragraph II, stated in the motion, and confines the discussion to it. The grounds include the hiring of any person as a public employee, and matters likely to affect adversely the reputation of a person other than a member of the body; choosing a member of the elected board fits neither cleanly. This is a statement of intent, not something done tonight; the November 1 recording would show what the board actually did and under which ground.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (2017 codification; the board fills vacancies); RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(b), II(c) (2016 codification; text in force for 2017)
