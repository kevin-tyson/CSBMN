OBSERVATION The teachers' contract costs go to voters only inside the operating budget, not as a separate warrant article

Seaman told the board the agreement's cost, "just over $1 million" in year one and about $572,000 more in year two (0:08:06, 0:08:44), would not be a separate warrant article: "approving the budget approves the contract, not approving the budget and ending with a default budget does not approve the contract." (0:08:54). He contrasted it with last year's four other agreements, which were separate articles. RSA 273-A:3, II(b) requires that "Only cost items shall be submitted to the legislative body of the public employer for approval at the next annual meeting", and RSA 273-A:1, IV defines a cost item as "any benefit acquired through collective bargaining whose implementation requires an appropriation". Routing the cost items through the operating budget does submit them to the voters, but in a form where they cannot vote on the contract alone, and a vote for the operating budget becomes a vote on the contract. This page has not verified a rule that requires a separate article, so this is an observation. The March 2023 warrant and the budget's labor lines would show how the cost items were presented.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (cost items submitted to the legislative body at the next annual meeting; 2013, 244:1); RSA 273-A:1, IV (cost item defined; 2014, 13:1)

OBSERVATION The agenda and minutes misdescribe the contract the board ratified

The agenda item reads "Discussion/Ratification of the SREA 2024-2025 contract." and the minutes record a motion "to accept the contact between the Claremont School Board and the Sugar River Educator Association". The tentative agreement in the packet is between "The Claremont School Board and the Sugar River Education Association" and runs "from July 1, 2020 2023" [2020 struck, 2023 inserted] to June 30, 2025, two contract years, 2023-24 and 2024-25. The motion on the recording names only "the Education Association" (0:13:20). The errors do not change what was ratified, since the packet carries the document, but a reader relying on the agenda or minutes would take the contract to cover a different period.

Sources: 1.9.23 CSB Agenda; draft 1.9.23 CSB Meeting Minutes; Tentative SREA Agreement 12.22.22 (Article XVIII)

OBSERVATION The agreement promises ESSER-funded stipends that nobody mentioned before the vote

Appendix A of the tentative agreement adds: "All employees shall receive a $1000 ESSER Stipend on the first payroll in September of the 2023-2024 contract year and $500 on the first payroll in September of the 2024-2025 contract year", prorated for part-time staff. ESSER is temporary federal pandemic relief money. Neither the presentation nor the board discussion (0:02:54 to 0:13:06) mentions the stipends, their total, or which ESSER grant would pay them, and Seaman's cost figures are given only as salary increases and benefit loading. Federal formula funds remain available for obligation only through a fixed period (one extra year under the Tydings Amendment), so a September 2024 payment depends on the grant's remaining period; the record does not identify the grant, so no finding is made.

Sources: Tentative SREA Agreement 12.22.22 (Appendix A); 20 U.S.C. §1225(b) (federal education funds available for obligation one additional fiscal year)

POSITIVE The board published the agreement and its evidence, and asked the public for comment before voting

The packet carries the full tentative agreement and a two-page analysis of teacher departures from 2005 to 2022 with salary comparisons to six neighboring districts, and Seaman walked through it on the record (0:03:24 to 0:05:58). Besides the citizens' comment period (0:01:39), the chair asked for public comment on the contract item itself before the vote (0:13:06). Publishing the evidence behind a negotiated cost of about $1.6 million over two years, and inviting comment on it, exceeds what RSA 91-A requires.

Sources: Additional Information 1.9.23.pdf; Tentative SREA Agreement 12.22.22 (PDF)
