Claremont School Board, September 2, 2020

Regular meeting on the eve of the hybrid reopening, held in person with two members on Google Meet: the FY20 surplus (about $1 million to $1.1 million), summer building projects, the superintendent's opening report, a long debate on moving to full in-person school sooner under new state guidance, two citizens' comments, a roll-call vote keeping the 48-hour community service graduation requirement, and the NHSBA delegate. RSA 91-A:2, II, as in force from January 1, 2019, required minutes naming the members and persons appearing, briefly describing the subjects and final decisions, and naming the mover and seconder of each motion, open to inspection within five business days.

MEDIUM Seven of the meeting's eight votes taken by voice while two members took part remotely

Lovett and Towle joined on Google Meet. The agenda (0:02:15), the August 2 minutes (0:04:13), the tabling of the August 5 minutes (0:04:51), both donations (0:10:11, 0:10:24), the NHSBA delegate slate (3:00:18) and adjournment (3:02:41) were all called as "all those in favor" voice votes. RSA 91-A:2, III(e) required every vote at a meeting with remote participation to be a roll-call vote. Emergency Order #12 relaxed only the physical-quorum requirement of III(b), and the chair named only two members as remote, so a quorum was evidently in the room.

Mitigation on the record: the chair did call a roll for the community service motion because two members were remote (2:48:31), and twice said aloud how the remote members voted (3:00:34, 3:02:46). Every voice vote appears unanimous, so no outcome is in doubt; the defect is that the record does not show each remote member's vote on five of the seven voice votes. MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the board observed the rule on its one substantive policy vote and nothing on tape suggests a contested result.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(b) and (e) (2019 codification); Emergency Order #12 (March 23, 2020)

For unanticipated funds under $5,000, RSA 198:20-b, III(b) (as in force in 2020) directed the board to post notice of the funds in the agenda and include notice in the minutes, with acceptance in public session of a regular meeting. The acceptance was public and on a regular meeting, which satisfies the last part; the agenda notice is the gap, since the gifts reached the agenda only by amendment. The section also conditions the board's authority on a district warrant article adopting RSA 198:20-b; no surviving record here shows that article, and a reader should check the district's annual meeting results.

OBSERVATION The chair's account of why the board cannot answer group emails

Of answering a group email, the chair said “That's that's an unwanted meeting.” (the transcript's wording; elsewhere it renders the phrase as unworn and non warrant) and later told James Ward “if four of us respond to either an email or a Facebook post, that's actually a quorum” (1:59:52). The statutes point the same way but less absolutely. RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as the convening of a quorum to discuss or act on the board's business, and RSA 91-A:2-a, II bars communications outside a meeting, including sequential ones, used to circumvent the chapter. Neither makes an individual member's reply unlawful; the risk is a quorum deliberating by email. The chair's practical rule (one designated reply, others copied) is a reasonable way to stay inside the law, and his advice is reported as coming from NHSBA counsel.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2019 codification); RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2008, 303:4, unamended)

OBSERVATION Year-end fund retention described as a board vote, 25 days before the law changed

Asked about the FY20 surplus, Seaman said “The only thing that we really have available to us is to retain the 2.5%, which is voted on by the board.” (0:13:26) The 2.5 percent figure was right for that date. Under RSA 198:4-b, II as it read on September 2, 2020, the district's legislative body (the voters) could authorize retention of year-end unassigned funds up to 2.5 percent of the net assessment, with no hearing requirement. 2020 N.H. Laws 38:25, effective September 27, 2020, raised the cap to 5 percent and added a public hearing before retained funds are spent.

The board acted under the new text five weeks later (see the October 7, 2020 page). Whether Claremont's voters had adopted the authorizing article, and in what terms, is not in any record this page could reach; that warrant article is the document that would settle how much the board itself decides.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2019 codification; 2.5 percent, legislative-body authorization); RSA 198:4-b, II (2020 codification; 2020, 38:25, eff. Sept. 27, 2020)
