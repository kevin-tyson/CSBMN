Claremont School Board, May 18, 2022

Regular meeting with no recorded votes beyond the agenda and adjournment. OBSERVATION A $500 private gift was acknowledged without an acceptance vote

The chair said that “because of the dollar amount, we do not need to vote to approve this” (1:58:31). RSA 198:20-b lets a school board accept unanticipated money from a private source only where the district has adopted the enabling article (¶I). For gifts under $5,000, ¶III(b) requires the board to "post notice of the funds in the agenda" and "include notice in the minutes", and says the acceptance "shall be made in public session of any regular school board meeting" (statute's words). The acknowledgment did take place in public session at a regular meeting, and the item was on the agenda by the chair's numbering (1:57:27). Whether the district has the enabling article, whether the posted agenda named the gift, and whether minutes record it cannot be checked, because no agenda or minutes were found (MEDIUM). The statute does not use the word vote, so this is an observation, not a finding.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(b) (2022 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION A food-service surplus of about $300,000 must stay in the food program, which limits the trust idea

The business administrator estimated a food-service surplus “of about $300,000” for the year (2:07:10) and said it “needs to be spent on food related, something”, such as kitchen equipment or a middle-school serving line (2:07:34, 2:08:08). That matches 7 CFR 210.14(a): school food authority revenue may be used only for the operation or improvement of the nonprofit food service; ¶(b) limits net cash resources to three months' average expenditures. The chair asked about putting the money in a trust to cover a future deficit (2:08:48), and the business administrator called that “another alternative”, including to “pay off any deficits” (2:09:10). Moving program money into a district trust outside the food-service account would raise the 210.14(a) question, and USDA guidance (SP 23-2017) says nonprofit food-service resources may not cover unpaid-meal bad debt, which must be restored from non-federal funds. No decision was taken; the June vendor and surplus discussion would settle it.

Sources: 7 CFR 210.14(a)-(b), 2022 edition (revenue used only for the program; three-month net cash limit); USDA FNS SP 23-2017 (Mar. 23, 2017), unpaid meal charges guidance (guidance, not regulation)

OBSERVATION The special education savings figure the board received counted two tuition students twice

The written summary gave “around 623,000” in savings or cost avoidance from three in-district programs (1:45:45). Before the meeting the director found that two tuition students from Fall Mountain were in both the cost-avoidance count and the revenue, and gave a corrected “around 585,000” not shown on the sheet (1:46:18, 1:46:45). The estimate also rests on assumptions stated aloud: every one of 17 students would otherwise be placed out of district (1:44:55), and state special education aid would come in at 68% (1:45:21). Catastrophic aid under RSA 186-C:18, III is prorated when the appropriation falls short, with no floor in the 2022 text, so the percentage changes year to year. No vote turned on the figure. The correction was made in public, but the board's copy still carries the overstated number.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2022 codification; proportional proration, no floor; source note ends 2021, 209:2)
