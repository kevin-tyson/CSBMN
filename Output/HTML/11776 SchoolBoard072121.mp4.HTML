Claremont School Board — July 21, 2021

Regular meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. A parent asked the board to let Wethersfield, Vermont seventh and eighth graders play Claremont Middle School tackle football; Assistant Superintendent Donna Magoon said goodbye; teachers presented the Fish Tank knowledge-based reading pilot, and a motion to suspend it was moved, seconded and withdrawn in favor of a directive to communicate with the public; the board deferred fall masking to August and heard concerns about the new state budget and Education Freedom Accounts. MEDIUM Votes were taken by voice while a member participated remotely, although the chair raised the roll-call question

Rob Lovett Jr. took part from outside the room (the chair at 0:00:39: "Sorry, Rob. We just did the pledge."; at 1:30:32 the chair checks whether the room can hear him). The Governor's Emergency Order #12, which had relaxed in-person requirements for public bodies, lasted only "for the duration of the State of Emergency declared in Executive Order 2020-04", and that state of emergency lapsed at midnight on June 11, 2021. This meeting therefore fell under the ordinary rule for remote participation, RSA 91-A:2, III, which in July 2021 read: "A member participating in a meeting by the means described in this paragraph is deemed to be present at the meeting for purposes of voting. All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote." Every vote on the recording was by voice: the agenda at 0:01:49 ("All those in favor say I"), the June 2 minutes at 0:02:49, and the closing motion at 2:39:25. The chair himself asked at 0:02:06, "Should we be doing roll call on all because it's.", and the question was not taken up.

Mitigation: all three votes appear unanimous, the chair noted the remote member's assent ("Rob is nodding"), a quorum was physically present as ¶III(b) requires, and no motion that carried consequences for money or policy was voted. The same paragraph also requires the reason in-person attendance was "not reasonably practical" to be "stated in the minutes of the meeting"; no reason is given on the recording, and the minutes are not available to check. Graded MEDIUM as a probable process gap rather than HIGH because the outcome of no vote is in doubt.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a), (b), (e) (2019 codification; unamended until 2023, 188:1): remote attendance only when in-person is not reasonably practical, reason in the minutes, roll-call votes; Emergency Order #12 pursuant to Executive Order 2020-04 (March 23, 2020): effective for the duration of the state of emergency; NHPR, June 10, 2021 (reporting): state of emergency to expire at midnight Friday, June 11, 2021.

OBSERVATION Three members met with administration on the Fish Tank concerns before the meeting

The chair explained at 0:36:06 that he, Heather Whitney and Michael Petrin met with administration, received presentations from the curriculum coordinator and the assistant superintendent, and "followed up with some conversations of our own" before deciding "we really shouldn't be having these conversations without the rest of the board." Three of seven is not a quorum, and RSA 91-A:2, I defines a "meeting" as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body". A separate question is whether a group of members convened by the chair on a board matter is a "committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body" of the board under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), which would carry notice and minutes duties of its own; the recording does not show that the board created such a group, and it was not described as a committee. The chair's choice to disclose the sessions and bring the matter to the full board in open session is to his credit (see flag 7).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2019 codification): a meeting is the convening of a quorum; RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification): a public body includes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto".

OBSERVATION Statements about Education Freedom Accounts differ from the statute as enacted

The chair said at 2:19:58 that under the new program families "get the state money and the local money. So now they get a check, they get 20. I believe they do. They get $20,000." The law enacted in the 2021 budget, RSA 194-F:2, directs the commissioner to transfer to the scholarship organization "the per pupil adequate education grant amount under RSA 198:40-a, plus any differentiated aid that would have been provided to a public school for that eligible student". That is state adequacy aid, not local spending. Whitney said at 2:31:03 the income limit was "something like $43,000 per family" and added that she "could be wrong with the number"; the statute as enacted set eligibility at household income "less than or equal to 300 percent of the federal poverty guidelines", a figure that varies with household size (raised to 350 percent in 2023). The chair's further statement at 2:30:04 that eligibility turns on a school being "at the 40th percentile or lower" on standardized tests does not appear in the statute's definition of an eligible student, which rests on residency, eligibility to enroll in a public school and income. RSA 194-F took effect August 24, 2021, a month after this meeting.

These were members' good-faith attempts to explain a law signed weeks earlier, offered for budget planning; the business administrator said he was not aware of the income limit. The concern is only that the public record of the meeting carries figures the statute does not support.

Sources: RSA 194-F:2 (2023 codification; source 2021, 91:431, eff. Aug. 24, 2021, unamended): grant equals the RSA 198:40-a adequacy amount plus differentiated aid; RSA 194-F:1 (source 2021, 91:431; 2023, 110:1): eligible-student definition, 350 percent after the 2023 amendment; 2023 HB 515 as introduced, reciting the pre-amendment text "300 percent of the federal poverty guidelines".
