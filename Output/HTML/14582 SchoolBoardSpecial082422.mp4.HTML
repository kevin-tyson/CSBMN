Claremont School Board, Special Meeting — August 24, 2022

A forty-minute meeting run by the vice chair in the chair's absence. The board approved the Stevens High School, Claremont Middle School and elementary handbooks after a long exchange on restorative justice, chose its New Hampshire School Boards Association delegate and alternate, and assigned members to six committees. The business administrator gave a short audit and grant update. Built from the recording, the posted agenda and the approved minutes. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION The minutes name the vice chair as both mover and seconder of adjournment, and reached the public share after they had been approved

At 0:39:45 the presiding vice chair moved to adjourn, and the word second follows in the same transcript line with no separate speaker. The minutes read: "A motion made to adjourn the meeting by Heather Whitney, seconded by Heather Whitney". RSA 91-A:2, II (in this form since 2018, 244:1, eff. January 1, 2019) requires that "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion shall be recorded in the minutes." A member cannot second her own motion, so the seconder is in effect unrecorded. Every other motion in these minutes names a mover and a seconder, including two the tape leaves unidentified; this is recorded as an observation because the motion was procedural and uncontested.

The only copy located is a Google Doc in the September 7 packet folder, created there on September 14, 2022: the fourteenth business day after the meeting (Labor Day excluded), and a week after the board approved it. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". A Drive created time dates when the district put the document in its public share, not when it was available at the SAU office, so this is an observation about online availability, not a finding that the deadline was missed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification: movers and seconders; five business days); RSA 288:1 (Labor Day a legal holiday); CSB minutes 8.24.22.

OBSERVATION The board staffed six committees and an ad hoc committee; each is a public body in its own right, whatever the quorum of the full board

From 0:26:21 the board named members and co-chairs for the Capital Improvement, Budget, Policy, Curriculum, Elementary Restructuring and middle/high/tech scheduling committees, and at 0:39:12 recorded an ad hoc parliamentary procedure committee that had not been on the agenda. The quorum remarks at 0:31:44 and 0:32:41 address a different question: whether a gathering becomes a meeting of the full board. Under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) a "public body" includes a school district board "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto", so each committee's own meetings carry notice and minutes duties under RSA 91-A:2, II regardless of how many board members attend. Two of these committees reported meeting within the month (September 7 and September 21 pages). The flag is prospective: it records the structure so that the committees' notices and minutes can be looked for; this page has not searched for them.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2022 codification; committees and advisory committees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification).

OBSERVATION The business administrator's schedule puts the fiscal 2020 audits more than two years after year end

Asked for a status report at 0:36:04, Seaman said the Unity and SAU audits for 2020 were expected "in the next couple of weeks", Claremont's by the end of September, fiscal 2021 in November and fiscal 2022 "by February for the Bartsch Single Audit, piece that we have to have done" (Bartsch is unexplained in the transcript). He also reported that two outstanding grant-compliance matters had been cleared by the Department of Education. The federal single-audit deadline and how far these years ran past it are set out in the September 7 page, flag 1, where the same schedule was discussed at length; it is recorded here as an observation so the finding sits on one page.

Sources: the recording at 0:36:04 to 0:37:46; 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2022 edition: nine months after the end of the audit period).

POSITIVE Three student handbooks were read and argued over in public, and the high-school text was changed during the meeting

The board spent more than twenty minutes on the Stevens handbook. Sprague's objection to restorative justice appearing beside felony-level offenses (0:02:26) was answered point by point by the high-school administrator, other members tested it with examples, and the vice chair reduced the disagreement to a drafting question (0:13:43). The administrator then edited the live document to read "restorative justice option upon reentry" (0:14:06). Members also raised VLACS weighting, a confusing grading chart (removed), student parking and the absence of policy JBAB from two handbooks. Handbooks are routinely approved without discussion; here the reasoning behind the discipline matrix is on the public record. No rule requires this; it is recorded as practice exceeding the minimum.

Sources: the recording at 0:02:26 to 0:22:49; CSB minutes 8.24.22. No legal requirement is claimed.
