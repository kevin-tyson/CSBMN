MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this meeting. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the 2016 text, required minutes with "names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute text). Here the gap matters twice: the board's own policy, as the superintendent read it, required the content of a member's emails to be placed in the minutes of the next regular meeting (flag 3), and no minutes survive to show that it was.

MEDIUM under the project's severity rule, because the record mitigates the gap: the October 5 minutes are moved and approved on tape (0:04:15), so minutes were being kept. No public copy survives.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2008, 303:4, so in force through 2016)

MEDIUM Board told that a subcommittee with fewer than a quorum of board members need not be noticed

Asked how the open-meeting law applies to committees (1:17:04), the chair answered correctly that “A quorum of your committee is a quorum for purposes of of open meeting”. The superintendent then told the board: “But if we don't have a quorum, we have two board members on a subcommittee, and there are seven of you. We don't have to warn that.” (1:17:37). Nobody corrected him.

RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) defines a public body to include "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto" (statute text), and RSA 91-A:2, II requires "a notice of the time and place of each such meeting" (statute text). A board subcommittee is a public body in its own right, and a convening of a quorum of its own members is a meeting that must be noticed, whatever share of the full board it contains. MEDIUM: advice on the record that, if followed, would leave subcommittee meetings unnoticed. The recording does not show that any subcommittee went unnoticed as a result.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2015 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification)

OBSERVATION Policy BHE required a member's emails to be read into the next minutes; the recording does not show their content

The superintendent put policy BHE on the agenda because a member's emails had reached a quorum (1:09:23), and read that under the policy “the contents of the electric communication will be publicly disclosed and included in the minutes of the next regularly scheduled board meeting” (1:10:09). The member described the emails as an attempt to be transparent about questions he was sending the chair (1:12:40). Their content is not read into the recording, and no minutes survive to show it was disclosed. The policy itself is not available; this page relies on the superintendent's reading of it. RSA 91-A:2-a, II provides that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter" (statute text). Nothing on the recording suggests a decision was made by email, so this is an observation about documentation, not a finding of a violation.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4)

POSITIVE An email lapse aired and explained in public session

Rather than handle the matter privately, the superintendent raised the emails on the agenda (1:09:23), the chair explained reply-all and one-at-a-time emails as risks under RSA 91-A (1:11:17), the member gave his account on the record (1:12:40), and a second member asked in public how to answer an applicant's email to all members (1:16:18). Public discussion of a possible open-meeting lapse, with guidance given on the record, goes beyond what RSA 91-A:2-a requires and lets residents see how the board polices itself.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification)

POSITIVE Vacancy candidates to answer the same prepared questions in a public interview

RSA 197:26 requires only that the board fill the vacancy until the next annual meeting. The board set a process in which each candidate gives an opening statement and answers “all the same sorts of questions”, with members' questions routed to the superintendent individually rather than circulated among members (0:15:59), and the interviews held at a regular meeting on November 2 (0:08:53). A uniform, public interview is a fairer process than the statute requires. The chair left open whether deliberation would be public (0:18:33); the November 2 recording will show how that was resolved.

Sources: RSA 197:26 (2016 codification; source note ends RL 139:25)
