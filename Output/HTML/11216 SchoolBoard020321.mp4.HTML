HIGH No minutes, agenda or packet for this meeting were found in any district share

A quorum met by Zoom and voted four times: to return every school to in-person learning and winter sports, to accept seven retirements, to appoint two members of the CCTV board, and to adjourn. MAP.md records that neither district Drive share holds a year folder older than 2022 and the Meeting Minutes share starts in 2023. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes open to public inspection “not more than 5 business days after the meeting,” recording the final decisions and the mover and seconder of each motion (statutory wording, 2019 codification). With every member remote, RSA 91-A:2, III(a) also required the minutes to state why in-person attendance was not reasonably practical.

Severity follows the project rule (html_briefing.md §5), unmitigated limb: the recording names no other place the minutes are kept. The minutes would also be the only record of whose retirements were accepted, since the superintendent said “we never identify the teachers”, and of the second CCTV appointee’s name. The finding is “not found”: pre-2022 records may survive outside the shares.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II and III(a) (2019 codification, in force January 2019 to October 2, 2023); MAP.md section 138 (plain text, unpublished).

MEDIUM The agenda was adopted by voice vote at a fully remote meeting

At 0:02:22 the chair said “We don't have Mary here.” and took the agenda vote as “All those in favor say aye.” RSA 91-A:2, III(e) provides that “All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote” when members participate remotely (statutory wording), and Emergency Order #12 waived the physical-quorum requirement, not the roll-call rule. The vote was procedural and unanimous among those on the call, and every later vote was a roll call, including one the chair called himself when the clerk dropped off (0:59:44). On January 13 the same chair stopped a voice vote for this reason (show 11262, dialogue row 91).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(e) (2019 codification); Emergency Order #12 (March 23, 2020); Dialogue/11262 SchoolBoard011321.mp4.CSV.

OBSERVATION The chair proposed that the voters’ annual-meeting sessions count as the board’s February meeting

At 1:13:23 the chair said “the special meeting we would have on the fourth would constitute our February meeting”, and at 1:14:55 that he would count “those days as the, as the, as the board meeting for the month of February”, skipping the regular third-Wednesday meeting. The February 1 and 4 sessions were sessions of the school district meeting, the voters’ legislative body, run by the moderator: on January 13 the moderator described deliberative sessions as hers to run (show 11262, dialogue row 255), and both sessions were opened and closed by her (see the 2/1/21 and 2/4/21 pages). A district-meeting session does not give the board a noticed board meeting for other business.

No rule found requires a February board meeting, and no vote was taken, so this is an observation. On the plan as stated, the board’s next meeting of its own after January 27 would be the March goals retreat.

Sources: Dialogue/11262 SchoolBoard011321.mp4.CSV (moderator’s remark); Dialogue/11276 SchoolDistrictMeeting020121.mp4.CSV; Dialogue/11281 SchoolDistrictMeeting020421.mp4.CSV.
