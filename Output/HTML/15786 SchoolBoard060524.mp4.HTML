MEDIUM The board approved, without a vote, minutes recording a ten-year seal with no statutory finding

The consent agenda (0:02:08) approved the May 22, 2024 minutes, which are in this meeting's packet. They record a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(c) from 6:10 to 6:45 p.m., and then: "Heather Whitney made a motion to seal the minutes for 10 years, Frank Sprague seconded the motion; Roll call vote taken; all members voting yes", followed by "Discussion regarding policy of sealing minutes". RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld beyond 72 hours only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" at which "it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism". The minutes record the vote but no determination. The by-laws the board adopted later this evening say the same thing in their own words: "The decision to seal minutes of non-public sessions must affirmatively state one of the statutory bases listed above." The May 22 meeting has no recording in the corpus, so the minutes are the only record; the finding may have been stated and not minuted. The board accepted these minutes by the chair's "seeing no objections", with no motion and no vote, and the chair referred to them as the 24th minutes.

Sources: 5.22.24 draft CSB minutes (1).pdf; RSA 91-A:3, III (2024 codification; seal requires a recorded 2/3 vote and a determination on one of three grounds); Exhibit E- Claremont School Board By-Laws .pdf, appendix on minutes of non-public sessions

MEDIUM The by-laws adopted tonight misstate the Right-to-Know Law's nonpublic grounds

Petrin moved the by-laws "as presented" (0:41:37) and they passed on a voice vote of the four members present (0:41:52). The adopted text (Exhibit E- Claremont School Board By-Laws .pdf, 22 pages) carries an appendix on nonpublic sessions that begins "RSA 91 -A:3 sets forth eight grounds upon which Board of School Committees may enter nonpublic session", then lists nine numbered grounds, then says a motion "must state one or more of the nine statutory bases cited above". "Board of School Committees" is not a New Hampshire term. RSA 91-A:3, II as in force on this date has twelve live grounds, (a) through (m) with (f) repealed; the appendix's list omits (g) security-related issues, (h) business finance authority applications and (j) confidential commercial or financial information. The by-laws tell members "It is vital that a specific reference to one of these grounds be made on the record", so an incomplete list in the board's own governing document matters in practice. The appendix's statement of the sealing rule is accurate (see flag 1). Where the two differ, the statute controls.

Sources: Exhibit E- Claremont School Board By-Laws .pdf (read with pdftotext -layout); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II(a)–(m) (2024 codification; source note ends 2023, 189:1)

OBSERVATION The business administrator describes purchase orders rolled across fiscal years and expensed in the earlier year

Henry described a past practice of carrying purchase orders "From fiscal year 22 or 21" forward "to use in 23, which you can't do" (0:44:02), and said a rolled balance "would then get expensed into 23. Even though you hadn't purchased the items, you hadn't encumbered them" (0:44:49). Governmental funds use the modified accrual basis, under which expenditures are recognized when the liability is incurred, not when a balance is rolled; recording an unencumbered, unpurchased amount as a prior-year expenditure would overstate that year's expenditures. Henry gave no amounts and no years beyond FY21 to FY24, so no figure on the record departs from the standard. The audited statements and the auditor's findings for those years would settle whether it affected reported totals. In the same discussion Pratt said the district has no capital improvement plan (0:47:37); no statute found requires a school district to adopt one.

Sources: GASB Statement 34, ¶79 (governmental funds: current financial resources focus, modified accrual basis); N.H. Admin. Rules, Rev 1102.03 (NH school districts follow GAAP, GASB first)

OBSERVATION The retreat report omits the retreat's collective-bargaining action item
