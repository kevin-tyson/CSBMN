SAU 6 Board, December 7, 2023

The SAU 6 joint board (Claremont and Unity) reviews Business Administrator Mary Henry's revised FY2025 SAU budget, recesses for a ten-minute Claremont caucus off the record, enters a nonpublic session on hiring and compensation, seals its minutes for 99 years on a divided vote, and then sends a $2,497,924 budget (a 4.25 percent increase) to public hearing without public deliberation. MEDIUM A quorum of the Claremont board caucused off the record on the SAU budget

Six of the seven Claremont School Board members were present: Hawkins, Sprague, Miles, Skillen, Crawford and Gallagher. Crawford asked for "the opportunity for the Claremont board. The caucus" (0:24:03); the chair granted it under board policy, "a caucus of reasonable duration may be called by any one of the member school boards" (0:24:18), and recessed the SAU board from 6:25 to 6:35 (0:25:04). The caucus is not on the recording and no record of it has been located. RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body" to discuss or act on "a matter or matters over which the public body has supervision, control, jurisdiction, or advisory power". The Claremont district pays its share of the SAU budget and its members vote on that budget, so the subject is plausibly within the Claremont board's jurisdiction. The only caucus exclusion in RSA 91-A:2, I is (c), "A caucus consisting of elected members of a public body of the same political party who were elected on a partisan basis", and Claremont board members are elected on the non-partisan ballot that RSA 671:30 requires of every school district, so that exclusion does not apply. A district policy cannot add an exclusion the statute lacks. The grade is MEDIUM, not HIGH, because no authority located for this page decides whether a member board's caucus inside an SAU meeting is a meeting of the member board, and the recess was called on the record with its purpose and length stated.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and I(c) (2023 codification; source note ends 2023, 188:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); RSA 671:30 (non-partisan ballot for school district officers; 1979, 321:1)

MEDIUM The nonpublic session, entered for hiring and compensation, produced the board's position on the whole budget

The session arose from one answer: Henry could not discuss a position "publicly" (0:22:48). The chair moved entry under RSA 91-A:3, II(b), "the hiring of any person as a public employee", and II(a), "The compensation of any public employee" (0:27:07, 0:27:27). The motion stated both grounds on its face, as RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires. RSA 91-A:3, I(c) then provides: "All discussions held and decisions made during nonpublic session shall be confined to the matters set out in the motion." On returning, the chair described the session as broader: "The board met to discuss the proposed budget that you provided for us tonight" (0:30:47), and announced positions on the general 3.75 percent raise, the assistant superintendent's 5 percent raise and $3,000 payment, the SAU's health-insurance share, the new position and a total increase of $101,923. The board then voted to send $2,497,924 to hearing with no discussion (0:33:31). Some of these items are compensation of public employees and may fit II(a); the health-insurance share of the budget and the budget total are harder to fit, and the chair's own summary says the budget was the subject. Mitigation: everything decided was then put to a public vote, and the public hearing followed on December 14. The sealed minutes are the only record that would settle what was discussed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(a), II(b) (2023 codification; source note ends 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023)

MEDIUM Minutes sealed for 99 years with no statutory determination and no announced tally

RSA 91-A:3, III requires nonpublic minutes to be disclosed within 72 hours "unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism." The roll call (0:30:14 onward) shows eight yes, Hawkins no and Erickson abstaining among ten present, which clears two-thirds. No tally was announced and no determination was stated: the motion is only "to seal the minutes for 99 years" (0:30:04), and the chair's lead-in is a question, "Do we need to seal the the minutes?" (0:29:55). RSA 91-A:3, IV, in force from October 3, 2023, adds that sealed minutes must be reviewed by majority vote no more than 10 years after the last vote to withhold them, so a 99-year term does not hold the minutes for 99 years without further votes. The same board sealed another nonpublic session for 99 years a week later. Because this meeting's own minutes are not public, the recorded vote the statute calls for can be checked only against the tape.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III and IV (2023 codification; ¶IV from 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); 12/14/23 draft minutes (Erickson's abstention added to these minutes)

OBSERVATION The budget's base-year column and assumptions are qualified by the business administrator herself

Henry, five months into the job, told the board that the FY2024 column matches the accounting system but "I can't say that they totally match Richard's numbers" (her predecessor's) (0:08:59); that she cut health insurance by assuming a two-person rather than a family plan, "I am going to take a gamble" (0:14:43); and that a payroll position had been reclassified before her arrival. Sprague said the board had been told that change was "negligible. It's about the same pay. That is not the same pay." (0:11:45). The packet budget shows the Superintendent's Office health-insurance line falling from $128,282.24 budgeted for FY2024 to $61,369.27 proposed, and the business office rising $176,915.33, the figure Henry then cut to $112,303. None of this is shown to be wrong; it is the kind of base-year and assumption risk the board was asked to vote on in one public motion without discussion. RSA 194-C:9 governs the SAU budget and sets no form or column requirement, so no rule is applied.

Sources: SAU6 FY25 Proposed Budget (1).PDF (printed November 30, 2023); Dialogue/15455 SAU6120723.mp4.CSV

POSITIVE The SAU budget went to a public hearing that the statute does not require, with three prior-year columns

RSA 194-C:9, I requires the SAU board to adopt its budget "At a meeting held before January 1" and imposes no public-hearing requirement. The board voted tonight to take the figure to a public hearing on December 14 (0:32:57), where it adopted $2,489,151.03, still before January 1. The packet budget shows FY2022 and FY2023 actual expenditures and the FY2024 budget beside each proposed FY2025 line, with dollar and percentage changes. Both go beyond the statutory minimum.

Sources: RSA 194-C:9, I (2023 codification); 12/14/23 draft minutes (hearing and adoption)
