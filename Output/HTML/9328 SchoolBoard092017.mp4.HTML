Regular meeting with Frank Sprague presiding in the absence of the chair and vice chair: a grandparent's challenge to the district's mandatory lead-screening policy, the new student representative's first report, the superintendent's response to the August 28 incident involving a young Claremont boy, the Stevens High School scheduling committee's plan and a vote letting the superintendent relax the committee's memorandum of understanding, a $478 National Honor Society gift, two student trips, and final adoption of the non-academic survey policy. The recording is the only public account of the MOU vote, the gift, the trip approvals and the policy readings, and the garbled roll call leaves attendance to inference. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force for calendar 2017, required minutes including "names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", open to inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting". The gift adds a second minutes duty: for unanticipated money under $5,000, RSA 198:20-b, III(b) required the board to "post notice of the funds in the agenda" and "include notice in the minutes", with acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting". The acceptance was in public session at a regular meeting (1:56:22); whether the agenda and minutes carried the notice cannot be checked. This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the board approved its September 6 minutes on tape (0:01:45) and worked from a written agenda and packet.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification, the text for calendar 2017); RSA 198:20-b, III(b) (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION A local lead-screening requirement was enforced with an exclusion warning and, at first, a demand for the results

By the superintendent's account the district was "the first school district in the state" to require lead screening for school entry (0:14:00), the policy has no waiver (0:06:11), and the state had not adopted a mandate (0:13:09). The commenter said a note warned that "your child won't be allowed to stay in school" without the paperwork (0:04:28) and that the nurse first asked for the test results (0:10:04); the superintendent said the district does not see results and called that "a miscommunication" (0:10:23). Immunization, which the superintendent compared it to (0:14:00), is a duty the legislature itself imposes: RSA 141-C:20-a, I requires parents to have children "immunized against certain diseases", and RSA 141-C:20-b has schools "keep immunization records for all enrolled children". No statute verified for this page makes lead screening a condition of attending school, so a warning of exclusion rests on the board's policy alone, and the policy's text is not on the record. Any results a nurse does collect become part of the student's education record, protected under FERPA. The board responded well: it agreed on the spot to revisit the language and invited the commenter back (0:15:51, 0:18:55).

Sources: RSA 141-C:20-a, I (2016 codification; immunization required by statute); RSA 141-C:20-b (2016 codification; school immunization records); 34 CFR 99.30(a) (consent before disclosing education records; unchanged since 2004)

OBSERVATION The board let the superintendent "relax" an agreement with the teachers' association, on a motion with no stated terms

The scheduling committee exists under a January 2017 memorandum of understanding between the board and the Sugar River Education Association that fixed its membership at five board and five union appointees and set its timeline (1:36:03, 1:42:52). The motion, assembled aloud with the presiding member supplying words, designated the superintendent "to relax some of the, some of the stipulations" and the "Restrictive language" (1:53:37); it names no clause, number or date. An MOU is a two-party agreement, so a change binds the association only if the association agrees; the record does not show its agreement tonight, although on August 2 the superintendent said the association supported the earlier change to the same MOU (August 2, 2017 page). Pierce asked whether the board should act with its chair and vice chair absent (1:54:14); a quorum was present, so it could. The concern is the record: the minutes that would say what was delegated do not survive, and the motion as spoken leaves the scope to the superintendent.

Sources: RSA ch. 273-A (public employee labor relations; agreements with exclusive representatives); Dialogue/9328 SchoolBoard092017.mp4.CSV (rows 375 to 377, 404 to 407, 447 to 477)

POSITIVE The board-and-union scheduling committee posts its minutes online

The committee's members said its minutes are posted on the Stevens High School website "for anyone to look at" (1:08:12, 1:28:27), and described a plan to collect views from parents, students and staff before drafting. A committee whose members are appointed in part by the school board to advise it is plausibly a public body in its own right: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) reaches "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto". That would require notice and minutes open to inspection. The 2017 text of RSA 91-A:2, II required minutes to be open to inspection, not posted online; putting them on the school website went beyond that. Whether those minutes survive today was not checked for this page.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (source note ends 2008, 354:1; in force throughout); RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification: inspection, no website requirement)

POSITIVE The survey-consent policy was carried through three readings and adopted as the new law took effect

The non-academic surveys policy had its first reading on August 2, its second on September 6 and final approval tonight, "The last policy that legally went into effect five days ago" (2:17:34). The 2017 codification of RSA 186:11, IX-d, effective September 16, 2017, bars requiring a student to take a non-academic survey "without written consent of a parent or legal guardian", with an exception for the Youth Risk Behavior Survey. The board took up the policy six weeks before the effective date and finished four days after it, without skipping a reading. Members also asked that future drafts highlight what changed (2:15:09); the superintendent called it a "Very fair request."

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-d (2017 codification, eff. Sept. 16, 2017); August 2, 2017 page (first reading); September 6, 2017 page (second reading)
