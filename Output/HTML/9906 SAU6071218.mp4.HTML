SAU 6 Board, July 12, 2018

SAU 6 board meeting during the interim superintendent search: the board adopted a self-evaluation tool for its July 29 retreat, heard that the search committee had chosen two certified candidates to interview the next day, and after six costed scenarios voted by roll call, with nine audible yes answers to Pierce's no, to set the interim's base salary at $120,000 rather than $130,000 for a five-day position. RSA 91-A:2, II, as in force throughout 2018, required minutes including "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". MEDIUM The interim search committee met on July 5 and interviews on July 13, with no record that either session was noticed or minuted

The chair reported that the committee "met on July 5th and reviewed all of the applications, and we chose two that are moving forward to interview", that "we have those interviews scheduled for tomorrow" and that "there will be a meeting after that just to assess the information" (0:37:05); she referred to "the interim superintendent search committee meeting on the fifth" (0:48:05). The committee was created by a vote of this board on June 28, with its membership fixed by motion. RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), unchanged since 2008, makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof" of a school board or SAU board a public body, so its meetings required notice under RSA 91-A:2, II and minutes. Reviewing applicants does not displace that: RSA 91-A:3, II(b) allows nonpublic session for "The hiring of any person as a public employee", but only on a motion stating the exemption and a roll-call vote under RSA 91-A:3, I(b), with minutes. At the June 28 meeting a member advised that notice might not be needed because the committee lacked a quorum (see the June 28, 2018 meeting page). This page grades the gap MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the absence of committee minutes is not specific: no 2018 SAU 6 records of any kind survive in the shares, and nothing on this recording says how the July 5 session was warned. The committee's notices and minutes, if they exist, would settle it.

Lowe pointed to the extra cost of certifying a one-year hire (0:41:34). Both candidates interviewed were certified, so the choice was within the June 28 criteria. The page notes the drift from preference to screen because the committee's sessions have no surviving record and this exchange is the only account of how the criteria were applied. OBSERVATION The salary option adopted was projected to leave the SAU budget a few thousand dollars short, with the remainder "spoken for"

Option E, an interim five days a week, was costed at "A deficit of $14,000." at the budgeted $130,000 base (1:01:30); the business administrator put the saving from a $120,000 base at "$10,075 to $0.38" (1:51:04), and a member noted that this "Still technically puts you in the hole for about four grand." (1:51:47). The assistant superintendent said "All of the rest of the money is spoken for" (1:21:17) and identified possible savings of "3 to 5000" from restructuring a vacant position (1:24:54). RSA 194-C:5, III gives the SAU board the power to fix the salaries of all SAU personnel, and RSA 194-C:9, I makes the adopted SAU budget the unit's spending plan; the record shows the board choosing an option it knew might need offsetting savings, not spending without authority. The presenter of the scenarios was also the employee whose acting pay options B and D would set, and she said so ("Since we're talking about me.", 0:58:23); the board chose the option with no acting days. This is recorded as an observation; the year-end SAU budget-to-actual report would show how the gap was closed.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (2017 codification; 1996, 298:3): SAU board fixes SAU salaries; RSA 194-C:9, I (2017 codification): SAU budget

POSITIVE The interim superintendent's salary was set by a recorded roll-call vote, in public, after the costing was shown

The business office and HR prepared six costed scenarios with benefit loads for each (0:56:31), the board debated them for about an hour in open session, and at Towle's request the vote was taken by roll call (1:53:25 to 1:54:24). RSA 91-A:2, II in 2018 required minutes to record "final decisions" but did not require roll calls or the names of movers. A named, recorded vote on a salary the SAU board fixes under RSA 194-C:5, III goes beyond that minimum and leaves a public record of who supported the figure.
