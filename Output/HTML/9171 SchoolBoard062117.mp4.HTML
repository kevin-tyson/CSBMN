MEDIUM The written meal-charge policy due by July 1, 2017 was put off to a July 19 second reading

USDA memo SP 23-2017 states that a written meal charge policy "is required for SFAs no later than July 1, 2017", under SP 46-2016. The superintendent told the board the requirement "goes into effect July 1st" (1:51:34), that the district's practice "it's not in writing" (2:05:53), and that "some districts have a policy like this in place. We do not" (1:53:46). He also told the board it "may skip some readings" and "go from first read to approval" (2:11:50). The board moved the policy to a second reading on July 19 (2:12:19, 2:12:46), eighteen days after the date. The state's own requirement, RSA 189:11-a, VIII (in force since July 2, 2016), covers what such a policy must ensure, including that "no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal", which the superintendent read to the board (2:06:21).

Mitigation on the record: the superintendent said the practice already met the substance ("We don't stigmatize", 1:52:10), members wanted time to study a long model policy, and the policy had blanks that depended on the new vendor (2:08:51, 2:10:01). This is graded MEDIUM, a probable process gap against a federal program deadline set by guidance rather than a statute; the July 19 record, if any survives, would show when the policy was adopted. The May 31 page (show 9128) records the first mention.

Sources: USDA FNS memo SP 23-2017 (Mar. 23, 2017): written meal charge policy required no later than July 1, 2017; RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2017 codification; 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016)

OBSERVATION Summer hiring authority and a nominee whose license was pending, under the statute that has school boards elect teachers

RSA 189:39 provides that "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers employed in the schools in their school administrative unit, providing such teachers hold a valid educational credential issued by the state board of education." Two items tonight touch it. First, the board authorized the superintendent to offer contracts to new hires over the summer (1:41:54, 1:42:25); the chair described the practice as staff being given contracts and "the board would approve it at a later date" (1:40:58). If each hire is later elected by the board, the statute's division of roles is kept, only out of sequence; the record does not show how the later approvals were made. Second, the board elected a nominee who was "going to be receiving her license next month" (1:46:10), after the superintendent said waiting for the license would be "black and white" (1:46:30). The statute ties election to holding a valid credential. Whether a credential issued before the employee starts satisfies it is not settled by anything verified for this page, so both points are observations.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (1971, 371:2, eff. Aug. 27, 1971; unamended)

POSITIVE The business administrator told viewers plainly that a food-service surplus cannot lower taxes

Asked what a guaranteed surplus meant for taxpayers, the business administrator said "that money cannot be used for anything but food service. So there would not be a tax impact" (1:29:27) and "I can't use it as revenue offset taxes" (1:29:57), while noting that a shortfall "does come from the general fund" (1:37:15). That matches the federal rule that school food service revenues are to be used only for the operation or improvement of the food service. He also described the contract as up to five years with yearly renewal (1:24:47), consistent with the federal limit of a one-year contract renewable for no more than four additional years. Correcting a misconception against the board's own interest in a selling point is worth recording.

Sources: 7 CFR 210.14(a) (2017 edition: food service revenues used only for the program); 7 CFR 210.16(d) (2017 edition: contract term and renewals)
