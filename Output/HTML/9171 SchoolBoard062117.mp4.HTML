Claremont School Board — June 21, 2017

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The board approved the Claremont Middle School handbook, heard first-year i-Ready growth results, held a brief nonpublic session, awarded the food service contract to the Abbey Group on its committee's unanimous recommendation, cancelled the July 5 meeting, approved one resignation and a slate of new hires, and took up four policies on first read, including a meal-charge policy that federal guidance required in writing by July 1, 2017. MEDIUM A written meal-charge policy was due by July 1, 2017; the board put it on first read ten days before, cancelled its July 5 meeting, and set approval for July 19

The U.S. Department of Agriculture required every school food authority in the National School Lunch or School Breakfast Program to have a written meal-charge policy "No later than July 1, 2017". The superintendent told the board the requirement "goes into effect July 1st" (1:51:34) and that in Claremont "It's been an ongoing practice, but it's not in writing" (2:05:53). The board moved the policy to a second read (2:12:55) after cancelling the July 5 meeting (1:40:24), with the next reading set for "July 19th" (2:12:19), although the superintendent had just advised that "the board may skip some readings. You could go from first read to approval" (2:11:50). On the recording, then, the district's written policy could not have been board-approved before the federal date. Mitigation, stated fairly: the practice described (every child receives a meal, "no student will be subject to different treatment") matches the state rule then in force, RSA 189:11-a, VIII, which since July 2, 2016 required a meal payment policy to ensure "all students have access to a healthy school lunch" and no different treatment; the blanks in the model policy (payee, mailing address) depended on the vendor chosen that night; and the recording cannot show whether an administrative written procedure existed by July 1. Graded MEDIUM as a probable process gap against a dated federal requirement, not a finding that any child was denied a meal.

Sources: USDA Food and Nutrition Service, Unpaid Meal Charges: Local Charge Policy Requirement ("No later than July 1, 2017 ... must have a written policy in place"); RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2017 codification; added by 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016).

OBSERVATION The board authorized the superintendent to offer contracts to new hires over the summer; the statute has the board elect teachers

With the July 5 meeting cancelled, the board voted to give the superintendent "Authority to to provide contracts to the, the the employees during the summer" (1:41:54), with the information to come to the board "At every board meeting". The superintendent himself noted the authority "has to be a vote of of the the board" (1:41:17). RSA 189:39 provides that "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers". A delegation to offer contracts is consistent with that only if the board later elects each nominee; the recording does not say whether the summer hires came back for election, and without the July and August minutes that cannot be checked here. The same night the board elected a counseling candidate whose license was expected "next month" (1:46:10); no rule on the timing of certification was verified for this page, so that is noted without a flag.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (source note ends 1971, 371:2; unchanged throughout the corpus).

POSITIVE The food service award was made on scored evaluations and site visits, with the federal money rules explained accurately to viewers

The committee scored both proposals category by category and reported the scores publicly (90 to 93 points, 1:19:17 to 1:21:27); members visited schools served by each vendor (1:21:52). The business administrator told the board that food service money "cannot be used for anything but food service" (1:29:27), and Sprague made a point of telling "the taxpayers that are watching" that a surplus would not lower the tax rate (1:37:09); that matches 7 CFR 210.14(a), under which revenues are "to be used only for the operation or improvement of such food service". The contract structure described (a one-year term renewable up to five years, 1:24:47, 1:38:14) matches 7 CFR 210.16(d), and the superintendent's reminder that "the vendor works for us" (1:24:32) reflects the district's duty under 210.16(a) to retain control of its food service. The motion's wording was restated by the chair and confirmed before the vote (1:39:01).

Sources: 7 CFR 210.14(a) (2017 annual edition); 7 CFR 210.16(a), (d) (2017 annual edition: contract "of a duration of no longer than 1 year", renewals "may not exceed 4 additional years").
