HIGH Twenty certified staff were reported as already hired with no board election, on the strength of a June 17 authorization the approved June 17 minutes do not record

The personnel report lists twenty certified hires (eighteen teachers and counselors, an assistant principal at Stevens and a career coordinator at the tech center) and states: "The Claremont School Board voted at its June 17 meeting to authorize the superintendent to hire certified staff during the summer months. Therefore, these employees have been hired, and no board action is required" (personnel report text, dated 7/22/2026). The superintendent told the board "the board has not been approving or notified of every hire, and you are supposed to be" (1:57:18) and "I've been hiring people because we needed to" (1:58:02); the chair closed the item with "So, no action tonight." (1:59:00). RSA 189:39 reads: "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers employed in the schools in their school administrative unit" (statute text). The board's own by-laws say the same: rule 5.01 requires "a nomination for election by the board", rule 5.03 bars any employee from beginning work "without the approval of the CSB" except under 5.04, and Appendix A, item 1 allows delegation "excepting that no teacher shall be employed who has not been nominated by the superintendent and elected by CSB" (by-laws text, live Google Doc modified March 23, 2026). The approved June 17 minutes record no vote authorizing summer hiring: the motions they record concern the Bluff student-activity fund, the negotiation-team appointment, the nonpublic session and its sealing, and volunteer painting at the middle school. Mitigation: the superintendent raised the problem himself and presented the list openly; rule 5.04 and Ed 302.02(i) let a superintendent provide "temporary staff to fill vacancies" with notice at the next meeting; and the August 5 packet carries a Personnel_Nomination Report.pdf, which may show later board action this page has not examined. Graded HIGH because the statute and the by-law both reserve election of teachers to the board and the record of this meeting shows hires made and no election.

HIGH The FY22 and FY23 audits, which include the federal single audit, arrived as drafts more than two years after the Uniform Guidance deadline, and both are disclaimers of opinion

The FY22 draft is titled with a November 19, 2025 date and the FY23 draft is stamped "DRAFT - 7-15-2026" (packet documents). The FY23 draft's contents list includes the "Single Audit Act Schedules", the "Report on Compliance for Each Major Federal Program ... Required by the Uniform Guidance" and the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (draft report text), so the auditor treats the district as subject to the single audit. 2 CFR 200.501(a) requires one of any entity that "expends $750,000 or more" in federal awards in a year, and 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) requires the reporting package within "the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period" (2023 CFR text). For years ending June 30, 2022 and June 30, 2023 that is March 31, 2023 and April 1, 2024 (March 31, 2024 was a Sunday). On tape: "You have received a disclaimer of opinion in the fiscal 22 and fiscal 23 reports." (0:57:24) and "There's a core failure in the general ledger" (0:57:43). The FY23 draft gives the basis: "bank accounts were not reconciled to the general ledger", the Major Grants Fund "lacked a functioning subsidiary ledger", capital-asset records were unreliable, and the General Fund cash account "was overdrawn by $757,639" at year end (draft report text). The superintendent noted the board had expected FY22 through FY25 by July and now expects FY24 and FY25 "by Thanksgiving" (1:05:57, 1:06:43). Mitigation: both years fall under the prior superintendent and business administrator (0:47:16), and the current administration presented the findings in public. The grading rests on the federal deadline, not on any finding about who caused the delay.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (single audit reporting package due within nine months); 2 CFR 200.501(a) (single audit at $750,000 in federal expenditures); FY23 draft audit report (7-15-2026); FY22 draft audit report (11-19-2025); Understanding Annual Audits presentation

MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes record a vote to seal with no mover, no seconder and no reason, a vote the public minutes do not mention; the minutes then appeared nine days later

The board entered nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(c) by a 7–0 roll call in public (2:38:54, 2:39:12), which meets ¶I(b). The nonpublic minutes form, after "Public session reconvened at 9:57 p.m.", leaves blank the lines for who moved and seconded sealing and ticks none of the three grounds, yet records a seven-member "Roll Call Vote to seal minutes" and "Motion: PASSED" (nonpublic minutes text). RSA 91-A:3, III allows withholding only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" with a stated determination (statute text). The draft public minutes go from the return to public session straight to adjournment at 9:57 p.m. and record no seal vote, and the board's own by-laws Appendix D says the reason for sealing "must be stated immediately after the non-public session and must appear on the record of the open meeting" (by-laws text). The recording ends at the nonpublic roll call, so it cannot show what happened after 9:57. If no valid seal was voted, ¶III required disclosure "within 72 hours"; the minutes document was created in Drive on July 30, nine days after the meeting (file metadata), and MAP.md records it as unsealed. The form's ground (f), "applications by the adult parole board", is a repealed paragraph of RSA 91-A:3, II; nothing turned on it here because (c) was ticked. Graded MEDIUM because the minutes exist, were published, and record "Discussion of personnel matters, no action taken" (nonpublic minutes text); the defect is in the record of the seal decision and its timing.
