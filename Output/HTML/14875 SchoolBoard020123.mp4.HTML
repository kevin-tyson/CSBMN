Claremont School Board, February 1, 2023

Regular board meeting at which the administration presented its plan for the $1,000,000 cut voted on January 17: move grade 5 to the middle school and grade 8 to Stevens High School, bring pre-K into the elementary schools, and reduce staff. No vote was taken on the plan. About an hour and a quarter of public comment, nearly all of it against the cut or the timing, followed. MEDIUM The default budget fell by $1,000,000 with the proposed budget, on an explanation that is not the statute's definition

The January 17 hearing summary gave the default as $37,342,948; the warrant in this meeting's packet gives $36,342,948, exactly $1,000,000 less, after the board cut the proposed budget by $1,000,000. Asked why (2:11:01), Seaman answered "It's based on the proposed budget that the board brings to the deliberative session" and that the board's vote "is begin to establish what the default budget will be" (2:11:39); Pope objected that the default is "based on the last year" (2:11:55), and the chair said the method had been run by counsel (2:12:00). RSA 40:13, IX(b) does not define the default from the proposed budget. It starts from "the same appropriations as contained in the operating budget authorized for the previous year", adjusts for debt service, contracts and other obligations, and reduces by one-time expenditures "and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget". A $1,000,000 reduction is lawful under that last clause only to the extent it consists of positions eliminated in the proposed budget, and the administration had not yet identified those positions (Koski: 6 to 9., Tempesta: "For 4 to 7, I think.", 1:30:25, 1:30:37). RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires a default budget form identifying each change "by account code" with reasons and the reductions for eliminated positions; members were asked to sign that form this night (2:39:10) but it is not in the packet. Seaman's answer to Crawford, that the default "stays set", is consistent with XI(b) ("This amount shall not be amended by the legislative body"), which also lets the governing body adjust the default before the ballots are printed when "acting on relevant new information", on an amended form. The form itself would settle whether the $1,000,000 is lawful eliminated-position arithmetic; the explanation given to the public was not the statutory test.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b), XI(a) and XI(b) (2022 codification; last amended 2019, 192:2); packet: warrant Article 2, default $36,342,948; Claremont Budget Summary 1.17.23 (default $37,342,948)

OBSERVATION A grade reconfiguration assembled in a week to fill an unallocated cut, with no board vote and a contract cost not recomputed

The plan answered a figure, not a program decision: Tempesta said the administration had "about 6 or 7 days" (0:15:37), and the packet heads it "Rationale for Grade Shift To Meet Budget Reduction for Deliberative Session". The 15/25/20/40 split (0:17:11) was the only allocation of the $1,000,000 on the record; the number of positions was given as a range that changed between two administrators (1:30:25, 1:30:37). The board took no vote on the reconfiguration; members spoke for it and the public speakers who addressed it asked, with few exceptions, for more time. Separately, Seaman confirmed that the warrant's Note A cost of the teachers' contract ($1,002,364 in the first year) was not recomputed for the staffing reductions (1:13:31). No verified rule requires a hearing or vote before a reconfiguration is proposed, so this is an observation; it records that the voters were asked to decide on a budget whose staffing consequences were still a range.

OBSERVATION A parent reported sexual harassment of students at the middle school and said an earlier report went unanswered

In public comment a parent described repeated incidents involving her child and other students at the middle school and said she had reported them to an administrator without follow-up (1:39:40, 1:43:41); later she said the principal had not been told (2:44:26). A board member made a formal request that the administration investigate (2:42:18), and the superintendent said "it is being dealt with" and urged that specifics not be discussed in open session (2:45:57). Under the Title IX rule in force in 2023 (the 2020 rule), "actual knowledge" includes notice "to any employee of an elementary and secondary school", and a recipient with actual knowledge "must respond promptly in a manner that is not deliberately indifferent." What the district did is not in this record, so this is an observation, not a finding; the parent's account, if accurate, describes notice to an employee. This page follows the project's privacy rule and does not repeat the details given at the podium.

Sources: 34 CFR 106.30(a) (2022 edition; actual knowledge); 34 CFR 106.44(a) (2022 edition; prompt response)
