MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed "for 99 years" by voice vote, with no statutory reason stated on the record

After the nonpublic session, an unidentified member moved "to save the nonpublic minutes for 99 years" (0:23:31). The chair asked whether a voice vote would do; Petrin suggested a roll call; the chair then took a voice vote (0:23:59). In 2019, RSA 91-A:3, III required nonpublic minutes and decisions to be disclosed within 72 hours unless, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", the board determined that disclosure would likely affect adversely the reputation of a non-member, render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism. On the tape no determination of any of those three kinds is stated, and only one Aye is audible; whether the vote was recorded member by member, and whether the minutes supply the finding, cannot be checked because no minutes are online. The statute also ties the withholding period to the circumstances rather than to a fixed term; a 99-year seal is a choice the statute does not describe. This is graded MEDIUM as a documentation gap, not a confirmed violation: the minutes may cure it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1 and 280:1).

OBSERVATION The nonpublic motion read the words of RSA 91-A:3, II(j), an exemption for adjudicative proceedings under RSA 541 or 541-A; the session's subject is not on the record

Petrin's motion (0:21:48) named "paragraph two" and then read, in substance, the text of ¶II(j): "Consideration of confidential, commercial, or financial information that is exempt from public disclosure under RSA 91-A:5, IV in an adjudicative proceeding pursuant to RSA 541 or RSA 541-A." RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires the motion to state the specific exemption, and reading the exemption's words does that even without the letter. What the recording does not show is the fit. The only item the chair linked to the nonpublic was the school funding / ConVal lawsuit, moved to "right immediately after we come out of our nonpublic" (0:06:02); a school funding suit is a court case, not an RSA 541 or 541-A administrative proceeding, and the exemption for pending litigation is a different paragraph (¶II(e), "pending claims or litigation which has been threatened in writing or filed"). The session may have concerned something else. Afterwards the chair reported "nothing much to share" and Zullo stressed that "no decision has been made" (0:28:46). With the subject unknown, this is an observation only.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(e) and II(j) (2019 codification).

OBSERVATION Unanticipated-funds hearing held, but the infrastructure fund amount was never settled aloud and no acceptance vote is on the tape

RSA 198:20-b, as it read in 2019, let a school board accept unanticipated money during the year only if the district had adopted the enabling article (¶I), and for $5,000 or more required a prior public hearing with newspaper notice of its time, place and subject at least 7 days before (¶III(a)). The board held the hearing, and the March 20 meeting had set it for this date (see the March 20 page). Two points remain open. First, the amount of the Public School Infrastructure Fund money was worked out at the table: $141,000 as the warrant, then "$564,000." on an 80/20 reading, then "470,000" and 04771 (0:04:29 to 0:04:45). A hearing is more useful to the public when the figure is stated. Second, the acting superintendent asked the board to accept the funds (0:02:59), but no motion to accept either the $21,150 grant or the infrastructure money appears in the meeting that follows; acceptance may have come at a later meeting. Newspaper notice and the enabling article cannot be checked from the recording.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a)-(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1; threshold raised to $20,000 by 2023, 38:1).

OBSERVATION A citizen said the audience could not hear the board

The only public commenter (Ward 3, 0:18:20) said members were speaking to their screens and the room could not follow, and that she could not watch at home. The chair did not respond to the substance; he explained that the board does not answer during citizens' comments. In 2019 RSA 91-A:2, III(c) required audibility to the public only for members participating remotely; no verified rule sets an audibility standard for members present in the room, so this is recorded as an observation. The complaint bears on whether the public can follow an open meeting in practice.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(c) (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1); recording.
