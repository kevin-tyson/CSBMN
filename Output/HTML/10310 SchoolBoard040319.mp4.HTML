Claremont School Board, April 3, 2019

Regular meeting preceded by a public hearing on two sources of unanticipated revenue (a $21,150 Couch Family Foundation grant and state Public School Infrastructure Fund money for the security warrant article). The board went into nonpublic session on a confidential-financial-information ground and voted to seal those minutes for 99 years, approved a July 2020 Stevens music trip, accepted a $760 American Legion donation, heard that the first draft of the incoming superintendent's contract went out that day, and passed around the FY2020 MS-22 ($35,524,063) for signature. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes were sealed "for 99 years" on a voice vote, with no count and no stated reason

After the session the board took up "a motion to save [seal] the nonpublic minutes for 99 years" (0:23:31). The chair asked whether a voice vote would do (0:23:53) and then called "All those in favor?" (0:23:59). One Aye. is audible, with no call for opposition and no count. Nobody states why the minutes should be withheld.

RSA 91-A:3, III, in the 2019 codification, requires nonpublic minutes to be publicly disclosed within 72 hours unless the body, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", determines that disclosure would likely affect a person's reputation adversely, render the proposed action ineffective, or relate to terrorism; the information may then be withheld "until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply". The statute names no fixed term. On the tape, the two-thirds count is not recorded and none of the three determinations is stated. The vote was taken in public session, and the minutes, which are not available, might record a tally and a finding; that possibility is why this is MEDIUM and not HIGH.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2019 codification; 72-hour disclosure unless sealed by recorded 2/3 vote with a determination); Dialogue/10310 SchoolBoard040319.mp4.CSV (rows 149 to 161)

MEDIUM Members were still collecting signatures on the MS-22 on April 3, three weeks after the March 12 vote

The business administrator presented the fiscal 2020 MS-22, with a total appropriation of $35,524,063, and the chair asked whether it "needs to get passed around so we can all sign that? Correct." (0:32:30, 0:32:53). The voters acted on the warrant at the official-ballot session of March 12, 2019. RSA 21-J:34, II requires the governing body of a school district to certify the appropriations voted "within 20 days of the close of the meeting". Twenty days from March 12 ended on Monday, April 1, 2019. A form still being signed on April 3 could not have been filed by then.

The record does not show when the form reached the Department of Revenue Administration, whether the department accepted it late or granted more time, or whether a signed copy had already gone in and this was a duplicate. Six days later, on April 9, the district was notified that its warrant, budget and default budget had not been posted on time, and it called a special meeting for May 15 to ratify the March results (as the acting superintendent reported on May 1; see the May 1, 2019 page), so the appropriations being certified here soon needed ratification themselves.

Sources: RSA 21-J:34, II (2019 codification; appropriations report "filed within 20 days of the close of the meeting")

OBSERVATION The nonpublic motion cited the confidential-financial-information ground, which applies only within an adjudicative proceeding under RSA 541 or 541-A

The motion (0:21:48) paraphrases RSA 91-A:3, II(j), "Consideration of confidential, commercial, or financial information that is exempt from public disclosure under RSA 91-A:5, IV in an adjudicative proceeding pursuant to RSA 541 or RSA 541-A" (statute's wording). The motion said "judicial proceeding". The chair had moved the school-funding lawsuit item to "right immediately after we come out of our nonpublic" (0:06:02), and after the session he reported only that the board was waiting on legislative and court developments (0:28:12). The ConVal school-funding case was filed in Superior Court in March 2019, according to the NH School Funding Fairness Project's case summary; it is a court action, not an administrative appeal under RSA 541 or RSA 541-A.

If the nonpublic subject was that lawsuit, II(j) does not fit it; the statute's grounds for litigation are II(e) (pending claims or litigation threatened in writing or filed against the body) and II(l) (consideration of legal advice from counsel). The recording does not say what was discussed, and the sealed minutes are not available, so this is an observation about the fit of the stated ground, not a finding.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(e), II(j), II(l) (2019 codification; motion states the exemption; discussion confined to it); NH School Funding Fairness Project, "ConVal Lawsuit" (advocacy summary; filed in Superior Court, March 2019)

OBSERVATION The unanticipated-revenue hearing was held, but no amount for the infrastructure grant was settled and no vote to accept either source is on the tape

The board held the hearing that RSA 198:20-b, III(a) requires for unanticipated funds of $5,000 or more, and invited comment on each source (0:03:06, 0:04:49). The amounts were uncertain in the room: the Couch grant was first put at "approximately $23,000" and then read from earlier minutes as $21,150 (0:01:38, 0:02:39); the infrastructure money was described as an 80/20 match on a roughly $141,000 warrant article, with "$564,000." and "So it's 470,000." both spoken and neither confirmed (0:04:35, 0:04:39). The acting superintendent asked the board to accept the funds (0:02:59), but neither source appears in the acceptance-of-donations item later in the meeting (0:28:52), which dealt only with the $760 gift.

The statute also requires newspaper notice of the hearing's time, place and subject at least seven days before, and it presupposes a district article authorizing the board to accept such money (198:20-b, I). Neither the notice nor the article can be checked here. Neither the April 17 nor the May 1 recording shows a vote accepting either source (on May 1 the board voted only on a $25,000 bequest; see the May 1, 2019 page), so no acceptance of this money appears on any recording through May 1.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2019 codification; prior public hearing with seven days' newspaper notice at $5,000 or more)
