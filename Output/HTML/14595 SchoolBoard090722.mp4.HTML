MEDIUM Claremont's FY2020 audit had not been drafted 26 months after year-end, and the stated single-audit deadline does not match the regulation

The business office reported that the draft Unity FY2020 audit had arrived that day, with the SAU's and then Claremont's FY2020 to follow, and all audits current “by February” (0:36:47, 0:37:14). Claremont's FY2020 (year ended June 30, 2020) was therefore still without a draft audit more than two years after year-end, with FY2021 scheduled for November. The administration itself described the single audit as “a federal requirement”, due “by the March 15th deadline”. The Uniform Guidance sets the due date as the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after year-end, which for a June 30 year is March 31, not March 15; a March 15 internal target is earlier and therefore harmless, but the record does not say whose deadline it is. For FY2020 and FY2021 the nine-month dates were March 31, 2021 and March 31, 2022, extended by six months under OMB M-21-20 for year-ends through June 30, 2021. Claremont's federal expenditures for those years are not stated on the record, so whether a single audit was required for each is not confirmed here; this flag stays at MEDIUM for that reason, matching the August 17 and 24 pages.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2022 CFR edition ($750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2022 CFR edition (earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); OMB M-21-20, App. 3 sec. IX (six-month delay through June 30, 2021 year-ends)

MEDIUM Federal grant management: $764,000 of Claremont awards not yet reimbursable three weeks before the September 30 deadline, and an administration that calls its own process “not a good process”

The assistant superintendent for finance reported $828,000 of Claremont grant money unspent or unreimbursed, of which $764,000 had no invoice behind it, with the grants “closing out the end of September” (0:38:40, 0:39:08, 0:56:41). Title I balances could carry over; Titles II, IV and V would be lost (0:40:01, 0:40:49). He described the cause as a control failure: activities should be approved by the state by August and executed, “That has not been going on in the past” (0:44:18), “Apparently, we have found over the years many ways not to have it done.” (0:48:08). Federal education formula funds are available for obligation for the award year and one additional year (the Tydings period), after which they lapse, and a recipient must maintain effective internal control over each federal award. Mitigation on the record: $1.1 million was claimed in the previous four months (0:38:10), the 2022-23 grants had been budgeted and approved in advance (0:56:26), COVID program cancellations explain part of the balances (0:59:00), and unspent grant money does not raise the local tax rate (1:00:44). Not graded HIGH because the final lapse amount after September 30 is not in this record.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §1225(b) (GEPA Tydings Amendment: funds remain available one additional fiscal year); 2 CFR 200.303(a), 2022 CFR edition (effective internal control over the federal award)

OBSERVATION The special-meeting vote cites RSA 197:3, and the reserve recommendation depends on the fund's own terms

The agenda and the motion cite RSA 197:3 (1:29:49). As codified in 2022, RSA 197:3 requires any special-meeting vote to raise or appropriate money to be by ballot with ballots cast equal to at least half the district's voters, and lets a board petition the superior court for permission to hold a special meeting in an emergency. RSA 197:3-a separately lets the governing body of a district that uses the official ballot (SB 2) form under RSA 40:13 hold such a meeting in a single session, after consultation with the budget committee, with a public hearing at least 14 days before and a posted warrant; Claremont is an SB 2 district. The record does not show which route counsel advised (the business office said it would ask, 1:30:21), and the “30 day notice” stated on the night (1:28:54) was not matched to a statute here. Separately, the recommendation to place the aid in the capital reserve rested on the statement that “It doesn't get spent without the approval of the board.” (1:23:34); under RSA 35:15, I trustees hold capital reserve funds until the district votes to withdraw them or has named agents to expend them, so the board's authority depends on the warrant article that created the fund, which is not in this record. Both points are for a reader to check against the special meeting's warrant.

Sources: RSA 197:3, 2022 codification (special meetings; ballot and one-half turnout; emergency petition; source note ends 2021, 77:1); RSA 197:3-a, 2022 codification (single-session special meeting for official-ballot districts; hearing at least 14 days prior; source 2013, 197:1); RSA 35:15, I (trustees hold capital reserve funds until the district votes to withdraw or has named agents; source note ends 2021, 105:2, eff. Aug. 30, 2021)

OBSERVATION Twenty-four teachers “hired between meetings” were elected by the board the day after school opened

RSA 189:39 provides that “Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers”. The superintendent described the 24 as “hired between meetings” (1:08:55), and the board elected them by a single motion on September 7, the day after classes began (1:16:09). The record does not say whether any of them taught before the vote; offers made subject to board election are common, and the board did elect them. Noted because the order of events, as described, put the hiring before the election the statute assigns to the board.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (superintendents nominate, school boards elect all teachers; source note ends 1971, 371:2)

POSITIVE The collective-bargaining session was disclosed on the agenda

Strategy or negotiations with respect to collective bargaining are excluded from the definition of a meeting under RSA 91-A:2, I(a), so the statute requires no notice of such a session. The district listed it on the public agenda anyway, with its time and the statutory basis, and the vice chair gave a one-line public status of the teacher negotiations (0:36:29). The agenda numbers the exclusion “(2)”, where the statute letters it (a).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I(a), 2022 codification (“Strategy or negotiations with respect to collective bargaining” excluded from “meeting”); Claremont School Board Agenda 9.7.22.pdf
