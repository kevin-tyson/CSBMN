Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer’s aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on and links the primary source; where no verified rule applies, the item is an observation. Severity: 0 high, 2 medium, 5 observations, 3 positive.

MEDIUM Both nonpublic sessions were sealed for one year with no RSA 91-A:3, III determination stated, on the recording or in either set of minutes

RSA 91-A:3, III makes nonpublic minutes and decisions public within 72 hours unless a recorded two-thirds vote, taken in public session, determines that disclosure would likely affect a non-member’s reputation, render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism. The determination is the condition for sealing. On the recording the first seal motion is only “Make a motion that we seal the minutes for one year” (Sprague, 0:15:26), first carried by voice and then re-taken by roll call after a point of order from Noelle Kronberg, who was appointed clerk later that evening: “We actually need a roll call. Vote to seal the minutes” (0:16:21). The second is “I’d like to make a motion to seal the minutes for one year” (Whitney, 1:00:41), carried by roll call. Neither motion names any of the three statutory grounds, and the draft and approved minutes record the motions and votes the same way, without a determination.

Mitigating facts: the first session was entered under RSA 91-A:3, II(c) (reputation) according to both sets of minutes, a ground that maps onto one of the ¶III determinations, so the omission may be one of recording rather than substance; and the second, under II(a) (compensation of named special educators), plausibly fits the reputation limb. The page cannot test that, because the board stated no finding. The first motion’s lettered ground is also not legible in the transcript (“Is it going to be RSA 91-A3 comma two”, 0:13:54; “say 91-331”, 0:14:02); the minutes supply II(c). Graded MEDIUM as a documentation gap, as the project’s anchor catalogue classifies a seal vote without the finding.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III (2023 codification: motion states the exemption; seal needs a recorded 2/3 vote and a determination); Approved minutes, 11/15/23; Draft minutes, 11/15/23

OBSERVATION The minutes and the recording disagree on several points the minutes exist to record

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to record final decisions and the names of the members who made or seconded each motion (in force since January 1, 2019). Points where the record differs: (1) Clerk appointment seconder. Both sets of minutes say Jennifer Gallagher seconded; the dialogue file attributes “I’ll second” (0:32:15) to Whitney Skillen, a cluster it rates medium confidence. The recording does not settle it. (2) Deliberative-session window. The minutes say the superintendent stated a window of February 1st–10th; on tape he said “the window opens the third through the 10th” (0:41:24). The tape is right: RSA 40:13, III sets the first session between the first and second Saturdays after the last Monday in January, which for 2024 was February 3–10. (3) Stale date. After the board voted to hold the session on Saturday, February 3 (0:46:33), both sets of minutes still list Deliberative Session 2/7/24 under Future Dates, and so do the December 6 agenda and approved minutes. (4) First seal vote. The draft minutes posted November 21 omit the vote to seal the first nonpublic session; the December 6 minutes record Arlene Hawkins’s correction adding it, and the approved text carries it. (5) Second nonpublic exit. The minutes give Candace Crawford as seconder; the dialogue file labels the “Second” at 1:00:25 Arlene Hawkins. (6) Interest figure. Mary Henry said the sweep account “made $6,300 in the month of October” (0:39:20); the minutes record about $6,300 earned since the change to a sweep account.

Items (1) and (5) depend on voice attribution and may be the transcript’s error rather than the minutes’. Items (2) to (4) are errors or omissions in the documents themselves, and (4) was corrected through the approval process, which is how it should work.

OBSERVATION The special-education stipend MOU was sent back to the union without a public motion, and the record does not say whether either MOU is a cost item

After the nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a), the chair announced: “Given the discussion that we held in public, we are choosing to, at this time, request that Mr. Contessa take the MoU, agreement request back to the union for reconsideration” (1:01:01). Contessa is a standing ASR garble of Tempesta, and the approved minutes word the basis as the discussion in non-public, which fits the sequence. No motion or vote on the special-education MOU appears on the tape or in the minutes. RSA 91-A:3, III requires decisions reached in nonpublic session to be disclosed; the announcement does that. What is missing is a recorded board action on an agenda item labeled for a vote.

Both MOUs are side agreements to the SREA contract (“MoU are basically short term one year agreements”, 0:47:56). Under RSA 273-A:1, IV a cost item is a benefit acquired through collective bargaining whose implementation requires an appropriation, and under RSA 273-A:3, II(b) only cost items go to the legislative body. Tempesta described the athletic-director stipend as replacing a full-time position (0:55:43), which suggests it is paid from existing appropriations, but nobody said so in terms. Superintendent Tempesta also called these “the first” such MOUs (0:52:41).

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, II(a) and III; RSA 273-A:1, IV (cost item); RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (cost items to the legislative body)

OBSERVATION About $300,000 of a $2.56 million ESSER II allocation went unclaimed

Business Administrator Mary Henry reported that all claims on FY22 grants open in FY23 had to be filed by November 14, and that ESSER II, “2.56 is what it was”, ended “just about 300,000” short (0:38:25): “We lost. Well, we’ll say lost because we hadn’t written anything for it” (0:37:18). The superintendent: “It’s not like it wasn’t allocated. We just didn’t get the hires” (0:38:05). Federal formula funds carry over for one additional fiscal year under the Tydings Amendment and then lapse; leaving funds unspent is not a violation. The figure is recorded here because it is the district’s own statement of federal money forgone, in a year when the board was building its first budget without ESSER positions.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §1225(b) (one additional year of availability)

OBSERVATION The explanation that every opt-out counts as a failing score is broader than the federal rule

Asked whether all students must take the state test, Assistant Superintendent Koski said: “There’s a parent opt out for the state test. It does count against the school grade. It’s not a blank. It counts as a as a non passing score” (0:25:39). Under ESSA the State measures at least 95 percent of students, and for proficiency it uses a denominator of the greater of 95 percent of students or the number participating. Opt-outs therefore count as non-proficient only to the extent participation falls below 95 percent. The board then discussed tracking opt-out counts (0:27:10), which is the right data for testing this.

New Hampshire’s own accountability plan may apply the rule in more detail; this page has not verified it. The point is that the statement on tape is not the whole rule.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §6311(c)(4)(E)(i)–(ii) (95% participation; denominator rule)
