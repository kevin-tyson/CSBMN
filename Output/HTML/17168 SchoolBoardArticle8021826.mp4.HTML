MEDIUM The article being heard departs from the ballot wording RSA 32:5-f, IV prescribes, and it goes to the ballot regardless

RSA 32:5-f, IV prescribes the adoption question with a blank for "_____ dollars per pupil cost" and an inflation index "as of October 1" (statute text). The article as the chair read it uses "the 2526 fiscal year per pupil cost" (0:00:28) and takes the index "as of January 1st" (0:01:16). Counsel's view, read aloud, addresses the first departure: "Changing a dollar amount to a fiscal year reference is not permitted under the statute." (0:02:49). The second is not mentioned. To the question "Why is it staying on the ballot?" (0:15:24) the answer heard is "no choice": RSA 40:13, VI places every warrant article on the official ballot, and the one amendment offered at the deliberative session failed (see the February 7 page). The petitioners wrote the wording; whether a cap adopted in this form would be enforceable is the question counsel left open (0:04:25).

Sources: RSA 32:5-f, IV (prescribed wording); 2025, 183:6, 7, eff. Sept. 13, 2025; RSA 40:13, VI (all warrant articles to the official ballot)

POSITIVE The budget-cap hearing fell inside the statutory window, and the chair read counsel's view to the public

RSA 32:5-f, III requires a public hearing on the budget-cap question "at least 15 days, but not more than 30 days, before the question is to be voted on" (statute text): February 8 to 23 for the March 10 vote. This hearing, on February 18, falls inside that window. The chair read the article as it would appear on the ballot and then counsel's written view, including that he would "likely advise the board that it is unenforceable" (0:04:25), so voters heard the legal problem before the vote. Every public speaker was heard, and the hearing closed only after the chair asked for further speakers (0:48:59).

Sources: RSA 32:5-f, III (hearing 15 to 30 days before the vote)
