Claremont School Board, March 6, 2024

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, all seven members present, six days before the March 12, 2024 election. After a public hearing the board votes to move $420,000 of retained fund balance into the FY2024 general fund against special-education overruns, so that the year-end surplus offsets the FY2025 tax rate, having first rejected a two-week postponement 1 to 5 with one abstention. It hears the Youth CAN, Turning Points and TLC presentation of 2021 Youth Risk Behavior Survey data, approves the eighth-grade Boston trip, adopts nursing-mothers policy ACN on a single reading and receives the draft by-laws. MEDIUM The board was told retained fund balance could be spent only on unanticipated expenses; that limit belongs to a different paragraph of the statute

The agenda and the motion describe the $420,000 as "Retained Fund Balance" (agenda; minutes). From the chair's opening the money is also called "our contingency fund" (0:05:50), and the chair argued against postponing the vote from "the RSA related to contingency funds": "We can't assign these funds to anything. It has to be unanticipated expenses" (0:09:35), "we can only use it for unintended expenses. So there will be no guarantee next year" (0:21:07). Gallagher repeated it before the final vote (0:33:51). RSA 198:4-b has two separate mechanisms. Paragraph I is a contingency fund established "annually by an article separate from the budget" for "unanticipated expenses". Paragraph II is retained year-end unassigned general funds, capped at 5 percent of the net assessment, and it states no purpose limitation; what it requires is that "Prior to expending retained general funds, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing", with newspaper notice at least 7 days ahead. The board did hold the hearing. But the reason given for refusing a two-week delay rested on paragraph I's limit, which does not govern paragraph II money, and the postponement then failed one to five. The board was free to act either way; the defect is the legal premise it was given.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, I and II(a)–(b) (2023 codification; source note ends 2020, 38:25, eff. Sept. 27, 2020); agenda, item III; approved minutes, item III

MEDIUM Policy ACN was adopted as "Required by Law" citing a state law not yet in force and a federal section that does not exist

The packet's policy ACN (Exhibit C) is headed "Category: Priority (Required by Law)" and lists as its authority "RSA 275:78-83" and "42 U.S.C. 218d", among others. The board adopted it on a single reading (1:41:06). RSA 275:78, the New Hampshire nursing-mothers section, carries the source note "2023, 191:20, eff. July 1, 2025": on March 6, 2024 it had been enacted but was not yet in force. The federal break-time requirement in force then was the PUMP Act, codified at 29 U.S.C. §218d (Pub. L. 117-328, Dec. 29, 2022); title 42 has no section 218d (42 U.S.C. §218 concerns the National Advisory Council on Migrant Health). The substance of the policy is consistent with the federal floor and in one respect exceeds it (flag 8), so the defect is in the citations a reader would use to check the policy, not in what the policy requires. Skillen described it as the same policy already adopted for the SAU board, so the same citations may sit in that policy too.

Sources: RSA 275:78 (source note: 2023, 191:20, eff. July 1, 2025); 29 U.S.C. §218d (PUMP Act); Exhibit C, policy ACN

OBSERVATION The newspaper notice the retained-funds hearing required is not in the record examined

RSA 198:4-b, II(a) requires notice of the time, place and subject of the hearing to "be published in a newspaper of general circulation in the relevant municipality at least 7 days before the meeting is held", that is by February 28, 2024. The hearing appears on the agenda, whose packet folder was created February 29. No newspaper notice is in the packet or the minutes, and no one mentions one on the recording. Notices of this kind are not normally filed in the packet, so this is a gap in what could be checked, not evidence that notice was missed.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II(a) (2023 codification)

OBSERVATION The walk-through was capped at three to avoid a board quorum, but three may be a quorum of the subcommittee

The chair limited the Maple Avenue walk-through to "no more than three" because more "would be considered" a meeting (1:47:46); the minutes read "No more than 3 can attend or it would be considered a quorum". Three of seven is below the board's quorum, so the limit works for the full board. The walk-through, though, was a Capital Improvement subcommittee activity with its chair (Miles), Sprague and Crawford attending, and when the board reread its subcommittee assignments on March 20, 2024, Capital Improvement was Miles, Sprague and Crawford, with Skillen stepping off. A subcommittee is itself a public body, and a quorum of it convened to discuss its business is a meeting. Whether the March 13 walk-through was noticed, and whether it involved discussion rather than a tour, is not in the record examined.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) ("or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto"); RSA 91-A:2, I (2023 codification)

POSITIVE The nursing-mothers policy pays hourly employees for nursing periods, which federal law does not require

Policy ACN, section C, provides that "A nursing mother who is an hourly employee will be paid during nursing periods", and section D requires a private place other than a bathroom in each building. Federal law requires reasonable break time and a non-bathroom space, but 29 U.S.C. §218d(b) says an employer "shall not be required to compensate an employee receiving reasonable break time" unless other law requires it. Paying for those periods goes beyond the federal floor in force on March 6, 2024.

Sources: 29 U.S.C. §218d(a)(1), (b); Exhibit C, policy ACN
