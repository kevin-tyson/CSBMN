Claremont School Board, May 1, 2019

Two public hearings preceded the regular meeting. The first covered E-Rate category two reimbursements (building amounts read as $60,720.67, $65,174.46, $9,899.10 and $56.15) and a $25,000 bequest to Stevens High School from a 1958 graduate; the acting superintendent said hearing E-Rate money was a procedure the district was now correcting. The second explained that the warrant, budget and default budget for the March 12 annual meeting had not been posted by the January 28 deadline, so voters would be asked on May 15 to ratify the results. The board then accepted the bequest, tabled a Primex rate-cap agreement whose response date had passed, heard a System of Care grant update and discussed school-funding advocacy. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on May 1, 2019, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The 2019 warrant, budget and default budget were not posted by the January 28 deadline; the March 12 results needed ratification

The acting superintendent reported that on April 9 she was notified, and the chair said the board had "learned from the Attorney General's office" and the Department of Revenue Administration, that the MS-26 budget form, the warrant and the default budget "were not posted in accordance with RSA 32 colon five" and that the signed final warrant "wasn't put in posted in the correct spot by the deadline", which was January 28 (0:13:35, 0:13:54, 0:14:38, 0:15:02).

RSA 40:13, II-a(d), in the 2018 codification, provides that "Warrants under RSA 39:5 and RSA 197:7 and budgets shall be posted and copies available to the general public on or before the last Monday in January" (statute's wording), which was January 28, 2019. RSA 32:5, VII, in the same vintage, requires the governing body to post certified copies of the budget with the warrant and, in an official-ballot district, certified copies of the default budget form with the proposed budget and warrant. A missed statutory posting deadline, confirmed by two state agencies, is HIGH under this project's grading.

The board's response follows the cure the statutes provide. RSA 40:16 lets an official-ballot district call a special meeting "for the exclusive purpose of curing such defect according to RSA 31:5-b", and RSA 31:5-b reaches "minor procedural irregularities" including failures of statutory notice timing; it requires a public hearing at least seven days before the curing meeting, at which the reasons are explained, and a curing meeting at least 21 days after the original vote. Tonight's hearing (May 1) came 14 days before the May 15 meeting, and that meeting falls more than 21 days after March 12. The defect also bears on the MS-22 certification circulated on April 3 (see the April 3, 2019 page).

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a(d) (2018 codification; posting by the last Monday in January); RSA 32:5, VII(a)-(b) (2018 codification; certified budget and default budget posted with the warrant); RSA 40:16 (2019 codification; curing meeting in official-ballot subdivisions); RSA 31:5-b (2019 codification; minor procedural irregularities; hearing 7 days before; meeting 21 days after the vote)

MEDIUM By the acting superintendent's account, E-Rate money had for years been taken in without the hearing the unanticipated-funds statute requires

Explaining why E-Rate reimbursements were "unanticipated", the acting superintendent said the district had received such funds "for as long as I can remember" and that "This is one of the procedural processes that we are correcting by actually having the public hearing to accept these funds instead of just accepting the revenue." (0:02:47). RSA 198:20-b, III(a), in the 2019 codification, requires a prior public hearing, with newspaper notice at least seven days ahead, before a board acts on unanticipated funds of $5,000 or more, and ¶I conditions the board's authority on a district article. The category two payment heard tonight runs well above $5,000 (the building amounts read add to $135,850.38, although the total was transcribed as "We received $133.38.", 0:00:49); the annual category one amounts are not stated.

Mitigation is on the record: the district identified the practice itself and held the hearing tonight. The board's later vote, however, accepted only the bequest (0:34:52); no motion to accept the E-Rate money is on the tape. Whether past amounts were each $5,000 or more, and whether the district had adopted the enabling article, would settle the earlier years.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2019 codification; prior public hearing with seven days' newspaper notice at $5,000 or more)

MEDIUM The regular meeting was called to order about nine minutes before the time in its posted notice

After the second hearing the acting superintendent checked the notice: "It says seven in the posting." and "We can sit in awkward silence for nine minutes." She offered the chair the choice to start, a member called it "a very good, very good choice", and the chair called the meeting to order (0:19:03 to 0:19:20). The board then amended its agenda, approved two sets of minutes and heard the student report.

RSA 91-A:2, II requires notice "of the time and place" of each meeting (2019 codification). A body that begins business before the noticed time defeats the notice for anyone who relies on it. Mitigation: the hearings had been noticed for 6:30, the audience was already in the room, no citizen came forward when comment was invited (0:34:13), and the early business was routine. The minutes would show the time of call to order. MEDIUM as a process gap.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; notice of the time and place of each meeting)
