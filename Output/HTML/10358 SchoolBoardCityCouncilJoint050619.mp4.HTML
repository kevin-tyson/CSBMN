Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting, May 6, 2019

Quarterly joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council: the city manager and superintendent searches, a state lead-poisoning pilot, a Division for Children, Youth and Families (DCYF) presentation on the foster-care shortage, the syringe service program, state education funding, mental health, voter turnout, and a proposal to share services between the city and the schools. No votes were taken except adjournment. The page is built from the recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. OBSERVATION The exchange on who pays to transport children in foster care described the law of an earlier period

Asked whether the school district is “on the hook to pay for that service” when a child is placed in another town (0:34:13), the DCYF worker answered Yes, ma'am. and the chair added “Technically they're homeless. Yes.” (0:34:31). By this date federal law no longer treated children awaiting foster care placement as homeless: the Every Student Succeeds Act struck “awaiting foster care placement” from the McKinney-Vento definition, effective one year after enactment (December 10, 2016) in most states and two years after in states whose own statutes used the phrase. In its place, Title I's state-plan provisions require that a child in foster care enroll or remain in the school of origin unless that is not in the child's best interest, and a district receiving Title I funds must have written procedures on how transportation to the school of origin “will be provided, arranged, and funded”. Where transport adds cost, the district provides it if the child welfare agency agrees to reimburse it, the district agrees to pay, or the two agree to share. So the answer is not a simple yes: payment turns on an agreement between the district and DCYF. Whether Claremont had such written procedures in 2019 is not in this record; they are the document that would settle it.

Sources: 42 U.S.C. 11434a(2)(B)(i) and note on the 2015 amendment (Pub. L. 114-95, sec. 9105); 20 U.S.C. 6311(g)(1)(E) (school-of-origin presumption); 20 U.S.C. 6312(c)(5) (transportation procedures and cost)
