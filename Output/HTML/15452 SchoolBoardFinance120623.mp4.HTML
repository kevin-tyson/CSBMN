Claremont School Board Finance Committee, December 6, 2023

An afternoon working session of the board's Finance Committee on the proposed FY2025 budget, held hours before the regular board meeting that evening, with two of its three members present. The buildings-and-grounds director answers on each school's maintenance lines; the special-education director presents out-of-district placements, contracted services and three coordinator proposals that are not yet in the budget; and the business administrator reports a special-education salary line already $100,000 over this year. HIGH A quorum of the Finance Committee met on the budget with no posted notice, agenda or minutes in any district share

RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes any committee of a school board a public body ("or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof", statute), and two of the committee's three members are a quorum of it. RSA 91-A:2, II requires a notice posted "at least 24 hours, excluding Sundays and legal holidays, prior to such meetings" and minutes "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute). The date and hour were set aloud five days earlier on the December 1 recording, which is announcement, not posting; the same evening's board agenda lists no Finance Committee session. MAP.md records no notice, packet or minutes in either share (checked 2026-08-28, including the next board meeting's folder and keyword searches). That evening the board's approved minutes say only that "Sessions have been recorded on CCTV" (minutes' wording); a public recording is not the minutes the statute requires. Graded HIGH under the project's rule for unmitigated absences: nothing on the recording or in any document says minutes were produced, and the deadline passed years ago. This matches the grading of the committee's December 1 and December 18 sessions.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (committees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification; notice and five-business-day minutes); approved board minutes, December 6, 2023, item 4(b); board agenda, December 6, 2023

MEDIUM The prior-year budget was built from filled positions only, and a special-education salary line is already $100,000 over

The special-education director explains that last year's budget "did not account for positions that were needed or vacant. It simply rolled over, filled positions" (0:51:55), so needed but vacant posts were left out (0:52:22). The business administrator then reports five people now in the Stevens special-education salary line and "Right now in salaries, we are $100,000 over in that line." (0:54:58). Other lines show the same weakness: a water-and-sewer history its own author doubts (0:05:52), prior-year postings that "need to be moved back into 22" (0:07:19), a supply line for a long-closed program (0:57:04) and a social worker counted twice (1:18:02). Crawford's summary is that "the last year's budget numbers are not reliable" (0:41:11). RSA 32:5, IV requires the budget form to show prior-year appropriations and expenditures for comparison (statute), and RSA 32:8 bars spending for a purpose with no appropriation while RSA 32:10 permits transfers within the total, so a single line overrun is not shown to be a breach. A comparison base that omits known needs and that members call unreliable is a control weakness in the document they were using to set the FY2025 budget. The committee's December 1 session found a negative $688,426.17 line in the same base (see that page).

Sources: RSA 32:5, IV (2023 codification; comparative columns); RSA 32:8 (limitation on expenditures); RSA 32:10 (transfers); Finance Committee, December 1, 2023 page, flag 2

OBSERVATION Questions about a position and its holder are answered "afterward", off the record

Asked about the qualifications behind the preschool director line, the special-education director says "I can't say that on air, candy." (1:04:33) and later "Speak to that on air. I'm happy to afterward." (1:07:17). Keeping an individual employee's details out of a broadcast is reasonable. But the two members present are a quorum of the committee, and if they received that information together after the camera stopped, RSA 91-A:2, I would treat it as part of a meeting. The lawful route for matters that would harm a person's reputation is a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(c), entered on a motion stating the exemption and a roll-call vote under 91-A:3, I(b), with minutes. Nothing on the record shows that any such conversation took place, so this is an observation.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2023 codification; definition of a meeting); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II(c) (nonpublic sessions)
