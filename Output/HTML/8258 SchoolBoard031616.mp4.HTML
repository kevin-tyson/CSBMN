Claremont School Board — March 16, 2016

Organizational and regular meeting summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. Three newly elected members were sworn in, Brian Rapp was elected chair 4-3 over Becky Ferland and Chris Irish vice chair, the clerk read the March 8 election results, and after nearly two hours of discussion and parent testimony the board adopted by roll call the administration's plan to end the tech center's early childhood program and move the district preschool into its space. The pre-employment drug testing policy passed first reading. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION The roll call that ended the tech center early childhood program is only partly audible; the tally is not on the record

Irish's motion "that we move forward with the recommendation proposed by the administration" (3:00:42) went to a roll call at a member's request. The recording captures three no votes (Adrian, Brent Ferland, Becky Ferland), a yes after a garbled name (Rosanna), Pierce's yes, one further yes, and the declaration Passes at 3:02:16. That is six audible answers for a seven-member board with everyone present; there is a gap of about 29 seconds between the fourth and fifth answers. A 4-3 result is consistent with the three recorded no votes, but the names of all four yes votes are not on the tape, and no minutes are available online to supply them. For the most consequential and most contested decision of the evening, the public record reachable today does not show how each member voted.

Sources: No verified rule requires a tally beyond the minutes' "final decisions"; see RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification; in force for all of 2016): minutes include names of members, persons appearing, and a brief description of the subject matter and final decisions.

OBSERVATION The superintendent told a member that September hires would likely be drug tested "even though the school board hadn't approved it"

The pre-employment drug testing policy was before the board only for a first reading. When Sprague asked that it be fast-tracked because positions were already posted, the superintendent answered at 3:08:06: "it's very likely you're going to have a pre-employment drug test even though the school board hadn't approved it." Two readings are possible: that the policy would be adopted by the time September hires were processed, or that the administration intended to test regardless of board action. Earlier in the same meeting the superintendent had described board policy that makes "policy making it the primary function of the school board" and its execution the superintendent's (0:23:05). The board approved the first reading as amended and set the second reading for April 6, so the policy's adoption falls on a later page. Recorded because the sentence, taken literally, describes a practice ahead of the governing policy.

Sources: No verified statute; the district's own policy as described on the recording at 0:23:05.

OBSERVATION "There are no specific line items": the chair's answer matched the transfer statute as it read in 2016

A parent argued that voters had approved "$236,000 as a budget for the Tech center program" and that moving it was "a quarter million dollar reallocation of funds" (2:12:13). The chair answered at 2:13:34: "Budgets are total budgets and there are no specific line items. It can be reallocated." In 2016 the operating budget was a single appropriation (Article 2, $34,131,283, on the deliberative session page), and RSA 32:10, I, in its 2004 text, let the governing body "transfer to that appropriation an unexpended balance remaining in some other appropriation", provided total spending stayed within the total appropriated and the records let any citizen "ascertain the purposes of appropriations to which, and from which, amounts have been transferred." The chair's statement is consistent with that text. Readers should not apply the later rule: 2017, 127:4 (effective August 15, 2017) added a paragraph making "purpose" include individual line items, which is not the law this board worked under.

Sources: RSA 32:10, I and I(a)-(b) (2016 codification; source note ends 2004, 113:1, so in force until Aug. 15, 2017).

OBSERVATION The recommendation rested on federal-law and state-directive premises that were stated but not documented, and speakers disagreed about what least restrictive environment requires

The administration's case had three legal premises. First, IDEA: the district lacked room to serve identified three- and four-year-olds (1:07:38) and the plan "it'll put us in compliance with the federal law" (1:11:23). The statute does require a free appropriate public education for children with disabilities and education "To the maximum extent appropriate" with non-disabled children. Second, a state directive: LeClair said the district "received direction from the Department of Education to change the CTE programing" (1:41:35) and the chair said "we're in noncompliance" (2:42:35); the tech center director cited new state guidelines (2:43:42). Third, certification: the superintendent said identified children cannot be mixed into the program without special education licensure (1:51:22).

None of the state documents was read into the record or is available online, and the state rule the superintendent cited (Ed 1105, as transcribed) has not been verified by this project. On least restrictive environment, a parent who works in preschool special education said a child's "natural environment is their least restrictive environment" (2:20:06), and the assistant superintendent agreed that was "also accurate" while holding that a district-run program needs dual certification (2:25:03). A reader should treat the premises as the administration's characterization, supported in general terms by IDEA, with the specific state requirements untested.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. 1412(a)(1)(A), (a)(3)(A) and (a)(5)(A) (FAPE ages 3 through 21; child find; least restrictive environment; (a)(5)(A) not amended after 2004).

POSITIVE New members were briefed on the open meeting law, and the chair urged a separate account for board email
