Claremont School Board, March 16, 2016

Organizational meeting after the March 8 election: three members sworn in, Brian Rapp elected chair 4-3 and Chris Irish vice chair, the election results read, and a roll-call vote adopting the administration's early education recommendation, which moves the district's preschool for identified children into the technical center and, as families and members described it, closes the center's tuition-funded child care program, after more than an hour of comment from parents and staff. Also a first reading of a pre-employment drug-testing policy. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. This meeting elected the board's officers, accepted the election results and adopted a recommendation that, as discussed, closes a child care program the parents said serves 27 families, and the recording is now the only public account. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force in 2016, required minutes including "names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the superintendent refers to the agenda and a packet (0:00:51, 0:19:33), and the board approves the March 2 minutes (0:09:08), so minutes were being kept. MEDIUM The administration told the board its preschool for identified children was not in compliance with the federal least-restrictive-environment rule

The superintendent described "the fact that we have not been able to comply with Ida" (1:07:16) and said the plan "it'll put us in compliance with the federal law. Because right now, if a parent of a child who's identified says you are not following ID, their correct" (1:11:23). Asked whether the Maple Avenue preschool meets the standard, a member answered that it does not "because it doesn't have the other students", and the superintendent added "It's mostly sped children." (1:44:12, 1:44:14). IDEA requires that "To the maximum extent appropriate, children with disabilities ... are educated with children who are not disabled". An administration's own statement that a program falls short of that requirement is a probable compliance gap, graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the board acted on it the same night and the remedy is a plan for the next school year, not a finding by any agency. OBSERVATION Two different accounts of what state law allows in the technical center building

The chair said the state "has acknowledged that it's not currently being used what it's meant for. So we're in noncompliance." (2:42:35). The center's director said new state guidelines permit district programs in a center "so long as it doesn't displace a C to E program. So the proposal is legal" (2:43:42). RSA 188-E:3, II, in the 2015 codification, provides that a regional career and technical education facility constructed or renovated under that section "shall become the property of the school district or public academy, for use by the career and technical education center exclusively." Whether the Claremont building was built under that section, what the state said about noncompliance, and what the new guidelines say are not established on the recording; neither document was read into the record. The plan places the district's preschool for identified children, not a CTE program, in the building, so the statute's word exclusively and the director's account of the guidelines point in different directions. A reviewer would need the state's written direction and the guidelines to resolve it.

Sources: RSA 188-E:3, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2015, 252:5, eff. July 1, 2015)

OBSERVATION The chair's answer on reallocating the $236,000 technical-center line was broadly right but incomplete

A parent said voters approved "$236,000 as a budget for the Tech center program" and that using it for the preschool move was "maybe not the most honest allocation of my tax dollars" (2:12:58, 2:14:35). The chair answered that "Budgets are total budgets and there are no specific line items. It can be reallocated." (2:13:34). RSA 32:10, as it read in 2016, lets the governing body transfer an unexpended balance from one appropriation to another "If changes arise during the year following the annual meeting", provided the total spent does not exceed the total appropriated, and requires records of the transfers. So a transfer within the operating budget is lawful, but it is the board's act as governing body, and the line-item budget the voters saw remains the public record of intent, which is the point Brent Ferland made about the "credibility to the budgetary process" (2:50:31). The money at issue is in the fiscal 2016-17 budget, which had not yet begun.

Sources: RSA 32:10, I (2016 codification; source note ends 2004, 113:1, so in force until Aug. 15, 2017)

OBSERVATION The medical-marijuana paragraph was struck on a premise the federal disability law does not clearly support

Brent Ferland proposed deleting the policy's paragraph on "the New Hampshire law legalizing medical marijuana" as redundant "given the American disability paragraph" (3:06:00). The chair asked the right question, whether people "being prescribed medical marijuana" would "necessarily fall under American with Disability Act" (3:06:57), and it went unanswered before the paragraph was deleted (3:15:27). The ADA excludes from protection "any employee or applicant who is currently engaging in the illegal use of drugs" (42 U.S.C. §12114(a)) and defines illegal use by reference to the Controlled Substances Act, with an exception for a drug "taken under supervision by a licensed health care professional" (§12111(6)).
