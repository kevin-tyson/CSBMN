Claremont School Board, June 20, 2018

Regular meeting, six days after SAU 6 and the superintendent parted ways: four citizens spoke, including a departing Stevens High School teacher who explained why she was leaving; the business administrator projected a fiscal 2018 fund balance of about $275,032 and described catastrophic aid prorated at 66 to 68%; the board heard reports on extended learning opportunities and the high school scheduling committee, approved the middle school and preschool handbooks, and elected four nominees. OBSERVATION The board's explanation of catastrophic aid gave the wrong state share and treated proration as a departure from the statute

The business administrator told the board that above three and a half times the state average cost per pupil the state would "give you back 50%" (0:32:16), that the state prorates to "about 66 to 68%", and that this happens "even though it's statutorily required that they pay us that." (0:32:47). A member concluded: "The state is not required to fulfill any of their obligations." (0:33:03).

RSA 186-C:18, III, as it read in 2017 and 2018, made the state "liable for 80 percent" of a child's special education costs above three and a half times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil, and for all costs above ten times. The same paragraph provided that when the appropriation is insufficient, payments are "prorated proportionally based on entitlement", and it set no minimum percentage; the 80%-of-entitlement floor some later sources describe was added in 2025. So the share stated at the meeting was low by thirty points, and the proration the board heard described as a broken promise was the mechanism the statute itself prescribed. The retroactive disallowance of non-educational hours (0:30:16) is reported as the speaker's account; this page did not verify the department's rule on it.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2017 codification; source 2017, 156:96-100, eff. July 1, 2017); RSA 186-C:18, III (2016 codification; same 80% share and proration)

OBSERVATION A $170,000 "carryover fund balance" described as available to supplement the return to taxpayers, with no stated authority for holding it

After projecting a fund balance of about $275,032 "which is money that would be returned to the taxpayers", the business administrator added: "We do have some carryover fund balance of 170,000 we can use to supplement that as well." (0:31:20). At the June 6 meeting he had described the same money as surplus from health trust refunds that "we carried over" from year to year, $600,000 at first, then $300,000, now "about 170" (June 6, 2018 page).

In 2018 RSA 198:4-b, II allowed a district's legislative body to authorize the retention of year-end unassigned general funds up to 2.5 percent of the current year's net assessment, for emergencies or to reduce the tax rate. Under GASB Statement 54, amounts are committed or assigned only by the actions the statement describes, and only the general fund reports positive unassigned fund balance. The recording does not say whether the voters granted retention authority, how the $170,000 is classified in the audited statements, or why it remained after the year it arose. Those documents would settle it; with the audits three years behind in June 2018 (see the June 6 page), none was available to the board.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:6; 2.5% retention); GASB Statement 54, paragraphs 6-17 (fund balance classes)

POSITIVE A district committee that posts its minutes and survey results, and handbooks that will carry board members' email links

The Stevens High School scheduling committee, formed under a memorandum of understanding and reporting to the board, told the board that "if you go on to the website, you can always read our minutes" (1:17:17), reported its survey response rates by group (1:05:16, 1:05:31) and said it had added students, a counselor and others when it found stakeholders missing (0:56:24). Whether such a joint committee is an "advisory committee" to the board under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), with the full duties of a public body, is not settled on this record; either way, publishing its minutes meets or exceeds what the statute would require. Separately, the board directed that the preschool handbook, like the middle school one, carry links to board members' email addresses so families can reach them (1:33:42, 1:35:20).

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (source note ends 2008, 354:1; in force in 2018); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification)
