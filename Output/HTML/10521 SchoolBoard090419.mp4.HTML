Claremont School Board, September 4, 2019

First regular meeting of the school year: the board gave final approval to the student handbooks and to the student discipline and due process policy JICD, reappointed Pauline Pelletier to the CCTV board, deferred a $20,000 Byrne Foundation gift to the technical center until a public hearing could be held, set an October 20 budget retreat, and heard State Representative Gary Merchant on the vetoed state budget. Jason Benware asked to step back from the four sets of contract negotiations. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 4, 2019, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM A policy committee member described continuing the committee's deliberations "online"

Asked what had changed in JICD, an unidentified member speaking for the policy committee said "we continued the discussions in our subcommittee online to try and update this as much as possible" and that the members argued over time frames, "we thought this was taking too long, we thought this wasn't enough time", before agreeing "that we needed to meet one last time" (0:28:26). On August 21 the committee's members were named as Lovett, Towle and Zullo (August 21 page). A board committee is a public body under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d). RSA 91-A:2-a, I, unchanged since 2008, provides that public bodies "shall deliberate on matters over which they have supervision, control, jurisdiction, or advisory power only in meetings held pursuant to and in compliance with the provisions of RSA 91-A:2, II or III", and II adds that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". RSA 91-A:2, III(c), in the 2019 codification, adds: "No meeting shall be conducted by electronic mail or any other form of communication that does not permit the public to hear, read, or otherwise discern meeting discussion contemporaneously at the meeting location specified in the meeting notice." Committee members debating a policy's time frames with one another online, outside a noticed meeting, is the kind of exchange those provisions bar. The page grades this MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the record does not show who took part, whether a quorum of the committee exchanged views, or what "online" meant; comments sent only to staff for a draft would be different. The committee's own messages would settle it, and they are public records only if retained.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (source note 2008, 303:4, never amended); RSA 91-A:2, III(c) (2019 codification); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (committees are public bodies)

POSITIVE The board held a $20,000 gift for the public hearing the statute requires

When the superintendent presented "an amazing donation of of $20,000" from the Byrne Foundation for the technical center (0:30:04), the chair asked whether it was "above the threshold" for a public hearing, Benware confirmed the $5,000 figure, and the board deferred acceptance: "We'd have to warn this as a as a public hearing." (0:30:53, 0:31:03, 0:31:08). RSA 198:20-b, III(a), in the 2019 codification, provides that "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with notice in a newspaper of general circulation at least 7 days before. The hearing was held at the start of the September 18 meeting, and the gift was accepted later that evening (show 10537). The record does not show the newspaper notice; the chair on September 18 relied on having warned the item at this meeting. Recognizing the threshold unprompted and waiting for the hearing is the procedure the statute sets.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1; threshold raised to $20,000 only in 2023)
