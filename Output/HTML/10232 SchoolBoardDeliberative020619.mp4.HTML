Claremont School District, Deliberative Session — February 6, 2019

First (deliberative) session of the Claremont School District's annual meeting under the SB 2 official-ballot system, summarized from the CCTV recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no warrant, rules sheet or minutes are online. Voters sent six articles to the March 12 ballot unamended: officers, a $35,293,063 operating budget that carried a three-year administrators' agreement, $100,000 for the Bluff roof, $80,000 for the Disnard fire panel, $249,950 for district-wide emergency radios, and $51,000 for bus cameras. The session lasted about an hour. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the session against the state law in force on February 6, 2019. A deliberative session is a meeting of the voters, so school-board rules (public-comment periods, nonpublic sessions, roll calls) do not apply to it; RSA chapter 40 as extended to school districts does. They are not findings of violation and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Six flags follow, ordered by severity. No HIGH or MEDIUM flag was found.

OBSERVATION No minutes, warrant or rules sheet for this session are online

RSA 197:20 makes the district clerk keep "a true record of all the doings of each meeting". The district's public Drive shares begin in 2022 and hold nothing for this session (MAP.md section 92, checked September 26, 2026). That is a limit of what the district posts online, not evidence that no record was kept: at this session the voters approved the minutes of the February 8, 2018 deliberative session (0:05:34, 0:06:04), which shows the clerk was keeping them, and the 2019 minutes would in the ordinary course have been approved at the 2020 session. The warrant and the rules-of-procedure handout the moderator referred to (0:02:43) are also not online, so the article texts on this page are as read aloud and transcribed. Graded an observation under the project's rule for missing records, consistent with the 2020 deliberative session page.

Sources: RSA 197:20 (1963, 120:5; unamended).

OBSERVATION The default budget was stated three different ways, and a vote was first taken on a wrong figure

Article 2 as read put the default at "$35,113,750" against a proposed $35,293,063 (0:08:39), a difference of $179,313, matching the business administrator's "$179,000 less" at the January 2 hearing (see the hearing page). The board chair then told voters the default was "$193,340 less than our proposed budget" (0:59:40). The moderator first put the voice vote on "the main budget in the $5,113,757" (0:31:16), then corrected herself and retook the vote on $35,293,063 (0:32:26); the correction was made before moving on, which is the right outcome. RSA 40:13, IX(b), as then in force, defines the default by reference to the prior year's budget, reduced by one-time expenditures and "by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget", and XI(b) provides that the default amount "shall not be amended by the legislative body", so the figure voters see on the ballot comes from the governing body's form, not from the floor. The $14,027 gap between the chair's figure and the article's is unexplained on the record; the ballot and the default budget form, neither online, would show which was right. The transcript's rendering of amounts is itself imperfect (Benware's later reading of the default is transcribed as 35,113 7050 at 0:11:52).

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI (2018 codification, in force until 2019, 192:2, eff. July 10, 2019).

OBSERVATION Voice votes were taken on every article, though every article goes to the ballot regardless

The moderator correctly told voters that articles "do not require a motion to be passed on to the voters" and are "automatically presented" to the March 12 ballot (0:02:43, 0:03:05). RSA 40:13, VI, as then in force, provides: "All warrant articles shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote, including warrant articles as amended by the first session." She nonetheless called a voice vote on each article (for example 0:07:39, 0:38:51, 0:57:05). No amendment was offered to any article, so nothing turned on these votes, and a voter could reasonably have taken a loud "nay" on Article 6 (0:57:05) as having some effect. The same practice appears on the 2020 page. Noted only.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and VI (2018 codification).

OBSERVATION District administrators spoke in favor of the radio article

A speaker the CSV identifies, with uncertainty, as the Stevens principal told the session "I'm speaking to fellow administrators. This is a great need." and asked voters to "recognize that this is not that amount of money for just radios" (0:43:00, 0:43:57). The technology director spoke for the same article, identifying himself as a Ward 1 resident (0:45:09). RSA 659:44-a, in force from January 1, 2017, provides that "No public employee, as defined in RSA 273-A:1, IX, shall electioneer while in the performance of his or her official duties", and defines electioneering as acting "in any way specifically designed to influence the vote of a voter on any question or office". Two things cut against applying it here. RSA 40:13, IV makes the first session one of "explanation, discussion, and debate of each warrant article", and the administrators were introduced at the outset as present in their roles (0:01:08), so explaining the district's need is part of what the session is for. And whether a principal speaking at the session is "in the performance of" official duties, and whether these remarks were "specifically designed to influence the vote" rather than to explain it, are not settled by the record or by any source this page verified. Elected board members presenting their own articles fall outside the statute (RSA 273-A:1, IX(a)). Recorded for the reader, not as a finding.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I and III (source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 40:13, IV (2018 codification).

POSITIVE The administrators' agreement went to the voters with its cost for each year and a plain statement of what a yes vote means

Article 2 as read stated that the operating budget "includes the sum necessary to fund the cost items" of "a three year collective bargaining agreement reached between the Claremont School Board and the Claremont Administrators Association", with estimated increases of 17,789, 31,197 and about 32,000 for the three years (0:09:25, 0:09:52), and that a favorable vote approves those costs. Benware then restated it: "If voters approved the budget this year, it means they are also approving the increases to the budget for this agreement for the following two years." (0:11:52). RSA 273-A:3, II(b) provides that "Only cost items shall be submitted to the legislative body" for approval; putting the year-by-year cost before the voters is what that provision is built around. The board had approved the agreement on December 19 without stating its cost (see that page); here the cost reached the voters in full. Embedding the items in the operating budget rather than a separate article means a default-budget outcome also defeats the agreement's cost items, a consequence the board did not spell out.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (source note ends 2013, 244:1).

POSITIVE Rules read, last year's minutes approved by the voters, reconsideration restricted as the statute allows, and the session held inside its window

The moderator read the rules, including written amendments and the secret-ballot procedure (0:02:43, 0:03:31, 0:04:42), asked a questioner to use the microphone so everyone could hear (0:47:50), and put the 2018 session's minutes to the voters (0:05:34). Reconsideration of Articles 1 and 2 and then of Articles 3 through 6 was restricted by vote (0:32:54, 0:57:21); RSA 40:10, I allows a meeting "at any time during a meeting, and without notice in the warrant" to "restrict reconsideration of any one or more votes previously taken at that meeting", and ¶V applies it to school district meetings. RSA 40:13, III, as then in force, placed the first session "between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January"; the last Monday of January 2019 was January 28, so the window was February 2 to 9, and February 6 falls inside it. The board chair's closing advocacy for the budget (0:57:57) was by an elected official, whom RSA 659:44-a does not reach (see flag 4).

Sources: RSA 40:10, I and V (1996, 64:1; unamended); RSA 40:13, III (2018 codification).
