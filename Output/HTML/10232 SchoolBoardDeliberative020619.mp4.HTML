Claremont School District, Deliberative Session, February 6, 2019

First session of the 2019 annual school district meeting under the official-ballot (SB 2) law: the voters heard the budget subcommittee's presentation of a $35,293,063 operating budget (the $31,828,063 presented at the January hearing plus grant and other offset funds) and sent six warrant articles to the March 12 ballot without amendment. Articles for the Bluff roof, the Disnard fire-alarm panel, district-wide emergency radios and bus cameras were explained by board members, the police chief and SAU staff. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no warrant, rules sheet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. OBSERVATION The warrant's operating budget is $3,465,000 larger than the figure presented at the budget hearing

At the January 2 hearing the board presented an operating budget of $31,828,063, the figure it had voted to send to the hearing on December 19 (January 2, 2019 page). Article 2 asks for $35,293,063 (0:08:39). Benware explained the difference here: the article "includes money from grants and other state and federal sources that don't impact our tax rate" (0:10:49). That is what RSA 32:5, III requires: "All appropriations recommended shall be stipulated on a 'gross' basis, showing anticipated revenues from all sources, including grants". RSA 32:5, II also requires that "All purposes and amounts of appropriations to be included in the budget" be "disclosed or discussed at the final hearing". The hearing transcript never states the gross figure; the business administrator's handouts at the hearing may have, but none survives. The record cannot show whether the $3,465,000 was disclosed on January 2, so this is an observation. A reader comparing the two meetings' figures should know they measure different things.

Sources: RSA 32:5, II and III (2018 codification; source note ends 2018, 246:1, eff. Aug. 11, 2018)

OBSERVATION Two different default-budget gaps were given to the voters

The warrant as read puts the default budget at $35,113,750 against a proposed $35,293,063 (0:08:39), a difference of $179,313, which matches the business administrator's "$179,000 less" at the January 2 hearing. In his closing remarks the board chair told the voters the default "It's actually $193,340 less than our proposed budget." (0:59:40), about $14,000 more. The moderator also first called the Article 2 vote at the default figure before correcting herself (0:31:16, 0:32:26). Under RSA 40:13, XI(b) the default amount "shall not be amended by the legislative body", so the warrant figure governs; the point is that the voters heard two numbers for the choice in front of them.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI(b) (2018 codification; text in force August 2018 to July 9, 2019)

OBSERVATION Each article was put to a voice vote, styled as a vote on an "amendment", though none was offered

The moderator correctly read that articles "are automatically presented as written or amended by the voters present to the March 12th ballot session" (0:03:05). She then called for speakers "against this amendment" and a voice vote on every article (0:30:49, 0:38:51, 0:50:02, 0:57:05), with nays heard on several. Under RSA 40:13, VI, as it read on this date, "All warrant articles shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote, including warrant articles as amended by the first session", so these votes decided nothing. The first session's power is to explain, debate and amend. A viewer could take "the ayes have it" as approval.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and VI (2018 codification)

OBSERVATION Public employees spoke for the radio article when the moderator called for speakers in favor

For Article 5 the police chief, a Stevens High School administrator and the SAU technology director each spoke in favor (0:40:37, 0:43:00, 0:45:09), and the chief said "I'm in support of this". RSA 659:44-a, I provides that "No public employee, as defined in RSA 273-A:1, IX, shall electioneer while in the performance of his or her official duties", and ¶III defines electioneering as acting "in any way specifically designed to influence the vote of a voter on any question". Several facts point the other way: the moderator recognized them in the article's debate, a deliberative session exists for "explanation, discussion, and debate" of each article (RSA 40:13, IV), the chief and the technology director introduced themselves as Ward 1 residents, and much of what they said was factual. Whether speaking at a deliberative session is within "official duties" is not settled by anything verified for this page. Elected officials are outside the section: RSA 273-A:1, IX(a) excludes "Persons elected by popular vote", so the board chair's closing plea (1:04:05) is not covered.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I and III (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 273-A:1, IX(a) (2019 codification); RSA 40:13, IV (2018 codification)

POSITIVE The contract cost items were spelled out for all three years, and the bus-camera answer matched the statute

Article 2 told the voters that it carries the administrators' agreement and gave the estimated increase for each year (17,789; 31,197; and a third figure), and the presenter explained in plain terms that approving the budget also approves the later years (0:09:25, 0:11:22, 0:11:52). Asked why the bus cameras would not record sound, the assistant superintendent gave an answer that matches RSA 570-A:2, II(k): audio on a school bus requires board authorization, notice to parents and students and a posted sign, and recordings are kept no more than 10 school days unless needed for discipline or ordered by a court (0:54:40). The session fell inside the statutory window, the first and second Saturdays after the last Monday in January (February 2 to February 9, 2019), and the moderator read the written-amendment rule before business began (0:03:31).

Sources: RSA 570-A:2, II(k) (2018 codification; source note ends 2016, 169:1 and 322:2, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 40:13, III (2018 codification)
