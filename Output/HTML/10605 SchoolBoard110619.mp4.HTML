Claremont School Board, November 6, 2019

Public hearing on a $650,000 special education expendable trust fund warrant for the November 21 special district meeting, followed by a regular meeting at which the board sent the warrant forward, voted 4-2 to meet once a month, heard that federal school-lunch reimbursements had been missed for months, and asked for a report on a personnel incident reported in the press. MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this hearing and meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this meeting. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force from January 1, 2019, required minutes listing "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", required that "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion shall be recorded in the minutes", and required them to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute text). Without them the two members who voted against monthly meetings cannot be identified (flag 4), and the hearing has no written record of who spoke.

MEDIUM under the project's severity rule, because the record mitigates the gap: the board approved minutes of this meeting on November 20, with Benware's correction that the trust-fund figure is 650,000, not 650 million (November 20, 2019 page). Minutes were written; no public copy survives.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2019)

MEDIUM The hearing on the special-meeting warrant was not noticed on the district website, by district counsel's later account

The board held this hearing under RSA 197:3-a, which requires a hearing "at least 14 days prior" to the special meeting and requires notice of its time, place and subject in at least two public places, "one of which shall be on the school district's website, if such exists, at least 7 days prior" (statute text). The 14-day requirement was met: November 6 is fifteen days before November 21. The website notice was not. On November 20 district counsel told the board that concerns had been raised about posting and argued that, because only the SAU 6 website exists, there may have been no duty to post there (November 20, 2019 page). At the December 12 curing meeting he listed the defects as no website notice of this hearing and a meeting notice posted six days ahead rather than seven (December 12, 2019 page). The chair's own explanation on this recording is that the timetable was compressed: “We learned from legal counsel at our last board meeting that” part of the money had to be committed this year (0:00:59).

MEDIUM, not HIGH: the defect is a missed notice requirement, but RSA 31:5-b, I treats failures "regarding time or place of notice" as minor procedural irregularities that a later meeting may cure, and the district used that route on December 12. The hearing itself was held in public on the record with questions answered.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, IV and V (2019 codification; source 2013, 197:1, eff. Sept. 7, 2013); RSA 31:5-b, I (2019 codification; source 1988, 284:1; 1989, 287:1); RSA 40:16 (2019 codification; source 1999, 86:4)

MEDIUM Federal school-lunch reimbursement claims went unfiled for months over two years

The business administrator reported that the district had caught up “all the stuff we missed last year and the year before” and that no claims for the current year had yet been filed because of a state system problem (1:06:34, 1:06:57); a member summarized, without contradiction, that the district had missed monthly reimbursements over a period of months across two years (1:07:16). Under 7 CFR 210.8 a final claim must be "submitted to the State agency not later than 60 days following the last day of the full month covered by the claim", and "Claims not postmarked and/or submitted within 60 days shall not be paid with Program funds unless otherwise authorized by FNS" (regulation text). Claims that far overdue are at risk of never being paid.

MEDIUM: a control weakness in the prior administration's handling of a federal program, stated on the record by current staff. The recording does not say how much reimbursement was lost or whether FNS authorized late payment; the November 20 recording reports the first current-year claims filed (November 20, 2019 page).

Sources: 7 CFR 210.8(b) (2019 annual edition; claims for reimbursement, 60-day deadline)

OBSERVATION A 4-2 vote to halve the meeting schedule with the dissenters unnamed

The board voted to move from two regular meetings a month to one, on the first Wednesday, with extra meetings as needed (0:58:36). The chair hesitated to vote with a member absent (“Rob's not here.”, 0:58:24); the vice chair warned against losing contact (0:59:26). The tally is heard as 4-2 (1:01:00) but not who voted no. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to record final decisions and the movers and seconders; it does not require individual votes on a voice vote. With no minutes surviving, the public record cannot say how each member voted on a change to the board's own schedule.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION Members say the board was not told of a personnel incident it read about in the press

Petrin asked for a report on a press-reported incident at the high school involving a former employee and suggested it may need a nonpublic session (1:07:44); another member said the board did not know it had happened (1:09:29). The vice chair's statement that once the person left district employment “it's no longer a personnel issue” (1:09:58) is her characterization, not a statement of law. If the board takes the report in nonpublic session, the motion must fit a specific ground: RSA 91-A:3, II(a) covers the dismissal or discipline of an employee and the investigation of charges against one, and II(c) matters likely to harm the reputation of a non-member. This page does not name the person or describe the incident.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(a) and II(c) (2019 codification)

POSITIVE The budget retreat was treated as a public meeting, minuted, and reported out

A weekend retreat of a quorum to set budget expectations is a meeting under RSA 91-A:2, I. The superintendent described the October 20 retreat as “it's a posted public meeting” (0:53:09); its minutes were moved, corrected and approved at this meeting (0:19:03); and the chair and members described in public what it was for and how long it ran (0:49:53 to 0:54:00). Noticing an off-site retreat, keeping minutes and reporting it at the next televised meeting is the practice the statute calls for, and the report-out goes beyond it. The notice itself does not survive, so the page relies on the superintendent's statement.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification)
