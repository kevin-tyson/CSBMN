Claremont School Board — February 15, 2023

Regular meeting summary generated from the meeting recording, the six-document posted packet and the official minutes. One week after the deliberative session put $1,000,000 back into the operating budget by a 41–27 secret ballot, the board met to decide the one budget number still in its own hands: the default budget, which governs the district if the March 14 ballot fails. Fifty-eight minutes of grade-reconfiguration presentation came first; then the chair stopped it ("the only thing we're here to decide tonight is the default budget") and Whitney Skillen moved to add $600,000. It carried 4–2 on a roll call the chair ordered after he could not hear a voice vote, and the resulting figure, $36,942,948, is the default budget printed on the March 14, 2023 ballot. HIGH The board added $600,000 to the default budget minutes after being told, on the record and on the authority of its own attorney, that the default budget already funded those positions

The default budget is the appropriation that governs the district if the operating budget fails at the ballot. Going into this meeting it stood at $36,342,948 against a proposed budget the deliberative session had just raised to $38,345,312: a gap of $2,002,364. Whitney Skillen moved at 1:10:13 "to restore $600,000 to the default budget, which… would be used to fund the lows [LOAs] that were going to be funded by Esser… and the positions that we're going to be funded by title grants." It carried 4–2 at 1:41:15. The chair had already stated the arithmetic: the default budget "would be $36,942,948", with the net to be raised by taxes rising to $15,845,186. That is the figure the March 14, 2023 ballot printed ("Should this article be defeated, the default budget shall be… ($36,942,948)"), so the vote taken here is the number Claremont voters saw four weeks later.

What the record establishes about those positions. Twenty-two minutes before the vote, at 1:18:28, the vice chair asked directly whether they were already covered: "am I correct as well. And I think I am. Is that all? All of the positions would be covered by the default budget if the proposed amended budget were to be were to fail. So every, every position that we're talking about refunding would already be funded by the default budget." She named her source ("that's what I understood from Matt Upton is regardless, all the positions would be funded") and the superintendent confirmed it, extending it to the positions cut in January: "Including the those that were. What the statute requires. RSA 40:13, IX(b) defines the default budget as "the amount of the same appropriations as contained in the operating budget authorized for the previous year, reduced and increased, as the case may be, by debt service, contracts, and other obligations previously incurred or mandated by law, and reduced by one-time expenditures contained in the operating budget and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget." Two further sentences of the same paragraph bear directly on this vote and neither was read aloud. First, the exclusion: "eliminated positions shall not include vacant positions under recruitment or positions redefined in the proposed operating budget." Second, the direction: "In calculating the default budget amount, the governing body shall follow the statutory formula which may result in a higher or lower amount than the proposed operating budget." The adjustment power the chair relied on, at XI(b), is real but conditioned: the amount "may be adjusted by the governing body… acting on relevant new information at any time before the ballots are printed, provided the governing body… completes an amended default budget form."

Why the two do not meet. On the board's own description, the motion changed a funding preference rather than a calculation. The chair summarised it at 1:20:09 (the positions "Are funded. Differently through taxes instead of through grants") and the vice chair agreed: "So we're not expanding anything or contracting anything. It's how those positions are funded." A default budget is not a plan for how to pay for things; it is the output of the IX(b) formula. And the category the mover named is the category the statute excludes. Her stated basis, at 1:38:26, was that the board may "restore funding for the positions that were redefined": using the statute's own word for positions that are not eliminated positions and so were never a lawful ground for reducing the default budget in the first place. The chair's version at 1:09:13 was the same: "restore positions that have been deemed to be cut or moved."

There is a lawful reading, and it is worth stating fully. If the original $36,342,948 had itself been computed by subtracting the salaries and benefits of these LOA and Title-funded positions as "eliminated", then that subtraction was wrong (redefined positions are excluded) and putting the money back is a correction the formula requires, not a discretionary top-up. On that reading the board did the right thing for imprecise reasons. But the record cuts against it: three people at the table said the positions were already in the default budget, and the two accounts given a week earlier at the deliberative session were that the whole difference was the teachers' contract, or that positions had been eliminated (see the February 8 page, flag 1). Nobody asked the one question that would have settled it (what the original default budget calculation subtracted, line by line) and the document that answers it, the DRA default budget form, is not in any district share this page could search (flag 2).

In fairness, four things. The board did not act in the dark: it sought legal advice, and the mover's account of that advice is that the board had the power she exercised. The chair's opening framing tracked XI(b) almost word for word, and the deliberative session's amendment is a plausible candidate for "relevant new information." The vote was taken by recorded roll call with the resulting figure stated aloud first, which is more transparency than the law then required. And the effect of the vote was to narrow the gap between proposed and default from $2,002,364 to $1,402,364, which is what the district's finance officer had asked the board to do from the floor of the deliberative session seven days earlier, so that the difference "helps preserve the vote" (February 8 page, flag 3). The relayed advice does not sit easily with the statute. RSA 40:13, IX(b) says in terms that eliminated positions "shall not include vacant positions under recruitment or positions redefined in the proposed operating budget." A power to restore funding for redefined positions is a power over the one category the paragraph carves out. It is possible (likely, even) that counsel said something more precise and that the precision was lost in relay; Skillen said as much before she started: "I'm not going to explain it 100% correct because I don't have a full grasp on it myself." That candour is to her credit, and it is also the problem: the board's authority for a $600,000 decision reached the public through a member who told them she might be getting it wrong.

On the meeting Sprague refers to. Nothing here asserts that an unnoticed meeting occurred, and the record does not establish one. New Hampshire's Right-to-Know Law expressly permits a public body to consider legal advice in nonpublic session: RSA 91-A:3, II(l), "Consideration of legal advice provided by legal counsel, either in writing or orally, to one or more members of the public body, even where legal counsel is not present", which was in force on this date (the section's amendment history shows no change between January 1, 2022 and October 3, 2023). Advice given in writing to individual members is not a meeting at all. But if a quorum did meet, the motion to enter nonpublic session must state the exemption on its face and be carried by roll call recorded in the minutes, and the minutes of the session are public within 72 hours unless sealed by a recorded two-thirds vote with a statutory finding. No such session appears in the minutes of the January 17, February 1 or February 15 board meetings, the February 8 deliberative session could not hold one, and this page found no other Claremont School Board record between February 8 and February 15, 2023 in any district share.

What would resolve it. Either a written opinion released to the public, or a sentence in the minutes recording when and how the board received the advice. Both are ordinary; neither is here. Recorded as MEDIUM rather than HIGH because the mitigating limb applies: the board plainly did seek counsel, the advice may lawfully have been written or delivered in a properly noticed session this page has not seen, and no gap in any required record has been demonstrated.
