RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be “open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting” and to record the names of members, persons appearing, and the mover and seconder of each motion (2019 codification, in force on this date). The packet folder for this meeting holds the minutes of October 5 and October 19, not of November 17, and the district’s Meeting Minutes share begins in 2023. Title and full-text searches of Drive found no copy. The record shows the minutes did exist: the draft December 7, 2022 minutes state that Frank Sprague “made a motion to approve the minutes as presented; seconded by Whitney Skillen. Without them, three points on this page cannot be checked: whether Horsky attended, who read the roll, and who seconded adjournment (heard as “Brittany” at 0:49:49). OBSERVATION Budget documents described as “Not for public consumption” were to be sent to the whole board

Sprague asked at 0:11:57 that the documents the budget subcommittee received the day before go to “the entire board”, adding at 0:12:22 that they are “Not for public consumption.” His point was that the full board should see the budget work early, which favors open deliberation. The legal status of the documents changes once that happens. Under RSA 91-A:1-a, III, “governmental records” include information “received by a quorum or majority of a public body in furtherance of its official function, whether at a meeting or outside a meeting of the body.” The draft-document exemption in RSA 91-A:5, IX covers preliminary drafts “not disclosed, circulated, or available to a quorum or a majority of the members of a public body.” Circulating the documents to all seven members would take them outside that exemption, although other exemptions might still apply to particular contents.

Nothing on the record shows a request for the documents or a refusal, so this is an Observation. The remark is recorded because it describes documents a resident could later ask for under RSA 91-A:4.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, III and VI(d) (2022 codification; records received by a quorum; subcommittees are public bodies); RSA 91-A:5, IX (drafts not circulated to a quorum; source note ends 2022, 122:3, eff. May 27, 2022)

OBSERVATION Subcommittee work reported tonight has no public notices or minutes in the district’s shares

Members reported a budget subcommittee meeting (0:10:59), a curriculum committee meeting held that afternoon (0:13:37), a visioning committee that meets monthly, and an ad hoc parliamentary procedure group whose work comes to the full board (0:19:06; the October 19 minutes record “three meetings so far”). A committee or subcommittee of a school board is itself a public body (RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d)), and its meetings carry notice and minutes duties under RSA 91-A:2. None of these bodies has a notice, agenda or minutes in the packet shares mapped by this project; MAP.md records that the first subcommittee packet ever posted there dates from August 2026 and that the district’s finance subcommittee folder is empty.

This is an Observation, not a finding. A “meeting” under RSA 91-A:2, I is “the convening of a quorum” of the body, and by the chair’s account the budget subcommittee session had one of its three board members present, which would not be a quorum. The membership of the curriculum and visioning committees was not established, and the project did not search for notices posted elsewhere, such as at the SAU office.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2022 codification; “any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto”); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification; meeting is the convening of a quorum; notice and minutes); CSB Hearing & Meeting Minutes 10.19.22

OBSERVATION The $1,450 grant was announced as needing no board approval

RSA 198:20-b, as it read in 2022, lets a school board “apply for, accept and expend” unanticipated money from a private source where the district has adopted an enabling article (¶I). Below $5,000, ¶III(b) requires the board to “post notice of the funds in the agenda” and to “include notice in the minutes of the school board meeting in which such funds are discussed”, and says “The acceptance of unanticipated funds under this subparagraph shall be made in public session of any regular school board meeting.” The agenda notice was given (see flag 6) and the grant was discussed in public session at a regular meeting. At 0:48:36 the chair said “So no need for us to approve this”, and no vote was taken.

The section does not say in terms whether acceptance requires a vote, and this page does not decide it. Two points remain open: whether Claremont voters adopted the ¶I enabling article (not checked), and whether the minutes carried the required notice, which cannot be checked because the minutes are not public (flag 1). The bank’s check is dated October 18, 2022, a month before the meeting.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(b) (2022 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1; $5,000 threshold before July 18, 2023); 2022 CSB Foundation Grant SHS Life Skills Program

POSITIVE The board takes public comment on each discussion item, beyond the comment period the law requires

RSA 189:74, in force since September 6, 2022, requires a school board to “provide the opportunity for the public to comment on school district matters” at its meetings, for a period of “no less than 30 minutes”. This board opened its citizens’ comment period at 0:03:59; no one came forward, so the 30-minute floor was not tested. In addition, the chair’s address at 0:07:56 commits the board to opening comment on each discussion item after members have spoken, and that is what happened on the restructuring item, where a student was heard at 0:26:33 and the chair asked for “Any further comments from the public on the restructure?” at 0:29:45. The same address asks members to speak only when recognized, a step toward the written norms the parliamentary procedure group is drafting.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022; public comment period of no less than 30 minutes)
