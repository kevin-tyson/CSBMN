Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting, November 15, 2016

Joint session of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council, held without a quorum of the council, on childhood lead poisoning (a presentation by the state's Healthy Homes and Lead Poisoning Prevention Program) and on the loss of the state stabilization grant. No votes were taken. OBSERVATION A joint meeting without a council quorum: the council could not act, and nothing was voted

The city manager noted at the roll call that the council "lacks a quorum" (0:01:14), and a resident said near the end that "for the entire joint meeting, a quorum of the city council is not present." (2:14:42). The city charter provides "a city council of nine (9) councilors" and that "A majority of the council shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business", so five were needed; four council voices are identified on the recording. RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body". The school board side was a meeting of the board; the council side, strictly, was not a meeting of the council. Members recognized the limit: We won't vote. On anything. (2:15:29). The one outcome, a December meeting with the delegation to be scheduled by the officers (2:14:12), was agreed by consensus, which suits a body that could not act. No rule appears to be engaged; the point is that the city side's participation carried no authority.

Sources: Claremont City Charter, secs. 16 and 21 (copy of undetermined date; nine councilors, majority quorum); RSA 91-A:2, I (2015 codification; meeting means the convening of a quorum)

OBSERVATION The stabilization grant cut is set by statute; the figures given are consistent with it

The business administrator said Claremont's fiscal 2012 stabilization grant of $6,282,807 is being reduced 4% a year, $251,312 a year, cumulatively over 25 years (2:00:42), and the superintendent attributed the change to Dover's lawsuit and "the legislation" that followed (2:02:53). RSA 198:41, IV(d), as amended through July 18, 2016, provides that from fiscal 2017 each municipality receives a percentage of its fiscal 2012 stabilization grant, "96 percent for fiscal year 2017", reduced by 4 percent of the 2012 grant each year after. Four percent of $6,282,807 is $251,312, so the arithmetic matches the statute. Whether the reduction followed from a court ruling, as the speakers said, is not something this page verified; the freeze the coalition sought would require an amendment to that paragraph.

Sources: RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 85:9, eff. July 18, 2016)

POSITIVE The mayor routed communications between the two bodies through the council clerk, citing the Right-to-Know Law

Asked how material should pass between the school board and the council, the mayor asked that anything for councilors go to the council clerk "because of 91 a issues", and the school board chair said its communications go "Typically through Doctor Goodwin." (2:16:16, 2:16:34). RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II required public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings" and provided that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". Sending shared material through staff to every member, instead of member to member, is a practical guard against that kind of serial deliberation.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I-II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4, eff. July 1, 2008)
