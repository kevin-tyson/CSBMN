Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — November 15, 2016

Joint session summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares, and the city's own records were not inspected. The City Council lacked a quorum from the start, so nothing was voted beyond adjournment. The bodies heard a state lead-poisoning prevention presentation, agreed to seek a December meeting with the legislative delegation on lead screening and on the phase-down of the state stabilization grant (put at $251,312 a year by the business administrator), and traded notes on health-insurance costs, employer rankings and marketing the schools. OBSERVATION The City Council never had a quorum, so for the council this was not a meeting; the school board did, and took no action beyond adjourning

The city manager said at 0:01:14 that the council "lacks a quorum", and a resident repeated it at 2:14:42; the council side acknowledged it had been noted and said no votes would be taken. Under RSA 91-A:2, I as it then read, a meeting is the convening of a quorum of a public body, so the council members present were not holding a council meeting and could not act for the council. The Claremont City Council has nine members, so its quorum is five. The school board's roll at 0:01:33 shows five of its seven members, a quorum, and the chair relied on it to adjourn (2:33:55). Nothing was voted except adjournment; the December meeting with the delegation and the joint letter were left as consensus for the mayor, chair, superintendent and city manager to arrange (2:14:12).

The practical point for a reader is that nothing said here bound the city. This page does not assess the city's notice or minutes: those would be in the city clerk's records, which were not inspected.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2015 codification): a meeting is the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body; Claremont City Charter sec. 16 (council of nine) and sec. 21 (a majority is a quorum) (copy of undetermined date); nine seats corroborated by the City of Claremont City Council page.

OBSERVATION The business administrator's figures on the stabilization-grant cut match the statute; the causal story told for it is the speakers' own

O'Neill told both bodies that Claremont's FY2012 stabilization grant of $6,282,807 would fall by 4% a year, "$251,312 per year", compounding to about a million dollars in four years (2:00:42 to 2:01:21). The arithmetic is right (4% of $6,282,807 is $251,312) and it matches the law then in force: RSA 198:41, as amended in 2015 and 2016, paid each municipality a percentage of its fiscal year 2012 stabilization grant, and "the percentage shall be 96 percent for fiscal year 2017", falling by four points each year after. The superintendent's account that the reduction followed litigation led by Dover (2:02:53), and the city manager's description of state funding as unconstitutional, are the speakers' characterizations; this page has not verified the litigation history.

Sources: RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 85:9, eff. July 18, 2016): stabilization grant at 96 percent for FY2017, reduced each year thereafter.

POSITIVE The mayor routed school-to-council communications through the council clerk, citing the Right-to-Know Law

At 2:16:16 the mayor asked that anything the school district wanted councilors to see go through the council clerk "because of 91 a issues". Sending material to a whole body through its clerk, rather than by chains of messages among members, keeps deliberation where RSA 91-A:2-a puts it: "public bodies shall deliberate on matters only in meetings", and communications outside a meeting, including sequential ones among members, "shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter." That paragraph was unamended from 2008 through the whole period this project covers.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I-II (source note 2008, 303:4 only, so the same text was in force in 2016): deliberation only in meetings; no circumvention by outside communications.
