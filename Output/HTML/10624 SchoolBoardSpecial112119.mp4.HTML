Claremont School District, Special District Meeting, November 21, 2019

Single-session special district meeting under RSA 197:3-a at which the voters debated and adopted, on a voice vote, a $650,000 special education expendable trust fund drawn from restored state aid, with no money raised from taxation. Its notices were later found defective and the vote was ratified at a curing meeting on December 12. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no warrant, notice or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on November 21, 2019, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The meeting's notice was posted a day late and its hearing was not noticed on the website, by district counsel's account

RSA 197:3-a, V requires notice of the meeting, including the warrant, in at least two public places "one of which shall be on the school district's website, if such exists, at least 7 days prior" to the meeting, plus newspaper publication; ¶IV imposes the same website rule on notice of the required hearing (statute text). The night before, counsel told the board the open question was whether the SAU 6 website counted (November 20, 2019 page). At the December 12 curing meeting he stated the defects as no website notice of the hearing and a meeting notice “posted six days ahead of the Special District meeting, not seven” (counsel on the December 12 recording, show 10649; December 12, 2019 page). Nothing on this recording tells the voters in the room about the problem.

MEDIUM, not HIGH: the requirement was missed, but RSA 31:5-b, I treats failures "regarding time or place of notice" as minor procedural irregularities, and the district cured them by a ratification vote on December 12, 21 days after this meeting, as RSA 31:5-b, II allows. That ratification, not this vote alone, is what established the fund.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, IV and V (2019 codification; source 2013, 197:1, eff. Sept. 7, 2013); RSA 31:5-b, I and II (2019 codification); RSA 40:16 (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION A $650,000 article adopted on a voice vote with no count in the record

After the question was called, the moderator took a voice vote, said a standing vote would follow if the result was questionable, and declared “May the ayes have it.” (1:00:52). No one questioned the result on the recording, no secret ballot was requested although the moderator had explained the procedure for one (0:03:05), and no count exists. A voice vote is a lawful way to decide a question; the observation is only that the record holds no margin. At the December 12 ratification the moderator took a standing vote with counters when the voice vote was questioned (show 10649).

Sources: RSA 197:3-a (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION Speakers disputed what the restored aid was for; the statute restores a grant percentage and the article spends half of it

Opponents called the money tax-relief money that must go back to taxpayers (0:21:07, 0:40:29); supporters called it restored stabilization aid (0:24:26, 0:16:01). The 2019 budget law amended RSA 198:41, IV(d) so that the stabilization-grant percentage, phased down to "88 percent for fiscal year 2019", returned to "100 percent for fiscal year 2020" (statute text; 2019, 346:238, eff. July 1, 2019). That paragraph sets the grant; it does not, in the text read for this page, earmark it for property-tax relief, and the article was brought under RSA 197:3-a, which exists for spending changes in adequacy aid during the year. The legislators' intent the speakers described, in emails read aloud, is their characterization and is not verified here. Half the money went to the trust fund and half was applied to the tax rate, as officials explained (0:35:56).

Sources: RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 346:235, eff. July 1, 2020; 346:238, eff. July 1, 2019); RSA 198:20-c (2019 codification; expendable trust funds)

OBSERVATION Counsel told voters the single-session procedure was required; the statute makes it a choice

Asked why the article was not going through the SB 2 process, counsel answered “This is the process that the statute requires.” (0:32:43). RSA 197:3-a, I provides that a district "may elect to hold and conduct the meeting in accordance with the provisions of this section in a single session" (statute text). The single session was the route the board chose, which the statute permits; the answer was right that the meeting was lawful in this form, and imprecise in presenting it as the only form. The resident's underlying point, that most voters had just been to the polls and few would attend a weeknight meeting (0:30:54), is the trade-off that choice carries.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, I (2019 codification)

POSITIVE A structured, two-sided debate with officials answering skeptics on the record

The moderator announced time limits and amendment rules at the start (0:01:38), alternated speakers for and against throughout, let residents who were neither for nor against speak, allowed opponents a second turn once everyone had spoken, and had the superintendent answer a parent's questions about guarantees and results directly (0:50:30 to 0:53:33). Eleven residents spoke: five for, three against, and three with questions or no position. RSA 197:19 gives the district moderator the powers of a town moderator, and RSA 40:4 lets a moderator "prescribe rules of proceeding" (statute text); announcing them in advance and applying them evenly to both sides goes beyond that bare authority and gave the small turnout a full hearing of the arguments.

Sources: RSA 197:19 (district moderator; source 1979, 321:6); RSA 40:4 (moderator's duties; source note ends 2019, 192:1, eff. July 10, 2019)
