Claremont School District, Deliberative Session — February 1, 2020

First (deliberative) session of the 2020 Claremont School District annual meeting under the SB 2 official-ballot system, summarized from the recording; no warrant, handout or minutes for this session are online. The body is the voters, not the board. The board brought a $38,202,661 operating budget (default $35,501,890) built partly on one-time state money, three collective bargaining articles and two new $500,000 capital reserve funds, for buses and for building repair. No amendment was offered; every article went to the March ballot as written. The session date is disputed in the record: see the Date row and flag 1. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the session against the state law in force in February 2020. A deliberative session is a meeting of the voters, so school-board rules (public-comment periods, nonpublic sessions, roll calls) do not apply to it; RSA chapter 40 as extended to school districts does. They are not findings of violation and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Eight flags follow, ordered by severity. No HIGH or MEDIUM flag was found.

OBSERVATION The record gives three different dates for this session

The project's file name and MAP.md heading date the session February 1, 2020 (a Saturday). CCTV's gallery title reads 2/5/20. The moderator opens "the February 6th deliberative session" (0:00:01), which may be a slip: the 2019 session whose minutes she then takes was held "February 6th, 2019" (0:05:14). At the January 21, 2020 board meeting the board voted to send the budget to the deliberative session, and the Valley News report of that meeting (January 22, 2020) gives the date and place as "Wed., Feb. 5 at 6:30 in the high school auditorium". The weight of the evidence is Wednesday, February 5, 2020. Nothing turns on it legally: RSA 40:13, III sets the first session "between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January"; the last Monday of January 2020 was January 27, so the window ran from February 1 to February 8 and all three dates fall inside it. The file name and MAP.md heading should be checked against CCTV's own record.

Sources: RSA 40:13, III (last amended 2019, 192:2, eff. July 10, 2019; in force on this date); Valley News, January 22, 2020 (reporting).

OBSERVATION The budget was shown to voters on three different bases, and a voter could not reconcile them until a board member explained it from the floor

The warrant article reads $38,202,661 with a default of $35,501,890 (0:15:30); the presentation worked from a local figure of about $34.7 million and a default of 32 [million] (0:46:10). David Larson, new to the city, said the slides and the document did not match and asked for "much more detail" and "a more timely fashion" (1:06:37). A board member then explained that grants, food service and other pass-through money take $38.2 million "down to the $34,737,000" and said "I actually explained it at the beginning and I didn't this year" (1:12:16). The default-budget explanation also bears reading against the statute: RSA 40:13, IX(b) reduces the default by one-time expenditures and by "salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget", and provides that "eliminated positions shall not include vacant positions under recruitment". The business administrator described the $759,000 as vacant positions the district does "not anticipate they will need to be filled" (0:47:07), which on its face fits the statute; the default budget form itself was not available to check. Recorded as an observation: no rule requires a particular presentation, and the reconciliation was given on the record.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) (definition of default budget; in force on this date); the recording at the times linked above.

OBSERVATION The business administrator removed a $65,000 prior-year fund transfer that he could not explain

"We had last year fund transfer of $65,000, which I have been unsuccessful in finding out what that is or why we had it. And if we couldn't find out why it was there, I took it out." (0:31:41). Removing an unexplained line is prudent; the admission that a $65,000 line in the prior year's adopted budget could not be traced four months into his tenure is a record-keeping question worth putting to the audited financial statements for fiscal 2019 and 2020. No verified rule is cited; recorded as an observation.

OBSERVATION Two $500,000 capital reserve funds would name the school board as agents to spend; voters objected that this removes their year-by-year vote

Articles 6 and 7 each establish a capital reserve fund and name the school board as agents to expend (1:55:39, 2:25:09). Cynthia Howard objected that "we are surrendering our our control over to the school board" (2:03:07); the business administrator replied "If you don't like board members, put in different board members" (2:08:19). The mechanism is lawful: RSA 35:15, I, as codified in 2019, has the trustees hold the fund until the district votes to withdraw or "shall have named agents of the town, district or county to carry out the objects designated", in the manner prescribed by RSA 35:3. So the objection is a policy choice for the ballot, as the moderator told the voter (2:03:56). Two points in the record are worth checking: a voter's belief that an earlier bus reserve already existed was answered by reading an article passed "two years ago", a five-year bus lease-purchase that set up no reserve (2:05:51); and the stated aim of "no further warrant articles" for "3 to 5 years" (2:25:37) can be tested against later warrants.

Sources: RSA 35:15, I (2019 codification; source note ends 2007, 178:3).

OBSERVATION Procedural loose ends: a restriction motion restated wider than moved, voice votes on articles, a non-voter refused, and a close without a vote

Sprague moved to restrict reconsideration of "warrant article two"; the moderator put the question on "articles one and two" (1:35:00, 1:35:18). RSA 40:10, I lets the meeting restrict reconsideration "of any one or more votes previously taken at that meeting, or warrant articles previously considered", so either scope was available, but the question put was not the motion made. The moderator also took voice votes on Article 1 (0:14:15), although under RSA 40:13, VI every article goes to the official ballot whatever the first session does; later she explained exactly that (1:50:45, 2:21:13). A resident who said she was "not a registered voter today" was told "You can't talk" (1:44:08); the moderator regulates the business of the meeting under RSA 40:4, and no statute found gives a non-voter a right to speak, so this is noted only. At the end the moderator refused a voter's follow-up question and closed the session on her own announcement while a motion to adjourn was being offered (2:27:11, 2:27:34). None of these is shown to have affected any article.

Sources: RSA 40:10, I and V (1996, 64:1; applies to school district meetings); RSA 40:13, VI; RSA 40:4 (moderator regulates the business of the meeting).
