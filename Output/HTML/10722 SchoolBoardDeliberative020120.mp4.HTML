Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each cites the rule it rests on, read as it stood on the meeting date; items with no verified rule are labeled Observation. A deliberative session is a meeting of the voters, so school-board rules (public comment periods, nonpublic sessions, board quorum) do not apply to it.

MEDIUM No minutes, warrant, posted budget or Rules of Procedure for this session are in any public district share

A school district meeting is a public body (RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) names the legislative body of any school district), so the clerk's minutes of this session were due for public inspection within five business days, and from January 1, 2019 they must also name who moved and who seconded each motion (RSA 91-A:2, II). RSA 197:20 makes the clerk keep "a true record of all the doings of each meeting" (statute's words), RSA 21-J:34, IV requires the clerk to certify the minutes of each annual school district meeting and file them within 20 days, and IV-a extends the same duty to any deliberative session of an official-ballot body at which appropriations may be amended. None of those minutes, nor the warrant, the posted budget, the default budget form or the Rules of Procedure handout the moderator mentions at 0:02:19, is in any public district share: MAP.md records that neither district share holds a year folder older than 2022 (checked 9/26/26), and under this run's rules the shares were not searched again.

Severity limb (html_briefing.md §5): MEDIUM, because the record mitigates the absence. The recording shows the district's practice of keeping minutes of every district meeting: at 0:05:14 to 0:08:03 this session approved, by voice vote, the minutes of the 2019 deliberative session and of the special district meetings of May 15, November 21 and December 12, 2019. So minutes of this session were very likely written and approved at the 2021 session; what is missing is public access to them. Two points for a reviewer who obtains them: the moderator told voters to approve minutes from their seats, "simply say so moved or I second" with no sign-in (0:04:40), and every mover and seconder of those votes and of the reconsideration and move-the-question motions is an unidentified floor voice on the tape, so check whether the minutes name movers and seconders as RSA 91-A:2, II requires.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification: a school district's legislative body is a public body); RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification: minutes open to inspection within 5 business days; movers and seconders named from Jan. 1, 2019); RSA 197:20 (district clerk keeps a true record of each meeting); RSA 21-J:34, IV and IV-a (2019 codification: clerk certifies deliberative-session minutes; filed within 20 days)

OBSERVATION The session's date is recorded three different ways; every candidate falls inside the statutory window

The video file name and the MAP.md heading give February 1, 2020 (a Saturday). The moderator opens with "welcome you to the February 6th deliberative session" (0:00:01), which matches the 2019 session's date, February 6, 2019 (0:05:14). The better evidence points to Wednesday, February 5, 2020: the Cablecast title reads 2/5/20; on January 21, 2020 Jason Benware's motion sending the budget and warrant to the deliberative session corrected itself from February 6th to February 5th, 2020, and Rebecca Zullo confirmed February 5th later that evening (Dialogue/10704 SchoolBoard012120.mp4.CSV, data rows 444 and 721); the Valley News notice of January 22 gives "Wed., Feb. 5 at 6:30 in the high school auditorium" (newspaper's words); and the moderator speaks of "this evening" and "tonight's meeting" (0:01:46, 2:27:34). RSA 40:13, III puts the first session between the first and second Saturdays after the last Monday in January, inclusive. The last Monday of January 2020 was January 27, so the window ran February 1 to 8, and February 1, 5 and 6 all fall inside it. The discrepancy is a records problem, not a compliance one; the clerk's minutes would settle it.

Sources: RSA 40:13 (official ballot; text in force from July 10, 2019); Valley News, Jan. 22, 2020 (Patrick O'Grady), notice of the session

OBSERVATION The warrant's $38,202,661 and the slides' $34,737,661 were reconciled only after a voter asked

Article 2 as read asks for "$38,202,661" (0:15:30), while the revenue and tax-rate slides work from "34. 737661" [$34,737,661] (0:41:48). David Larson, Ward 1, told the session he could not see how the two were reconciled and asked the board for information "in a more timely fashion and much more detail" (1:06:37). Rob Lovett Jr. then explained the difference: $650,000 of food service money, $2.7 million of grants and another $115,000, all pass-through amounts that must sit in the warrant (1:12:16, 1:12:44). Those three figures account for the gap exactly ($38,202,661 less $3,465,000 is $34,737,661). Lovett added "I actually explained it at the beginning and I didn't this year." The warrant figure is the gross figure the law calls for: RSA 32:5, III requires appropriations to be stipulated on a gross basis showing anticipated revenues from all sources. The point is presentation, not legality: the gross-to-net bridge was not on the slides.

Sources: RSA 32:5, III (2019 codification: appropriations stipulated on a gross basis); dialogue Dialogue/10722 SchoolBoardDeliberative020120.mp4.CSV

OBSERVATION The teachers' contract cost items ride inside the operating-budget article, so they stand or fall with it

Article 2's text, as Benware read it, says the operating budget includes the first year of a three-year agreement with the Sugar River Education Association (estimated increases read as $421,267, $420,245 and $419,222 for fiscal 2021 to 2023) and that "A favorable vote on this article main budget shall be considered not only the approval of the proposed operating budget" but also of the cost items (0:16:04, 0:16:47). The three support-staff agreements went separately as Articles 3 to 5. Richard Seaman stated the consequence: "If the main budget doesn't pass, the teachers do not get their contract." (0:46:36). RSA 273-A:3, II(b) sends "only cost items" to the legislative body, and RSA 273-A:1, IV defines a cost item as a benefit whose implementation requires an appropriation. This page did not verify any rule on how cost items must be packaged on an official ballot, so this is an observation: voters could not approve the teachers' cost items while rejecting the rest of the budget, or the reverse.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (only cost items go to the legislative body); RSA 273-A:1, IV (cost item defined)

OBSERVATION Board members described both capital reserve funds more broadly than the articles' stated purposes
