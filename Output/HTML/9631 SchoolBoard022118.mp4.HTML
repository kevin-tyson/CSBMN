Claremont School Board, February 21, 2018

Regular meeting three weeks before the March election. After a brief nonpublic session, the board accepted Dr. Alex Herzog's resignation from the board and, on the superintendent's nomination, appointed him director of the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center; accepted three donations, including $5,000 from the Jack and Dorothy Byrne Foundation; approved a sexual-abuse-prevention curriculum for grades 2 to 5 and the state's K-8 competencies; and heard a parent describe bullying at Claremont Middle School. No agenda or minutes survive in the district's online shares; the page is built from the recording's dialogue transcript. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

HIGH A $5,000 private gift was accepted the night it was added to the agenda, with no sign of the prior public hearing the statute requires

RSA 198:20-b lets a school board accept "unanticipated money from a state, federal or other governmental unit or a private source" once the voters have adopted the enabling article, but ¶III(a) adds: "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with notice of the time, place and subject in a newspaper at least 7 days before. The Jack and Dorothy Byrne Foundation's gift to the preschool was exactly $5,000 (1:36:37), so the threshold is met. The superintendent added it to the agenda that night ("I would like to add two additional donations", 0:03:26), and the board accepted it with the cheer-team gift on a single voice vote (1:38:57 to 1:39:07). Nothing on the recording refers to a hearing, and a hearing noticed a week ahead would ordinarily have put the gift on the posted agenda rather than among the night's additions. The gift was not a surprise: the student representative announced it earlier in the meeting (0:06:32). If the board relied on some other authority, the record does not say so. Graded HIGH because the record points to acceptance without the statutory step; the finding would fall if the district holds a hearing notice and minutes for this gift. The two smaller gifts ($250 and $2,500) were under the threshold, where ¶III(b) requires notice in the agenda and in the minutes and acceptance in public session at a regular meeting; acceptance in public session is on the recording, and the agenda and minutes do not survive.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a)-(b) (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, eff. Aug. 29, 2005; the $5,000 threshold was raised only in 2023)

OBSERVATION The retention period the superintendent described for bus audio would exceed the statute's ten school days

The bus-surveillance policy moved to third reading (2:04:37). The statute that allows school-bus audio recording also limits how long it may be kept: "In no event, however, shall the recording be retained for longer than 10 school days unless the school district determines that the recording is relevant to a disciplinary proceeding, or a court orders that it be retained for a longer period of time." Rapp read the ten-school-day limit correctly (2:01:43). The superintendent described his former district's practice of "something like a month of recordings" (2:02:21) and suggested erasing "after, let us say, a month or three weeks" (2:03:25). A month of school days is about twice the limit. He said he would confirm the procedures with legal counsel, and this was a description of intent, not of a practice in place, so it is an observation. The administrative procedure the policy calls for would settle it.

Sources: RSA 570-A:2, II(k) (source note ends 2016, 169:1, eff. June 3, 2016; 322:2, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION A parent said the school did not send the notices the bullying statute requires; the record cannot test the claim

In citizens' comments a parent said that over two years of reported incidents "One letter has been sent home saying there was one incident. No follow up letters." and cited "RSA 193-F4" (0:39:24). In 2018, RSA 193-F:4, II required each district's policy to provide for notifying the parents of both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator within 48 hours of an incident report, and for an investigation to begin within 5 school days of the report. The superintendent did not dispute the account; he said he could not discuss other students publicly, that he had been working with the family for "well over a month", and that "The issue is not resolved" (0:45:44). The board did not discuss individual students, which was correct. Whether the notices were sent is a fact only the district's incident records can establish, so this is an observation. The statute was amended in 2025; the text cited here is the one in force on the meeting date.

Sources: RSA 193-F:4, II(h) and (j) (2017 codification; source note ends 2010, 155:2, eff. July 1, 2010)

POSITIVE A sensitive curriculum came with the statutory parent notice and an opt-out, and a reporting policy was aligned with the reporting duty

The board approved the sexual-abuse-prevention pilot with "a two week sort of posting period", a letter home the next day, a parent review session on March 7 and an opt-out procedure, and a start date of about March 16 (1:51:15 to 1:51:56). RSA 186:11, IX-c, as amended effective September 16, 2017, requires "not less than 2 weeks advance notice of curriculum course material used for instruction of human sexuality or human sexual education" and an alternative for a parent who objects; the plan described meets the notice period with time to spare. The curriculum also passed the district's Curriculum Council and the SAU cabinet before reaching the board (1:48:44). Separately, the superintendent rewrote the draft child-abuse reporting policy so it would not imply that a principal decides whether to report, because "It's the staff member who has the concern." (1:57:31). That matches RSA 169-C:29, which places the duty to report on each teacher and school official who suspects abuse. And the January 31 question about bus audio was answered at the next meeting with the statute in hand (2:01:03).

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-b and IX-c (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 251:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2017); RSA 169-C:29 (2016 codification; source 1979, 361:2)
