MEDIUM Draft minutes reached the public share four weeks after the meeting

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be “open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting” (statute text); for a Wednesday, January 17 meeting that is Wednesday, January 24, 2024. The draft minutes appear in the district's Drive share only inside the next meeting's packet folder (4. CSB 2.21.24): Drive records the file as created 2024-02-14 at 21:09 UTC and last modified the same afternoon (20:45 UTC), so the modification date does not show an earlier version. That is about twenty business days. Severity follows the project's rule for missing records: the minutes exist, the meeting was noticed and packeted, and the share date shows when the public could see them online, not whether a paper copy was available at the SAU office, so this is MEDIUM rather than HIGH. The same pattern (minutes posted only when the next packet is assembled) affects the January 3 minutes approved at this meeting.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification, in force from Oct. 3, 2023; minutes within 5 business days); draft minutes CSB meeting 1.17.24 (1).pdf (Drive metadata read 2026-10-03)

MEDIUM A board session was planned as an informal “work session” with phone participation

At 2:27:39 the chair described the extra session as one that “would be a work session. We wouldn't need to do it in this formal setting”, with “no votes taken”, and agreed Gallagher could call in. Under RSA 91-A:2, I a meeting is the convening of a quorum to discuss or act on a matter within the body's jurisdiction; the statute's four exclusions do not include a work session, and the purpose stated here (assigning the board's deliberative presentation and preparing answers to public questions) is board business. Remote participation is allowed under RSA 91-A:2, III(a) only when attendance “is not reasonably practical” (statute text), with the reason stated in the minutes. The approved minutes record the plan as “January 24th at 6:00 at Dow (working session)”. The only surviving record of that session is a set of minutes dated January 29, 2024, filed in the 2/21/24 packet, which assigns slides to members and directs a motion to restrict reconsideration but gives no attendance, times or location; no notice, agenda or recording has been located. That session is outside this page; the flag is the plan as stated on the record here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I, II and III(a) (2023 codification; meeting defined; 24-hour notice; remote participation); Minutes from CSB working session 1.29.24 (1).pdf

OBSERVATION The business administrator says prior years' audits were still open

Explaining why the board held back fund balance, Mary Henry said at 2:01:30 “we're still in the process of being audited”, that she had done the DOE-25 and straightened out FY23, and “I still think that 22 and 21st May fall” (transcript as it stands; the sense appears to be that FY2022 and FY2021 figures may still change). Federal rules require a single audit for any year in which a district spends $750,000 or more in federal awards (the FY2021 to FY2023 threshold), submitted within the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or “nine months after the end of the audit period” (regulation text); for a July to June year ending June 30, 2022 that was March 31, 2023. The district was spending ESSER money in those years, so the threshold was very likely met, but this recording does not say which years' audits were unissued or whether reporting packages were filed. The Federal Audit Clearinghouse record for the district would settle it. RSA 21-J:19 governs state-side audits and is permissive as to timing.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2023 edition (due nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501 (single audit at $750,000 for fiscal years before Oct. 1, 2024); RSA 21-J:19 (audits; permissive)

OBSERVATION ESSER money voted for a modular classroom before any approval

The board voted at 1:50:53 to “utilize the Esser funds to purchase a modular classroom for the academy” (clerk's reading). The chair said at 1:30:56 that “we still need to get everything approved through SR” and that “they're really scrutinizing construction projects”. Under the 2023 Uniform Guidance, capital expenditures for buildings are unallowable as direct charges “except with the prior written approval of the Federal awarding agency or pass-through entity” (regulation text), here the NH Department of Education. Crawford gave the deadline as “September 24th” (1:31:37); the U.S. Department of Education's date is that ARP ESSER funds “must be obligated by September 30, 2024” (ED memo). The vote is lawful as an expression of intent; whether approval was sought and obtained, and whether the unit was obligated in time, is not in this record. The amendment's fallback (one more year at the tech center) covers the case where it was not.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.439(b)(1), 2023 edition (capital expenditures need prior written approval); U.S. Department of Education, ARP ESSER and EANS obligation deadlines (obligate by Sept. 30, 2024)

OBSERVATION A non-CTE program in a state-aided CTE building, without the memorandum the state asked for

RSA 188-E:3, II, as it read in January 2024, makes a state-aided regional center facility district property “for use by the career and technical education center exclusively” (statute text). The tech center's director raised exactly this at 1:42:32 and said attorney Upton “couldn't say either way” whether housing The Academy there affects renovation funding (1:41:05); Exhibit A states the opposite conclusion (“no impact on renovation funding”, quoted from Exhibit A) without giving a source. Herzog also said the state had advised a one-year memorandum of understanding two years earlier and the former superintendent refused it (1:45:25). The board's amended motion keeps the tech center as the fallback location for 2024-25, and the minutes record that an MOU will be drawn up. No state ruling is in the record either way, so this is an observation: the open legal question is the state's view of temporary non-CTE use, which an Education Department letter or the MOU would settle.

Sources: RSA 188-E:3, II (2023 codification; source note ends 2021, 210:2; exclusive CTE use); Exhibit A-The Academy @ SHS (1).pdf

OBSERVATION The legal basis for holding $420,000 is not stated

Henry said at 2:00:34 that the board “chose to hold 426,000” of FY23 fund balance and “This year they've chosen to hold 420,000”, and at 2:07:29 that “you can't hold it for more than that year”. The statute that lets a New Hampshire district keep year-end unassigned funds, RSA 198:4-b, II, requires that the legislative body first authorize retention, caps the total at 5 percent of net assessment, and says “Prior to expending retained general funds, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing” with 7 days' newspaper notice (statute text). It sets no one-year limit. The minutes record that using the money “will have to hold a public hearing”, which matches the statute; but the recording does not say whether the $420,000 is RSA 198:4-b, II money or simply surplus the board chose not to apply to the tax rate. The audited fund-balance note (GASB 54 classification) or the district's retention warrant article would settle it.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II and II(a) (2023 codification; source note ends 2020, 38:25; 5% cap; hearing before expending); GASB Statement 54, paragraphs 6 to 17 (fund-balance classes)

POSITIVE Finance subcommittee budget sessions were televised live
