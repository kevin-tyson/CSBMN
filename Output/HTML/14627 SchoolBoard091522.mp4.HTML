Claremont School Board, September 21, 2022

Regular meeting, five of seven members present. The board voted to call a public hearing on October 5 and a special school district meeting to place $429,867.51 of new Senate Bill 420 state aid in three reserve funds (energy, transportation, capital improvements), moved policies JICD and JICK to a third reading, heard an ESSER III spending report and a curriculum presentation reporting that the summer program's growth data was lost, and learned that a 2019 special education expendable trust still holds $650,000. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 21, 2022, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The board committed the whole SB 420 grant to fuel, bus and building reserves without discussing the plan the grant law requires

Seaman described the $429,867.51 as SB 420 aid for communities with the most unbalanced ratio of free and reduced lunch pupils to property valuation (1:21:30). That is the extraordinary need grant created by 2022 N.H. Laws ch. 318 (SB 420), now RSA 198:40-f. Paragraph II of that section, in force from July 1, 2022, reads: "In order to receive an extraordinary need grant, the eligible school district shall provide a plan to the department of education outlining how the district intends to use grant award funds to improve the educational achievement and growth of students", and the plan must include "an accountability component designed to generate data that measures student academic achievement and growth of knowledge and skills in reading and language arts and/or mathematics" and states that "The primary goal of this grant is to improve student achievement and growth" (statute text). The board's discussion (1:24:23 to 1:32:28) treated the money as a windfall to bank against energy, bus and roof costs, and no one on the recording mentioned the plan, the Department of Education or student achievement before the vote. The requirement was known to administration: eight days later the superintendent told the SAU 6 board that "We have to have accountability plans for both saying how we're going to use the money" (show 14630, quoted from that meeting's transcript; see SAU 6 Board, September 29, 2022). Mitigation: the vote only set a hearing on a draft warrant, and on October 5 a member raised exactly this question and the board tabled the warrant to get counsel's advice. Whether the statute restricts what a district may spend the grant on, as opposed to requiring a plan, is a legal question this page does not decide; the gap is that the board voted without the question being asked.

Sources: RSA 198:40-f, I-II, 2022 codification (source: 2022, 318:1, eff. July 1, 2022): extraordinary need grants; plan to the department with an accountability component; RSA 198:41, I(e), 2022 codification (2022, 318:2): the grant is added to the municipality's total education grant

OBSERVATION First meeting under the new public-comment statute: a seven-minute period limited to non-agenda items, with questions sent to email

RSA 189:74, effective September 6, 2022, requires a school board to "provide the opportunity for the public to comment on school district matters" at its meetings and sets the period at "no less than 30 minutes" (statute text). Fifteen days later the chair opened citizens' comments at 0:03:03 with the reminder that they "are for non-agenda items" and closed them at 0:10:40 after two speakers, about seven and a half minutes. No one was turned away, and the text does not settle whether the 30 minutes is a floor on the opportunity or on elapsed time, so this is not graded as a violation. Two points remain for review: the statute speaks of "school district matters", while the board's rule confines the period to non-agenda items; and Crawford's three questions were deferred to email with no answer on the record. The chair did let her ask a question during the graduation-profile item (0:40:15), and Sprague's committee report proposed a process so the whole board hears such answers (0:31:46). On October 5 Crawford said she was still waiting for one of them.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022): public comment at school board meetings, no less than 30 minutes

OBSERVATION The earliest public copy of these minutes is the approved version, posted 27 days after the meeting

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute text), which for September 21, 2022 was September 28. The only copy located is Approved CSB Meeting Minutes 9.21.22, created in the October 5 packet folder on October 18, 2022. A draft must have existed for the board to approve it on October 5, but none was found in Drive. The September 7 minutes, filed in this meeting's folder, were created there on September 23, also more than five business days after their meeting. Drive posting dates show when the public share received a file, not whether minutes were available for inspection at the SAU office, so this is an observation rather than a finding. On the content side, the approved minutes do name the mover and seconder of each motion, as RSA 91-A:2, II has required since January 1, 2019.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2022 codification (source note ends 2018, 244:1): minutes, mover and seconder, five business days

OBSERVATION The motion, the agenda, the minutes and the later warrant give different statutes and dates for the special meeting

The agenda cites "Special Town Meeting (RSA 197:3)"; Horsky's motion, as transcribed, cites RSA 197 followed by Paragraph three (split across two transcript rows) and sets the vote "on October 19th" (1:32:28); Seaman also proposed the 19th (1:20:45). The approved minutes record the special meeting as "10.20.22 at Stevens High school auditorium" (minutes text), and the draft warrant presented on October 5 calls the meeting under RSA 197:3-a for "Wednesday, October 20, 2022" (warrant text), a date that fell on a Thursday; the error was corrected aloud on October 5. RSA 197:3-a is the specific procedure for a single-session special meeting in an official-ballot district responding to a change in aid under RSA 198:41; it requires the hearing "at least 14 days prior to the meeting" (statute text), which either date satisfies after an October 5 hearing. The minutes describe a decision the recording does not show being made; the change from the 19th to the 20th is not explained on this tape.

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, IV-VII, 2022 codification (source: 2013, 197:1): hearing at least 14 days before; notice 7 days before hearing and meeting; no other business; Claremont Special District Warrant - 10.20.22 - FINAL.pdf

OBSERVATION A $650,000 special education trust sat unused for nearly three years while staffing for students with IEPs was described as a crisis

Seaman confirmed that the expendable trust created at the December 2019 special meeting "still has a balance of $650,000" because the program it was meant to fund was paid from the local budget (1:36:07). Skillen reported "a lot of concern in the community among parents of students with IEPs" (1:36:47); Nester attributed the shortfall to the labor market, not money (1:37:22). No rule requires a trust to be spent, and drawing on an expendable trust under RSA 198:20-c requires a prior public hearing, so the board's next step would be a request from administration, which Skillen invited (1:38:11). The figures and the request are recorded here so later pages can follow what happened to the fund.

Sources: the recording (1:34:00 to 1:38:11); RSA 198:20-c, 2019 codification (source ends 2012, 219:2, 3): expendable trust funds; public hearing before expenditure

POSITIVE A candidate's introduction was handled evenhandedly

Before a state House candidate spoke, the chair said the board had invited candidates before, stated "This is not in any way an endorsement of this board", and extended the same invitation to every candidate watching (0:03:27, 0:03:55). The approved minutes repeat the disclaimer. No law requires this; it is the kind of even handling that keeps a public meeting from becoming a campaign platform for one side.

Sources: the recording (0:03:27 to 0:04:09); Approved CSB Meeting Minutes 9.21.22

POSITIVE Final adoption of two policies was held back so the absent members could weigh in

With two members absent, a member asked to wait, the mover withdrew a motion for third and final reading, and the board instead moved the policies to a third reading "just in case they had something to add" (0:34:55 to 0:35:31). The two policies were adopted at the next meeting. Taking an extra reading is not required by statute; it gave the full board a voice on a student-discipline policy.

Sources: the recording (0:34:01 to 0:35:38); Approved CSB Meeting Minutes 9.21.22
