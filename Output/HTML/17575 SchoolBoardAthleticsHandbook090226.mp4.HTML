Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 2, 2026, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM An athletics checking account of over $20,000, signed on by school staff, sits outside the treasurer's books

At 0:20:53 the superintendent described an athletics checking account, used to pay game officials, holding more than $20,000 at the start of the year, with the athletic director, the Stevens principal and the Stevens assistant principals as signatories, plus a separate checking account held by each principal (0:20:30). He linked it to a policy line on members of the same immediate family handling parts of one purchasing action and said "that is the case right now" (0:21:21). Paying game officials is an ordinary district expense. If the account holds district money, RSA 197:23-a puts all money belonging to the district in the treasurer's custody, paid out only on the school board's orders or those of two or more members the board empowers; an account drawn on by building staff sits outside that chain. If instead the account holds student-raised money, GASB 84 still treats funds the district controls as district (governmental) activity, not fiduciary money held for others, so it belongs in the district's books either way. The district's own draft FY22 corrective-action plan lists finding 2022-012, "Student Activity Fund Maintenance (Material Weakness)", and promises a policy by June 30, 2027 (packet document). Mitigation stated on tape: the superintendent raised the account himself, said he will change it and will report in detail; he said he does not plan to act immediately this year. The record does not show the account's funding source, its bank, or the family relationship he alluded to. That is what a reviewer would need.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (treasurer's custody of district money; payment only on board orders); GASB Statement 84 (fiduciary activities; district-controlled activity is not fiduciary); Corrective Actions - Claremont FY22 Audit - Sept 2 2026 DRAFT.docx.pdf (finding 2022-012)

OBSERVATION The coaches' handbook vote is recorded only as a chorus of nays

At 0:22:20 the chair calls the ayes and the nays together in one breath and closes with "I think that is overwhelming." No aye and no count is audible, and the chair never announces that the motion failed. The draft minutes fill the gap: "Voice vote taken, all present voting in opposition" (minutes text). The outcome is not in doubt, but the recording alone would not let a viewer say what the board decided. A stated result (the motion fails, with the count) is the cheap fix. No statute requires a chair to announce a result aloud; RSA 91-A:2, II requires final decisions to be in the minutes, and here they are.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes record final decisions); 9.2.26 DRAFT CSB Minutes.docx

OBSERVATION Both handbook drafts stated rules the board could not trace to policy or to the governing body's rules

Members found statements in the drafts that cite no source or conflict with board policy: a mouth-guard rule attributed to "NHIAA by-laws" for soccer and basketball, which Rapp says the association only recommends (0:09:22); no Unified sports team in the sports list (0:01:08); an unnamed "appeals committee" (0:05:33); overnight trips approved by the athletic director with no board approval (0:17:32); a school-rules section that "comes across as district policy" with no policy behind it (0:17:55); and transportation to non-playing events charged to student activity funds (0:16:30). The packet copy of the players' handbook carries the mouth-guard sentence as Rapp read it and lists no Unified team. This page did not verify the association's by-laws or the board's field-trip policy; whether each statement is wrong is for the revision to settle. The board did not adopt either document, so none of these took effect through this meeting.

Sources: Stevens_Athletic_Handbook.docx.pdf (packet); SHS Coaching Handbook (DB).docx.pdf (packet)

POSITIVE The board read both handbooks line by line in public and declined to approve them as drafted

Rather than approving the documents on a motion, members put written critiques on the record (0:00:44 to 0:09:08, 0:12:37, 0:13:31 to 0:19:31), the mover withdrew the players' motion on the merits (0:06:22), and the board voted the coaches' handbook down (0:22:20). Lavalette offered to help the athletic director with the rewrite (0:22:05), and the superintendent volunteered a control problem in the athletics accounts before he had full information (0:19:59), saying he should "disclose things to you as I learn about them" (0:21:44). No law requires a board to approve handbooks at all; the point here is practice, not compliance.
