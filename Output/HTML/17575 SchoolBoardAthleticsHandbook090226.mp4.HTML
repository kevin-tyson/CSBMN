Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer’s aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; an Observation rests on no confirmed rule. Flags 2 to 4 rest on the agenda and minutes, because the recording does not cover those parts of the meeting.

MEDIUM An athletics checking account holding over $20,000 is signed by school staff outside the treasurer, and the superintendent says family members are among the signers

The superintendent told the board at 0:20:53 that an athletics checking account, used to pay game officials, has had the athletic director, the Stevens principal and the Stevens assistant principals as signatories and carried “over $20,000 at the beginning of the year.” Each principal also has a separate “principals account” (0:20:30). He linked the athletics account to a policy line “about members of the same immediate family,” adding “that is the case right now” (0:21:21). The coaching handbook under review routes trip costs and fundraising through student activity accounts (0:16:30, 0:17:07). By statute the district treasurer has “custody of all moneys belonging to the district” and pays them out only on orders of the board or of members it empowers. Whether the money in these accounts belongs to the district is the question the corrective action will have to settle; money used to pay game officials for district teams is hard to classify otherwise. Related signers on one account weaken the separation of duties a second signature is meant to provide. Mitigation: the superintendent raised it himself, said student activity funds as such are acceptable, tied the fix to an FY22 audit corrective action, and said he is already changing the family-signer situation.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (treasurer’s custody of all district moneys; payment only on board orders); the recording at the timestamps above; draft minutes, item V.3 (“an athletics checking account to pay game officials”).

MEDIUM The draft minutes record a 5–2 decision on hats with no motion, mover or seconder, and omit who produced them

Under the dress-code item the draft minutes read “5-2 believe hats should be allowed”, with no motion, mover, seconder or names. On September 16 a board member asked that the minutes be corrected to say “the board voted 5 to 2 to allow hats” (see the September 16 page). RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to record “the names of the members who made or seconded each motion,” and, for meetings from August 22, 2025, “the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes.” The draft gives the end time (10:23 p.m.) but only the scheduled 6:30 p.m. start, and names the clerk in its header without stating who produced it; the nonpublic minutes do state it (“recorded by: Noelle Kronberg”). This part of the meeting was not transcribed, so the page cannot say whether a motion was made. Mitigation: the draft was in the Meeting Minutes share on September 9, within five business days, and is subject to correction on approval.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (mover and seconder; start and end time and producer, 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025); 9.2.26 DRAFT CSB Minutes.docx.pdf.

MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes record a decision on tuition agreements without each member’s vote, and nothing recorded fits the second exemption cited

The agenda and draft minutes give the grounds as RSA 91-A:3, II(c) and (k). The nonpublic minutes describe only “Discussion of tuition agreements and anchor school identification”, which fits II(k), a school board’s consideration of a pupil tuition contract. Nothing recorded fits II(c), matters likely to affect adversely the reputation of a person other than a board member. The minutes also record a decision: “the Board authorized the Superintendent to review and negotiate all tuition agreements.” RSA 91-A:3, III requires nonpublic minutes to “record all actions in such a manner that the vote of each member is ascertained and recorded”; these give no vote, and the public draft minutes do not mention the decision. In the text version of the nonpublic minutes read for this page, the statutory-reason field reproduces the full list of exemptions from II(a) to II(l) and the roll-call table shows no marks; the form’s check boxes may not have survived conversion, so the PDF should be checked. Mitigation: the public minutes record the mover, seconder and a roll call to enter, and the nonpublic minutes were filed unsealed in the public share. Any tuition contract that follows must be approved in public after the contract and these minutes are made public, under II(k).

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), I(c), II(c), II(k), III (2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023; text read 2026-09-25); 9.2.26 Nonpublic Minutes.docx.pdf; agenda “CSB Agenda - September 2 2026”.
