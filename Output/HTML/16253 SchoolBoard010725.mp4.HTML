Claremont School Board, January 7, 2025

Board meeting called on December 18 to ratify two collective bargaining agreements and receive the Finance Committee's FY26 budget. The board ratified the paraprofessionals' and administrators' agreements, accepted a ski-program donation, adopted four policies, and voted to move the budget "as presented" to the January 15 public hearing: a 6.74% general-fund increase, $350,330 to raise after new revenue before the agreements, and the elimination of the district preschool over one member's written dissent. After the recording ends, the board went into nonpublic session and, according to minutes it sealed for six months, authorized a $255,000 property purchase. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on January 7, 2025, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM A $255,000 property purchase was authorized in nonpublic session and sealed, and the public minutes do not mention the decision

On tape the chair described the added nonpublic session as "one related to compensation and one related to purchase of property" (0:01:36); the motion, the roll call and the session itself came after the recording ends (2:08:45). The public minutes record a motion under "RSA 91-A:3, II (a) & (d)", roll calls in and out, and "a motion to seal the minutes for 6 months" on a roll call, all in favor. The nonpublic minutes, since unsealed, record the only business: "permission for Superintendent Chris Pratt to purchase property at 40 Maple Ave in Claremont" for "the district alternative program in the amount of $255,000", moved by Whitney, seconded by Sprague, "voice vote taken: all voting in favor." Three points need checking. First, RSA 91-A:3, III allows minutes to be withheld only on a two-thirds recorded vote that also determines that disclosure would likely harm a person's reputation, render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism; the public minutes record the vote without any determination, and the text of the nonpublic form does not show which ground was marked. Second, power "To build, purchase, rent, repair, or remove schoolhouses" and to procure land is given to school districts by RSA 194:3, I–II, and RSA 32:8 bars a school board from incurring a liability for any purpose without an appropriation; nothing in this meeting's record shows the appropriation or voter authorization the purchase rests on. Third, no compensation decision is recorded although compensation was one of the two stated subjects. Graded MEDIUM: the statute permits property negotiations in nonpublic under II(d), the seal was voted by more than two-thirds, and the authority question may be answered by a document outside this record (a warrant article, a reserve fund, or a later district vote).

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(d), III (nonpublic motion, property exemption, sealing with a determination); RSA 194:3, I–II (district powers over land and buildings; 1979, 459:4); RSA 32:8 (no liability without appropriation); 1.7.25 CSB nonpublic minutes Unsealed

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2024 codification; five business days; business day defined); 12. Approved CSB minutes 1.7.25

OBSERVATION Preschool was eliminated with two different cost figures on the record and no vote on the promised follow-up

The approved minutes also record Whitney's assurance that "there will be no reduction in services embedded in IEPs"; federal rules still require a continuum of placements and placement in the least restrictive appropriate setting decided with the parents (34 CFR 300.115(a), 300.116), and Hawkins's dissent raises exactly that: "how long will we provide services offsite?" and the loss of typical peers (1:38:35). OBSERVATION A member's description of the catastrophic-aid statute does not match its text; the representative's answer does

Crawford said "the law says that it will reimburse at 95% anything over 70,000" and asked how the state could pay 67% (0:06:35). RSA 186-C:18, III, as in force in FY2025 (2023, 79:141, 142), makes the state liable for 80 percent of a child's costs between 3½ and 10 times the state average expenditure per pupil and for all costs above 10 times; no 95 percent figure and no $70,000 figure appear in it. The paragraph also provides for proration when the appropriation is insufficient, with no minimum share at that time; Damon's answer (0:07:17) matches it. A floor of 80 percent of entitlement was added later, by 2025 N.H. HB 2 §137, and does not apply to FY2025. Recorded as a speaker's characterization corrected on the record.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2024 codification; last amended 2023, 79:141, 142); 2025 N.H. HB 2, §137 (later floor; not in force in FY2025)
