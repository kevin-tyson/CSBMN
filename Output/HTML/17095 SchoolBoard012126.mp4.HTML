Claremont School Board, January 21, 2026

Regular meeting opening with a public hearing on $134,883.11 in trust income, held the night after the budget hearing. The board cut the aligned-model operating budget to $42,957,713 by a 4–3 vote, approved a $44,449,704 default budget, built the 2026 warrant (dropping a separate athletics article and voting not to recommend the petitioned budget cap), and voted 6–1 to pursue an executive-director model for district leadership. MEDIUM The approved minutes give no call-to-order time, and in several places they differ from the recording on who seconded, how a vote went, or what was said

Since August 22, 2025, RSA 91-A:2, II has required minutes to include "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes" (statute text). The approved minutes name their author and give the end ("Consent adjournment at 9:40pm") and the hearing's close ("Closed at 6:34pm"), but no call-to-order time; the 6:30 PM beside the heading is the agenda's scheduled time carried into the document. Several entries also disagree with the recording. The minutes record the trust vote as unanimous where the chair's words are transcribed "Any nays? One." (0:03:42). They give Hawkins's salary point as "the salary would not be over $200,000 for an executive director" (minutes text) where she says "the salary that was discussed was over $200,000" (2:54:10). They name Sprague and Whitney as mover and seconder on JLCK, where the dialogue file labels the motion Petrin (3:06:11) and the second Sprague (3:06:16). At the February 4 meeting the board approved a correction to page 15 of these minutes, without for with in the bullet on Hawkins's concern; as read on 2026-10-03, the posted approved minutes still say she "is worried about putting someone in the position with the correct experience" (minutes text). The seconder differences may be the dialogue file's error (see the method note); the start time, the salary sentence and the uncorrected bullet are the minutes' own. Graded MEDIUM: the content requirement is binding, the record mitigates it (the minutes name every mover, seconder and split vote, and their times reconcile with the recording), and the defects are of completeness and accuracy rather than absence.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes contents; start and end time and producer added by 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025); 1.21.26 Approved CSB Meeting Minutes

OBSERVATION The board cut $1,886,660 from the bottom line on the night it had to finalize, leaving the posted line-item form to show athletics at $1 while the majority said athletics stays funded

Whitney's amendment took option A from $44,844,373 to $42,957,713 (1:25:26), 4–3, the day after the budget hearing. A post-hearing reduction is lawful: RSA 32:5, I has the body "finalize the budget" after public testimony. The difficulty is what the voters will see. Angell explained that the MS-26 lists "budgeted expenditures by line item" (1:48:59) and must be posted by January 26, the last Monday in January under RSA 40:13, II-a(d); without a further meeting, athletics appears at $1 and the board would move money into it later under its transfer power (1:54:08, 1:55:36). The chair said "I'm not defunding athletics" (1:52:19); Angell said that guaranteeing it would run "would be dishonest to the voters" (1:56:42). No allocation of the cut was adopted. Separately, the default budget form ($44,449,704) had "three signatures on it" before the board voted on it (1:57:56, 1:59:00); the public vote then approved it, which cures the sequence, but the form was circulated for signature ahead of the meeting. The default exceeds the proposed budget by $1,491,991, so defeating the budget article would raise spending. No rule requires a line allocation at this stage; the observation is that the posted document and the board's stated intent diverge.

Sources: RSA 32:5, I (budget finalized after the conclusion of public testimony); RSA 40:13, II-a(d) and IX(b) (budgets posted by the last Monday in January; default budget); RSA 32:10 (governing body may transfer between appropriations within the total voted); 1.21.26 Approved CSB Meeting Minutes

OBSERVATION A separate athletics article was dropped without a motion or vote, on legal advice relayed by text during the meeting

Angell's draft warrant carried an athletics article; he told the board to "Ignore article three." (2:02:45) once the bottom-line amendment passed. Crawford asked, "So may I make a motion that we put article three forward?" (2:06:04). Before a motion was made, Angell relayed that he had texted counsel during the amendment and that a defeated athletics article would give a "strong argument to have no athletics at all next year" (2:06:10). The discussion ended with "It didn't get added." (2:08:54); the minutes record "The article won't be included". A board may decline to place an article, and counsel's caution is a reasonable one, but the board never decided the question by vote, and the advice reached it second-hand from a text exchange no one read aloud. No verified rule is offered; the record would be clearer with the request put to a vote.
