Claremont School Board, Organizational Meeting, March 20, 2024

Organizational meeting of the Claremont School Board after the March 12, 2024 election, six of seven members present. With Interim Superintendent Chris Pratt presiding, the board re-elects Heather Whitney chair and Frank Sprague vice chair by voice vote; it accepts a $462,328 SAFE grant after a public hearing, accepts a lead-remediation grant whose amount is never stated, approves tuition agreements with Croydon and Goshen, seats its subcommittees by consent, hears the Bluff and Maple Avenue principals, and puts off the draft by-laws to a next meeting that the record does not show taking place. MEDIUM The board accepted a lead-remediation grant without any amount on the record

Asked "Is there a number?", the mover answered "Yeah. No. For that." (1:13:56 to 1:13:58), and the motion read back was simply "A motion to accept the lead remediation grant." (1:14:53). The agenda, Exhibit D and the minutes state no amount either. RSA 198:20-b sets different duties by size: for unanticipated funds of $20,000 or more a prior public hearing with newspaper notice (III(a)); below that, notice "of the funds in the agenda", notice in the minutes, and acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting" (III(b)). The item was on the agenda and accepted in public session, and eight fixtures suggest a modest sum, but without a figure no reader can tell which limb applied, and the board voted to accept money it had not quantified. Koski said the minutes would be sent to the state to unlock reimbursement (1:11:59).

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a)–(b) (source note ends 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023); Exhibit D; approved minutes, item VII.2

MEDIUM The draft minutes reached the public share 27 days after the meeting

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting", which for Wednesday, March 20, 2024 was Wednesday, March 27. OBSERVATION Lead above the action level at eight outlets; parent notice is not in the record, and the units were misstated

Exhibit D (Steven Holt, February 28, 2024) reports "8 outlets requiring remediation between the Claremont Middle School and the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center" that "tested higher than the allowable 5 parts per billion", most already refitted. RSA 485:17-a sets the 5 parts-per-billion action level and, on an exceedance, requires notice to parents within 5 business days, an alternative supply of compliant water, and a remediation plan. Koski described the district's interim measure of not using first-draw water (1:12:44); the record examined does not show whether parents were notified. Pratt and Koski both spoke of "parts per million" (1:13:14, 1:14:34), a thousand times the unit in the exhibit and the statute, which understates nothing but could mislead a listener about the scale.

Sources: RSA 485:17-a (lead in school drinking water; 2018, 4:18, eff. July 1, 2019; 2022, 325:1, eff. July 8, 2022); Exhibit D

OBSERVATION The SAFE grant hearing required published newspaper notice that is not in the record examined

At $462,328 the SAFE grant falls under RSA 198:20-b, III(a): "the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with notice "published in a newspaper of general circulation in the relevant municipality at least 7 days before the meeting is held", here by March 13, 2024. The agenda cites the paragraph and the board held the hearing (0:04:43 to 0:11:06). The packet folder was created March 15, so the agenda itself could not serve as the 7-day notice; no newspaper notice is in the packet or minutes, and no one mentions one. Notices of this kind are not normally filed in the packet, so this marks a gap in what could be checked, not a finding that notice was missed.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a); agenda, item IV
