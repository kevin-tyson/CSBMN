OBSERVATION A middle-school trip was cleared by informal board consensus, with no motion and an unanswered liability question

The Claremont Middle School athletic director told the board he had been told by administration that the district fleet could not be used, that he could not buy tickets or hire a bus himself because, as a school employee, that would make it a school-sanctioned trip, and that the district would then be liable (1:14:17, 1:25:07). The superintendent explained the bus decision by a shortage of drivers: “we don't have backup drivers.” (1:21:14). The chair asked “are we are we actually liable.” (1:26:23) The superintendent answered that “I didn't get into that depth with with principal Romeo or Sean” (1:26:28), and no one answered the legal question. Members then told the director he could proceed: “So you can do the transaction.” (1:29:13); “Yeah, I don't care.” (1:29:14); and the chair closed with “So I don't think we need to vote or anything.” (1:32:05) The result reverses an administrative instruction on the strength of remarks around the table. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to record final decisions; a direction given without a motion leaves nothing for minutes to record, and the liability question the chair raised stayed open. Members also compared the refusal with the board's approval of a high-school trip to Puerto Rico (1:17:32, 1:28:00), which the student representative said has 52 students signed up (1:57:36).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2021 codification): minutes record final decisions; the recording

POSITIVE COVID-19 cases reported to the public by school in aggregate counts, with no student identified

The lead nurse walked the board through September and October counts for each school: students placed on protocol, household contacts, those who chose not to test and those who tested positive (for example “in September for Stevens High School, there were 79 students that we put on protocol” (0:40:06)). She also disclosed an error, a fourth-grade cluster at Maple Avenue that the state posted before the district reported it: “So this this cluster was not reported. And I want to apologize to all of you.” (0:49:13) The district's own tracking sheet holds identifiable information (“It includes the child's name their their grade, whether or not they've been vaccinated” (0:37:52)), and the nurse said exposure letters are kept general because “we also need to protect privacy.” (0:57:13) On the ordinary reading of FERPA, a district record like that sheet is an education record, and FERPA bars disclosing personally identifiable information from them without signed parental consent except as 34 CFR 99.31 allows. Reporting only aggregates in public meets that rule; reporting them at this level of detail, in public and on camera, goes beyond any requirement this page found. One remark should not be relied on: Whitney said HIPAA “Only has to do with information from a healthcare provider to an insurance.” (0:43:39) HIPAA's definition of protected health information excludes health information in FERPA-covered education records (current eCFR text; this page did not verify the 2021 edition), so FERPA, not HIPAA, is the rule for school health records.
