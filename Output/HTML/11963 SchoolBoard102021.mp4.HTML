Claremont School Board — October 20, 2021

Regular meeting summary, preceded by a public hearing on two donations, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. In just over two hours the board heard no public comment on a $10,500 grant and a $1,000 donation and later accepted both, tabled the September 15 minutes, heard the lead school nurse's school-by-school COVID-19 protocol figures, told a middle school athletic director he could charter a bus for a Boston game, learned from its Capital Improvement Committee that the Maple Avenue roof had reportedly been paid from the emergency reserve fund without a vote anyone could recall, and discussed board members' mask use. MEDIUM The Maple Avenue roof was reportedly paid from the voter-created emergency reserve fund, and no member could recall a board vote authorizing it

Reporting for the Capital Improvement Committee at 1:37:47, Rob Lovett said the fund was established "through a warrant article last year" for emergency building maintenance, that the committee understood "any funds being removed from that would have to be approved by the board", and that the committee had been told the summer's Maple Avenue roof repair "Was paid for through the emergency reserve fund". The chair: "Well, I think we have to vote it out." Whitney said her own notes showed "nothing indicating that we discussed it as an approval" and recalled telling voters that the fund would not be spent "unless the board by majority vote, agree with it." Horsky recalled the opposite answer at an earlier meeting, that the fund "wasn't touched for the roof". The superintendent said "I don't have that record" and would follow up with the business administrator, who was absent.

Two things are unsettled on this record: whether the money actually came from the fund, and what the warrant article said about who may authorize spending. If the fund is a capital reserve fund under RSA chapter 35, RSA 35:15, I provides that the trustees "shall hold the same until such time as the town, district or county shall have voted to withdraw funds" or has named agents to carry out its purposes; the text in force on this date was the 2021 amendment, effective August 30, 2021. If the district named the school board as its agent, spending is a board decision, which is what the members understood. Either way, a withdrawal from a voter-created reserve that no board member could connect to a board vote is a probable control gap. It is graded MEDIUM because the record shows an unresolved question, not an established unauthorized payment. Follow-up belongs on a later page: the warrant article, the trustees' records, and any later board ratification.

Sources: RSA 35:15, I (capital reserve funds held until the district votes or names agents; source note ends 2021, 105:2, eff. August 30, 2021).

OBSERVATION The board's new per-item public comment procedure was not offered for most items, and members disagreed about what it required

On September 15 the board adopted, temporarily and pending a policy, comment by citizens on each discussion and action item with a three-minute limit (see the September 15 page; Horsky restates the motion at 0:28:50). At this meeting no comment was invited on the COVID-19 report, the trips or the subcommittee reports. At 1:41:11 Stone raised it: "I know that we didn't do this all meeting". Petrin understood it applied "only if there's a motion on the floor"; Horsky said "We did it open ended". The chair then invited comment on the donation motion. No one in the room appears to have sought to speak, so no one is shown being turned away. New Hampshire's statutory 30-minute public comment requirement for school boards, RSA 189:74, was enacted in 2022 and did not apply on this date, so this is recorded as an observation about the board's own rule, not a statutory one.

Sources: No statute in force on this date; the rule is the board's own motion of September 15, 2021 as restated on this recording.

OBSERVATION The chair put on the record that his remote yes in the September 15 Fish Tank vote was meant for a different question

At 0:30:10 the chair said he had participated remotely on September 15 while home with COVID-19, and that "even though I voted yes, my vote was for Mike's motion to move the question not on fish tank." He did not ask to change the vote and did not say how he would have voted. On the September 15 recording, the members later concluded that the call of the question had never been seconded, so the roll call stood as the vote on the motion itself. The September 15 minutes were tabled at this meeting with corrections pending, and neither they nor any later version are online, so how the district finally recorded his vote cannot be checked. On the audible roll, the motion carried whether or not his vote is counted.

The episode bears on RSA 91-A:2, III as it read in 2021, which allows remote participation only when attendance "is not reasonably practical", requires the reason to be "stated in the minutes", and requires that "All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote." The September 15 page examines that meeting's compliance. The chair's own conclusion, that motions should be restated before votes, is the right one; the SAU 6 meeting the next evening shows the SAU chair doing exactly that.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a) and (e) (2019 codification; in force until October 3, 2023); the September 15, 2021 page.

OBSERVATION A board member stated that Fish Tank had been implemented as the elementary social studies program contrary to the board's instruction, and the superintendent accepted responsibility

POSITIVE The board held a public hearing before accepting the $10,500 grant and accepted both gifts by a public vote

RSA 198:20-b as it read in 2021 let a school board accept unanticipated money "without further action by the school district" once the district had adopted the enabling article, and provided: "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing", with newspaper notice of its time, place and subject at least 7 days before. The $10,500 grant was above that threshold and was heard at 0:00:11; the $1,000 gift, below it, was heard too. Acceptance came by motion and public vote at 1:50:54, with citizens invited to comment. The chair's recollection of the threshold, 5000, was correct for this date; the superintendent's 500 was not, and the chair said he would check. Whether the newspaper notice ran and whether the district had adopted the enabling article cannot be confirmed from the recording. The $20,000 threshold in today's statute dates from 2023 and does not apply here.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, in force until 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023).

POSITIVE The lead nurse gave school-by-school COVID-19 figures in public, disclosed a cluster the district had failed to report, and identified no student

From 0:35:37 the lead nurse explained the send-home process, the tracking spreadsheet, and September and October counts for each school of students on protocol, household contacts, those who chose not to test, and positives. At 0:49:13 she volunteered that a Maple Avenue fourth grade cluster "was not reported. And I want to apologize to all of you." She explained that exposure letters are kept general partly to "protect privacy", and agreed to consider naming grade levels at a member's request. Board members pressed on the accuracy of vaccination figures and on asking students rather than parents; the nurse said the office would begin following up with parents. None of this detail was required by statute; it let the public see how the protocol was working while keeping individual students out of view.

Sources: Practice beyond statutory minimums; no statute required this report.
