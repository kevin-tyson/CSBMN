Claremont School Board, November 5, 2025

Regular board meeting, Chair Heather Whitney presiding, six members present. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on November 5, 2025, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The interim business administrator reported that a $150,000 capital reserve withdrawal in May 2025 was made on a board vote alone, where a district vote was required

Under "Past Trust Fund Withdrawals" Angell said that "the building improvement and maintenance capital reserve fund", one funding source he found while reviewing the Masonic Temple purchase, "required voter approval to withdraw the funds" (1:34:11), that the prior business administrator told the trustees "the board was authorized her to withdraw the money" (1:34:39), and "It was just done incorrectly." (1:38:38). Crawford agreed: "we were told incorrectly that we could just vote. And we in fact, needed the district vote to do that." (1:37:11). The document linked from the agenda, Trust Fund Withdrawal 5-20-25_0001.pdf, is Mary Henry's May 20, 2025 letter to the Trustees of Trust Funds requesting, "on behalf of the Claremont School Board", $888,998.42 from five funds, including "$150,000.00 from the Building Repair & Renovation Capital Reserve Fund for roof replacement Stevens High School", with the May 7, 2025 board minutes attached. Those minutes record each withdrawal approved by a board voice vote after Henry told the board that "the Board has the right to remove money from trust funds". RSA 35:15, I directs trustees to hold capital reserve funds "until such time as the town, district or county shall have voted to withdraw funds", and ¶II(a) requires, for a school district, "a majority vote of the legal voters present and voting at an annual or special meeting". What the record does not settle: whether any district meeting article authorized the board to withdraw from this fund; whether the other capital reserve withdrawals in the same letter ($283,860.00, $28,788.01 and $1,350.41) stand on the same footing; and what the money paid for, since the minutes say "FY24 roof expenditures", the letter says roof replacement, Henry presented the package as covering special-education overruns, and Whitney said this fund "was liquidated to offset unanticipated special education expenses" (1:37:57). Angell said he is still reviewing it.

Sources: RSA 35:15, I and II(a) (capital reserve funds withdrawn only on a vote of the district meeting); Trust Fund Withdrawal 5-20-25_0001.pdf (letter of May 20, 2025 with the May 7, 2025 minutes)

MEDIUM A $16,000 grant was accepted although the agenda gave no notice of it

The board accepted $16,000 from Dartmouth Health and the New Hampshire Charitable Foundation by voice vote (0:58:37). Asked about a hearing, the chair said "No, it's under 20." (0:57:55), and the draft minutes record "No hearing is needed as it is under $20,000". That is correct as far as it goes: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) requires a prior public hearing only for $20,000 or more. But ¶III(b) adds that for amounts under $20,000 "the school board shall post notice of the funds in the agenda and shall include notice in the minutes of the school board meeting in which such funds are discussed." The posted agenda lists the superintendent's report topics and does not mention this grant. The minutes do record it. The authority to accept such funds also depends under ¶I on a district meeting article "authorizing indefinitely" the board to accept unanticipated money; whether Claremont has adopted one was not verified for this page.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a)–(b) (unanticipated funds; agenda notice under $20,000; as amended by 2023, 38:1); CSB Agenda 11.5.25.pdf

MEDIUM The nonpublic session: a tuition-contract exemption used for a general tuition rate, a disputed mover, and minutes posted after 72 hours

The agenda and the motion cite RSA 91-A:3, II(k), "Consideration by a school board of entering into a student or pupil tuition contract", which applies where discussion in public "would likely benefit a party or parties whose interests are adverse".
