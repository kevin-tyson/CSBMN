Claremont School Board, October 5, 2022

Public hearing on a three-article warrant to place $429,867.51 of Senate Bill 420 state aid in capital, transportation and energy reserves, followed by the regular meeting. After an hour of public testimony questioning whether the money could lawfully go to reserves, and whether the amount was right, the board tabled the warrant pending written advice from district counsel. The regular meeting adopted policies JICD and JICK, heard an ESSER II status report ($503,663.77 unbudgeted, deadline September 30, 2023) and presentations on achievement data, elementary restructuring and a new high school schedule, and accepted a $500 donation. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on October 5, 2022, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The board and public were told the SB 420 money was "not a grant" and carried no conditions; the statute calls it a grant and ties it to an achievement plan

Seaman told the hearing "it's not a grant. It's additional adequacy aid." (0:27:21) and "this is what's considered unrestricted dollars, there isn't a restriction tied to it as far as how it's used" (0:18:37), relying on what he said the Department of Education and Department of Revenue had stated at a briefing (0:56:11). The statute in force on this date, RSA 198:40-f (2022, 318:1, eff. July 1, 2022), is headed "Extraordinary Need Grants" and provides: "In order to receive an extraordinary need grant, the eligible school district shall provide a plan to the department of education outlining how the district intends to use grant award funds to improve the educational achievement and growth of students." It requires an accountability assessment and "an annual grant accountability progress report", and states "The primary goal of this grant is to improve student achievement and growth" (statute text). RSA 198:41, I(e) does add the grant to the district's total education grant, which supports describing it as adequacy aid, and the statute contains no express bar on placing the money in reserves. But the text does not support telling the public it was "not a grant" or that the plan was a mere repetition of existing reporting. Two citizens read the statute aloud (0:25:24, 0:36:21), and a member asked for the consequence of being wrong (1:02:49). Mitigation: the board tabled the warrant for written advice from counsel (flag 5).

Sources: RSA 198:40-f, I-II, 2022 codification (2022, 318:1): extraordinary need grants; plan, accountability assessment, annual progress report; RSA 198:41, I(e), 2022 codification (2022, 318:2): grant added to the total education grant

OBSERVATION The draft warrant carried two errors, and the amount itself was disputed by about $60,000

The warrant file posted the morning of the hearing, Claremont Special District Warrant - 10.20.22 - FINAL.pdf, calls the meeting for "Wednesday, October 20, 2022" (warrant text), a Thursday, and writes Article 3's sum as "ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND TWENTY NINE THOUSAND EIGHT HUNDRED SIXTY SEVEN DOLLARS AND FIFTY ONE CENTS" (warrant text) beside the figure $129,867.51. Both were caught aloud (0:10:57, 0:13:22). This recording does not resolve which figure is right. RSA 197:3-a, III requires the warrant to "specify, in one or more articles, the amounts of appropriations proposed" (statute text), so an unconfirmed total is a reason the tabling was prudent rather than a violation.
