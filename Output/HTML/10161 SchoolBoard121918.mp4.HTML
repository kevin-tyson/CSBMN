Claremont School Board, December 19, 2018

Regular meeting: the board sent a proposed FY2019-20 operating budget of $31,828,063 to the January 2 public hearing, redirected lunch-debt donations to school food shelves, asked the administration to stop a la carte charging at a zero balance and to warn seniors that unpaid balances could cost them their diplomas, authorized the chair to sign the administrators' contract, and moved a group of policies toward final reading. OBSERVATION The board asked the administration to warn seniors that unpaid lunch balances could cost them their diplomas

The chair proposed withholding diplomas from seniors with balances (0:45:59, 0:46:29), and the motion carried as a recommendation to "put seniors on, warning that outstanding balances could result in them not receiving their diploma" (0:49:09, vote at 0:52:15). The governing state statute, RSA 189:11-a, VIII (in force from July 2, 2016), requires a meal payment policy to ensure "that all students have access to a healthy school lunch" and "that no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures". It speaks to the cafeteria, not to graduation, and this page verified no New Hampshire or federal rule on the date of the meeting that addresses withholding a diploma for a meal debt. Members said the standard meal itself is still served regardless of balance (0:46:48, 0:43:05), which is what VIII protects. The point is recorded because a diploma sanction ties a parent's debt to the student's credential and was adopted without written policy text; whether it was ever applied is not in this record.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2018 codification; source note ends 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016)

OBSERVATION Gifts sent to pay down lunch debt were accepted for the food shelves, without the checks, amounts or donors on the record

The assistant superintendent said the checks came in "because of the food service debt" and that "the donations were for the food service debt" (0:07:35, 0:09:30), yet the board voted to apply them to school food shelves, as it had done with a prior "$2,300 and change" (0:07:09, vote at 0:22:55). OBSERVATION The vice chair told the public the school board "cannot fire staff"; state law gives the board that power over teachers

Speaking to "some communication out in the public", the vice chair said "the school board cannot fire staff" and "we cannot fire you" (1:53:30, 1:53:56); another member said the only employee the board controls is the superintendent (1:54:23). RSA 189:13, as it read in 2018, provides that "The school board may dismiss any teacher found by them to be immoral, or who has not satisfactorily maintained the competency standards established by the school district", after notice of the cause and "a full and fair hearing", and RSA 189:39 makes the board the body that elects teachers on the superintendent's nomination. The statement was meant to reassure staff and does describe day-to-day practice, where evaluation and discipline run through the administration, as the interim superintendent added (1:54:37). As a statement of the board's legal authority it is incomplete.

Sources: RSA 189:13 (2018 codification; source note ends 2005, 178:1); RSA 189:39 (source note ends 1971, 371:2; unchanged throughout)
