Regular meeting a week after the joint session with the City Council: the board reviewed its September 30 directive to cut about $1.2 million from the FY19 budget, approved a German exchange trip, agreed to study class rank and to revise the lead-screening policy, and heard the Stevens principal oppose a demonstration planned outside the schools. OBSERVATION The April lead-screening policy required documentation that the superintendent now says state law does not support

The superintendent told the board that the immunization statute he cited, RSA 141-C:20-a, covers communicable diseases, that lead poisoning is not transmissible, and that the policy's testing requirement cannot be enforced: “it's not enforceable” (1:48:44); he proposed an opt-out. The statute as in force on October 4, 2017 bears that reading: it requires immunization against listed diseases and, in a paragraph added by 2017, 137:1 effective August 15, 2017, provides that nothing in the section requires immunization for noncommunicable diseases. It does not mention lead screening. The chair's understanding that Claremont used universal testing (1:49:05) and his remark that state law requires doctors to test children (1:56:18) were not resolved on the record, and the April policy text is not available here.

The board responded to a resident's objection by reviewing the policy and asking for a revision; on October 18 it adopted a rewrite with an opt-out paragraph (see the October 18, 2017 page). The observation is that a requirement adopted in April was, on the superintendent's own reading, one the district could not enforce.

Sources: RSA 141-C:20-a (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 137:1, eff. Aug. 15, 2017)
