The SAU 6 board's first meeting with a quorum since November 2016: it approved eleven-month-old minutes, a Primex insurance resolution and a professional growth plan already in use, and carried forward a $1,955,423 FY19 SAU budget to a November 2 public hearing. OBSERVATION The SAU 6 board went eleven months without a quorum, and the record shows what that cost

The minutes approved tonight were from November 14, 2016, and the chair said “We haven't had a quorum for three meetings.” (0:02:42). The recording ties specific consequences to the gap. The SAU did not join the Primex contribution-cap program for 2017-18 because there was no meeting to sign it (0:05:18), and its property and liability contribution rose about 10% against the 7% cap (0:06:28); on a contribution of about $7,000 the difference is small, but it was avoidable. The Professional Growth Master Plan, which the assistant superintendent described as required by the Department of Education and which the Department approved on December 15, 2016, governed certified staff for a school year and a half before the board approved it tonight (0:10:54). And the FY19 SAU budget, which the superintendent said should have been discussed in June, reached the board in October (0:21:35).

The members gave the reason: while Cornish was still formally a member but had stopped attending, the board needed nine members present and repeatedly had eight (1:37:28). With Cornish gone, six are needed. RSA 194-C:9 requires the SAU board to adopt its budget at a meeting held before January 1 and to certify each district's share before January 15; tonight's schedule, with a November 2 hearing, leaves room to meet that. The members also noted that the SAU paid CCTV for meetings that failed for want of a quorum (1:36:02) and discussed releasing the crew when no quorum is confirmed (1:38:35); for the public, a meeting held without CCTV present would also leave no recording. No rule verified for this page sets a meeting frequency for an SAU board, so this is an observation about governance continuity.

Sources: RSA 194-C:9 (2017 codification; budget adopted before January 1, apportionment, certification before January 15); Dialogue/9386 SAU6101217.mp4.CSV (rows 14 to 136 and 777 to 829)

OBSERVATION The decision to apply $55,000 of fund balance was made by consensus, and the recording garbles the figures behind it

The motion the board voted carried forward the expenditure total of $1,955,423 for the November 2 hearing (1:34:24); O'Neill said the fund-balance choice affects “my revenue sources but not the budget”. That choice, $55,000 of accumulated fund balance to bring Claremont's assessment roughly flat and reduce Unity's, was settled by the chair's question “Is there a logical reason why we would not do it at at 55?” and general agreement (1:12:54), not by a vote. The SAU's fund balance, by O'Neill's account, came from indirect-cost revenue above budget and from spending less than budgeted; his figures are fused in the transcript, and this page reconstructs the June 30, 2017 balance as about $200,267 from his own revenue and expenditure totals.

Applying fund balance changes what each district is assessed, which is the figure the districts must raise. With no minutes available, a reader cannot confirm the amount the board ultimately applied or how it was recorded. No rule verified for this page requires a separate vote on revenue sources for an SAU budget, so this is an observation about the clarity of the record.

Sources: RSA 194-C:9 (2017 codification: apportionment and certification of district shares); Dialogue/9386 SAU6101217.mp4.CSV (rows 397 to 568 and 765)

POSITIVE A detailed public budget presentation, and a hearing set early enough to act on what the public says
