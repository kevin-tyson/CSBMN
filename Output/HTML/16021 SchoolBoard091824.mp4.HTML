Claremont School Board — September 18, 2024

The last regular meeting of the Claremont School Board before 30 September 2024, the date by which every dollar of federal ARP ESSER money had to be obligated or lost. The board’s entire treatment of that deadline occupies about ninety seconds of a sixty-two-minute recording, and ends with the subject deferred to October. The same night the board received the calendar for the FY26 budget: a calendar that is lawful on every statutory anchor and that nobody voted on.

The federal deadline
Twelve days. The U.S. Department of Education’s grantee memorandum of 12 June 2024 states that “all ARP ESSER and ARP EANS funds, including reverted ARP EANS funds, must be obligated by September 30, 2024”, and that “all funds must be liquidated within 120 calendar days after the close of the obligation period, or by January 28, 2025”, citing 2 CFR 200.344(b). Neither date is spoken at this meeting and neither appears in any document in the packet (flag 1).

POSITIVE The FY26 budget calendar set on this night is lawful on every statutory anchor, with the arithmetic done in days: including a snow date that falls on the last day the law allows

The dates. From Exhibit A and from the agenda’s own list of future dates: public hearing 2 January 2025; deliberative session Saturday 1 February 2025, snow date Saturday 8 February 2025; voting day 11 March 2025.

  • The 25-day floor. RSA 32:5, I requires at least one public hearing on the budget “not later than 25 days before each annual or special meeting”, on seven days’ notice. 2 January to 1 February is thirty days. The floor is cleared with five days to spare, and the interval becomes thirty-seven days if the snow date is used. This is a minimum interval, not a calendar deadline, and it must not be confused with the SAU-budget deadline in RSA 194-C:9, I, which is a different statute governing a different body.
  • The January ceiling. RSA 40:13, II-a: “Budget hearings under RSA 32:5 and RSA 195:12 and hearings under RSA 33:8-a shall be held on or before the third Tuesday in January.” The third Tuesday in January 2025 is 21 January; the hearing is on the 2nd. The same paragraph makes “the second Tuesday in January” (14 January 2025) the final date for posting notice of that hearing, so Claremont held its hearing twelve days before the last day on which it could lawfully have posted the notice for it.
  • The first-session window. RSA 40:13, III: the first session “shall be held between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January, inclusive of those Saturdays”. The last Monday in January 2025 is 27 January; the first Saturday following is 1 February and the second is 8 February. Both the deliberative session and its snow date fall inside the window, and the snow date is the last day of it: which means the calendar is lawful but has no second fallback.
  • The ballot. RSA 40:13, VII: the second session “shall be held on the second Tuesday in March”. The second Tuesday in March 2025 is 11 March, which is the date on the calendar.
  • What the hearing is for. RSA 32:5, I ends “and after the conclusion of public testimony shall finalize the budget to be submitted to the legislative body.” The calendar’s 2 January row assigns the board “Vote on budget, answer public questions”, which is that clause working as written. A reduction voted after the hearing closes is what the statute contemplates, not a breach of it.

The one January date the calendar does not carry. RSA 40:13, II-a also provides that “Warrants under RSA 39:5 and RSA 197:7 and budgets shall be posted and copies available to the general public on or before the last Monday in January”: 27 January 2025. Exhibit A runs straight from the 2 January hearing to the 1 February deliberative session with nothing between them, and the agenda’s list of future dates does the same. The duty exists whether or not the calendar names it.

Why this is a positive finding and not a neutral one. Four separate statutory anchors, three of them with day-of-week arithmetic that has to be redone every year, and the calendar hits all four. This project has recorded a district budget calendar being got wrong; this one is right, and it was right on a document put in front of the board seven days before the meeting.
