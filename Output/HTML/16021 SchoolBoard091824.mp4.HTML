Claremont School Board, September 18, 2024

Regular board meeting with the chair absent and Vice Chair Frank Sprague presiding. MEDIUM The Finance subcommittee met with the administration the day of this meeting; no notice or minutes of that session were found

Sprague: "We met today. We'll talk about that under the committee report." (0:22:24). Crawford then reported what the subcommittee did: it reviewed the FY24 close, unfilled positions, absenteeism and its schedule, and asked for reports (0:41:04 to 0:45:13). A subcommittee of a school board is a public body under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), which ends "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto", so the meeting needed 24 hours' posted notice and minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II. This board's agenda lists Finance subcommittee dates only from November 12, 2024. No notice or minutes of a September 18 session were found in this meeting's packet folder, in the October 2 packet folder, or by a Drive search for the date; a negative from search alone is not proof that none exists. Graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH under the project's severity rule because the record mitigates it: the subcommittee's business was reported in public session the same evening and is summarized in the approved minutes.

MEDIUM Three years of district audits outstanding in September 2024; if federal spending reached the threshold, the single audits were years overdue

Henry reported the FY21 audit "almost done" (0:27:31), FY22 and FY23 targeted before June 30, 2025, and would not promise FY24 (0:36:27); she had reconciled cash "all the way back to 2020". Under the Uniform Guidance in force for those years, an entity that "expends $750,000 or more" in federal awards in a fiscal year must have a single audit, and the reporting package is due "nine months after the end of the audit period" (30 days after the auditor's report if sooner). For a June 30 fiscal year that put FY21 at March 31, 2022 and FY23 at March 31, 2024. The record does not state the district's federal expenditures by year, but it describes "Three years of Esser funding" (0:44:37) and a Title I "corrective action plan that they have us on" with funds to be released once policies are adopted (0:46:28). This is graded MEDIUM because the threshold is not established on this record; if it was met, each of these audits missed its federal deadline. The candor of the report is credited in flag 9.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2023 edition; $750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2023 edition; nine months)

Henry said the encumbrance "has gone down to 65,000" because the roof purchase order was rolled into the new year (0:23:43, 0:24:07). On the exhibit's own arithmetic, a $65,000 encumbrance leaves about $1,128,077, not $544,145.29; the difference, $583,931.76, matches the roofs only if their cost has been charged elsewhere, which the report did not say. Members then discussed the $544,000 as the year's result (0:37:10), and a retention vote was set for October (0:24:28); RSA 198:4-b, II caps retained unassigned funds at 5% of the net assessment, so the starting figure matters. Separately, Pratt said "we have the end of September to spend the money" (0:33:25); the U.S. Department of Education's notice set September 30, 2024 as the deadline to obligate ESSER funds and January 28, 2025 to liquidate them. No GASB departure is shown by a single board report, so this is an observation; the audited FY24 statements would settle the figure.
