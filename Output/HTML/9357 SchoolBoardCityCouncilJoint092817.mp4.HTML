Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — September 28, 2017

Joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council, presided over by Mayor Charlene Lovett, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no school-side agenda, packet or minutes are posted in the district's online shares, and the city's records were not inspected. The two bodies heard updates on state legislation and the lead-poisoning bill SB 247, the school lead-screening policy and city screening results, the district's Gear Up grant, the One for All family center and a free preschool with transportation, and workforce training; the superintendent described the district's response to the late-August incident, and the mayor framed a shared commitment to "cultural change". The planned poverty discussion was deferred to a December 14 meeting. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION No school-side agenda or minutes is online, and the city's records were not inspected

A joint meeting is two public bodies meeting together, each with its own notice and minutes duties and its own quorum. MAP.md section 41 records that neither district Drive share holds anything older than 2022 (checked 9/26/26); that is a limit of what the district posts online, not evidence that records were never made. The city's notice, agenda and minutes would sit in the city clerk's system, which was not searched for this page, so no city-side negative is stated. The recording itself shows minutes being kept for joint meetings: approval of earlier minutes was tabled (0:01:23). RSA 91-A:2, II as it read in 2017 required minutes naming members and "persons appearing before the public bodies", with a brief description of the subject matter and final decisions, open to inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting". The joint roll call (0:00:53) is too run together to confirm each body's quorum separately. The project's missing-records rule is applied on its online-availability limb.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 29:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017, so in force for all of 2017).

OBSERVATION Members were asked to send their views on the next topic to a staff member between meetings

To frame the poverty discussion, the mayor asked each member to "identify three top contributors to poverty" and to send them to a staff member within the week (2:10:26, 2:11:15, 2:11:31); a councilor asked that everyone receive the compiled list (2:10:52). RSA 91-A:2-a, unchanged since 2008, provides that public bodies "shall deliberate on matters only in meetings" and that communications outside a meeting "shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose" of the chapter. Gathering members' suggestions to set an agenda is ordinary preparation, and the stated purpose was to pick a focus at the next public meeting. It is recorded because the same exercise, if the compiled list circulated among a quorum and narrowed the choice before the meeting, would be the kind of sequential communication the statute addresses; publishing the compiled list with the agenda materials would answer that concern. What happened next is not shown in the corpus.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (source 2008, 303:4, unamended, so the same text in 2017).
