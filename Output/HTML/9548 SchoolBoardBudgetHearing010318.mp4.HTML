Claremont School Board, Budget Public Hearing (part 2 of 2) — January 3, 2018

Second of two recordings of the public hearing on the fiscal 2019 (2018–19) school budget, summarized from the CCTV recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no notice, agenda, budget handout or minutes are online. It carries the rest of the public testimony: residents and staff defended the adult education director, the crisis counselor and teaching positions, the vice chair and a member defended the board's $31,148,257 bottom line, the superintendent said he had asked the board to reconsider its October directive, and the business administrator explained how fund balance had been returned or retained. Part 1 is Cablecast show 9547. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION No hearing notice, agenda, handout or minutes are online, but the record shows minutes were kept and an agenda was posted

RSA 32:5, I, as in force on this date, required notice of the budget hearing "at least 7 days in advance". RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force from January 1, 2018, required minutes naming the members, the "persons appearing before the public bodies", a brief description of the subjects and final decisions, open to inspection within five business days. None of these documents is in the district's public shares, which begin in 2022; that is a limit of what the district posts online, not evidence they did not exist. The record points the other way: a voice at the board table in part 1 says the three-minute limit "says so on the agenda", and on January 31, 2018 the board took up "minutes from the public hearing" with a correction naming a speaker "Patty Harrison of Wethersfield, Vermont" who had been listed as from Ward 3 (Cablecast show 9586). OBSERVATION Fund balance was explained under the wrong statute, and the retained amount is measured differently in the law

Asked how leftover money is handled, the business administrator said the district voted "to allow a carryover of 2.5% of fund balance under RSA 198 20" (0:47:29). The provision in force that lets a school district retain year-end money is RSA 198:4-b, II: the legislative body may authorize retention of "year-end unassigned general funds in an amount not to exceed, in any fiscal year, 2.5 percent of the current fiscal year's net assessment", for "emergency expenditures and overexpenditures under RSA 32:11" or "to reduce the tax rate". The cap is a percentage of the net assessment, not of fund balance, and the statute sets no approval by state agencies; the administrator's description of using retained money only "to offset taxes in future years" (0:48:35) matches the second permitted use. His figures (about $600,000 retained after a $1.8 million surplus; $584,480 returned for 2015 and about $326,000 for 2016) and the vice chair's "roughly $2.2 million" over four years (0:42:36) are the speakers' own and could not be checked against audited statements. Whether RSA 198:20 (a different section) was what he meant, or a slip, the recording cannot say.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:6, eff. Sept. 9, 2014).

OBSERVATION Members describe budget work done together away from the chair and the administration

The superintendent said "The board has for the past couple of years, had the desire to meet alone, without administration and do the work" (0:38:59), and placed the $1.2 million directive at a meeting on September 30, 2017 "in the community center in the afternoon" (0:39:26); in part 1 he calls that session "the public meeting". The vice chair then said of the chair, "he has no idea what this board has been doing when we've met together because he has not been there" (0:40:49), and spoke for "The four of us" (0:21:38). Four is a quorum of this seven-member board. Under RSA 91-A:2, I, as then in force, the convening of a quorum "for the purpose of discussing or acting upon" a matter within the board's jurisdiction is a meeting, which needs notice and minutes; and RSA 91-A:2-a requires deliberation only in meetings, and bars communications outside a meeting used "to circumvent the spirit and purpose" of the chapter. The recording does not say whether these sessions were noticed, how many members attended, or whether they were the board's own budget work sessions held in public. Other recordings answer much of this. The November 29, 2017 budget study session, held without administrators, is itself a televised public meeting with all seven members (November 29 page), and on December 20 the assistant superintendent said she had watched "the tape" of the members' Saturday follow-up session (December 20 page); the superintendent calls the September 30 session "the public meeting" in part 1. So the sessions the vice chair describes appear to have been noticed, recorded board meetings that the chair did not attend. The notices and minutes of fall 2017, which are not online, would settle it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2017 codification); RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (source 2008, 303:4, unamended).
