OBSERVATION The retained-fund account given at the hearing does not match the retention statute's terms

The business administrator said voters had authorized "a carryover of 2.5% of fund balance under RSA 198 20", about $600,000 (0:47:29), and that it could be spent "only with the approval of the drey [DRA] and the Doe" or used to offset taxes (0:48:35). The citation is as transcribed; the retention authority that matches his description is RSA 198:4-b, II, which in the 2017 text let the legislative body authorize the district "to retain year-end unassigned general funds in an amount not to exceed, in any fiscal year, 2.5 percent of the current fiscal year's net assessment", for "emergency expenditures and overexpenditures under RSA 32:11" or as a revenue source to reduce the tax rate. Two points differ from what was said: the cap is measured against the net assessment, not the fund balance (Putnam understood it as a percentage of the budget, 0:48:21), and the statute names the two uses rather than an agency approval. His statement that tax relief is "the only purpose that we've been using it for" fits the statute. The vice chair's figure of about $2.2 million returned over four years (0:42:36) is answered on the same recording: most of it was a one-time health-insurance refund (0:52:20).

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:6; 2.5 percent of net assessment until 2020, 38:25)

OBSERVATION A $70,000 paving job was paid from savings in the prior year's budget rather than a line item or article

The business administrator said the parking-lot paving "was basically part of money that we were able to save through last year's budget" (0:12:03). A resident read that as over-budgeting (0:12:11); the former board chair said his board would have put a known repair on the budget "as a line item" so the public knew (0:26:21). RSA 32:10, I, in the text in force before August 15, 2017, allowed the governing body to transfer an unexpended balance to an appropriation that needed more money, provided total spending stayed within the total appropriated, and required records detailed enough that a citizen asking under RSA 91-A:4 "may ascertain the purposes of appropriations to which, and from which, amounts have been transferred". A transfer of this kind is lawful. The transfer records were not produced at the hearing and are not public now, so the page cannot say which appropriation the paving was charged to.

Sources: RSA 32:10, I(a)-(b) (2016 codification; source note ends 2004, 113:1, in force until Aug. 15, 2017)

OBSERVATION Members describe budget work "without administration"; the record does not show how those sessions were noticed

The superintendent said the board had "the desire to meet alone, without administration and do the work" (0:38:59), and that the $1.2 million directive came "on September 30th, 2017, when they met in the community center in the afternoon" (0:39:26); in part 1 he called that session "the public meeting". The vice chair said the chair "has no idea what this board has been doing when we've met together because he has not been there" (0:40:49). Under RSA 91-A:2, I, as it read in 2018, a meeting is "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body"; four of the seven members are a quorum, and RSA 91-A:2-a, I required public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings" held under the statute. The September 30 session appears to have been a noticed public meeting. Whether the other occasions the vice chair describes were noticed meetings, gatherings of fewer than four members, or the board's own public sessions is not in this record, and the page draws no conclusion.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (2017 codification, in force for 2018); RSA 91-A:2-a, I (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4, unamended)

POSITIVE Every resident who wanted to speak was heard, and a second round was offered

Despite the disorder in part 1, the chair kept taking speakers until no one else came forward, promised that "after everyone's had a chance to speak once people will be allowed to speak again" (0:07:43), and opened a second round (0:44:36).
