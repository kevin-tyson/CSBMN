Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting, March 30, 2017

Joint meeting of the school board and the city council: the new city manager introduced himself, the two bodies and outside partners reviewed lead-poisoning screening and abatement, and attorney Tom Connair led a long discussion of the state's duty to fund education, the stabilization-grant phase-out and the 2017 voucher bill. No votes were taken. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. OBSERVATION The stabilization-grant phase-out the members discussed was written into the statute

A member asked how the state could phase out Claremont's stabilization grant when "there wasn't any any legislative decision or any change in the law to my understanding" (1:52:05), and asked whether that was grounds for a lawsuit; counsel said he would need to look (1:53:03). The superintendent connected the change to a Supreme Court ruling of September 2016 (2:07:37). The statute itself sets the phase-out: RSA 198:41, IV(d), as codified in 2016, provides that for fiscal year 2017 "the percentage shall be 96 percent", and that it "shall be reduced by 4 percent of the amount of the 2012 education grant for each" fiscal year after. That matches the mayor's "we're still losing 4%" (1:35:06). Whether the phase-out is constitutional is a separate question this page does not reach, and the court decision the superintendent described was not verified for this page. The point is that viewers heard the reduction described as having no legislative basis, and it had one.

Sources: RSA 198:41, IV(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 8:4, 5 and 85:9, eff. July 18, 2016)

OBSERVATION The chair's 60 percent special-education figure is unexplained

The chair said the state "doesn't want to fund a, special education the way that they should. I think we're getting 60% of what we're supposed to from the state." (1:26:14). He did not say which aid he meant. The state's main special-education aid to districts is catastrophic aid under RSA 186-C:18, III, which in the text in force in 2017 made the state liable for 80 percent of a child's costs above 3 1/2 times the state average expenditure per pupil, and provided that an insufficient appropriation is prorated proportionally, with no minimum share. A district receiving 60 percent of its calculated entitlement would therefore be consistent with the statute, not a breach of it, if the appropriation fell short. The figure itself is the chair's estimate and is not documented in the record.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2016 codification: 80 percent above 3 1/2 times average expenditure; proration "proportionally based on entitlement")
