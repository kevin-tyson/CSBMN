Claremont School Safety Public Discussion, March 17, 2025

A public forum convened by Superintendent Chris Pratt five weeks after the February 11, 2025 lockdown at Stevens High School, in which four students were detained and a BB gun was found. The Stevens principal gave his account, a panel of principals answered prepared questions on safety and bullying, and fourteen members of the public spoke. It was not a school board meeting: no vote was taken and no agenda, notice or minutes have been found. Built from the dialogue transcript; no district document exists for this forum. OBSERVATION Gaps in the February 11 response, described by the district itself

The panel volunteered several weaknesses. Four students were in the halls and bathrooms for "about 30 or 40 minutes before the lockdown started" (0:08:56). At least two occupied offices "did not hear the lockdown" (0:12:30). Responders were confused by floor numbering (0:21:51). Building-to-building radio communication had been unreliable until this school year (0:22:55). No one watches the camera feeds live (0:51:00), and the superintendent said a monitor "would have made a difference in hindsight" (0:51:28). Administrators reported fixes for several of these: renumbering, the offices, radios, strobes, added cameras and a no-wandering rule. One principal said the state requires "at least two lockdowns a year and two fire drills each year" (0:17:54); this page did not verify a drill statute or rule this run, so that figure stays the speaker's characterization. No rule is cited for the gaps themselves; the item is recorded so the board can follow up on which fixes are complete.

Sources: the recording at the times linked above

OBSERVATION State bullying reports: a public claim of zero incidents at Stevens, and the superintendent's answer

RSA 193-F:6 requires each school district to "annually report substantiated incidents of bullying or cyberbullying to the department of education" (statute text; unamended since 2010, 155:3). A community member said the department's figures show zero bullying incidents at Stevens for 2021 through 2024 (1:19:32). The superintendent answered that the state "probably haven't gotten around to updating it" and that "we definitely have bullying" (1:20:05), and earlier said that investigators can be "95% sure it's happening" without enough evidence to act (0:29:15). The statute counts substantiated incidents only, so a report of zero could reflect the substantiation threshold rather than an absence of bullying, or it could reflect an unfiled report. This page did not check the department's data. The district's annual 193-F:6 reports for 2021–22 through 2023–24 would settle which.

Sources: RSA 193-F:6 (annual report of substantiated bullying incidents)
