Claremont School Safety Public Discussion — March 17, 2025

A public forum on school safety convened and run by Superintendent Christopher Pratt five weeks after the February 11, 2025 lockdown at Stevens High School. It was not a meeting of the Claremont School Board. No agenda, packet or minutes exists in either district share, so this page is built from the recording and its dialogue transcript alone. OBSERVATION No agenda, notice or minutes exists for the forum, and on this record the Right-to-Know Law’s minutes duty did not attach

MAP.md records no packet folder and nothing in the Meeting Minutes share for March 17, 2025, so the Cablecast recording is the only public record of the evening. The threshold question is whether a public body met. The Right-to-Know Law defines a meeting as “the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body”. The recording shows the superintendent convening and running the forum (“The reason I wanted to conduct this forum was a way to get the community engaged in my short time here,” 0:00:30), a panel of administrators and police, and no chair, roll call, motion or vote in 1:31:46. The only board member identifiable is William Madden, speaking from the podium as a parent, one member of a seven-member board. On this record no quorum is shown, so this page does not say minutes were owed. The limit: a recording cannot show who sat in the audience. If a quorum of the board was present, the analysis would change.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I (meeting means the convening of a quorum) and II (minutes; text read 2026-09-25); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (public bodies); MAP.md section 79.

OBSERVATION The lockdown did not reach two occupied offices, and parents asked for real events to be announced as real; the governing procedures are state rules and local policy, not on this record

Herrington reported that “least two offices with people in them that did not hear the lockdown” (0:12:30); the superintendent said at 0:22:15 that this has been addressed. Ashley Goff asked that a real lockdown be announced as one, because students drilled since kindergarten treat it as routine (0:55:57–0:56:55). Blount described the state requirement as “at least two lockdowns a year and two fire drills each year” (0:17:54); that is his characterization, and this page has not verified the drill rule. The safe-school-zones statute directs the State Board of Education to adopt rules that include procedures for reporting theft, destruction or violence, and leaves pupil conduct and discipline policy to local boards. It sets no parent-notification or announcement duty of its own. Whether the district’s written emergency procedures match what was described cannot be answered from this recording.

Sources: RSA 193-D:2 (state board rules on safe school zones; local discipline policy; 2020, 38:13; text read 2026-09-25); the recording at the timestamps above.

OBSERVATION A commenter said the state’s published data shows zero bullying incidents at Stevens for 2021–24; the superintendent said the district does have bullying

The commenter’s figure (1:19:32) was not disputed on substance: “So we can say we definitely have bullying” (1:20:05). The superintendent attributed the published figure to the state not updating its data and offered the district’s own pre-submission numbers (1:20:44). Districts file statistical reports with the Department of Education, including safety information, under a certification that the information is “true, accurate, and complete”, and the commissioner may withhold aid for non-compliance. This project has not checked the Department’s published data for those years, and the record does not show whether any gap lies in the district’s submissions or the state’s publication. The district’s own figures, which the superintendent offered, would settle it.

Sources: RSA 189:28 (statistical reports, including safety information; certification; 2021, 44:2, 3; text read 2026-09-25); the recording at the timestamps above.
