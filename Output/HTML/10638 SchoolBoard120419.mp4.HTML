MEDIUM By the district's own account, the November 6 hearing and November 21 meeting notices did not meet RSA 197:3-a's website rule

The chair read district counsel's statement into the record (0:00:12 to 0:02:01). The November 21 special district meeting was held under RSA 197:3-a, the single-session procedure for responding to a change in state education aid. Paragraph IV requires notice of the hearing's “time, place, and subject” in at least two public places “one of which shall be on the school district's website, if such exists, at least 7 days prior to the hearing”, and paragraph V requires the same for the meeting notice and warrant. By counsel's account the November 6 hearing notice was not posted on the SAU 6 website at all, and the meeting notice was posted there six days ahead, not seven. The Department of Revenue Administration recommended a second meeting under RSA 40:16 to cure the defect.

This page grades the point MEDIUM, not HIGH, for two reasons on the record. The statute's website requirement applies to “the school district's website, if such exists”, and counsel's position is that the SAU 6 page “technically is not the district's website”, so whether a binding deadline was missed is contested. The next evening the Claremont chair told the SAU 6 board that the Department of Revenue Administration took the opposite view, that the district had adopted the SAU 6 page as its own (see the SAU 6 Board, December 5, 2019 page). And the district chose to cure the defect rather than defend it (flag 6). What the record does establish is a process gap: notices for a $650,000 vote went out without the district being sure which website rule applied, and two residents at tonight's hearing say they learned of the vote late or misread who could vote (0:03:43, 0:11:50).

Sources: RSA 197:3-a, IV and V (2019 codification; hearing and meeting notices, website posting 7 days prior); RSA 40:16 (special meeting to cure defects in official-ballot subdivisions)

OBSERVATION Tonight's hearing notice did not state its subject, and the board's answer to that complaint was left conditional on counsel

Patrick Adrian said the posting for tonight's hearing “doesn't say what it's about” (0:04:35), and Cassie Burke said she found out about the second vote that day from a community Facebook group (0:11:50). The cure procedure in RSA 31:5-b, II requires a public hearing not less than seven calendar days before the cure meeting “at which the reasons for the special town meeting shall be explained” (¶II(c)), and requires the municipality to “comply with all statutory notice and procedural requirements for holding special town meetings” (¶II(d)). The ordinary meeting-notice rule, RSA 91-A:2, II, requires notice of time and place only. No provision verified for this page expressly requires a 31:5-b hearing notice to state its subject, so this is an observation, not a finding.

Two further points sit in the same area. A staff member told the board the 7-day and 14-day rules Adrian read come from RSA 197:3-a, “not from the RSA 40” (1:18:17). That is right as to the 14-day hearing interval, which 31:5-b replaces with seven days, but ¶II(d) carries the special-meeting notice requirements into the cure, so the website question does not simply fall away. The clerk said the December 12 meeting would use the November 21 checklist (0:17:56); RSA 197:3-a, VI requires “The most recently updated checklist”, and whether the supervisors of the checklist updated it in between is not in this record. The board's response was to approve moving the hearing results forward “contingent upon learning from our attorney” that Adrian's concerns were unfounded (1:20:28); the attorney's answer is not on this recording.

Sources: RSA 31:5-b, II(b)-(e) (2019 codification; cure meeting not less than 21 days after the original vote, hearing not less than 7 days before it, notice and procedure); RSA 197:3-a, IV-VI (2019 codification); RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; notice of time and place)

OBSERVATION Unpaid school-meal debt is growing, and the board has no plan yet for covering it

Whitney put the debt at “a 25, 35” [thousand dollars] now and warned of $40,000 to $50,000 if payment comes to be seen as optional (1:29:26); the chair called it a receivable that will have to be written off and built into next year's budget (1:31:47). Benware traced the problem to “this law” about feeding every child (1:28:19). The state law in force is RSA 189:11-a, VIII: a meal payment policy “shall ensure that all students have access to a healthy school lunch” and that “no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures”. So the district may not withhold or substitute meals to collect the debt. Federal guidance, USDA memo SP 23-2017, adds that bad debt may not be paid from the nonprofit school food service account and “must be restored using non-Federal funds”, which is what the chair's write-off plan implies.

The figures are members' estimates stated aloud; no ledger or vendor report is in the record. Benware's point that an outside vendor's price makes each unpaid meal cost more than an in-house program would (1:33:07) is arithmetic offered in discussion, not a finding about the contract.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 301:1, 2 and 346:321, 322, eff. July 1, 2019); USDA FNS SP 23-2017 (Mar. 23, 2017; bad debt restored with non-federal funds)

OBSERVATION The SRVRTC director reports state letters about the preschool “encroachment” on CTE space

Dr. Herzog told the board that the state has sent “a couple of letters about encroachment” as the preschool takes over areas left by closed CTE programs (0:50:46), and cited the building statute (0:50:21). RSA 188-E:3, II provides that a constructed or renovated CTE facility becomes district property “for use by the career and technical education center exclusively”. The board did not discuss the letters or the preschool's use of the space. The letters are not in the record, so their terms, and whether any condition attaches to the state's 75% renovation share that Herzog described (0:56:28), cannot be checked here.

Sources: RSA 188-E:3, II (2019 codification; CTE facility for exclusive CTE use)

POSITIVE The board cured a contested defect rather than defend it, on a timetable that meets RSA 31:5-b

Counsel's statement says the board “still questions whether there is a legal requirement” to post on the website but chose the cure process to “ensure transparency and preserve the integrity of the vote” (0:02:01). The timetable fits RSA 31:5-b, II: the cure meeting on December 12 is 21 calendar days after the November 21 vote, the minimum ¶II(b) allows, and tonight's hearing is eight days before it, against ¶II(c)'s seven. As ¶II(c) requires, the reasons were explained at the hearing, by reading counsel's statement aloud before taking comment, and the chair took the hearing before the regular agenda so that comment was heard first. Re-running a $650,000 vote when the board disputes that the defect exists is a choice above the legal minimum.

Sources: RSA 31:5-b, II(b), (c) (2019 codification; source note 1988, 284:1; 1989, 287:1); RSA 40:16 (2019 codification; source note 1999, 86:4)

POSITIVE The board designated two members to answer public questions online, so that a quorum would not deliberate on social media

On a seven-member board four members are a quorum. The chair warned “We have to be careful that four of us don't respond” to residents' questions on the community Facebook group (1:19:45), and the board named Benware and Towle as the only responders, “And the rest of us stay away from it.” (1:20:12). RSA 91-A:2-a requires public bodies to deliberate only in meetings, and its ¶II provides that communications outside a meeting, “including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members”, “shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose” of the chapter. Setting a rule in public before the question arose goes beyond what the statute requires.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I-II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4, unamended through 2022)
