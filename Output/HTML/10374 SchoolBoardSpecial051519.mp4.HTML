Claremont School Board, Special Meeting, May 15, 2019

HIGH The 2019 budget, default budget form and warrant were not posted as RSA 32:5 required, and the State found the annual meeting procedurally defective

The board chair told the district meeting that on April 9, 2019 the Department of Revenue Administration notified the district that “the default budget form miss 26 [MS-26], budget, form and warrant were not posted in accordance with the with RSA 32 colon five”, so that the February 6 deliberative session and the March 12 vote “were procedurally defective and invalid.” (0:02:45). The defect was raised with the State “by a concerned citizen.” (0:03:16), not found by the district. RSA 32:5, VII, in the text in force for the 2019 annual meeting, required the governing body to “post certified copies of the budget with the warrant for the meeting”, and an official-ballot district to post certified copies of the default budget form with the proposed operating budget and the warrant. The DRA notice itself is not in the record; this page relies on the chair's reading of it.

This is graded HIGH because the district's own account is that a binding posting requirement for its annual meeting was missed. The mitigation is real and is recorded here: the law provides a cure for this kind of irregularity, and the district used it at this meeting (see flag 3 for what the record does and does not show about that cure).

Sources: RSA 32:5, VII(a)-(b) (2018 codification; source note ends 2018, 246:1, eff. Aug. 11, 2018)

OBSERVATION The recording does not show the public hearing or the vote count that the curing statute requires

An official-ballot district that discovers a procedural defect may call a special meeting “for the exclusive purpose of curing such defect according to RSA 31:5-b with a single session for deliberating and voting to cure such defect.” (RSA 40:16). RSA 31:5-b, II sets the conditions: the meeting may not be held less than 21 days after the original vote (May 15 is 64 days after March 12, so that is met); “Not less than 7 calendar days prior” the governing body must hold a public hearing explaining the reasons; statutory notice requirements for special meetings apply; and the majority needed to cure is “the same as the majority as required for passage of the original article.”

The recording covers only the meeting itself. No one mentions a prior hearing or the warrant for this meeting. The cure was a single motion ratifying all “the results of the March 12th Annual Meeting” (0:02:02), decided by voice vote with no count, and the transcript renders the call for opposition as “I oppose. Nay,” (0:03:52), so it cannot be told whether anyone voted no. If any March 12 article needed more than a simple majority, a voice vote does not show that the same majority was reached. The hearing notice, the warrant for this meeting and the clerk's minutes would settle all three points.

Sources: RSA 40:16 (2019 codification; source 1999, 86:4); RSA 31:5-b, II(a)-(e) (2019 codification; source ends 1989, 287:1)

OBSERVATION The chair suggested the board could let the superintendent hire over the summer without board approval

Discussing whether to meet in July, Sprague said summer meetings are mainly for accepting nominations of new hires and that “We can actually, give the superintendent the right to hire without without our approval.” (1:09:36). RSA 189:39 provides that “Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers employed in the schools in their school administrative unit”. A standing delegation of teacher hiring to the superintendent would sit uneasily with that division of roles. No action was taken; the chair proposed waiting for the new superintendent's view (1:10:04). Earlier the same evening the board followed the statute's sequence, with the acting superintendent bringing two names forward and the board voting on them (1:06:12 to 1:07:27). The question returned at the SAU 6 board on June 4, 2019 (Dialogue/10407 SAU6060419.mp4.CSV).

Sources: RSA 189:39 (superintendents nominate, school boards elect teachers; source note ends 1971, 371:2)
