MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for a fixed 97 years by voice vote, with no tally and no statutory reason stated

Benware moved "to seal the minutes of the nonpublic session for 97 years" (0:00:53), and the chair took it as "All those in favor? Aye. Opposed?" (0:01:04). Under RSA 91-A:3, III as it read in 2018, nonpublic minutes and decisions had to be disclosed within 72 hours unless the body, by a recorded vote of two-thirds of the members present taken in public session, determined that disclosure would likely affect adversely someone's reputation, render a proposed action ineffective, or relate to terrorism; the information could then be withheld until, in a majority's opinion, those circumstances no longer applied. The statute sets no fixed term. On the recording no reason is stated, no count or names are given for the vote, and the term is a flat 97 years. Six members were present, so a two-thirds vote needed four; a unanimous voice vote may well have met that, and the minutes, if they survive, may record a finding and a tally the recording does not. This is a documentation gap to check against the minutes, not a finding that the seal was invalid.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION The same-meal rule was described as federal law; it is New Hampshire law, and federal rules limit how an uncollected lunch debt can be covered

LeClair attributed the duty to serve every child the same meal to "the federal law change" (0:19:24), and O'Neill dated the debt to the policy the district adopted under it (0:18:13). The requirement that no student be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal is in RSA 189:11-a, VIII, added by 2016, 48:1, effective July 2, 2016. The federal change of that period was different: USDA required every school food authority to have a written, communicated meal-charge policy by July 1, 2017, and its guidance left the choice between a reimbursable meal and an alternate meal to local policy. The same USDA guidance says the nonprofit school food service account may not be used to cover bad debt from unpaid charges; such losses must be restored with non-federal funds. That bears on the $32,000 the board was told came from 2017-18 (0:17:40) and on the donations it agreed to accept. Zullo's statement that feeding every child "the same meals" is "the law" (0:27:01) is accurate as to state law.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2018 codification; 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016); USDA FNS memo SP 23-2017 (Mar. 23, 2017): written meal-charge policy required; bad debt restored from non-federal funds

No vote was taken on donations; the board reached consensus that gifts toward the debt would be accepted if received by 4:30 p.m. on Tuesday, November 6 (0:42:54), with "a public hearing and acceptance of donation at the next board meeting" (0:40:16).
