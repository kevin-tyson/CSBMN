MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes were sealed "for 97 years" by voice vote, with no statutory determination stated on the recording

The recording starts as the board leaves a nonpublic session (0:00:12), so the motion to enter it, and the exemption it had to state, are not on the tape. Once out, Benware moved "to seal the minutes of the nonpublic session for 97 years." (0:00:53), and the chair took a voice vote (0:01:04). In 2018 RSA 91-A:3, III required nonpublic minutes to be disclosed within 72 hours unless, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session", the board determined that disclosure would likely affect adversely the reputation of a non-member, render the proposed action ineffective, or concern terrorism; such information "may be withheld until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply." Three points are open on the tape. No determination is stated in the motion or before the vote. The vote was by voice with no count read, so whether it was recorded as a two-thirds vote depends on minutes that are not online (the roll call just before showed six members present, and no one is heard opposing). And the statute sets no fixed sealing term; it ties withholding to the determined circumstances, so a flat 97 years is a term the statute does not supply. Benware himself remarked that the motion "sounded bad". The nonpublic minutes may record the entry motion and a determination; they are the place to check. The limb applied is a documentation gap, not a confirmed violation, which is why this is MEDIUM.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III (2018 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017).

OBSERVATION The same-meal rule was attributed to federal law; the binding rule in 2018 was New Hampshire's RSA 189:11-a, VIII

LeClair described "the federal law change that we are required to give the same meal to every child" (0:19:24); Zullo said every child gets "exactly the same meals" and "And that's the law." (0:27:01); O'Neill traced it to "a new policy that said that you can't differentiate between what children are eating" (0:18:13). As verified for this page, the federal requirement was procedural: USDA memo SP 46-2016 required every school food authority to have "a written and clearly communicated system to address meal charges", and USDA's 2017 guidance left serving an alternate meal to local choice. The substantive rule came from the State: since July 2, 2016, RSA 189:11-a, VIII has required a district's meal payment policy to ensure "that all students have access to a healthy school lunch" and that "no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures." So the members' bottom line (every child is fed the same meal, and that is the law) was right for New Hampshire in October 2018; only the attribution to federal law was imprecise. Recorded as an observation because the public explanation of why the debt accrues depends on getting the source of the rule right.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2018 codification; source note ends 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016); USDA FNS memo SP 23-2017, Unpaid Meal Charges: Guidance and Q&A (March 23, 2017), restating SP 46-2016.

OBSERVATION The recovery plan does not say which fund absorbs debt that is never collected

The plan set out at 0:23:03 (policy review, a contractor meeting, a food service coordinator, volunteer callers, donations) addresses collection going forward. LeClair added: "I don't think it's reasonable to think we would end the year without anybody in debt" (0:24:23). USDA's 2017 guidance states that unpaid meal charges that become bad debt cannot be covered from the nonprofit school food service account: "these losses must be restored using non-Federal funds." Nothing on this recording says where the uncollected part of the $32,000 would be charged. The board's October 3 vote to write off small balances raises the same question (see the October 3 page). No violation is shown; this is noted so a reader knows what to look for in the budget.

Sources: USDA FNS memo SP 23-2017 (March 23, 2017), bad debt.

POSITIVE Small lunch-debt donations were routed through a public hearing and a fixed deadline

About $280 had come in when the board discussed donations (0:40:43). Under RSA 198:20-b, III(b) as it read in 2018, unanticipated funds under $5,000 needed only notice in the agenda and the minutes and acceptance in public session at a regular meeting; a hearing was required at $5,000 or more. The administration nonetheless proposed "a public hearing and acceptance of donation" at the next meeting (0:40:16), set a public cutoff with a named payee (0:42:54), and the board pointed later gifts to the Trinity Church backpack program rather than letting an open-ended fund grow (0:41:24). Pierce asked that the money be for students only (0:42:04). Whether the hearing and acceptance happened is for the November 7 record.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a)-(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, so the same text governed in 2018).
