MEDIUM An ad hoc board committee that met over "a couple of years" has no notice or minutes in the public record, and its work product is not in the posted packet

The chair named the committee's members as himself, Whitney and Sprague (0:33:55); Whitney said the superintendent and business administrator attended "1 or 2 of the meetings" (0:34:44); Sprague said "it really took us a couple of years to really finish it" (0:45:01). Under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), a public body reaches, after its list of bodies, "or any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto" (statute), so the committee's meetings carried the notice and minutes duties of RSA 91-A:2, II. OBSERVATION The $7,000 gift got the public hearing the law then required, but the newspaper notice is not in the record

Before July 18, 2023, RSA 198:20-b, III(a) required that "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with notice "published in a newspaper of general circulation in the relevant municipality at least 7 days before" (statute; the threshold rose to $20,000 under 2023, 38:1). The board held the hearing (0:03:45 to 0:05:21) and voted acceptance afterward in public session (0:46:16), which is the order the statute requires. The record does not show the seven-day newspaper notice: the packet holds no notice, the agenda reached the packet folder on February 28, and the donation document was added the morning of the meeting (Drive file creation, 16:00 UTC, March 1). Whether the district had adopted the enabling article RSA 198:20-b, I requires is also not in this record. A copy of the published notice would settle the first point.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2022 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1); agenda, item III

OBSERVATION A member's questions to the whole board are routed through a staff member to avoid a violation

Sprague recognized that sending his ESSER questions to the full board himself could be a problem ("Because I can't really send it to you without violating") and proposed that the superintendent's assistant forward them so "the board can maybe put some follow up questions on it" (0:26:20). RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings", and II provides that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter" (statute). Distributing a member's questions for the administration's answer is not deliberation by itself; inviting members to add follow-up questions to a circulating document begins to resemble the sequential communication paragraph II addresses, and routing it through staff does not change its character. The answers would serve the public better if presented at a meeting.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2022 codification; source 2008, 303:4)
