SAU 6 Board, October 21, 2021

Regular meeting of the SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity) that opened the FY2023 SAU budget. The board learned that its 3 percent merit pool, about $33,000, had been paid to every employee without a formal evaluation and voted to withhold it until an evaluation process exists; it also heard of a state-driven rise in retirement contributions and a website conversion. They did not exist then. OBSERVATION A 3 percent merit pool of about $33,000 had been paid to every SAU employee without a formal evaluation

Asked how the budgeted merit pool is distributed, the assistant superintendent for finance said it was budgeted at 3 percent (0:15:49), “For this year $33,000.” (0:16:02), and that it goes to “Everyone within the saw.” (0:16:07) When Sprague asked “So is it fair to say that there's not really an evaluation process that to to give that out?” (0:16:10) he answered “Yeah, I think that's a fair statement.” (0:16:17) and that supervisors' evaluations are “not something that's formalized into the into the files.” (0:17:23) The director of special education recalled an evaluation tool from about 2018-19 that “got lost and then chaos ensued.” (0:18:36) Erickson, a long-serving Unity member: “to my knowledge, in three years it's never been utilized the way we had envisioned it” (0:19:57). Horsky: “I don't think it was supposed to be just a flat 3% slush fund.” (0:19:32) RSA 194-C:5, III makes the SAU board responsible for fixing the salaries of all SAU personnel. A pool the board created for merit, paid uniformly without the evaluation the board intended, is a gap between the board's decision and its execution. Neither the original vote creating the pool nor the payment records are in the public record, so this page cannot say who authorized the uniform payouts.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (2021 codification; 1996, 298:3, unamended): the SAU board fixes the salaries of all SAU personnel; the recording

POSITIVE The board withheld the merit pool until a documented evaluation exists, and restated the motion before voting

Horsky moved “to suspend the use of the merit pay.” (0:23:02), adding “we can budgeted in, but I don't want it released.” (0:23:02); Stone seconded (0:23:23). Before the vote the chair said “So we're working on making sure motions are clear.” (0:25:38) and restated it: “your motion is to suspend the increases until the, formalized evaluation process is put in place.” (0:25:38). Members made clear the aim was documentation, not withholding pay as such: Whitney, “It doesn't have to be draconian.” (0:24:37); the chair, “We're looking to get the formalized process in place in order to give those those merits out.” (0:24:58) The superintendent committed to a process before the June payout (0:24:23). Tying a pay pool to a documented evaluation, and restating a motion so the record of what passed is unambiguous, go beyond what any statute requires. The motion carried by voice (0:25:58); the tally is not recorded on tape.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (2021 codification); the recording

POSITIVE Budget calendar set to finish before the January 1 statutory deadline, with drafts circulated a week ahead

The assistant superintendent for finance proposed a first-draft meeting in November and final approval about December 16 (0:15:04, 0:29:04), and offered to “send it out to the board for a week prior and electronic form” (0:28:14). He described the salary schedule as position by position, noting “Salaries because that's public information.” (0:26:19) while health-care details in the same schedule are not (0:26:19), and agreed to add a prior-year column at Sprague's request (0:27:04). RSA 194-C:9, I requires the SAU board to adopt its budget at a meeting held before January 1 and to apportion it among the member districts, half on average membership and half on equalized valuation. The calendar meets that deadline with 16 days to spare, and the advance circulation and position-level detail exceed it.
