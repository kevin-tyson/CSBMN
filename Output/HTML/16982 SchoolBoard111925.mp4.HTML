Claremont School Board, November 19, 2025

Regular board meeting, three hours long, with Frank Sprague back at the table after his November 5 return and William Madden absent. The interim superintendent and student-services staff reported unfilled positions against Ed 306 and against one-to-one aides prescribed in IEPs; the business administrator reported $307,000 cash on hand and a projected year-end deficit of $1.7 million; the board learned it was bound to salaries for two school-psychology interns it had not known were hired; it reinstated the truant officer as a hybrid position 4-2, accepted a Home Depot flooring donation, adopted six policies and rejected a head-lice policy revision 2-3-1. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on November 19, 2025, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM One-to-one aides prescribed in IEPs were unfilled in three self-contained programs, and one program had no special educator

Student-services staff told the board that the one-to-one paraprofessional counts "are based on what's prescribed" in students' IEPs (0:49:08), and then that the early-education program had five of six, the grades 2-3 program was "one short" (0:50:21), and the grades 4-5 Visions program had no teacher and "Seven pairs. Four are filled." (0:51:24, 0:51:59). The middle-school Camp program had no special educator, with "specialized services" provided by another teacher (draft minutes; on tape from 0:42:04). Federal regulations require that "As soon as possible following development of the IEP, special education and related services are made available to the child in accordance with the child's IEP" (34 CFR 300.323(c)(2)). The district described active recruiting, contracted services and board-certified behavior analyst support, which may cover some of the gap; the record does not show whether each child's prescribed services were delivered. Graded MEDIUM as a probable compliance gap reported by the district itself. Student counts in these programs are omitted here.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.323(c)(2) (services made available in accordance with the IEP); 11.19.25 draft minutes (staffing summary)

MEDIUM Two school-psychology interns were employed at salaries the board says it never approved, and the district is bound to them

The chair said the board "had not been aware that these positions had been filled, by interns" and that counsel confirmed "we are obligated to maintain the salaries as signed by the agreement" (1:44:36); a health-benefit offering not in the agreements was also reported (1:45:08). She put the pay at about $80,000 ("So I would never have agreed to appropriate $80,000 for an intern.", 1:51:44) and said the board learned of it "essentially by accident" (1:53:49). She also raised contracts signed with a signature stamp; the business administrator said the employer signature in the system is "kind of like a rubber stamp electronic" (1:52:15). The board's adopted by-laws provide: "No Claremont School District employee shall begin employment without the approval of the CSB unless authorized by Section 5.04" (by-law 5.03); 5.04 covers temporary staff to fill vacancies between meetings, reported to the board at its next meeting. The record shows no board approval and no report. MEDIUM: a hiring-control failure against the board's own rule with a continuing cost. The fix the administration offered (monthly lists of offers and acceptances, 1:52:46) is noted.

Sources: Claremont School Board By-Laws, adopted June 5, 2024, rules 5.03 and 5.04; intern contracts linked from the agenda

MEDIUM Minutes of September 17 and October 1 reached the public share five to six weeks late, and approval of three sets was deferred again

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". Drive metadata dates the first copy of the September 17 draft (08. draft CSB meeting minutes 9.17.25.pdf) to November 3, 2025, the 32nd business day after that meeting, and the October 1 draft in this packet to 8:29 p.m. on November 12, after the close of the 28th business day (counting Columbus Day and Veterans Day as holidays under RSA 288:1). Creation in Drive dates when the public could see a file there, not when it was written. At this meeting Hawkins removed all three sets of minutes because "We got them very late" (0:09:54). Graded MEDIUM, not HIGH, because the record mitigates it: the chair explained on tape that the clerk was on maternity leave and the board had no web access (0:18:04, 0:18:34), and the minutes exist. The November 5 minutes, by contrast, reached this packet on the fifth business day.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (five business days); RSA 288:1 (legal holidays); 08. draft CSB meeting minutes 9.17.25.pdf; draft CSB Meeting Minutes 10.1.25.docx.pdf

MEDIUM The draft minutes give no actual start or end time

Since August 22, 2025, RSA 91-A:2, II has required minutes to include "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes." The draft minutes name their author but carry only the agenda's clock headings, ending "VI. 8:30 PM Adjournment" with the entry "Consent adjournment". The recording runs 3:10:02 from the call to order, so a meeting that began at 6:30 p.m. ended well after 8:30 p.m.; the 8:30 heading cannot be read as the end time. MEDIUM: a documentation gap under a three-month-old requirement, on minutes that are otherwise detailed.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (as amended 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025); 11.19.25 draft minutes

OBSERVATION Six policies were adopted at their first reading, though the board's policy-process policy calls for a second reading

The board adopted EFA, IMBA, DAFB, BCA and KFA together "after first read" (2:41:42, 2:42:04) and adopted the new JLP the same evening (2:56:34). Policy BGAA, which the October 15, 2025 draft minutes record the board adopting, provides that new policies and revisions receive a "first reading" and that "At the next Board meeting (or a later meeting if so agreed by the Board), the policy shall be placed on the agenda for a second (or additional) reading, and action"; on "matters of unusual or unexpected urgency" the board may waive the second meeting, and "the meeting minutes should reflect the nature of the circumstances warranting the suspension". The draft minutes record no urgency finding. Hawkins described the set as "missing required policies" and said the documents were "last minute" (2:34:20, 2:39:18). The BGAA text read here is the NHSBA sample filed in the October 15 packet, with blanks unfilled; the adopted wording was not located. A district policy is not statute, so this is an observation.

Sources: Policy BGAA, sections E.2, E.5 and G.2 (October 15, 2025 packet copy); 10.15.25 draft minutes (BGAA adopted)

OBSERVATION The manifest was deferred to the end of the meeting and not taken up on the recording

The chair said "We also have an approval of manifest that we will do this evening as well. At the end of the meeting." (0:10:02). The recording ends at the chair's adjournment (3:09:54) with no manifest item, and the draft minutes list "Approval of Manifest" with no action recorded. Sources: RSA 197:23-a (treasurer pays on orders of the board); 11.19.25 draft minutes

OBSERVATION The superintendent reported Claremont Middle School below Ed 306 in health education, music and computer science, and without a nurse

By the interim superintendent's own comparison, the health and wellness teacher was cut although "that is legally required for the state" and a substitute who "isn't necessarily, a certified teacher" covers it (0:28:23); music is vacant; computer science is needed "to meet the requirements for Ed 306" (0:29:01); instructional hours meet the 945 and 990 thresholds (0:32:00). This page has not verified the specific Ed 306 subsection for each subject, so it records the superintendent's account and cites the rule part. On the nurse: New Hampshire law lets a board provide school nurse services ("may provide school health services") rather than requiring a nurse, so the vacancy is not itself a breach; the immunization and examination duties RSA 200:38 assigns to the nurse go unheld while it lasts, which the superintendent described as other staff "having to assume the nurse responsibilities" (0:30:57).

Sources: Ed 306 (minimum standards for public school approval; Ed 306.15 hours); RSA 200:27 (board "may provide" school health services)

OBSERVATION Budget reports still charge closed Bluff School with staff who now work elsewhere

Crawford noted the budget report still carries Bluff School salary lines (1:03:33); Angell said "we haven't transitioned some of these employees from bluff to the schools" and is building a staffing map to move them "to the appropriate accounting category" (1:03:57); the Maple Avenue special-education aide line is "383,000 over" for the same reason (1:06:35). Location coding follows the NH Financial Accounting Handbook, which Rev 1102.03 ranks as other guidance, and the totals are unaffected, so this is an observation about the usefulness of the school-level figures until the recoding is done.

Sources: NH Financial Accounting Handbook for Local Education Agencies; N.H. Admin. Rules, Rev 1102.03 (GAAP hierarchy)
