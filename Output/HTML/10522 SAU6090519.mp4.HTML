MEDIUM No minutes, agenda or packet for this meeting are in the district's public shares

The board worked from an agenda (“We have an agenda in front of us”, 0:01:19), a printed cost sheet for the position vote (0:11:33) and an IRS letter the superintendent says was “included” in the members' materials (0:07:17). None of these, and no minutes, is in any district share: the Drive shares begin in 2022 and the Meeting Minutes share in 2023. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be open to public inspection within five business days and makes them “permanent records”; from January 1, 2019 it also requires the minutes to name who made and seconded each motion, which matters here because the recording does not identify the mover or seconder of any motion. Graded MEDIUM under the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the absence: at the October 10, 2019 SAU 6 meeting a motion was made “that we accept the minutes of September 5th, 2019”, corrections to the date and to the chair's name were read, and the minutes were approved (Dialogue/10567 SAU6101019.mp4.CSV, about three minutes in). So minutes were written; they are simply not in any public location this project can reach. A records request to SAU 6 would settle whether they survive.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2019 codification (minutes within 5 business days; permanent records; mover and seconder named); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), 2019 codification (a school administrative unit's board is a public body)

MEDIUM District audits more than two years behind: Claremont's FY2017 audit still closing and FY2018 not yet begun

At 0:08:06 the superintendent reports the auditors “closing the Claremont 17 audit now” and expecting to start “the 18 next week”; Unity's FY2017 audit is closed and goes to the Unity board for signature (0:08:32). Fiscal 2017 ended June 30, 2017 and fiscal 2018 on June 30, 2018. Where a district spends $750,000 or more of federal awards in a year, the Uniform Guidance requires the single-audit reporting package within nine months of year end, which would have been March 31, 2018 for FY2017 and March 31, 2019 for FY2018. This recording does not state either district's federal expenditures, so the page cannot confirm that the threshold applies; that is why this is graded MEDIUM rather than HIGH. If Claremont's federal spending met the threshold, both deadlines had passed by this meeting. In mitigation, the board asked to hear from the auditors in public (0:09:36) and Towle asked about their control recommendations (0:10:20).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2019 edition (report due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2019 edition ($750,000 threshold)

OBSERVATION Position merger and a $170,000 assessment reduction approved from a handout the public cannot see

The board approved replacing a part-time grants manager (about $70,000 a year from a consultant line) and an unfilled assistant business manager position with one full-time accountant/grants manager (0:13:36 to 0:21:33). The superintendent's figures depend on a cost sheet that is not in the public record, and the transcript garbles the key numbers: the actual salaries read “36,030 7000”, and the amount “to be returned” reads “56,000, $57,000, 40,000 in Claremont to be returned and 17,008” (0:16:27). A reader cannot reconstruct the net effect on either district's assessment from the recording. No statute is engaged by the decision itself; the SAU board sets its own staffing. The gap is one of documentation, and the missing minutes (flag 1) would have recorded the motion and its sponsors.

Sources: the recording; RSA 91-A:2, II, 2019 codification (minutes record final decisions and movers)
