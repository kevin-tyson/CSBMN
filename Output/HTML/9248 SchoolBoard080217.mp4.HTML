MEDIUM No agenda, packet, minutes or nonpublic record for this meeting survives in any district share

The recording is therefore the only public account of the nominations, the two policies sent to final approval, the MOU amendment and the CCTV appointment, and of a nonpublic session whose outcome was not recorded. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force for calendar 2017, required minutes including "names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", open to public inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting"; RSA 91-A:3, III required nonpublic minutes and decisions to be disclosed within 72 hours unless withheld by a recorded two-thirds vote. This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the board approves its June 21 minutes on tape (0:04:19), the superintendent describes a written 116-page agenda (0:04:44), and on August 16 the board approved minutes of this meeting with one correction (August 16, 2017 page). Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2016 codification, the text for calendar 2017; minutes content and five business days); RSA 91-A:3, III (2016 codification; 72-hour disclosure of nonpublic minutes)

OBSERVATION Two program matters were said to rest on what members heard in nonpublic session

Adrian said principals had briefed the board in a nonpublic session on the cost of addressing behavioral issues across the district, with the understanding that the matter "needed to be discussed publicly" (0:33:14). Later, defending the Owls and Alt restructuring, the superintendent told Irish "if you also remember Chris in Nonpublic, there was another discussion" (1:52:35) and that "the other thing you heard we couldn't discuss publicly" (1:57:19). RSA 91-A:3, II lists the only grounds for a nonpublic session (employee discipline or compensation, hiring, reputation, property, litigation and others), and I(c) confines discussion "to the matters set out in the motion". A district-wide budget discussion, or the design of a program, fits none of them unless it turns on particular employees or students. The motions and exemptions used for those earlier sessions are not in this record, so no violation is shown; the record does show that the board treated policy-level information as heard in confidence, and that the public explanation of the restructuring was deferred to August 16.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(c) and II (2016 codification, text for calendar 2017); Dialogue/9248 SchoolBoard080217.mp4.CSV (rows 145 to 148, 519 to 559)

OBSERVATION HIPAA was given as a reason not to tell parents about lice cases

Explaining why the nurses do not send a letter home for every case, the high-school nurse said a notice of how many students "were diagnosed with something" is "kind of against HIPAA" (0:44:17). The HIPAA Privacy Rule's definition of protected health information excludes health information "In education records covered by the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act", which is where a school nurse's student records generally sit, so student health privacy in a public school is a FERPA question. A notice that lice were found in a classroom, naming no one, would not be a disclosure of personally identifiable information under either law. The protocol itself may be sound (she and the superintendent cite the nurses' association survey and medical guidance), and members settled on periodic general reminders; the observation is only that the legal reason given on the record does not hold as stated.

Sources: 45 CFR 160.103, "Protected health information" (2016 annual edition; excludes FERPA education records); 34 CFR 99.30(a) (consent before disclosing personally identifiable information from education records; last amended 2004)

OBSERVATION The school lunch meal charge policy reached final approval after USDA's July 1, 2017 deadline, without its text on the record

The board waived the third reading and sent the "school lunch program meal charges" policy to final approval (2:00:38, 2:01:35). USDA required every school food authority in the National School Lunch Program to have a written meal-charge policy in place no later than July 1, 2017. If this was the district's first written policy, it came a month late; if it revised an existing one, there is no issue. The record does not say which, and the policy's text is not read. State law since July 2, 2016 has required any meal payment policy to "ensure that all students have access to a healthy school lunch" and that "no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures"; whether this policy meets that test cannot be checked without the document. USDA's requirement is set by guidance memorandum, not regulation, which is why this is an observation.

Sources: USDA FNS SP 23-2017 (March 23, 2017): written meal charge policy required no later than July 1, 2017; RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2017 codification; added 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016)

POSITIVE A survey-consent policy started ahead of the new law's effective date

The board gave a first reading to a non-academic surveys and questionnaires policy "to be in compliance" with legislation the superintendent said "goes into effect next month" (2:01:45), and he described the change correctly: parental consent replaces the old opt-out, with an exception for the Youth Risk Behavior Survey (2:03:46, 2:04:00). The 2016 codification of RSA 186:11, IX-d provided only an opt-out with ten days' advance review; the 2017 codification, effective September 16, 2017, bars requiring a student to take a non-academic survey "without written consent of a parent or legal guardian" and excepts the Youth Risk Behavior Survey. Starting the policy six weeks before the effective date gave the board time for the required readings.

Sources: RSA 186:11, IX-d (2016 codification: opt-out); RSA 186:11, IX-d (2017 codification, eff. Sept. 16, 2017: written consent, YRBS exception)

POSITIVE The new meal system is built so that no one at the register knows who is free or reduced

The food service contractor described a process in which approved applications flow straight into the point-of-sale system: "Everybody's treated the same way. Nobody knows who's free or reduced." (1:09:08), and all choices are open to all students. The business administrator said online applications cut down "on who sees it" (1:06:54), and the superintendent asked that applications go out before school starts so parents' "private business is not going to be public banter" (1:01:20). Federal rules bar "overt identification" of children receiving free or reduced-price meals; the plan described goes further by keeping eligibility out of sight of kitchen staff as well, and the contractor's plan to place paper forms at fire stations, libraries and doctors' offices keeps the process open to families without internet access.

Sources: 7 CFR 245.8 (2017 annual edition: no overt identification; no separate line, entrance or meal time)
