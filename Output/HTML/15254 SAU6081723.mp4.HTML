SAU 6 Board, August 17, 2023

Regular meeting of the SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity), chaired by Arlene Hawkins with eight of twelve members present by the recording's count. The board holds a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(c) and seals its minutes for two years, seats a superintendent-evaluation subcommittee and hires a facilitator for up to $5,000, suspends its own evaluation policy CBI for the cycle, elects Bonnie Miles treasurer and Frank Sprague NHSBA delegate, hears the exit-interview results and an HR report on the Lupini study, and votes to approve a Tyler software purchase of about $120,000 that the business administrator says was already made with ESSER money. MEDIUM A software purchase of about $120,000 in federal ESSER money was made before the board's vote, and the motion approving it stated no amount

The agenda listed the Tyler upgrade for a vote. Before the vote the business administrator said "We've already purchased this software through SRS" [ESSER] (1:12:19), relying on a prior discussion in which David Jack, who ran the business office before her, "had come out and said that it was good to go" (1:12:31), and added "I know you haven't voted" (1:13:44). She put the cost at "it was 120,000" (1:14:47), with about $20,000 to $28,000 a year falling on the budget once ESSER ends (1:14:13, 1:15:25). The chair asked whether the motion should carry an amount; the mover said it was not necessary (1:16:12), and the minutes record only "approve the purchase of the Tyler Software Upgrade" (minutes). Under the Uniform Guidance in its 2023 edition, a recipient spending federal award money "must have and use documented procurement procedures" and "must maintain records sufficient to detail the history of procurement" (regulation). Nothing in this meeting's record shows the quotes, sole-source justification or contract behind the purchase, or which entity bought it; those documents would settle the point. The flag is MEDIUM because it rests on the business administrator's own account and no rule is shown to require prior board approval for this purchase; it is a control weakness on the record, not a finding that the procurement itself was improper.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.318(a) and (i) (2023 annual edition); draft minutes, item VI.3

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for two years with no statutory determination stated; the session's length is not recorded anywhere

RSA 91-A:3, III, as it read on this date, lets a public body withhold nonpublic minutes beyond 72 hours only by a recorded two-thirds vote of the members present, taken in public session, that determines divulgence "likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism" (statute). The seal vote was in public and by roll call with eight yes votes (0:07:09 to 0:07:26); the motion was only "to seal the minutes for two years" (0:06:53), and the minutes record no determination. The session was entered under II(c), the reputation ground, so a finding may be implied, but the statute asks that it be made. On the recording, 56 seconds of room chatter separate the entry vote (0:04:18) from the call to reconvene (0:05:14). That is either a session of under a minute or, more likely, a paused or edited recording; the project has found the second pattern on other SAU 6 recordings, and the tape cannot decide it. The minutes give no times, so the session's length cannot be recovered.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2022 codification); draft minutes, item III
