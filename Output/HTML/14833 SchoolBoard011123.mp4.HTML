Claremont School Board, Public Hearing & Board Meeting, January 11, 2023

A public hearing on a $10,000 donation for the Claremont Middle School scoreboard, followed by the regular meeting. The board reviewed the proposed FY24 budget of $39,325,854 (a projected tax rate of $25.84, up $2.26), directed the administration without a vote to drop two new tech-center positions and aim for an increase under a dollar, accepted the donation, and voted to leave Joshua Lambert's seat vacant until the March election. MEDIUM The board voted not to fill a vacancy that RSA 671:33 says the board "shall fill"

RSA 671:33, II(a) reads: "The school board shall fill vacancies occurring on the school board", with appointees serving "until the next district election when the voters of the district shall elect a replacement", and a fallback to the selectmen if the remaining members "are unable, by majority vote, to agree upon an appointment". After Joshua Lambert's resignation the board voted, on Sprague's motion, not to post for an appointment and to wait for the March 14 election (2:24:39, 2:24:45); the minutes record Heather Whitney voting no. Sprague said he "couldn't find any specific RSA" (2:22:56). In mitigation, the statute sets no deadline, an appointee would have served at most two or three meetings (2:21:54), the seat was already on the March ballot as a one-year seat (2:21:28), and Seaman recalled the board doing the same ten to twelve years earlier (2:23:55). The vote leaves a six-member board through the budget hearing and the deliberative session. Whether a deliberate decision not to appoint satisfies "shall fill" is for counsel; this page does not decide it.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a), 2022 codification (school board shall fill vacancies; appointee serves to next election; 2021, 42:1 and 91:318); draft 1.11.23 CSB meeting minutes

MEDIUM The minutes omit the board's budget direction to the administration

The board did not vote on the budget, but it reached a decision by consensus that shaped it: the chair called it "just a directive to administration" (1:48:08), and Seaman restated it as removing the two tech-center positions and aiming for about a $0.94 increase, the FY22 rate of $24.53 (1:48:12, 1:48:40). The February 1 packet then carries a document titled Budget Reduction Emergency Action from 1.17.23 CSB Budget Hearing. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to include "a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions". The draft minutes describe the discussion but record neither the direction nor the target, and give the tax effect of removing the tech programs as twenty-six cents where Seaman said "The tax rate reduces by $0.28." (1:42:23). A reader of the minutes would not learn that the administration had been told to cut about $700,000.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2022 codification (minutes: subject matter and final decisions); draft 1.11.23 CSB meeting minutes

MEDIUM The minutes reached the public share on the sixth business day

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". For a Wednesday, January 11 meeting, with Monday, January 16 a legal holiday, the fifth business day is Thursday, January 19. The only copy, a draft, was created in the February 1 packet folder on Friday, January 20, 2023, the sixth business day. Applying the run's severity rule: the meeting was noticed and packeted and only the minutes are late, so this is MEDIUM. The Drive date shows when the public share received the file, not whether a copy was available at the SAU office earlier.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2022 codification (five business days); RSA 288:1 (legal holidays)

OBSERVATION A "NON-Meeting" at 6:00 p.m. was noticed with no stated basis

The agenda and the minutes both list "NON-Meeting" at 6:00 p.m., half an hour before the hearing, with no subject, no exemption cited and no record of who attended. Under RSA 91-A:2, I, a gathering of a quorum is not a "meeting" only for the listed purposes: strategy or negotiations with respect to collective bargaining, consultation with legal counsel, a political-party caucus, or circulation of draft documents formalizing decisions previously made. The record does not say which, if any, applied. A party caucus is not available to this board, whose members are elected on a nonpartisan ballot. Stating the basis on the agenda would let the public tell a lawful non-meeting from an unrecorded meeting.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I(a)-(d), 2022 codification (gatherings excluded from "meeting"); RSA 671:30 (nonpartisan ballot for school district officers); 1.11.23 CSB Agenda
