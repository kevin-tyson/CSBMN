SAU 6 Board, April 9, 2026

Regular meeting of the joint SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity), the first with the two Claremont members sworn in on March 18. Rocco Ruggeri was confirmed as chair and Candace Crawford elected vice chair for the remaining three months; the board heard that the interim business administrator had signed a fourth TMSolution contract, for human-resources mentoring, that the chair said the board never discussed, and that the "how this happened" operational review contracted in September had not begun; two members volunteered to review the SAU's sealed nonpublic minutes for release; and staff and the Unity chair described the work of separating Unity's technology, records, payroll and finances before and after June 30. MEDIUM No minutes of this meeting are publicly reachable; the draft the May 28 agenda links cannot be opened

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes, including the movers and seconders of each motion and, since August 22, 2025, the start and end times and the name of the person who produced them, to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (here, by April 16, 2026). SAU 6's own Policy BBBH-S, §I.5.f, applies the same duty to the joint board. A two-stage search was made: this meeting's packet folder 13. The May 28 agenda, created May 22, lists for approval a "4.9.26 DRAFT SAU6 Board Minutes" Google Doc, which shows a draft was written, but that document returns "not found" to this project, and the May 28 meeting itself has no minutes or recording. Graded MEDIUM under the project's rule: the meeting was noticed, packeted and recorded, and only the minutes are missing. The movers and seconders for the officer elections, the agenda and adjournment are inaudible or unidentified on tape, so the minutes are the only place they could be recorded.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes within 5 business days; movers and seconders; start and end time); 5-28-26 SAU6 Board Agenda (links the 4.9.26 draft); 14. SAU6 5.28.26

OBSERVATION Nine months into the year, SAU 6 had no opening balance and its trial balances had not gone to the auditors

Asked for the SAU's starting number for the fiscal year, the interim business administrator said he still had to prepare SAU 6's trial balances and send them to the auditors, who already had Claremont's and Unity's (0:06:54). The chair expected the audits only "after the separation" (1:13:12). The finance report itself was given orally and does not appear in the packet; its first revenue figure is garbled in the transcript (0:05:41). New Hampshire school districts and SAUs report under GAAP (Rev 1102.03), and audited governmental-fund statements begin from the prior year's ending fund balance; until SAU 6's trial balances reach the auditors, neither the board nor the public has an audited starting position for the SAU's final two-district year. No filing deadline for the SAU's own audit was verified for this date, so the point is recorded as an observation.

Sources: Rev 1102.03 (NH school districts follow GAAP)

POSITIVE The board set two members, one from each district, to review every sealed SAU 6 nonpublic minute for release

At a member's request the board took up the agenda item "Discussion on making non-public meeting minutes public" and named Ms. Whitney and Ms. Popescu to go through all sealed SAU 6 nonpublic minutes with counsel (0:36:35), with the stated aim that "Even if they're redacted on some level. Whatever we can do is what we should do." (0:35:15). RSA 91-A:3, IV, added in 2023, lets a body adopt a review procedure and requires review of a seal no more than ten years after the vote that imposed it, with older unreviewed seals released by operation of law; a voluntary review of every sealed minute before the SAU's membership changes goes beyond that minimum. The interim superintendent's remark that some seals ran "for like 99 years" (0:36:46) is the kind of seal the 2023 amendment targets. No vote was taken, and the outcome is not in this record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III and IV (sealed minutes; review procedure and ten-year review, 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023)
