Claremont School District, Deliberative Session — February 3, 2016

First session of the 2016 annual school district meeting under the official ballot (SB 2) procedure, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no warrant, budget, minutes or other document for this session is posted in the district's online shares. With the district moderator absent, the voters chose a temporary moderator, heard the superintendent's budget presentation, and moved all nine warrant articles forward without amendment, including a $34,131,283 operating budget. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the session against the law in force on February 3, 2016, citing each provision as it then read. They are not findings of violation, and nothing here is legal advice. A deliberative session is a meeting of the voters, so the school board's own open-meeting rules (nonpublic sessions, board public comment) do not apply to it. 6 flags follow, ordered by severity.

OBSERVATION With no moderator present, the district attorney rather than the clerk conducted the choice of a temporary moderator, citing a statute the transcript cannot resolve

At 0:00:21 Upton told the room "we do not have a moderator" and that "State law RSA 6716 does allow the school district to elect a temporary officer." He then took the motion, called for discussion and put the vote himself. The statute that governs this situation, RSA 197:20, has been unchanged since 1964: the district clerk "shall act as moderator of any meeting until a moderator pro tempore shall be chosen, if the moderator is absent or the office has become vacant." The clerk, Mary Woodman, was present: she took the mover's name at 0:01:10 and administered the oath at 0:03:09. On the statute's text she, not counsel, was the officer to preside until the choice was made.

The outcome is what the statute contemplates: the meeting itself chose a moderator pro tempore by a recorded voice vote, and nothing that followed turned on who put that one question. Two details are worth checking against the minutes if they surface: RSA 6716 does not correspond to a section number as transcribed, and the oath read at 0:03:09 runs "until March 5th, 2016", three days before the March 8 second session. The same evening the clerk told the board that no one had filed for moderator for the coming year (February 3 board meeting), and the temporary moderator said she was stepping in "for Tom" (1:31:01).

Sources: RSA 197:20 (2016 codification; source note ends 1963, 120:5, eff. Jan. 1, 1964): clerk acts as moderator until a moderator pro tempore is chosen.

OBSERVATION The default budget read from the warrant was $291,603 higher than the proposed budget; one sentence of the presentation says the opposite

The warrant as read at 0:45:01 puts the proposed operating budget at $34,131,283 and the default budget at $34,422,886, so a defeat of Article 2 would have left the district with the larger figure. The superintendent's presentation agrees in one place ("The default budget would be if that was approved would be a much larger increase", 0:34:39) and then says at 0:35:21 "That's why our default budget is less." Read in context he may have meant the default budget was smaller than it would otherwise have been, or the transcript may have lost a word; the recording should be checked. Voters relying on the spoken sentence alone could take away the wrong comparison.

For the vintage: the 2014 text of RSA 40:13, IX(b) (in force until the 2018 amendments) defined the default budget as the prior year's operating appropriations adjusted for debt service, contracts and other obligations and reduced by one-time expenditures; it did not yet contain the later eliminated-positions clause. No default budget worksheet for FY17 is available online to test the $34,422,886 figure.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, 7:1-4 and 190:1-3, 8-10, eff. Sept. 9, 2014, so the text in force on February 3, 2016).

OBSERVATION The presiding moderator closed the session with a personal appeal in favor of investment

Before taking the adjournment motion, the temporary moderator said "I always believe that the moderator should be unbiased" (1:31:01) and then added at 1:32:05: "sometimes we have to invest in order to move forward. It's just my opinion. And I get to do that now." All nine articles had already been disposed of, so the remark could not have affected any vote taken at the session; it was addressed to the March 8 ballot. No statute in force in February 2016 that this project has verified forbids a moderator from expressing a view after the business is done. Recorded as an observation about neutrality of the chair, not a rule-based concern.

Sources: No verified rule; see the timeline at 1:31:01 and 1:32:05.

OBSERVATION The motions to "accept" each article had no legal effect on whether it reached the ballot

The moderator took a motion, second and voice vote on every article (for example "Can I have a motion to accept article two?" at 0:47:06). RSA 40:13, VI, as it read in 2016, provides: "All warrant articles shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote, including warrant articles as amended by the first session." The first session's role was explanation, discussion, debate and any amendment; a vote to accept or reject did not keep an article on or off the ballot. The warrant as read at 0:43:45 says as much: "The final vote of all warrant articles shall be conducted by the official ballot at the second session." The practice is harmless, but a voter could take a voice vote here for a decision. The board repeated it later the same evening by voting to place Articles 1 through 9 on the ballot (February 3 board meeting).

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and VI (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, so in force on February 3, 2016): first session for explanation, discussion, debate and amendment; all articles go to the ballot.

POSITIVE Held inside the statutory window, with every article explained and every voter question answered on the floor

For a March voting district in 2016, RSA 40:13, III placed the first session "between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January, inclusive of those Saturdays", that is, January 30 to February 6, 2016; Wednesday, February 3 is inside it. The purpose RSA 40:13, IV gives the session, "explanation, discussion, and debate of each warrant article", was met article by article: the moderator called for speakers for and against on each, the business director set out the cost items of all four bargaining agreements, and the three questions from the floor (Common Core costs at 0:53:57, what happens to unspent money at 1:17:02, how the trust figure was set at 1:29:26) each got a direct answer. The ground rules adopted at 0:07:23 allowed written amendments, though none was offered.

Sources: RSA 40:13, III and IV (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, so in force on February 3, 2016).
