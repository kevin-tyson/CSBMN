Claremont School Board, September 5, 2018

A regular meeting the day after school opened. The board received the district's FY2014 audit more than four years after that year closed, with staff reporting a backlog of 14 audits; granted Valley Regional Hospital permission to host a syringe service program inside the drug-free school zone on a 5-2 roll call; adopted the head lice policy on third reading; sent the cell phone policy to second reading with IEP and 504 exceptions; split 3-3-1 on joining the opioid litigation; and accepted a $500 donation already spent. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 5, 2018, or for the audit years at issue, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

HIGH The district's FY2014 audit reached the board in September 2018, with FY2015 through FY2017 still open

LeClair reported "14 outstanding audits last December", seven finished since, and a goal of completing the rest by December 31, 2018 (0:15:02). The audit before the board was Claremont's FY2014; "15 is the next one", then "1617 and 18" (0:20:32). O'Neill attributed the delay to staff turnover at the district and the audit firm and to records that were "very difficult to locate, the filing system was a mess" (0:24:17), and said the FY2014 audit cost "about twice as much as it should have" (0:24:58). The board received the report without a vote and set the questionnaire for the next meeting (0:27:59).

A district that spends federal awards above the single-audit threshold must file its audit within "the earlier of 30 days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period". FY2014 and FY2015, which began before December 26, 2014, fall under OMB Circular A-133 ($500,000 threshold, sec. 320(a)); FY2016 on falls under 2 CFR 200.501 and 200.512 ($750,000). On those rules FY2014 was due by March 31, 2015 and FY2017 by March 31, 2018. The recording does not state Claremont's federal expenditures; the Title I and E-rate funding discussed on August 15 (August 15, 2018 page) and the "$8 million for special education" cited here (2:13:34) make crossing the threshold likely, and the FY2014 report's schedule of expenditures of federal awards would settle it. HIGH because, on the staff's own account, the FY2014 through FY2017 audits were each past that deadline, FY2014 by more than three years. The staff's plan to catch up by year end, and O'Neill's description of the cause, are noted.

Sources: OMB Circular A-133 (2007 revision), secs. 200(a) and 320(a); 2 CFR 200.110(b) (Subpart F applies to fiscal years beginning on or after Dec. 26, 2014; 2016 annual edition); 2 CFR 200.501(a) and 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2018 annual edition)

OBSERVATION Members described signing old warrants, and the auditor asked whether the board authorizes disbursements before funds are released

The questionnaire asks "Does the board formally authorize all disbursements, both vendor and payroll, prior to the release of funds?" (0:18:05). Pierce then recalled that she and the chair "signed a lot of warrants that were great that were found, excuse me, that were from way back", and the chair answered that "They weren't this far back though" (0:27:31, 0:27:50). Horsky did not want the chair to sign answers for years no current member served (0:25:16).

RSA 197:23-a has the treasurer pay out district money "only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of the 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole to authorize payments." Signing warrants long after payments were made would mean money left before the board's order. The recording does not say when the payments behind those warrants were made, or whether the found warrants were unsigned copies of orders given at the time; the auditor's questionnaire is the place that answer would appear.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (2017 codification; source note ends 2015, 272:45, eff. Oct. 1, 2015)

OBSERVATION A $500 private donation was accepted after it had been spent

LeClair said district policy requires board approval for donations of $500 or more, and that Claremont Savings Bank's $500 "was used to purchase supplies to, give out to families at the festival" before the item reached the board (2:21:42). The board accepted it by voice vote (2:22:15).

RSA 198:20-b lets a board "apply for, accept and expend" unanticipated money from "a private source" only where the district has adopted the enabling article; for amounts under $5,000 the board "shall post notice of the funds in the agenda" and minutes, with acceptance "made in public session of any regular school board meeting". The item was on the agenda and accepted in public session; the sequence, spending first and accepting afterward, is the point for a reviewer. Whether Claremont had adopted the enabling article is not on the recording.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(b) (2017 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION Two points of public access: the room could not hear, and a member believed notice must be printed in a newspaper

Late in the meeting Susan Mason told the board the audience had barely heard the discussion all night and "couldn't hear anything" until the syringe-program speakers came to the podium (1:45:49, 1:46:06). Separately, during the item on publication costs Horsky said "there is a specific phrase in the RSA that says it has to be in print" (2:27:56); the district was spending "roughly $25 a meeting" on notices (2:23:54).

No rule this page verified sets a sound standard for people in the room at an in-person meeting. On notice, RSA 91-A:2, II as it read in 2018 required notice "posted in 2 appropriate places one of which may be the public body's Internet website, if such exists, or shall be printed in a newspaper of general circulation" at least 24 hours ahead. Newspaper printing is one alternative, not a requirement. The board took no action and LeClair undertook to confirm (2:28:23).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; the text in force for 2018)
