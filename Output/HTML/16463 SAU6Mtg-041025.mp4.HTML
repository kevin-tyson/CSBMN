MEDIUM The nonpublic motion cites RSA 91-A:3, II(j), which applies only in an adjudicative proceeding

The agenda in the minutes and the minutes' motion both cite "RSA 91-A:3, II (a & j)", and the minutes print the text of (j). That ground covers confidential commercial or financial information "in an adjudicative proceeding pursuant to RSA 541 or RSA 541-A". Nothing in the record suggests the SAU board was conducting such a proceeding, so (j) was not available to it. The session can still stand on (a), personnel, which was also cited. On tape the chair names "two a and J" at 1:41:29, and Whitney's motion is transcribed "two A and a" at 1:41:51; ¶I(b) requires the motion to "state on its face the specific exemption", so the spoken motion should be checked against the recording. Mitigation: a roll-call vote was taken in public, as ¶I(b) requires, and the tape shows it starting at 1:42:11.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(a), II(j) (text as amended 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); draft minutes, item VI.

MEDIUM Minutes sealed for one year with no statutory finding recorded

The minutes record: "Heather Whitney made a motion to seal the minutes for 1 year, Loren Howard seconded the motion; roll call vote taken by Noelle Kronberg, all present voting in favor". RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld beyond 72 hours only by a recorded two-thirds vote taken in public session and only where the body determines that disclosure would likely adversely affect a non-member's reputation, render a proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism. The vote count meets the two-thirds rule; the minutes state no finding at all. The vote happened after the recording stopped, so the tape cannot supply it. A fixed one-year term is not the statute's measure either: the statute withholds until a majority finds the circumstances no longer apply. No unsealed copy of these nonpublic minutes was located (a Drive title search is not reliable, so this is "not found", not "does not exist").

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (72-hour disclosure unless sealed by 2/3 recorded vote with a stated ground) and IV (review of sealed minutes); draft minutes, item VI.

OBSERVATION A four-year audit backlog with repeat control findings, presented in April 2025

The audit presented is for FY2021; the auditor says it carries a 2024 issuance date (0:07:20), the FY22 draft arrived that afternoon, and FY23 fieldwork was to begin within the month. The findings discussed: an electronic signature used with no treasurer review of check registers, purchase orders not attached to invoices, journal entries posted and reversed without review, deposit support that could not be located (some records likely lost in a building renovation, per the auditor), late inter-entity payments tied to grants unclaimed for about two years, and accumulating stale-dated checks. Management says each has been corrected from FY24. In April 2025 no located New Hampshire statute required an SAU to be audited on any schedule: RSA 21-J:19 names towns, school districts and village districts, not SAUs, and is permissive; RSA 194-C:4, II(a) lists audits among the services an SAU provides. That changed later: 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), approved July 2, 2026, effective July 1, 2026, brings SAUs under RSA 198:4-d's audit requirement with a nine-month filing deadline. It does not apply to this meeting and is noted only so readers can follow the thread.

Sources: RSA 194-C:4, II(a) (audits among SAU services); RSA 21-J:19 (permissive audit authority; does not name SAUs); 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), RSA 198:4-d, VI and VIII as reenacted (later law, not in force on this date).

OBSERVATION SAU and Claremont costs crossed in both directions during FY25

Three items in the financial report move money between the SAU and one member district. An SAU special-education position (about $99,000 with benefits, per Crawford at 1:05:26) was paid from Claremont's budget for the year while its SAU appropriation went unspent, which accounts for much of the SAU's projected surplus. In the other direction the SAU paid a Claremont fuel-oil bill of $2,848.58 and $217 of Claremont FSA premiums, now being billed back through receivables (1:15:55, 1:17:35). RSA 194-C:5, III has the SAU board apportion "the expense of the salaries and benefits" of SAU personnel among the districts, and RSA 194-C:9, I fixes the apportionment formula; an SAU position carried for a year in one district's budget changes what each district bears, and Unity was a member that year. The record shows the reasons given, not whether the apportionment or any year-end settlement accounted for the shift. The business administrator reports the corrections openly, which counts in the district's favour.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III (SAU board apportions salaries and benefits among districts); RSA 194-C:9, I (apportionment formula).

POSITIVE The board organized inside the statutory window and filled the treasurer vacancy the auditor's finding exposed

RSA 194-C:5, I requires the SAU board to meet between April 1 and June 1 and to organize by choosing a chairperson, a secretary and a treasurer. The board met April 10, elected a chair and vice chair, and, prompted by the auditor's point that the treasurer should review check registers, elected a treasurer and two voucher signers on the spot, with one Unity and one Claremont signer (0:16:27–0:17:45). The minutes record the chair noting "that the SAU Board currently has no treasurer". Limits: the minutes record no election of a secretary, and the vice chair is not a statutory office.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, I (annual organization between April 1 and June 1; chairperson, secretary, treasurer).

POSITIVE A line-by-line budget-to-actual review with each variance explained

The business administrator walked every department of the FY25 SAU budget, explained each overage (encumbrance timing, health-plan elections, a contractual mileage figure budgeted at half its value, misposted Claremont bills), named the corrections already made, and took questions from four members. No statute requires an SAU board to receive a mid-year budget-to-actual report; doing so in public gives members and voters something to check the year-end figures against.
