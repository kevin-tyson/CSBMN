OBSERVATION One agreement's cost items went into the operating budget instead of a separate article

The chair explained that putting a contract on its own article means "the voters have the opportunity to vote up or down on each one of these items separately" (0:08:57). Three agreements were handled that way. The Claremont Administrators Association agreement, a bargaining unit by the superintendent's own description (0:12:12), was approved "within the main warrant article" (0:14:35), at a cost of $38,890 after O'Neill's correction (0:13:01). RSA 273-A:3, II(b) provides that "Only cost items shall be submitted to the legislative body of the public employer" for approval at the next annual meeting. Whether placing a unit's cost items inside the operating-budget article satisfies that submission requirement is a legal question; the board had asked for a legal opinion on exactly this inconsistency a year earlier (January 21, 2015 recording). The record here does not show that the question was answered. The warrant read at the February 3, 2016 deliberative session partly answers the concern: its Article 2 states the $38,890 and that a favorable vote "shall be considered not only the approval of the proposed operating budget" but also approval of the cost item, so voters were told, though they could not vote on the item separately.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (source note ends 2013, 244:1, eff. Sept. 22, 2013; same text in the 2015 codification); January 21, 2015 meeting page; February 3, 2016 deliberative session page

OBSERVATION Retained year-end funds discussed for a building purchase, a use the statute does not list

The business administrator described a "2.5% carryover fund" of $376,000, later $395,321 (1:11:38, 2:00:37), and when a member asked whether it could fund a building for the alternative program, answered "It's on the edge. That's a that's an interpretation question." (1:31:59). RSA 198:4-b, II, as it read in 2016, let the voters authorize retention of year-end unassigned funds "not to exceed, in any fiscal year, 2.5 percent of the current fiscal year's net assessment", to be used for "emergency expenditures and overexpenditures under RSA 32:11, or to be used as a revenue source to reduce the tax rate". A property purchase is neither. No spending was decided, and the chair turned instead to a separate warrant article (1:32:10), which is the route the statute leaves open.

Sources: RSA 198:4-b, II (2015 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:6, eff. Sept. 9, 2014)
