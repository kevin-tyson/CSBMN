OBSERVATION The superintendent's account of the background-check law matched the statute then in force, which changed that July

The superintendent told the board that the state police would not give him full records "if money wasn't an issue" because "The RSA prevents school superintendents or public schools from accessing that information" (0:20:28). RSA 189:13-a, III, in the 2015 codification, had the state police check state and FBI records and "report the presence or absence of any such crime", and provided: "Under no circumstances shall the criminal records be released to the school administrative unit, school district, chartered public school, or public academy." His statement was accurate. Irish's suggestion that other districts using third parties were "skirting if not breaking the intent of the law" (0:20:50) is a member's hunch; nothing on the recording tests it. The bill the chair urged members to support (0:25:15) became 2016 N.H. Laws ch. 117 (SB 152), effective July 19, 2016; in the 2016 codification paragraph III has the state police report to the superintendent, who reviews the information for the hiring decision under confidentiality and destruction rules. The 2016 change postdates this meeting.

Sources: RSA 189:13-a, III (2015 codification; source note ends 2014, 55:1, eff. May 27, 2014); RSA 189:13-a, III (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 117:1, eff. July 19, 2016)

OBSERVATION A ballot-placement vote the statute does not require, first proposed for the wrong number of articles

At the clerk's prompting the board voted to place articles one through nine "as written and voted upon in the public. Deliberative session" on the March 8 ballot (0:54:59 to 0:55:28). Under RSA 40:13, VI, as it read in 2016, "All warrant articles shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote, including warrant articles as amended by the first session." Placement follows from the statute, so the vote could neither add nor remove an article; it records the board's intent and does no harm. The chair first proposed the motion for "article one through eight" until a voice corrected him to nine (0:54:49, 0:54:57), which matches the nine articles the deliberative session sent to the ballot (see that page). The clerk's statement that the mover of an article usually states it is "being placed on the warrant" (0:53:09) describes local practice, not a statutory step.

Sources: RSA 40:13, VI (2015 codification; source note ends 2014, 190:1-3, 8-10, eff. Sept. 9, 2014); February 3, 2016 deliberative session page

The student report thanked a local ExxonMobil station "for the generous donation of $500." to Stevens High School (0:06:30). RSA 198:20-b lets a school board accept unanticipated money from a private source if the district has adopted the enabling article (¶I); for amounts under $5,000 the board "shall post notice of the funds in the agenda and shall include notice in the minutes", and acceptance "shall be made in public session of any regular school board meeting" (¶III(b)). No acceptance of this gift is heard here, and with no agenda or minutes surviving it cannot be shown whether it was accepted at an earlier meeting.
