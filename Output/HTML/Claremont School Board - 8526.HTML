Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on August 5, 2026, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies. Counts: 2 HIGH, 1 MEDIUM, 5 OBSERVATION, 2 POSITIVE.

HIGH July payments went out under the former treasurer's electronic signature, some never on a manifest the current treasurer saw, and some backdated

Treasurer Luke Diamond reported that the lines in red on his July reconciliation "Were checks or EFTs that were issued this past month that I didn't know about" and that "The auto pen of the previous treasurer was used, even though she's no longer the treasurer" (1:28:14). He said the superintendent "confirmed with our attorneys that this is in fact, quite illegal and we should not be doing this." Dr. Broadrick explained the staff belief that fiscal 2026 bills could carry the prior treasurer's signature and said "So that has been corrected" (1:31:27). Diamond also described "a practice of backdating checks", calling it, in his view, "falsifying public records" (1:33:16), and five checks issued at the end of July "were all dated April 30th" (1:34:28).

The reconciliation in the agenda (Treas Reconciliation - July 2026 - rev 8.4.26.pdf) lists, among others, a $996,439.93 EFT on July 13 and a $41,017.51 payroll EFT on July 10 whose voucher signature column reads ?, and five vendor checks numbered 353966 to 353970 dated 04/30/26 that cleared on 07/31/26 over the former treasurer's electronic image (report text). RSA 197:23-a gives the treasurer custody of district money and lets the treasurer pay it out "only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of the 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole to authorize payments" (statute text). A payment the sitting treasurer did not authorize, signed with the image of a person who no longer holds the office, is outside that structure on its face. The auditor separately told the board that manifests had been reviewed after the fact, which "doesn't comply with state statutes" (0:50:14). Graded HIGH as a likely departure from a binding rule; mitigation on the record is the superintendent's statement that the practice was corrected and that counsel was consulted on the backdating (1:34:12).

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (treasurer pays out "only upon orders of the school board"; keeps independent books); treasurer's July 2026 reconciliation (rev. 8.4.26); 8.5.26 draft minutes

HIGH The FY2022 and FY2023 audits were still drafts in August 2026, years past the federal single-audit deadline

The board received the FY22 and FY23 audit reports as drafts. The FY22 draft is stamped "DRAFT - 11/19/2025" and the FY23 draft "DRAFT - 7-15-2026" (document text). The chair said the administration had held the FY22 draft since November, waiting "until the 23 came out to release them both at the same time" (0:25:04). The auditor said the reports carry disclaimers and "it's going to take two years to get out of a disclaimer" (0:39:55), and reminded the board that the federal clearinghouse still expects single-audit filings: "I've never heard of a school district that was able to get away with not doing one when they had a single audit" (1:15:01).

Under 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) the single-audit reporting package is due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after the end of the audit period. For June 30 fiscal years that is March 31, 2023 for FY22 and March 31, 2024 for FY23. The $750,000 federal-expenditure threshold of 2 CFR 200.501 applies to both years (the $1,000,000 threshold begins with fiscal years starting October 1, 2024). The superintendent put Claremont's federal allocations at "a couple million dollars a year" (0:27:59), well above the threshold. The current eCFR text allows a cognizant agency to extend the nine-month period; nothing in this record mentions an extension. Graded HIGH as a missed federal deadline. Looking forward, 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 now requires each district's audit report for the last completed fiscal year within 9 months of year end (March 31, 2027 for FY26) and directs the commissioner to "withhold state grant funding, not including RSA 198:40-a funding" from non-compliant districts (chaptered text); the auditor said FY24 and FY25 work is limited by the business office's capacity (0:37:58).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (single-audit reporting package due nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501 (single audit at $750,000 of federal expenditures for these fiscal years); 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), approved July 2, 2026, eff. July 1, 2026, reenacting RSA 198:4-d, VIII (audit within 9 months); FY22 draft annual financial report; FY23 draft annual financial report

The same form records a "Roll Call Vote to seal minutes" with seven Y entries, Howard's included, though only six members were present; the mover and seconder lines are blank and none of the three statutory findings is ticked (document text).

RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" and only on one of the stated findings (statute text). The district later filed the document in its Unsealed Minutes share, so the practical effect is limited, but the record as written cannot be accurate in all its parts. Graded MEDIUM as a documentation defect in a required record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b)-(c), II and III (nonpublic motion, grounds, minutes, sealing "taken in public session"); nonpublic minutes; 8.5.26 draft minutes
