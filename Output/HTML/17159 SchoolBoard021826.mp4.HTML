MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for six months with no statutory determination on the record

After the 24-minute nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a), a member moved "to seal the minutes for six months" (1:47:02), Sprague seconded (1:47:05) and the roll call was unanimous (1:47:12). The approved minutes record the same: "Michael Petrin made a motion to seal the minutes for 6 months, Frank Sprague seconded the motion" and "Roll call vote taken by Noelle Kronberg, all present voting in favor". RSA 91-A:3, III requires nonpublic minutes to be disclosed within 72 hours unless, by a recorded two-thirds vote in public session, "it is determined that divulgence ... likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism" (statute text). The vote meets the two-thirds requirement; neither the recording nor the minutes states which of the three grounds the board found. The entry motion itself was in order (Flag 8). The clerk had told the board an hour later that unsealed nonpublic records "have to be posted within 72 hours" (1:55:12). The sealing period will have run in August 2026; whether the nonpublic minutes were then released was not checked.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (72-hour disclosure; sealing by 2/3 recorded vote with a determination on one of three grounds); source note ends 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023; approved minutes in the appendix.

MEDIUM Three years of financial audits (FY2023 to FY2025) still open, past the federal single-audit deadline for the first two if the threshold was met

The chair asked about "the annual audits that we are currently in arrears with" (1:35:30). The business administrator is sending Claremont's trial balances "to the auditors for 20, 23, 24 and 25 concurrently" (1:36:15), will not finish by early March (1:37:14), and says the auditors have "pretty much gone through, all the three years on the Grant side" (1:37:55). Crawford: "It's us that is delaying it" (1:37:42). Under the Uniform Guidance a district that spends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a fiscal year beginning before October 1, 2024 must have a single audit, and the reporting package is due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after year end: March 31, 2024 for FY2023 and March 31, 2025 for FY2024. FY2025 (July 2024 to June 2025) also began before October 1, 2024, so the $750,000 threshold applies and its deadline is March 31, 2026. This page does not have the district's federal expenditure totals, so whether the single-audit requirement applied each year is not established here; the board's own February audit review shows the general-fund work, not the grant work, holding the audits back. State law at the meeting date made the audit permissive (RSA 21-J:19); 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272, effective July 1, 2026, came later and does not apply to this meeting. Mitigation on the record: payroll staffing loss, right-to-know requests and the forensic audit are named as competing work (1:39:00, 1:39:27).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501 (single audit at $750,000; $1,000,000 for fiscal years beginning on or after Oct. 1, 2024); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (due 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); RSA 21-J:19 (audits by independent accountants; permissive)
