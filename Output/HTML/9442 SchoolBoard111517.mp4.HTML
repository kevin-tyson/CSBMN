Claremont School Board, November 15, 2017

Regular meeting, chaired by vice chair Chris Irish, at which a parent questioned kindergarten class sizes at Bluff School, Stevens High School presented its extended learning opportunities, the technology department defended its staffing, and the superintendent handed out a first-draft FY2019 budget that did not meet the board's $1.2 million reduction directive. MEDIUM No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

MAP.md records no Drive material for this meeting: the district's shares hold no year folder older than 2022, and the Meeting Minutes share starts at 2023. The gap matters more than usual here, because the recording also misses the start of the meeting (flag 3), so no surviving record shows the call to order, the attendance or what was done in the first hour. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text in force for 2017, required minutes including "the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting". This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record mitigates the gap: the board approves the November 1 minutes on tape (0:02:00), and the superintendent refers to a memo “that's in your packet” (2:25:23), so the board was producing these documents. The 2017 statute required minutes to be open to inspection, not posted online, so their absence from today's shares does not show they were never available.

MEDIUM Voucher signatures still behind two weeks after the superintendent raised it

On November 1 the superintendent told the board that the weekly voucher signatures had lapsed and needed catching up. Two weeks later he brought “a box full of vouchers” and said only one member had come forward: “The second board member has yet to step up, but we need to have our Bouchier signed by board members. It is a requirement. We are behind.” (1:02:35). Three members volunteered. RSA 197:23-a provides that the treasurer "shall pay out the same only upon orders of the school board or upon orders of the 2 or more members of the school board empowered by the school board as a whole to authorize payments." A backlog of unsigned vouchers suggests payments preceding the orders the statute requires, though the recording does not say whether the vouchers were already paid or awaiting payment. No vote empowering particular members is heard at either meeting. A probable control gap; the treasurer's records would settle it.

Sources: RSA 197:23-a (2017 codification; source note ends 2015, 272:45, eff. Oct. 1, 2015); November 1, 2017 page, flag 3

OBSERVATION "We don't follow the Board of education"

Asked whether Bluff's kindergarten rooms meet the state's minimum size, the superintendent answered “We don't follow the Board of education. We follow the state fire marshal and the Claremont Fire Department.” and “it's called local control.” (0:12:11, 0:12:27). As a general statement this is inaccurate. RSA 186:8 directs that "The state board of education shall adopt rules, pursuant to RSA 541-A, relative to" among other things "Minimum standards for public school approval for all grades of the public schools" (¶II). Those are the Ed 306 rules, which bind the district. Whether Bluff's classes or rooms met the class-size and space provisions in force in 2017 was not verified for this page: the citizen's figure of “1000ft² minimum for a kindergarten classroom or 50ft² per child” is his characterization, and the Ed 306 text of that date was not retrieved. The superintendent's broader point, that class size alone does not measure instruction, is a separate matter of judgment.

Sources: RSA 186:8, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 84:1, eff. July 18, 2016); N.H. Admin. Rules, Ed 306 (minimum standards for school approval; cited as a part, without subsection numbers, because the 2017 numbering was not verified)

OBSERVATION A class-size fix was to be taken to the board in nonpublic session

The superintendent said the administration had studied the Bluff kindergarten for three weeks and “We were going to be presenting to the school board and nonpublic a resolution to this matter.”, adding “We haven't made it public” (0:14:04); the fix would cost money and needs board approval (0:18:34). RSA 91-A:3, II limits nonpublic sessions to listed grounds, among them personnel matters under II(a) and II(b) and reputation under II(c). A decision to add a class or staff, with a budget effect, is not on that list unless it is discussed in terms of particular employees. Whether a nonpublic session was ever held on this, and on what ground, is not on any record this project holds. The superintendent did describe the issue and the planned correction publicly here, in answer to the citizen.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

POSITIVE The board reordered its agenda to protect a citizen's chance to speak

In 2017 no statute required a school board to hold a public comment period at all; RSA 189:74's 30-minute rule dates from 2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022. When a resident objected that moving presentations forward would cost him his chance to speak (0:01:00), the presiding officer asked “What's the will of the board?”, and the board amended the agenda again on Sprague's motion to put citizens' comments first (0:01:51). Pierce had raised the same concern before the first vote (0:00:17). The resident then spoke at length, received answers from the superintendent and members, and was invited to the budget meetings. Later, the presiding officer kept staff email to the board to information only, “As long as we don't get into a dialog” (2:25:49), which is the line RSA 91-A:2-a, II draws against using communications outside a meeting to circumvent the law.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022; not law on this date); RSA 91-A:2-a, II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4)
