Claremont School Board, August 20, 2025

Six days after disclosing the cash crisis, the board met before a crowd that filled the room and an overflow gym. It read a letter from the Commissioner of Education, heard counsel and the district's auditor, and took more than an hour of public comment from about thirty speakers. The business administrator's presentation was struck from the agenda at the start; the superintendent was absent at the board's request; a newly hired comptroller said the books had no beginning balances. The auditor said the last signed audit was FY2021. No motion was made, so school stayed set to open August 28. Built from the dialogue transcript, the amended agenda and the approved minutes. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid: points where the record raises a question under a statute, rule or accounting standard, or where the board did something worth noting. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on.

MEDIUM The auditor confirms FY2021 as the last signed audit and a disclaimer of opinion coming for FY2022

Campo told the meeting that FY2022 was nearly finished and that you're going to have a disclaimer of opinion because the records did not support the numbers that were in the financial statements (0:25:58); asked for the last signed audit, he answered FY2021 (1:27:46). FY2023 had only begun and FY2024 and FY2025 had not (0:24:52). The commissioner's letter asked the district to complete all required financial audits (0:08:03). Federal law sets a deadline only where a year's federal expenditures reach $750,000 (2 CFR 200.501(a)); the single audit is then due within nine months of year end (2 CFR 200.512(a)(1)), March 31 of the following calendar year. This record does not state the district's annual federal expenditures, so the flag stays MEDIUM. State law in force in August 2025 was, as Representative Damon said, permissive: RSA 21-J:19 provides that an audit "may" be done. That changed later: 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), approved July 2, 2026 and effective July 1, 2026, requires the audit report for the last completed fiscal year within nine months of year end and lets the commissioner withhold state grant funding (not adequacy aid) from districts that do not comply. It post-dates this meeting and is cited only as the legislative sequel to the pledge made here (2:01:31, 2:10:59).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2022 edition); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2022 edition); RSA 21-J:19 (permissive); 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), chaptered text, RSA 198:4-d, VIII as reenacted (later law, context only)

MEDIUM The district's books had no beginning balances, so no FY2025 figure presented to the board could be relied on

The new controller: When I looked in the system, there are no beginning balances. You can't rely on the numbers. (2:16:54). Counsel said the amount of the deficit is not known (0:17:31), and the chair said the board had been operating a no accurate data (2:27:33). This bears directly on the August 14 meeting, where the packet's FY2024–25 report showed general-fund spending $1.53 million above budget (see the August 14 page, flag 1): that figure may be wrong in either direction, and RSA 32:8's bar on spending beyond appropriations can only be tested once the year is closed on reliable opening balances. Where a district spends federal awards, 2 CFR 200.302(b)(3) requires records that identify "the amount, source, and expenditure of Federal funds" and 2 CFR 200.303(a) requires "effective internal control over the Federal award" (current eCFR text); a general ledger without beginning balances meets neither in substance. Graded MEDIUM because the cause and extent are not yet documented; the restated opening balances in the FY2022 to FY2025 audits will be the evidence.

Sources: RSA 32:8 (no expenditure beyond appropriation); 2 CFR 200.303 (internal control over federal awards); 2 CFR 200.302(b) (financial management records)

MEDIUM Draft minutes reached the public share on the eighth business day

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (business days exclude national and state holidays). From Wednesday, August 20, the fifth business day was Wednesday, August 27. The draft minutes were last modified and placed in the September 3 packet folder on Tuesday, September 2 (both stamps that afternoon), the eighth business day after the meeting, counting Labor Day as a holiday. This rests on the document's appearance in the district's public share, which shows when the public could see it there but does not rule out earlier inspection on request; that is why the grade is MEDIUM. The 2025, 112:1 amendments to RSA 91-A:2, II took effect on August 22, 2025, two days after this meeting, and are not applied here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (five business days; 2024 codification); Drive metadata for DRAFT CSB Meeting Minutes 8.20.25 (read 2026-10-03)

OBSERVATION A controller hired, a superintendent asked to stay away, a business administrator removed from the finances: none of it decided in a public session on this record

Counsel said he helped the district hire Matt Angell two days before the meeting (0:15:42), that Henry was no longer in charge of the district's finances (0:41:17) and that the superintendent was absent at the request of the board (0:41:28). No public vote between August 14 and August 20 appears in this record; the only intervening session was the August 14 nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a), (c) and (l), whose minutes the board sealed for five years. Counsel also said the SAU 6 board, the employer of Pratt and Henry, had not yet met (0:40:49). Two questions follow and the record answers neither: which body authorized the controller's engagement and on what terms (an expenditure that, under RSA 32:8, has to fit within an FY2026 appropriation), and whether "the request of the board" was a decision of the board as a body or of its chair. Under RSA 91-A:3, III, decisions reached in nonpublic session must be disclosed within 72 hours unless properly sealed. The draft minutes of a Claremont board meeting on August 21, 2025 in the September 3 packet, and the SAU 6 minutes of August 21 and 26, are the next documents to read.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (decisions in nonpublic session disclosed within 72 hours unless sealed); RSA 91-A:2-a, II (communications outside a meeting); RSA 32:8

POSITIVE An extended, orderly public forum well beyond the statutory minimum

RSA 189:74 requires a school board to allow at least 30 minutes for public comment. This board set up a portable PA and an overflow room so the whole crowd could hear (recess 6:40 to 6:44 p.m., minutes), brought in an experienced moderator to run three-minute turns with a sign-in book so the clerk could record names and wards (0:29:20, 0:30:33), let the session run from 0:28:05 to 1:43:42, about 75 minutes and some thirty speakers, extended it twice at the facilitator's request (1:33:59, 1:39:39), had counsel and the auditor answer questions on the spot, and invited the district's state legislators and Executive Councilor to speak. The minutes then summarized every speaker by name and ward.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (public comment, no less than 30 minutes; 2022, 333:1); policy BEDH
