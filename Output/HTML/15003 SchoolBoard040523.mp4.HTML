Claremont School Board, April 5, 2023

First meeting of the board elected on March 14, 2023. Heather Whitney is elected chair and Frank Sprague vice chair; the board accepts the superintendent's postponement of the elementary grade reconfiguration, approves a one-season parent-driving waiver for middle-school athletics, accepts $3,000 in donations, hears the SRVRTC Visioning Committee's action plan, commits $47,900 of ESSER money to a Southern Regional Education Board study after learning that part of a $100,000 allocation went elsewhere, and holds a nonpublic session whose minutes are sealed for five years. MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for five years with no statutory determination, and an exemption the minutes do not specify

RSA 91-A:3, III, as codified for this date, requires nonpublic minutes to be disclosed within 72 hours "unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person" other than a member, render a proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism (statute). The vote here was a public roll call (2:03:11), and the chair noted the two-thirds rule; that part was met. No determination was stated: the motion was "to seal the minutes for five years" (2:03:00), and the minutes record only "Minutes sealed for 5 years from today's date." RSA 91-A:3, I(b) also requires the motion to "state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II." On the recording the chair names two C together with a garbled second citation (2:00:26); the minutes record only "RSA 91-A:3 II", with no lettered exemption. The session itself is not on the recording, and the minutes give no times for it. Two smaller discrepancies: the dialogue file gives the seal motion to the superintendent, who is not a member, where the minutes credit Sprague; and the minutes' seal roll call lists six members and omits Skillen, whose Yes is audible at 2:03:31.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(c) and III (2022 codification; source note ends 2021, 48:7, 163:1, 172:1); draft minutes, item VI

MEDIUM The minutes reached the public share on the seventh business day

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute). The earliest copy located, the draft in the April 19 packet folder, was created on Drive on Friday, April 14, 2023 at about 1:53 p.m.; the PDF's own modified time is about fifty minutes earlier the same day, so nothing dates it sooner. Counting from Wednesday, April 5, the fifth business day was Wednesday, April 12. Drive's created time shows when the public could see the document on the share, not when it was written, and a draft may have been available at the SAU office on request; nothing in the record says so. Because minutes exist and the gap is two business days, this is graded MEDIUM under the project's severity rule for late rather than missing minutes.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes within 5 business days); packet folder 10. CSB 4.19.23 (Drive metadata)

MEDIUM About $47,000 of a $100,000 ESSER activity the board had pointed at the Visioning Committee was spent on another program without the board's knowledge

The committee's vice chair reports that in July 2022 the board allocated "up to $100,000" to the Visioning Committee (1:39:45; he says he reviewed the July minutes, 1:46:25). Asked for the money, the superintendent explains that about $47,000 of the activity paid CTE teachers to run classes for eighth graders, an effort "independent outside of the recommendations of the Visioning Committee" (1:48:06, 1:48:11). The chair says "I don't remember" that use; a member asks what happened to the rest. The figures given for what remains differ within minutes of each other, "about $55,000" (1:46:55) and "$53,000 because 47 has been used" (1:50:00), with the superintendent saying he does not "have exact numbers in my head" (1:50:09). A member says he has asked "several times for a detailed to the penny accounting" of ESSER money without receiving it (1:49:27). The Uniform Guidance requires a recipient to "Establish and maintain effective internal control over the Federal award that provides reasonable assurance" of compliance (2 CFR 200.303(a)). Spending within an approved grant activity may have been lawful under the grant; what the record shows is a board that could not say where its own allocation had gone. The July 2022 minutes were not read for this page.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.303(a) (internal controls, 2023 edition); draft minutes, item V.4

OBSERVATION A $47,900 consulting engagement paid from federal funds, with its price shown only on screen and one other firm considered

The SREB engagement was presented with a slide deck that is in the packet; the price is not in it, and the figure is read aloud as "doing this work is $47,500" (1:40:09) and then as 47,900 (1:45:49); the minutes record $47,900.00. The motion directs a purchase order "tomorrow morning" (1:52:00). Because the money is ESSER, federal procurement standards apply. Above the micro-purchase threshold and below the simplified acquisition threshold, "price or rate quotations must be obtained from an adequate number of qualified sources" (2 CFR 200.320(a)(2)(i)). The record says one other consultant was vetted (1:44:47); whether quotations were obtained and documented is not shown. The board did ask the right questions on scope and competition (1:42:41, 1:44:33).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.320(a)(2)(i) (small purchase procedures, 2023 edition); 2 CFR 200.318(a); SREB CPR Overview handout

OBSERVATION The waiver's "pending legal approval" condition drops out of the motion as finally restated

Horsky's original motion approved the waiver "pending legal approval", to be obtained "by Friday" (0:44:41), and he said a negative opinion would bring it back to the board (0:53:20). The superintendent reported that counsel had advised against the practice the year before (0:47:24). The amendment added a notary and a spring-only limit (0:58:57). Whether the condition survived, and whether counsel reviewed the document, is not in the record available here. The item itself was added to the agenda at the meeting (0:03:38); RSA 91-A:2, II requires notice of the meeting, not of each item, so the addition is not itself a defect. The waiver document reached the packet folder on April 6, the day after the vote.

Sources: Transportation Waiver.pdf; draft minutes, item V.2

OBSERVATION The reconfiguration postponement was decided by consent from a report item, over a point of order, with no motion

The grade reconfiguration came up in the superintendent's report, not as an action item. A member raised a point of order that the board "can't take action upon it" (0:36:03); the chair proceeded by consent, asking that the minutes reflect no objection (0:38:30). The minutes record "postponement passes" with no mover or seconder, because there was no motion. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to name "the members who made or seconded each motion"; a decision by unanimous consent has none to name, so no rule is clearly broken, but the record of a significant policy decision consists of an absence of objection. The superintendent's packet slide states the administrative team's decision as already made. The agenda, the March 1 minutes and the waiver's addition were also taken by consent.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (contents of minutes); Supt Report Claremont 4.5.23.pdf

OBSERVATION The audit was still not final in April 2023

The superintendent's packet report says Michael Campo of the auditors "will present the audit findings when finalized to the board (May meeting)". The record does not name the fiscal year. If it is the audit of the year ended June 30, 2022, and the district spent $750,000 or more in federal awards that year, the Uniform Guidance deadline for the single-audit reporting package, "nine months after the end of the audit period", was March 31, 2023, already past (2 CFR 200.512(a)(1)). This page cannot confirm either condition from tonight's record. The business office was also losing its administrator on April 14 (packet; 0:12:17).

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2023 edition); Supt Report Claremont 4.5.23.pdf
