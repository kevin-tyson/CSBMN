Claremont School Board, June 2, 2021

Regular meeting, the first after the death of board member Carolyn Towle, at which the board appointed Nicholas Stone to her seat, made masks optional at its meetings, heard the superintendent's strategic-plan and federal relief (ESSER) report and the FY2022 federal grant assurances, referred out-of-district middle school athletes to the policy committee, and let the superintendent fill budgeted vacancies over the summer. OBSERVATION Summer hiring delegated to the superintendent, with the board's election of teachers deferred to August

RSA 189:39 provides that "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers employed in the schools in their school administrative unit". The chair described the usual sequence ("he would bring nominations to us", 2:08:41) and the purpose of the motion: "we're going to allow you to defer those nominations to, to August." The motion as amended let the superintendent "fill vacancies" in budgeted positions from June to August 18 (2:09:17, 2:11:22) and carried by voice vote (2:11:30). Read with the chair's explanation, the board kept its election and moved it to August; whether the August meetings in fact took up the summer hires as nominations is the point to check (see the August 4, 2021 page). The superintendent also described relief-funded positions outside the regular budget (2:11:16), and Whitney asked whether the authority reached "additional positions" (2:09:37); the amendment to "budgeted positions" answers her question on the face of the motion.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (superintendents nominate, school boards elect teachers; 1971, 371:2, unamended)

OBSERVATION About $10.6 million in federal relief described as "outside of the budget", with no acceptance record available

The superintendent told the board that "Claremont has almost $10.6 million" in COVID relief to allocate over roughly two and a half more years, "outside of the budget" and "not taxpayer money" (0:31:45, 0:32:08, 0:38:32), and listed outdoor classrooms, playgrounds and summer programming among the uses. In New Hampshire a school board may accept and spend unanticipated money from "a state, federal or other governmental unit" without a district vote only if the district has adopted an enabling article, and for amounts of $5,000 or more "the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken" (RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a), text in force since 2005). This recording does not say whether Claremont adopted the article or whether hearings were held for the ESSER allocations; no agenda or minutes survive to show it. The point is recorded so a reviewer can check the district's hearing notices for 2020 and 2021; it is not a finding that a hearing was missed.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION The assistant superintendent reports a past federal-program citation for missing sole-source documentation

Reading the FY2022 assurances, Magoon said that when she started, a state review found the district "were not doing sole source documents" and "weren't reporting out anything over $10,000", and that "since they came out and cited us on it, we need to make sure that we're following all those procedures" (1:27:06, 1:27:24). Under the Uniform Guidance a non-federal entity "must have and use documented procurement procedures", and a noncompetitive (sole-source) purchase is allowed only in listed circumstances, one being that "The Federal awarding agency or pass-through entity expressly authorizes a noncompetitive procurement in response to a written request" (2 CFR 200.320, opening and (c)(4), 2021 edition). Her account fits that rule and describes the problem as corrected. The citation itself, its date and the grant it concerned are not in any document available for this page, so this is recorded for follow-up, not as a finding. Reading the assurances aloud at a public meeting, as Magoon said the program requires (1:23:13), put the district's federal obligations on the public record.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.320 (2021 annual edition; documented procurement procedures; noncompetitive procurement under (c)(4))

POSITIVE Vacancy advertised, applicant heard in public, appointment voted in open session, statute cited

Carolyn Towle died on May 11, 2021; three weeks later the board filled her seat in open session. The chair placed a newspaper advertisement under past practice, said openly that he "kind of acted unilaterally" and that a special meeting would have been better (1:06:35, 1:07:06), consulted the School Board Association's attorney, and cited the governing statute by number (1:07:23). The applicant spoke during citizens' comments (0:11:19), members debated the process, and the appointment carried on a motion and voice vote with the oath given on camera (1:13:17, 1:13:54, 1:14:10). RSA 671:33, II(a), as it read on June 2, 2021, says "The school board shall fill vacancies occurring on the school board" and that appointees "shall serve until the next district election", which matches the chair's statement that a replacement serves until the next election, not the balance of the term (1:11:23). The chair's paraphrase, that the statute leaves it to the board to "determine how to, replace vacancies" (1:07:23), is looser than the text, which is a duty to fill. The statute does not require advertising or interviews, so the advertisement and public hearing of the applicant go beyond it.

Sources: RSA 671:33, II(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2017, 3:2; next amended 2021, 91:318, eff. July 1, 2021 and 42:1, eff. July 16, 2021)
