SAU 6 Board, September 12, 2024

Regular meeting of the joint Claremont and Unity board, eight of twelve members present. The chair formally announced Christopher Pratt's appointment as superintendent, effective in June; the board approved the April 11 and May 29 minutes, adopted four policies (BEDG on second reading; BBBH-S, DIE and ADC on first reading), heard the FY24 close and the health-plan change, and received the Claremont board's Unity-withdrawal report, which the two Unity members present objected to. No vote was taken on the report. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript, the agenda, the thirteen-document packet and the draft minutes. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on September 12, 2024, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies. SAU 6 policies are cited as the board's own rules, not as law.

MEDIUM This meeting's minutes reached the public share eight weeks late, and it approved two sets that had waited five and three and a half months

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes "promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting", which made September 19, 2024 the deadline for this meeting. The draft minutes' Drive file was created on November 8, 2024 in the next meeting's packet folder (its modified date is November 7), about seven weeks after the deadline. The two sets approved this night (0:01:56, 0:02:12) first appear in this meeting's packet folder, created September 11: the April 11, 2024 draft five months after its meeting and the May 29, 2024 retreat draft three and a half months after. This is graded MEDIUM, not HIGH, under the project's severity rule: the minutes exist, and Drive dates show when the public could read them online, not whether a draft could be inspected at the SAU office earlier. Policy BEDG, adopted this night, itself says drafts of public sessions "must be available within 5 business days of the meeting". Part of the lag follows the board's own schedule: with no June, July or August meeting there was no packet to carry the minutes.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2024 codification; 5 business days); 9.12.24 draft minutes (Drive createdTime 2024-11-08); 4.11.24 draft minutes (createdTime 2024-09-11); 5.29.24 retreat draft minutes (createdTime 2024-09-11); Policy BEDG, §C.3

MEDIUM The revised BBBH-S lets a single member demand a weighted vote, which the statute reserves to a majority

The chair said counsel recommended the new §D sentence "In order for us to be in compliance with RSA" (0:38:35): "Unless a weighted vote is requested by a board member, every school district board member shall be entitled to one (1) vote" (Exhibit C). RSA 194-C:8 says "Weighted votes shall only be used upon the demand of a majority of the members of any board present and voting in the school administrative unit." A policy that lets one member trigger a weighted vote does not match that text, and the statute governs. The same adoption (0:40:44, 0:41:22) added "and benefits" to the board's power to fix salaries in §E.3 on Sprague's reading that the RSA "says fixes salaries and benefits" (0:40:18); RSA 194-C:5, III has the board "fix the salaries of all school administrative unit personnel" and "apportion the expense of the salaries and benefits among the several districts", so benefits appear in the apportionment clause, not the fixing clause. Both changes were adopted on a first reading, and the policy can still be corrected before it settles.

Sources: Exhibit C, policy BBBH-S revision; RSA 194-C:8 (weighted votes on demand of a majority); RSA 194-C:5, III (salaries fixed; salaries and benefits apportioned)

MEDIUM Two policies adopted this night rest on statutes that do not say what the policies say

Policy DIE, adopted on first reading (0:42:11, 0:42:59), says it is established under "RSA 197:25 and RSA 671:5" and that the audit report shall be "filed with the New Hampshire Department of Education as required by RSA 671:5". RSA 671:5 provides for electing district auditors and contains no Department of Education filing requirement; RSA 197:25 is a school-district fallback for locally elected auditors when a district has not hired one under RSA 21-J:19, not an SAU provision. The project's research located no statute requiring an SAU to be audited annually before July 1, 2026; RSA 194-C:4, II(a) lists audits among the services an SAU provides. Policy BBBH-S §E, readopted with amendments the same night, still says the board provides superintendent services "in accordance with RSA 194:4", which is the district notes-and-borrowing section. Ruggeri's amendment to DIE concerned where its references sit (0:42:29), not what they say. A policy that misstates its legal basis misleads every reader who relies on it.

Sources: Exhibit D, policy DIE draft; RSA 671:5 (district auditors elected); RSA 197:25 (district auditors, fallback); RSA 194:4 (notes of districts); RSA 194-C:4, II(a) (audits among SAU services)

MEDIUM The board's working calendar plans to finish June business "through electronic means", including the superintendent's evaluation results

Exhibit G, the pre-populated agenda the chair presented (0:50:00), reads under June: "NOTE: No June meeting; bulleted items to be finalized through electronic means", and the first bullet is "Review of Superintendent Evaluation Results". The chair said "I don't think we need to vote on it. This is just for our own working efficiency." (0:51:29). RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires a public body to deliberate only in meetings held under RSA 91-A:2, II or III, and ¶II says communications outside a meeting "shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". Finalizing board business by e-mail is the practice that provision addresses. The June 2024 meeting itself was not held (0:36:43). This is graded MEDIUM because it is a plan in a board document, not a decision shown to have been taken outside a meeting; the board did not adopt the document by vote.

Sources: Exhibit G, proposed recurring agenda items 8.25.24; RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2008, 303:4; deliberation only in meetings)

OBSERVATION No SAU audit was paid for in FY24, and the board asked only now for a timeline

Exhibit A shows "Business Office Auditors" budgeted at $10,000 with $0.00 spent and the full $10,000 encumbered; Henry kept it because "that's something we're going to spend. I just don't know when." (0:16:20). The chair put a "timeline for audit reporting by the auditors, the SA six audits" on the November agenda (1:25:12). The board's own policy BBBH-S §J says "The SAU Board shall authorize an audit of the SAU accounts annually", and DIE, adopted this night, says "We have to have this annual audit" (0:41:53). No located statute required an SAU audit on this date (RSA 194-C:4, II(a) lists audits as an SAU service; the 2026 law that brings SAUs under an audit mandate took effect July 1, 2026), so this is an observation measured against the board's own policies.

Sources: Exhibit A, financial statement 6-30-2024; policy BBBH-S §J; RSA 194-C:4, II(a); 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), approved July 2, 2026, eff. July 1, 2026, RSA 198:4-d, VI (SAUs audited)

OBSERVATION SAU staff health plans were changed before the board was told, and the board learned it with an apology

The superintendent and business administrator moved SAU office staff to Health Trust plans and then apologized for doing so without the board: "assumptions get us in trouble" (0:31:21); "I used to have the authority as well to change plans." (0:32:03). The SAU board fixes the salaries of SAU personnel under RSA 194-C:5, III, and its policy §E.3 as amended this night would have it fix "salary and benefits" (0:41:16). The record does not show a board vote on the change at this or any earlier meeting. Pratt's statement that "by law, we have to provide equitable" coverage between groups (0:26:50) is his characterization; this page has not verified a rule to that effect. The change was disclosed in public and costed (0:28:54), so this is an observation about who decides employee benefits, not a finding.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, III; policy BBBH-S §E.3

Sources: Exhibit H, report of 12 September 2024; RSA 194-C:9, I

POSITIVE A minutes policy with a schedule for reviewing sealed minutes, and the review started

The chair announced that the policy committee would review the sealed-minutes log on October 2 and recommend which minutes to unseal (0:45:03). Acting on the review before any deadline arrives exceeds the statutory minimum.

Sources: Exhibit B, policy BEDG; RSA 91-A:3, IV (review of sealed minutes; 2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023)
