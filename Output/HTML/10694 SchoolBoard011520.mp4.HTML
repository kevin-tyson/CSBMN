Claremont School Board, January 15, 2020

Regular meeting given over mostly to the administration's first public presentation of the FY2021 budget ($34.7 million local, about $38 million with grants), its warrant articles and default budget, and the superintendent's proposal to reorganize the three elementary schools by grade level. OBSERVATION The teachers' agreement has three different first-year figures in eight days

On January 8, ratifying the Sugar River Education Association agreement, the business administrator said its cost was "approximately $680,000" and "in the first year it's a 680. In the second year it's in the 340 000" (show 10683; January 8 page). Tonight the same agreement's impact in the budget is "$403,000" (0:18:47), and "the first year impact on that is the 403,000." (0:19:38), while the default budget removes it as "a reduction of $421,000" (0:46:36). The figures may measure different things (total cost against net change after turnover, or salary only against salary and benefits), but none of the three statements says so. RSA 273-A:3, II(b) sends "Only cost items" of the agreement to the voters, and because this agreement sits inside the operating budget rather than its own article, a voter sees its price only in presentations such as this one and the default-budget arithmetic. A reviewer would want the cost-item worksheet the board ratified.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) (cost items to the legislative body; source note ends 2013, 244:1)

OBSERVATION Board and business administrator disagree on whether the one-time state aid is restricted

Whitney says the state "designed" the one-time money "specifically for capital improvement projects" and that it "really can't be used necessarily to offset a tax rate" (0:36:40). Seaman answers that, from his conversations with the state, "those dollars are considered unrestricted" and were "rolled into the adequacy aid" (0:36:53 to 0:37:21). The amounts named are $322,904 in free and reduced additional aid and $2,583,000 in fiscal capacity disparity aid (0:27:32, 0:28:04). This page has not read the state law that created those grants, so it cannot say which characterization is right; each is labeled as the speaker's. The answer decides whether the $1.5 million capital line and the two $500,000 reserves were a choice or a requirement. The enacting law, or NHDOE's FY2021 aid notice to the district, would settle it.

Sources: the recording (above); no statute verified for this flag

OBSERVATION The February 23 retreat set tonight was held and minuted, but its draft minutes were challenged as incomplete

The chair says a retreat is required "by policy" (1:23:41), and the board fixes Sunday, February 23 at 10 a.m. (1:29:01), to "be a reflection on our goals" (1:25:57). A retreat at which a quorum discusses board business is a meeting: RSA 91-A:2, I defines one as "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body ... for the purpose of discussing or acting upon a matter or matters over which the public body has supervision, control, jurisdiction, or advisory power", and RSA 91-A:2, II then requires notice and minutes with "a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions". The board treated it as one: on March 4, 2020 a member moved to accept "the minutes of February 23rd, 2020", and another member objected that "the notes were not complete. There was a lot of things missing", so they were tabled (that meeting's transcript, show 10768: Dialogue/10768 SchoolBoard030420.mp4.CSV, rows 18 to 29). MAP.md holds no recording of the retreat, and whether it was noticed cannot be checked from here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification)

POSITIVE The default budget was explained line by line, a week before the hearing the statute requires

The business administrator built the default budget down from the proposed budget and named each reduction and its amount: the one-time $1.5 million of capital work, the teachers' agreement, which voters had not yet approved ($421,000), about 33 vacant paraprofessional positions eliminated in the proposed budget ($759,000), and $20,500 of debt service, with the resulting tax effect (0:46:11 to 0:48:42). Those are the categories RSA 40:13, IX(b) uses (one-time expenditures, obligations, and "salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget"). RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires the default budget to be "disclosed and presented for questions and discussion at the first budget hearing"; presenting it at a televised meeting a week earlier, and taking a board member's plain-language question on what a default vote would cost (0:49:53), goes beyond that minimum. The vacant positions were vacant, and the record does not say whether any was under recruitment, which IX(b) would exclude.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 192:2)
