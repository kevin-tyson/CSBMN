Claremont School Board, FY24 Budget Public Hearing, January 17, 2023

One recording of four back-to-back sessions at the Sugar River Valley Regional Technical Center: the SAU 6 board's FY24 budget hearing and meeting, then the Claremont School Board's FY24 budget hearing and board meeting. The Claremont board heard a $38,345,312 proposed budget, failed to advance it on a secret ballot the minutes record as 3 to 3, and then sent $37,345,312, a $1,000,000 cut with no line items identified, to the deliberative session. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript, the posted agenda and budget summary, and the draft minutes. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on January 17, 2023, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies. RSA 91-A:2 is cited from its 2022 codification (source note ending 2018, 244:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2019), the text in force until 2023, 188:1 took effect on October 3, 2023.

HIGH The board took its budget vote by secret ballot

After a voice vote the chair counted four to two (2:35:46) was disputed and a roll call was abandoned after two answers (2:36:36), Horsky moved "to have a private ballot" (2:39:24), Whitney seconded, and Jeff Small collected paper votes (2:40:41). The draft minutes record: "Steven Horsky motions to hold a secret ballot; seconded by Heather Whitney; Secret ballot vote is 3 yes, 3 no." The question was whether to send the $38,345,312 operating budget to the deliberative session; on a tie it failed, and the $37,345,312 figure that went to the voters followed directly. RSA 91-A:2, II provides: "Except for town meetings, school district meetings, and elections, no vote while in open session may be taken by secret ballot." A school board meeting is none of the three. No member or administrator raised the rule on tape. How each member voted on the motion that decided the size of the operating budget is therefore not in the public record. Mitigation: the motion to use the ballot was itself made and seconded in public, and the next motion was decided by a public show of hands.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification; secret ballot barred in open session except town and school district meetings and elections); draft 1.17.23 CSB meeting minutes

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting." Five business days from Tuesday, January 17 ran to Tuesday, January 24. Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification; minutes open to inspection within 5 business days)

MEDIUM The approved minutes depart from the recording on the budget figures, the final tally and a seconder

The February 1 board approved these minutes "as presented". Against the recording: (1) the minutes state both motions as "thirty eight million three hundred forty five thousand three hundred and thirteen dollars" and "thirty seven million three hundred forty five thousand three hundred and thirteen dollars", where Sprague's and Horsky's motions and the posted summary say $38,345,312 and $37,345,312 (2:42:20); the chair himself says "$37,345,313" after the vote (2:43:53), which may be where the error began, and the warrant prints $37,345,312. (2) The minutes record the final motion as "all in favor via show of hands with Whitney Skillen not voting"; the chair announced "I had 5 to 1." (2:43:46), and the district's own February 1 presentation describes it as "motion voted (5-1)". (3) The minutes name Bonnie Miles as seconder of adjournment; the chair says "seconded by Heather" (2:49:30). (4) The minutes omit the first voice vote, its disputed four-to-two count, the re-vote motion and the abandoned roll call. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to record "final decisions" and "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion". A one-dollar error in the figure sent to the voters, and a tally that differs between the record and the minutes on the decisive budget vote, are corrections the approving body should have made.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification; minutes contents, movers and seconders); 1.CSB Meeting Minutes 2.1.23 (approval "as presented"); Budget Reduction Emergency Action ... for CSB Meeeting 2.1.23 ("voted (5-1)")

OBSERVATION The detailed budget posted for the hearing was a superseded version

The hearing folder's Claremont Proposed FY 24 Budget (uploaded about 12:39 p.m. on the hearing day) shows the tech center at $1,359,697, up $321,054, annotated "Includes new programs". The one-page Claremont Budget Summary 1.17.23.pdf uploaded that afternoon, and Seaman's presentation, put the tech center at $1,133,625, up $94,983, with the new programs removed. Putnam read the old figure at the podium (1:33:34); Seaman: "So we clearly need to coordinate better." (1:34:40). Mary Lovett said the document was "very hard to read through" (1:56:18). RSA 32:5, I requires a district that budgets by sub-account to "make that data available for public inspection at the public hearing". Whether a current line-item version was available in the room is not shown, so this is an observation; what the public share holds for this hearing is line-item data that does not match the budget heard.

Sources: RSA 32:5, I (2022 codification; sub-account data available at the hearing)

OBSERVATION A $1,000,000 cut sent to the voters with no line items, on top of $754,470 not yet identified

The budget heard already carried "a total reduction of $754,470" that the administration expected to find during the year (1:05:20). Horsky's $1,000,000 motion (2:42:20) identified no accounts; immediately after the vote Seaman asked "where you'd like that million dollars allocated" so the MS-26 and default forms could be completed (2:46:07), and the board gave no voted direction. A post-hearing reduction is lawful: RSA 32:5, I directs the body to "finalize the budget" after testimony closes, and ¶II bars only inserting amounts not disclosed at the hearing. The observation is about information, not legality: the voters received a figure whose composition was unknown on the night it was set. The $37,345,312 sent forward is also $2,364 above the $37,342,948 default shown in the hearing summary, so the board's figure and the default nearly coincided. The February 1 page records the administration's reduction plan.

Sources: RSA 32:5, I and II (2022 codification; finalize after testimony; bar on later insertions)

OBSERVATION No notice of the budget hearing is in the packet folder

RSA 40:13, II-a(a) set the second Tuesday in January (January 10, 2023) as the last day to post notice of budget hearings under RSA 32:5, and RSA 32:5, I requires 7 days' notice. The hearing itself was on time: II-a(c) required it "on or before the third Tuesday in January", which January 17, 2023 was. The Claremont folder holds the agenda (uploaded about 4:51 p.m. on the hearing day), the summary, the line-item budget and the January 9 minutes, but no public notice; the February 1 folder, by contrast, holds its notice ad. A notice may have been posted elsewhere, so this is an observation; the notice would settle it.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a(a) and (c) (2022 codification; SB 2 January calendar); RSA 32:5, I (7 days' notice)

OBSERVATION The SAU 6 portion: a budget adopted after January 1, a hearing nearly closed before its noticed hour, and a disputed figure in its minutes

The SAU 6 board is a separate body; its records were not reviewed for this page. On the recording: the SAU 6 hearing began and was about to close when Gary Merchant objected that the noticed hearing had to stay open until 7:00 (0:21:32); the chair agreed and invited comment again after 7:00 (0:24:57). The chair later said the January 3 SAU minutes "appeared to be wrong" on the budget figure (0:49:09) after the same minutes had been approved with a different correction (0:27:31). The board then adopted a $2,396,000 SAU budget (1:01:07). RSA 194-C:9, I provides: "At a meeting held before January 1, the school administrative unit board shall adopt a budget", and requires certification of each district's share "Prior to January 15".
