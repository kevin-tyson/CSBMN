Claremont School Board, January 6, 2021

Regular board meeting held in person and by video during the COVID-19 state of emergency, opening with a public hearing on a $5,000 gift to special education. The board accepted the gift, sent the proposed fiscal year 2022 budget to its public hearing, and voted to support the administration's move to remote learning from January 8 through January 19, 2021. HIGH The budget subcommittee met four or five times, and that morning directed eight position cuts, with no notice or minutes of any session on record

Sprague, Benware and Petrin are the board's budget subcommittee (0:36:59). Sprague says it met 4 or 5 times with administrators and building leaders "and we met again this morning and, gave our final marching orders to the administration" (0:38:37); the business administrator describes "our meetings on Wednesday morning" (0:40:16) and says the morning session gave "direction to bring that increase down below at least $1" on the tax rate, after which "we determined today to reduce an additional eight positions" (0:48:31, 0:49:05). RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body" of a school board a public body, so each session at which two of the three met to discuss the budget was a meeting requiring 24-hour notice and minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II. This page applies the HIGH limb of the project's severity rule: a public body met, no notice, agenda or minutes for any session exist in any district share, and nothing on the record explains the absence or says the sessions were noticed. The limit of the evidence must be stated: the district's online shares hold nothing at all from before 2022, so absence there does not by itself prove the records were never made. District files from December 2020 and January 2021 could rebut this flag; none has been found. The December 10, 2020 SAU 6 page records Sprague describing the same series of meetings.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1)

MEDIUM No agenda, notice, budget presentation or minutes for this meeting survive in any district share

The recording is the only public account of a meeting that accepted a gift, sent a budget of about $37.8 million to its hearing and moved the schools to remote learning from January 8 through January 19. RSA 91-A:2, II, in the text then in force, required minutes with members, persons appearing, subject matter, final decisions, and movers and seconders, open to inspection within five business days; paragraph II-b required approved minutes to be posted on the website or a notice of where to review them. This page applies the MEDIUM limb because the record mitigates the gap: at this meeting the board approved its December 16 minutes (0:04:39), so it was producing minutes. The "four page presentation" the business administrator emailed to members (0:40:49) and the notice carrying Emergency Order #12's access information are also missing.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II and II-b (2019 codification); Emergency Order #12

MEDIUM Three $500 donations were presented without agenda notice, and no vote to accept them is heard

The superintendent described three gifts "below the $5,000 threshold", said "I didn't have them expressly on on the agenda" (0:08:12), and asked to "accept those" (0:08:40). RSA 198:20-b, III(b), in the text then in force, provides: "For unanticipated funds in an amount less than $5,000, the school board shall post notice of the funds in the agenda and shall include notice in the minutes of the school board meeting in which such funds are discussed. OBSERVATION Retained fund balance is described at 2.5% and as available to use next year; the statute had just changed

The business administrator said the district has "historically maintained about a $400,000 surplus, and we put aside 2.5% as a part of the statute this year" (0:53:13). RSA 198:4-b, II was amended by 2020, 38:25, effective September 27, 2020: the legislative body may authorize retention of year-end unassigned general funds up to 5 percent of the net assessment (before that, 2.5 percent), and "Prior to expending retained general funds, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing" with newspaper notice. The 2.5% figure fits the older cap or a lower voter authorization; which applies, and whether any use of retained funds next year will be preceded by the required hearing, cannot be told from the recording. Under GASB Statement 54 such amounts are unassigned unless formally committed or assigned. The audited fund-balance note for fiscal 2020 would settle the classification.
