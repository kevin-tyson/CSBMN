MEDIUM A grant of about $35,000 accepted the night it was added to the agenda, with no public hearing on the record

The chair added “Acceptance of Grant” to the agenda at the start of the meeting (0:04:32). The assistant superintendent described a Department of Health and Human Services award “to the tune of mid 30,000” and asked that the board accept it (2:06:19); the board did so by voice vote (2:07:51). She called it “a different funding source than I've worked with in the past”, so it does not read as money already in the adopted budget.

RSA 198:20-b lets a board accept unanticipated money from a governmental unit only where the district has adopted the enabling article, and for "unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with newspaper notice at least 7 days before (statute text). No hearing is mentioned on the recording, and the item's addition to the agenda that night makes a noticed hearing at this meeting unlikely. MEDIUM because the facts point to a process gap but the record does not exclude a hearing held elsewhere or an acceptance under some other authority; the notice of hearing, if any, and the district's RSA 198:20-b warrant article would settle it.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2016 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)

OBSERVATION Roof contract approved at $9,500 above the warrant article, with no funding source stated

The business administrator told the board “There is a warrant article we did for 100,000” and recommended the $109,500 bid (0:52:14); the board approved it by voice vote (0:58:32). Nobody on the recording says where the other $9,500 comes from. RSA 32:8 bars a school board from agreeing to pay "for any purpose in excess of the amount appropriated by the legislative body for that purpose" except as RSA 32:9 to 32:11 allow (statute text). RSA 32:10 lets the governing body transfer unexpended balances into an appropriation, but its paragraph I(d) says an amount appropriated under a special warrant article "may be used only for the purpose specified in that article and shall not be transferred" (statute text), and paragraph I(b) requires records showing what was transferred.

The contract is lawful if a transfer from another line covered the difference, so this is an observation, not a finding. The documents that would settle it are the 2016 warrant article (to see whether it was a special warrant article and how it was worded) and the FY2017 transfer records.

Sources: RSA 32:8 (2016 codification; 1993, 332:1); RSA 32:10, I(a), I(b), I(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2004, 113:1)

OBSERVATION Background-check questions raised at first read, checked against the statute's text

The board debated a draft policy written to the July 2016 amendment of RSA 189:13-a and removed one paragraph pending a check of the law (1:56:35). The enacted text, read for this page, bears on three points raised. First, the statute requires a check of "every selected applicant for employment" before "a final offer of employment" and contains no provision for re-checking current employees, which matches the superintendent's “I don't think it is” and supports the redaction. Second, the district "may require the selected applicant for employment or designated volunteer to pay the actual costs" of the check, so the applicant-pays practice the chair asked about (1:57:10) is authorized. Third, the statute itself gives discretion: "The superintendent ... may deny a selected applicant a final offer of employment if such person has been convicted of any crime in addition to those listed" (statute text, ellipsis marks the omitted words). The attorney's advice relayed at 1:42:11 would make the policy stricter than the statute; that is a policy choice open to the board, not a legal requirement.

On timing: Barry describes a signing in the governor's office “this afternoon”, while the statute's source note dates the amendment to 2016, 117:1, effective July 19, 2016, the date the superintendent gives (1:39:50). The afternoon event was presumably ceremonial; the record does not say.

Sources: RSA 189:13-a (2016 codification; source note ends 2016, 117:1, eff. July 19, 2016)
