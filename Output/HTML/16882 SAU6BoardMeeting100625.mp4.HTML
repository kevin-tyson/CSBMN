MEDIUM No minutes of this meeting exist in any district share

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be “promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting,” naming the members, persons appearing, the mover and seconder of each motion, and, from August 22, 2025, the start and end time and the “name of the person who produced the minutes.” An SAU board is a public body under RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d). The chair announced at 0:00:57 that the clerk “who normally takes the minutes” was on leave, and no one was designated in her place. Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025; text read 2026-09-25); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (school administrative units are public bodies); folder 11. SAU6 10.6.25.

MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for five years with no statutory ground stated in public

Under RSA 91-A:3, III, nonpublic minutes “shall be publicly disclosed within 72 hours of the meeting, unless, by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism” (statute). The recorded vote was taken in public (0:06:55), but the only ground spoken was the source of the advice: “on advice of our attorney, Sarah Friedman, to seal the minutes for five years” (0:06:45), and Ruggeri’s vote “per the attorney” (0:07:12). No member said which of the statutory determinations the board was making. Mitigation: the session was entered under II(a) and II(c), and the reputation ground is one of the statute’s own; counsel may have identified it; and the determination may be recorded in the sealed minutes. Because no public minutes exist, the public record contains no determination at all.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, II(a), II(c), III (2023, 189:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023; text read 2026-09-25).

OBSERVATION The posted agenda named the emergency-functions exemption; the board meant the legal-advice exemption

The agenda heads its nonpublic item “RSA 91-A:3, II (a, c & i)” and then prints the text of (a), (c) and (l) (agenda). Paragraph II(i) concerns preparation for emergency functions; II(l) is “Consideration of legal advice provided by legal counsel” (statute). The chair corrected the letter from the chair at 0:01:27, before any motion, and the motion at 0:05:31 named (a), (c) and (l). RSA 91-A:3, I(b) attaches the specific-exemption requirement to the motion, which was correct; no verified rule governs how an agenda must cite an exemption. The point is that a reader of the posted notice saw two different third grounds, and the correction reached only the room.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(i), II(l); SAU6 School Board Agenda 10.6.25.

Kennedy reported at 0:03:53 and 0:04:14 the resignations of the HR director (effective October 17), a second HR employee (effective October 1) and the assistant superintendent (effective October 20), leaving “Mr. Angel and me” (0:04:53). Laws ch. 272). No verified rule sets SAU staffing levels. Recorded as a capacity observation.

Sources: RSA 194-C:4, II(a); 2026 N.H. Laws ch. 272 (SB 586), approved July 2, 2026, eff. July 1, 2026 (post-dates this meeting; cited only to show no earlier SAU audit deadline).

POSITIVE A board in a financial crisis held its training in public and put its own failures into the questions

The chair said the session was to clarify roles “for us, as well as for the public” (0:07:27), and it was broadcast. Members used it to state what had gone wrong: Crawford on the annual audit (0:53:12) and on reports requested and not received (0:54:09); Whitney that the board “were receiving reports that was not reflective of our actual, financial situation” (0:57:12); Crawford that members sign the MS-25 without the means to verify it (0:59:18). Sources: RSA 189:39 (superintendents nominate and school boards elect all teachers); the recording at the timestamps above.
