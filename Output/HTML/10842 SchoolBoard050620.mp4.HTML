Claremont School Board, May 6, 2020

First recorded Claremont board meeting held remotely during the COVID-19 closure, by video conference and telephone. The superintendent opens an organizational meeting at which Frank Sprague is re-elected chair and Heather Whitney elected vice chair, both unanimously. Two public hearings: an $8,877.67 New Hampshire Charitable Foundation gift to the Stevens library and a $325,000 transfer from the Special Education Trust Fund for an autism program and social workers. The board sets the last day of school at a half day on June 5 (5-2), rejects a raise in the clerk's meeting pay (2-5), and hears that the FY2018 audit is still unfinished. Built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on May 6, 2020, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule settles the point.

HIGH The FY2018 audit was still unfinished in May 2020, past the federal single-audit deadline, and the business administrator said the district had not met that deadline "in a number of years"

Seaman told the board "Right now we are working with Nick on our fiscal 18 audit." (1:58:51) and that the FY2019 single audit "needs to be delivered to the federal clearinghouse on our on our federal grants", adding that meeting the deadline "will be the first time that we will have met that in a number of years." (2:00:35). Under 2 CFR 200.501(a), an entity that "expends $750,000 or more during the non-Federal entity's fiscal year in Federal awards must have a single or program-specific audit conducted for that year", and under 200.512(a)(1) the audit and reporting package must be submitted "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period" (regulation text).

For the fiscal year ended June 30, 2018 the nine-month date was March 31, 2019. OMB Memorandum M-20-17 (March 19, 2020), item 13, let entities that had not yet filed and had fiscal year-ends through June 30, 2020 delay submission "to six (6) months beyond the normal due date" (memo text); for FY2018 that is September 30, 2019, already past when the memo issued. On the administrator's own account the FY2018 audit was at least thirteen months overdue at this meeting, and the district had missed the deadline in earlier years.

Two cautions. The page assumes the FY2018 audit is a single audit; Seaman ties the single audit expressly to FY2019, and the district's federal spending (school meals, Title I, special education) makes FY2018 very likely to cross the $750,000 threshold, but the record here does not state it. And the delay was the district's and its auditor's together; Seaman says the district is pushing the firm. The audit firm is named only as Nick and "Platnick" in the transcript; the attribution report identifies it, as a hypothesis, as Plodzik & Sanderson.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2019 annual edition): report due earlier of 30 days after auditor's report or nine months after period end; 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2019 annual edition): single audit at $750,000 federal expenditures; OMB M-20-17 (Mar. 19, 2020), item 13: six-month single-audit extension

OBSERVATION A caller asked whether trust money could pay for social workers; the trust's stated purpose is not in the record, and the cost breakdown was given three different ways

RSA 198:20-c, I lets a district create expendable trust funds "for specific purposes" and provides that "Expenditure from such trust funds shall be made only for the purpose for which the trust fund was established" (statute text). Adrian asked whether the district was "using money for positions that was not part of the original description to the voters" (0:26:11). The superintendent answered that the autism program matches what voters were told and that only "$60,000 for, for one social worker at the elementary" is new (0:27:56), but he had described the social workers as serving "the entire school population at each level" (0:14:16). Whether that falls within the purpose voted in the fall of 2019 turns on the warrant article's wording, which this recording does not contain.

The figures also shift: "a 190 zero" contract with "approximately 20,000 of other materials" (0:13:08); then 190,000 plus "$20,000 for supplies and equipment" (0:23:01); then "10,000 for equipment and furniture" with paraprofessional and social-worker lines that do not sum cleanly to $325,000 as transcribed (0:47:52). The board approved $325,000 without a written breakdown on the record; the superintendent promised "a complete accounting" of any unused balance.

Sources: RSA 198:20-c, I-II (2019 codification; source note ends 2012, 219:2, 3)

POSITIVE Both statutory hearings were held before the board acted, and both were re-run when the broadcast feed came back

The board heard the $8,877.67 gift and the $325,000 transfer before voting on either, took one telephone comment, and, after counsel reported "the community TV has just joined" (0:19:56), re-opened both hearings "just in case" (0:20:04). POSITIVE The first remote meeting followed the remote-meeting rules the emergency order left in place
