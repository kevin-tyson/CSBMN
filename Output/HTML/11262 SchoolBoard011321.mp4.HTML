HIGH The default budget was not disclosed or presented at the first budget hearing

This was the public hearing on the FY 2021–22 budget. One week earlier, on the 1/6/21 recording (show 11175, dialogue row 324), Benware moved the proposed budget to the public hearing, which makes this the first budget hearing of the cycle. At 0:17:19 the assistant superintendent for finance told the hearing “I haven't quite concluded the calculation of the default budget”, offered only that it “will be comparable to the recommended and proposed budget”, and said he would finish it “over the course of tomorrow or the day after”. No default figure was given, and the chair closed the hearing at 0:21:26.

RSA 40:13, XI(a), as in force on this date, reads: “The default budget shall be disclosed and presented for questions and discussion at the first budget hearing held pursuant to RSA 32:5 or RSA 197:6.” (quoted from the statute). A default budget described as not yet calculated was neither disclosed nor open for questions. Mitigation on the record: the warrant article read at the February 1 information session states a default of $37,762,398, and Seaman explained its calculation and its $24.50 tax rate at the February 1 information session (show 11276, dialogue rows 129 to 136). That cures the voters’ information, not the hearing requirement. No member of the public spoke at this hearing, so no question went unanswered on the record.

Sources: RSA 40:13, XI(a) and II-a(c) (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 192:2, the text in force in January 2021); Dialogue/11175 SchoolBoard010621.mp4.CSV (1/6/21 motion); Dialogue/11276 SchoolDistrictMeeting020121.mp4.CSV (2/1/21 default-budget presentation).

OBSERVATION Candidate-filing information given on the record was inconsistent

At 2:08:04 the clerk announced filing for the three expiring seats “anytime between January 20th to the 28th”. RSA 671:19 applies RSA 669:19 to school district elections, and RSA 669:19 sets the filing period from “the seventh Wednesday” through “the Friday of the following week” before the election (statutory wording). For the March 9, 2021 election that is Wednesday, January 20 through Friday, January 29. On January 27 the clerk gave the closing day as Friday, so the record corrects itself; the January 28 date stated here was one day short.

The chair also reported that the newspaper notice listed two seats where three expired (2:08:58); the clerk said the paper “got the information wrong” because Whitney “was was appointed for a two year term”. RSA 671:33, II(a) provides that board appointees serve only until the next district election, and the project roster records Whitney as elected in March 2019, which fits Benware’s account that she “took the remainder of, the other” [term]. The record does not settle which; no published notice is on hand to compare.

Sources: RSA 671:19 (2020 codification); RSA 669:19 (2020 codification; source note ends 2011, 40:1); RSA 671:33, II(a) (2019 codification); Dialogue/11216 SchoolBoard020321.mp4.CSV (1/27/21 filing remark).

OBSERVATION The board replaced the floor deliberative session with a board-amended warrant; the statute itself was not read for this page

Under the House Bill 1129 process as the district’s attorney described it, voters submit comments in writing and the board members “actually act on behalf of the voters in amending the budget”; the 2/1/21 session announcement adds that no live comments from voters would be allowed at either virtual meeting. Benware objected that “there is no deliberation any longer”, and the motion carried 6–1. The voters kept a check: the first ballot question asked whether they approved the process, and Upton said a disapproval leaves the board to convene another meeting before September 1 or adopt the prior year’s budget (0:33:35).

This page states the process only as the attorney and the district described it. The enacted text is 2020 N.H. Laws ch. 8 (HB 1129); its chaptered text could not be read from this environment, so no compliance question is graded here. The attorney’s statement that he originated the process is his own characterization.

Sources: HB 1129 (2020), bill status page (chapter 8; chaptered text not read); Dialogue/11276 SchoolDistrictMeeting020121.mp4.CSV.
