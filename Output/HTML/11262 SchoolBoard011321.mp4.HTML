Claremont School Board — January 13, 2021

Remote budget hearing and board meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, notice or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The board held its public hearing on a $37,785,406 budget for 2021–22 (a proposed tax rate of $24.53 against $23.93) with no public speakers and without a default budget, which the business administrator had not finished calculating; sent the budget unanimously to the annual meeting; voted 6–1 to replace the in-person deliberative session with the virtual procedure of HB 1129; and, facing the district's first in-school COVID cluster and 27 staff out, set a re-evaluation of remote learning for January 27. Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the meeting against the state law in force on January 13, 2021, including the Governor's COVID-19 emergency orders then in effect. They are not findings of violation, and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Six flags follow, ordered by severity.

HIGH The budget hearing was held without the default budget, which the statute requires to be presented at the first hearing

Claremont votes on its budget by official ballot under RSA 40:13. Paragraph XI(a) of that section provides: "The default budget shall be disclosed and presented for questions and discussion at the first budget hearing held pursuant to RSA 32:5 or RSA 197:6." At this hearing the assistant superintendent for finance said at 0:17:19: "I haven't quite concluded the calculation of the default budget", offered only that he anticipated it "will be comparable", and said at 0:18:23 that he would have it "finalized over the course of tomorrow or the day after". The public comment period that followed (0:18:55) therefore had no default figure to ask about, and the board sent the budget to the annual meeting the same night (0:26:41). The figure, $37,762,398, first appears on the record on February 1, when the recording opens with the corrected document being circulated minutes before the session (see the February 1 page, flag 3). Two points narrow the consequence without changing the rule: the default proved only about $23,000 below the proposed budget, and a hearing on January 13 was itself timely under RSA 40:13, II-a(c) ("on or before the third Tuesday in January", January 19 in 2021), which also displaces RSA 32:5's 25-day spacing for official-ballot districts. The flag would fall away only if an earlier budget hearing disclosed the default; nothing in the record suggests one did.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a(c) and XI(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2019, 192:2, so this is the text in force in 2021): hearing deadline; default budget disclosed and presented at the first budget hearing.

POSITIVE Every vote in this remote meeting was taken by roll call, and the chair corrected himself when he began a voice vote

The board met by video conference. When any member participates other than in person, RSA 91-A:2, III(e) as it read in 2021 required that "All votes taken during such a meeting shall be by roll call vote." Emergency Order #12 waived only the physical-quorum requirement of III(b), on public-access conditions. At 0:24:20 the chair began "All those in favor?" and stopped: "Oh, I can't do it that way. Can you roll call, please?" The clerk then called the roll on the agenda, the budget (0:28:07), the HB 1129 procedure (1:05:38), the re-evaluation motion (2:00:43) and the adjournment (2:10:00), so the split vote on HB 1129 is on the record by name. Two weeks later the same board adopted an agenda by voice vote when the clerk was offline (see the January 27 page, flag 1).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(b), (e) (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1): roll-call votes when a member participates remotely; Emergency Order #12 (March 23, 2020, under Executive Order 2020-04).

OBSERVATION The board replaced the voters' deliberative session with a virtual procedure in which only the board could amend, over one member's objection

By 6–1 on a roll call (1:01:47 to 1:06:45) the board adopted the optional annual-meeting procedure of 2020 N.H. Laws ch. 8 (HB 1129). As the district's attorney explained it, voters could send comments and "request an amendment" (0:40:48), but at the second virtual session "the board members actually act on behalf of the voters in amending the budget" (0:30:56). NHMA's guidance on the enacted law confirms that the governing body "shall discuss, debate, and be permitted to amend the posted warrant" and that the first ballot question asks voters to approve the procedure. Under RSA 40:13, IV the first session would otherwise have been the voters' own "explanation, discussion, and debate of each warrant article". Benware's objection is on the record: "Once they these these ideas are all sent in, there is no deliberation any longer." (0:44:18). The case for the change is also on the record: county cases had risen from 95 to 240 in a month (0:49:04), and the moderator said she could not attend in person. The law offered the choice and the board made it openly, so no rule is engaged; the observation is what the choice removed. At the February 4 session no voter submission of any kind was received (see the February 4 page, flag 1). The attorney's statements that drive-up voting is "required by law" and that he originated the process are his own characterizations and were not verified.

Sources: 2020 N.H. Laws ch. 8 (HB 1129), approved July 10, 2020 (bill status; chaptered text not read); NHMA, Guidance on 2021 Annual Meeting (December 4, 2020); RSA 40:13, IV (2019 codification).

OBSERVATION The requested two-week extension of remote learning was never itself voted; the board voted only to re-evaluate, and the sports suspension continued by default

The superintendent asked "the board to continue remote for another two weeks" (1:11:48). The only motion that would have adopted that recommendation, Petrin's, was withdrawn (1:56:50). The motion that passed was to "reevaluate the Covid impact matrix, determine which schools will be safe to open in person on February 1st" (1:59:07); its date, the 27th, was settled in discussion but is not in the motion as finally worded. The assistant superintendent treated remote learning as already required by the matrix the board had approved (1:31:09), and asked afterwards whether sports remained suspended because "That wasn't in the motion." (2:01:29). The board's authority over reopening in 2021 rested on its own earlier decisions and the state's guidance rather than on any statute identified here, so no rule is engaged. For a reader the result is that the continued closure and the sports suspension have no motion of their own on the record; the January 27 meeting then voted by roll call to return (see the January 27 page).

Sources: no statutory rule identified; dialogue rows at 1:11:48, 1:56:50, 1:59:07 and 2:01:29.

OBSERVATION Citizens' comments at the board meeting were forgotten until after the decision they addressed

The hearing took public comment (0:18:55), but none was offered. The board meeting's own comment period was skipped; the chair returned to it at 2:02:08, "since we've ceased our deliberations on that", after the re-evaluation vote. Both speakers then addressed the decision just made: Ray on winter sports elsewhere and Lisa, a third-grade teacher, on young children needing to be in school. No statute required a public comment period at a New Hampshire school board meeting in 2021 (RSA 189:74 was enacted by 2022, 333:1, effective September 6, 2022), and the chair did come back to it unprompted. The observation is the order: comment that could have informed the vote came after it.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (source: 2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022): not in force in 2021.
