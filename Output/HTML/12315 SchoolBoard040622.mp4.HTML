Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer’s aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each names the rule it rests on and the record behind it; where no verified rule applies, the item is labeled an observation. Statutes are cited as they read on April 6, 2022.

MEDIUM A goal-setting retreat of the whole board was scheduled for Sunday, May 1; the record holds no notice, minutes or recording of it

Under the retreat-planning item the chair proposed the board’s customary post-election retreat: “we've been in the habit of having a retreat after the new members are on the board” (2:16:32), held in “the community room across the street” (2:16:54). Members settled on Sunday, May 1, “Ten to noon” (2:18:43), and the chair described its business as setting the board’s goals: “come up with some goals as a board as a whole”, adding “we've had administration there in the past” (2:19:04). Frank Sprague asked that the strategic plan be brought so the goals would align with it (2:19:39). The 5/4/22 recording (Dialogue/12366 SchoolBoard050422.mp4.CSV) has the superintendent referring to what was discussed at the retreat, about 24 minutes into that meeting, so the session took place.

If a quorum attended, as a session of the whole board to set its goals implies, the gathering was a meeting under RSA 91-A:2, I (a quorum convened to discuss matters within the board’s jurisdiction), and needs 24-hour notice and minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II. This project holds no notice, agenda, minutes or recording of a May 1, 2022 session; there is no MAP.md section and no Cablecast show for it. The severity rule applied is the mitigated limb (MEDIUM), not the unmitigated one: the district’s Drive shares do not reach back before July 2022, so a notice or minutes posted elsewhere at the time cannot be ruled out from here. What would settle it is the district’s posted notice for May 1, 2022 and the retreat minutes, if any were kept.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2022 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1: meeting defined as the convening of a quorum; notice; minutes with mover and seconder, open to inspection within 5 business days); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2022 codification)

OBSERVATION The ARP ESSER (ESSER III) allocation was filed with the state a month before the board discussed it; the board took no vote, and the written transition plan members asked for is not in the record

The superintendent presented “the actual submission to the state that we did last month” (0:12:23): after-school and enrichment $320,000, CTE visioning $100,000, curriculum adoptions about $300,000, “1.4 million in that hiring, retention of professional staff” and teacher performance bonuses of $100,000 (0:14:40, 0:15:08, 0:16:16). He reported 196 survey responses (0:12:48) and that “20% minimally of the Esser money has to be used to address specific learning losses” (0:13:27), and said the full document “is posted online right now for the public to review” (1:20:33). Those statements track the federal requirements: consultation and public input, a plan posted on the district website, and the statutory 20 percent learning-loss reservation.

The open question is board oversight. The item was listed for discussion; no motion was made on it. Heather Whitney asked for “I want it in writing, I want to plan” (1:39:47); Whitney Skillen asked “Do we have a risk log. Do we have a transition plan. Do we have a sustainability strategy?” (1:49:51); Steven Horsky asked for a return-on-investment model (1:58:28). The superintendent answered “We can add that to this” (1:50:22). No federal rule located requires a school board vote on an ARP ESSER plan, so this is an observation. The documents that would settle what the board was given are the filed ARP ESSER plan and any written sustainability plan presented at a later meeting.

Sources: 86 FR 21195 (Apr. 22, 2021), ARP ESSER interim final requirements (LEA plan, meaningful consultation, public input, plan posted on the LEA website; ARP Act section 2001(e)(1) 20 percent learning-loss reservation)

OBSERVATION A $1,600 in-kind gift to the tech center was described as already accepted before it came to the board

After the $8,000 vote the superintendent presented a recognition of “about $1,600 worth of materials” for the plumbing and HVAC program (2:15:19) and said “It's already been donated and accepted” (2:16:09); the chair noted the difference from the cash gift was the dollar amount (2:16:15), and the superintendent agreed: “the public hearings for over the 5000” (2:16:19). The donor’s name is garbled in the transcript; the minutes spell it Steven Fiskio.

RSA 198:20-b, III(b) requires that unanticipated funds under $5,000 be noticed in the agenda and minutes and accepted in public session of a regular board meeting. The statute speaks of money; whether a gift of materials falls under it is not settled by the text, so no violation is asserted. The point is narrower: on this record the acceptance happened before the board saw the item, and no board vote was taken on it.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2022 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1: unanticipated money; prior public hearing at $5,000 or more; under $5,000, notice in agenda and minutes and acceptance in public session)

OBSERVATION The only located copy of the minutes was filed 106 days after the meeting, and its masthead lists the board as it stood before the March election

The minutes (CSB Board Public Hearing & Regular minutes 4.6.22.pdf) sit in the packet folder for July 20, 2022, with a Drive creation date of July 21, 2022, 106 days after the meeting. That date shows when this copy reached the public share, not when the minutes were written, and the share holds nothing older than the July 2022 folders, so RSA 91-A:2, II’s five-business-day rule cannot be tested from here.

The text has three defects a reader should know about. The masthead lists the members as chair Frank Sprague, vice chair Heather Whitney, Rob Lovett Jr. and Nicholas Stone among them, which was the board before this meeting; the roll-call line in the body lists the seven members actually seated (Horsky, Lambert, Miles, Petrin, Sprague, Skillen, Whitney). The closing sentence of other business is cut off mid-word, ending at to the boa. And the minutes record Steven Horsky seconding both officer nominations, while the dialogue file gives the only audible second for the chair nomination (“As board chair. I'll second.”, 0:03:21) to the superintendent’s voice cluster; the superintendent is not a member and cannot second, so the minutes’ attribution is the more plausible one. On the positive side, the minutes name a mover and seconder for every motion, which RSA 91-A:2, II has required since January 1, 2019.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2022 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1: meeting defined as the convening of a quorum; notice; minutes with mover and seconder, open to inspection within 5 business days)

POSITIVE The $8,000 scholarship gift was given a public hearing before the board accepted it in public session

The meeting opened with a public hearing on an $8,000 gift from Margaret Cooney to four Stevens High School scholarship funds (0:05:09). The chair took comment, a Ward 2 resident asked its purpose, and the superintendent answered that it funds scholarships “for graduates of Stevens High School” (0:06:28). Two points remain unverified from this record: the seven-day newspaper notice the same paragraph requires, and whether the district had adopted the enabling article RSA 198:20-b, I makes a precondition.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (2022 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1: unanticipated money; prior public hearing at $5,000 or more; under $5,000, notice in agenda and minutes and acceptance in public session)
