Claremont School Board, June 20, 2024

Last regular board meeting of the 2023-24 school year. The board approved the Stevens High School handbook with a move from block to period scheduling, approved a 2024-25 student trip to Puerto Rico, voted $583,000 from the year-end surplus for two roofs, heard SREB's review of the tech center and a cell phone policy survey, formed an ad hoc communication committee and said farewell to the special education director. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on June 20, 2024, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The board voted to "appropriate" $583,000 for roofs, described as coming from fund balance, a power a school board does not have

Crawford's motion, prompted by the chair's "We appropriate", was to "appropriate, 583,000 to cover the, repairs to the roofs" (1:43:25); the approved minutes record "a motion to appropriate $583,000". Petrin described the subcommittee's view as taking it "out of the the fund balance" (1:42:14). Appropriations are made by the district meeting. A school board may not pay or incur liability "for any purpose for which no appropriation has been made, except as provided in RSA 32:9-11"; RSA 32:10 lets the governing body transfer an unexpended balance from one appropriation to another, provided "The total amount spent shall not exceed the total amount appropriated" and no money goes to a purpose the meeting deleted or rejected. Unexpended FY24 appropriations lapse on June 30 unless "encumbered by a legally-enforceable obligation, created by contract or otherwise" before then (RSA 32:7, I), which is why Henry said the purchase order had to issue before June 30 (1:42:53). Read that way, the vote is a year-end transfer and encumbrance of unspent FY24 operating appropriations, which the statutes allow; fund balance itself is what remains after June 30 and is not available to the board. The record does not say which lines were transferred from, and the motion's wording does not match the authority. The arithmetic is also tight: Exhibit D's surplus of $870,035.10 is measured against a budget of $35,300,311.92 into which Henry added the $420,000 the board had promised to return to taxpayers (1:40:25); $870,035.10 less $583,000 is $287,035.10, below $420,000, so the promise depends on the roughly $200,000 of extra revenue and the out-of-district encumbrance releases Henry anticipated (1:45:03). The audited FY24 statements would show the transfers, the encumbrance, and whether the $420,000 survived.

Sources: RSA 32:8 (no expenditure without an appropriation, except under RSA 32:9-11; 1993, 332:1); RSA 32:10, I (2024 codification; transfers by the governing body within the total appropriated); RSA 32:7, I (2024 codification; lapse unless encumbered by a legally enforceable obligation); Exhibit D-Claremont May 2024 Budget Report 6-12-24 (1).pdf (grand total line)

OBSERVATION The agenda promised a collective-bargaining update on the schedule change; none was given before the vote

On June 5 the board tabled the Stevens handbook because counsel wanted to discuss "any impact bargaining" with the union first (see the June 5 page). Tonight's agenda item reads "Discussion concerning proposed period schedule at SHS and update on collective bargaining impact related to schedule change". The chair asked for "the teachers reception to this and the collective bargaining update" (1:19:13); Pratt answered that faculty polls showed "overwhelming support" (1:19:20) and Herrington described a staff survey (1:19:49). Neither said whether counsel had completed the impact bargaining or what it produced, and the minutes record only teacher support. The board then approved the handbook "including the schedule change" (1:12:14, 1:21:21). Bargaining strategy may be discussed outside a public meeting, so the absence of detail is not itself a defect; the observation is that the public record does not show the condition on which the item was tabled being met.

Sources: CSB Agenda 6.20.24.docx.pdf; RSA ch. 273-A (public employee labor relations); RSA 91-A:2, I(a) (bargaining strategy excluded from the definition of a meeting)

OBSERVATION Minutes and committee chairs were settled by consent, including minutes that omit a vote

The chair approved the consent agenda on "Seeing no objection" (0:01:10) although the agenda marks it "(vote required)". The by-laws adopted on June 5 provide for consent-agenda items to be moved "by consent", so this follows the board's own rule. What it approved, without discussion, were June 5 minutes that record no vote on that evening's middle school handbook motion (flag 3 of the June 5 page). Committee chairs for Capital Improvement and the newly permanent tech visioning committee were also settled by "no opposition" and "a consent" (1:50:28, 1:51:13). The by-laws give the chair power to appoint standing committees "with board consent", which covers this. No rule found makes either step unlawful.
