Claremont School Board, September 4, 2024

Regular board meeting, five of seven members present. The board accepted a $10,000 donation for batting cages at Barnes Park, approved the Region 10 career and technical education agreement, struck acting from the Tech Center assistant director's title on a 3–2 vote, heard three options for leading the Tech Center, and received its exploratory subcommittee's analysis of Unity leaving SAU 6. MEDIUM The public packet posts a photograph of the donor's cheque with its bank routing and account line legible

Exhibit A, Exhibit A- Donation from McGee Toyota of Claremont.jpg, uploaded to the public packet folder on August 29, 2024, is a photograph of the business's $10,000 cheque. It shows the drawee bank, the cheque number and the full machine-readable routing and account line at the foot of the cheque. Nothing in the hearing (0:02:05 to 0:05:15) needed those numbers: the agenda, the minutes and the board's motion all state the donor, the amount and the purpose. This page reproduces none of them. RSA 91-A:5, IV exempts "confidential, commercial, or financial information" from disclosure; it permits withholding rather than requiring it, so this is a control weakness in what the district chooses to publish, not a breach of the Right-to-Know law.

Sources: Exhibit A (packet, 3. CSB 9.4.24); RSA 91-A:5, IV (exemption for confidential, commercial or financial information; permissive)

MEDIUM The approved minutes misstate who was authorized to sign the Region 10 agreement, and misreport a figure and a date

Petrin's motion on tape asks "Our school board chair to sign." (0:12:24), which is what the agreement requires: clause XIV.3 calls for an agreement "signed by the chairs of each of the Sending and Receiving District Board", and its signature page carries a notarized block for the "Chairperson, Claremont School Board" and none for a superintendent. The approved minutes record the motion as "to accept the Region 10 agreement and for the Superintendent to sign the agreement". The approved minutes are the board's record of what it authorized, and RSA 91-A:2, II requires them to describe "final decisions". Two smaller departures sit in the same document: the minutes say Unity's share "is only equal to 0.05% of the total Claremont budget", where the chair said "roughly .00 5%" (0:55:43) and the arithmetic is about 0.51% ($193,000 of $38,000,000); and they date the strategic-plan community night "September 26th" where the chair said "September 25th" (1:05:11). The board approved these minutes without correction. Read with flag 7: the same minutes are timely and name every mover and seconder.

Sources: approved minutes 9.4.24, item V.1 and V.3; Exhibit B, Region 10 agreement, XIV.3 and signature page; RSA 91-A:2, II (2024 codification; minutes contents)

OBSERVATION The Region 10 agreement was approved with one typo fixed and other defects left in, including a director clause the board's own options discussion did not meet

The board corrected one "Region 17" reference on page two (0:12:31, 0:13:20). The agreement, read from the packet copy, carries others the discussion did not reach: section IX.2 still sends notice of a program termination to "the Region 17 CTE Advisory Committee"; IX.1 refers to "the provisions of paragraph 19", and the agreement has no paragraph 19; and section VII allocates "The balance of region tuition reimbursement" as "80% Newport, 20% Claremont", which nobody mentioned. The page-two sentence the board fixed paraphrases RSA 188-E:5, I, which speaks of "the reasonable business and industry needs of the area" and names no region. Section III.4 says the receiving districts, Claremont and Newport, "at their own expense, shall employ a full-time Director of CTE". Minutes later the superintendent presented a shared director with Newport, or himself as director of record, as the options (0:21:57, 0:22:44); neither is a full-time director employed for the Claremont center. Pratt described the document as "the annual agreement" (0:05:50); RSA 188-E:1-a, I has a regional agreement "renewed every 4 years and submitted to the commissioner for review and approval", and clause XIV.1 makes it effective for four years. No verified rule makes these drafting defects unlawful, so this is an observation; the commissioner's review is where they would be caught.

Sources: Exhibit B, Region 10 agreement (scanned; read by OCR); RSA 188-E:1-a, I (2024 codification; RCTEA renewed every 4 years); RSA 188-E:5, I (2024 codification; program serves needs of the area)

OBSERVATION The Unity analysis the board sent on to the SAU 6 board stated the wrong basis for Unity's share, and the correction made on tape was not recorded

Exhibit D, the subcommittee's minutes, says: "NOTE: Current percentages are based on student population per district." RSA 194-C:9, I apportions SAU costs "1/2 on the average membership in attendance for the previous school year and 1/2 on the most recently available equalized valuation". Mary Henry said so in substance at the table: "it's based 50% on assessed value and 50% on student ratio" (0:57:45) (the statute's measure is equalized, not assessed, valuation). The minutes do not record the correction, and the board agreed to send the report on to the SAU 6 board: "It's fact. It's data." (1:02:12). The version filed for the SAU 6 meeting of September 12 (Exhibit H there) was revised to read "based on student population and property values per district", nearer the statute but still not its terms. The presentation also left the scope of the 7.8% unsettled (Hawkins: "Whatever comes out of the Dow building"; Henry: "The entire budget.", 0:53:40, 0:53:53), and the chair's "It's minuscule" rested on a percentage stated as .00 5% (0:55:43) that is about 0.51% on her own figures. The subcommittee says it was created at the board's August 7, 2024 meeting; that meeting has draft minutes but no recording in the Cablecast archive. No rule governs what a board subcommittee's analysis must say, so this is an observation; the statute settles the basis.

Sources: Exhibit D, Exploratory Ad Hoc subcommittee minutes of August 21, 2024; RSA 194-C:9, I (2024 codification; apportionment ½ average membership, ½ equalized valuation; ¶IV not yet in force on this date)

POSITIVE A $10,000 gift handled with more process than the statute requires

For unanticipated funds under $20,000, RSA 198:20-b, III(b) requires the board to "post notice of the funds in the agenda" and "include notice in the minutes", and to accept them "in public session of any regular school board meeting". The board did all three and also held a full public hearing with a presenter and questions (0:01:55 to 0:05:30), which the statute requires only at $20,000 or more (III(a)). The threshold has been $20,000 since 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) and III(b) (2024 codification)
