Claremont School Board Finance Committee, December 10, 2025

Televised morning work session of the board's Finance Subcommittee, its first on the FY2026-27 budget: both members (Candace Crawford, presiding, and Heather Whitney), the interim superintendent and the interim business administrator. They reviewed NESDEC's enrollment projection (1,379 students next year, 1,089 in ten years), the payroll effect of a grade-aligned four-school model (about $79,000 over the current year), a three-school model with grades 7-12 at Stevens, and errors found in the district's payroll and health-insurance data and in the FY26 budget's assumptions. No motion or vote was taken. No agenda, packet or minutes for the session was found. OBSERVATION No public comment period; whether the 30-minute rule reaches a subcommittee work session is unsettled

The presiding member told viewers that "the finance committee meetings isn't the place for that discussion" and that input belongs at regular board meetings, while allowing technical questions (0:05:02, 0:06:36); the board chair said the same at the December 3 board meeting. No member of the public was heard, and no one is recorded as being turned away. RSA 189:74 requires the opportunity for public comment, of no less than 30 minutes, "at a meeting of the school board held under RSA 91-A:2", with exceptions only for emergency and nonpublic-only meetings. The statute says "school board", not "committee", and this page has found no authority deciding whether it reaches a subcommittee. The board's own policy BEDH, which the project's working notes describe as requiring comment at all board meetings, was not re-read for this page.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (public comment at school board meetings, at least 30 minutes; 2022, 333:1); Claremont policy BEDH (Public Comment and Participation at Board Meetings)

On the record: the presiding member says the approved FY26 budget contained "incorrect amounts" (0:01:04); the business administrator says it appears to have budgeted health insurance only for filled positions, not vacancies (0:36:52), that eight positions were funded with a zero FTE (0:58:50), that 44 of about 270 insured employees' records need correction (0:24:53), and that premiums were paid for people who had left, in actual spending though not in the budget (0:36:25, 0:36:36). OBSERVATION The default-budget statute supports "redefined" positions, but leaves the determination to the governing body

Angell says positions can be moved within the organization so that converting one type of position to another is not an elimination for default-budget purposes; such a conversion "doesn't exist under the law. I'm making" the move, he says (0:59:15, 0:59:36). RSA 40:13, IX(b) defines the default budget as the prior year's operating budget reduced, among other things, "by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget", and provides that "eliminated positions shall not include vacant positions under recruitment or positions redefined in the proposed operating budget, as determined by the governing body". His reading has textual support. The determination, though, belongs to the governing body (here the school board), not to the business office, and the default-budget form must identify reductions for eliminated positions. With 63 vacancies in the census (0:44:08), how the board classifies them will move the default budget materially. This is a point for the board's later default-budget vote, not a defect at this session.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI(a) (default budget definition; eliminated and redefined positions; in force since 2019, 192:2)
