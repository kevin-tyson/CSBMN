SAU 6 Board, April 11, 2019

Joint board of the Claremont and Unity school districts. The board heard why the district lost National School Lunch Program reimbursement two years running (the second year because a Unity food-service RFP was late, so the application was not complete until January 30, 2019), learned that SAU 6 has no adopted policies of its own and named a three-member policy subcommittee, elected Carolyn Towle treasurer after hearing that cash had gone unreconciled by a treasurer for some time, and voted, with two nays, to wait for Superintendent-elect Michael Tempesta before commissioning an efficiency study. MEDIUM Federal lunch reimbursement was lost two years running, the second time because a required food-service contract was not rebid in time

The acting superintendent said the loss reported in the press covered two years, "not just it's not 460, $60,000 for this year" (0:10:20); the figure as transcribed is garbled, and no exact amount is given on the tape. For 2018–19 the business administrator said an RFP for Unity's food-service contract "was not sent out in a timely fashion" (0:26:40), and the application was not complete until January 30, 2019 (0:28:23). For 2017–18 he pointed to the July 1, 2017 switch to the Abbey Group (0:11:05) and to a change in the state's handling, from "filing them in November before not getting any claims suspended to all of a sudden, you got to go back to the first day of the year to have it filed" (0:16:40). Until a compliance hire in November 2018, claims work "had to go through Mike", which the acting superintendent called "a bottleneck" (0:20:33).

Federal school-lunch rules require a school food authority that contracts with a food service management company to keep signature authority over its agreement with the state agency and over its claims, and limit such a contract to one year with no more than four additional one-year renewals (7 CFR 210.16(a)(5) and (d), 2019 edition), so a rebid falls due on a fixed cycle. The Uniform Guidance requires a recipient of federal awards to "Establish and maintain effective internal control over the Federal award" (2 CFR 200.303(a), 2019 edition). Losing reimbursement in two consecutive years, the second time over a procurement step whose timing the rules fix in advance, is a control weakness on the record. The mitigation stated on the tape is also recorded: a dedicated compliance hire, redistributed permissions "so that not everything falls to one person" (0:20:56), a corrective action plan, and a written timeline promised to the boards. The business administrator's account of an RFP term "limited to three" (0:26:47) is his characterization; this page has not verified a New Hampshire rule to that effect.

Sources: 7 CFR 210.16(a)(5) and (d) (2019 edition; signature authority on agreement and claims; one-year contract, four renewals); 2 CFR 200.303(a) (2019 edition; effective internal control over federal awards)

MEDIUM SAU 6 had gone a while without a treasurer, and the treasurer's monthly cash reconciliation was not being done

Before the election the business administrator explained that the SAU treasurer signs vouchers and reconciles cash against his books each month, and that "right now we don't have that being completed. And it's been a while since we've had a, an acting treasurer at the. Say you." (0:48:22). The new treasurer spoke of making sure "the backlog gets taken care of" (0:48:40). RSA 194-C:5, I requires the SAU board to meet between April 1 and June 1 each year and to "organize by choosing a chairperson, a secretary, and a treasurer" (statute's wording). A period with no treasurer means a period with no independent reconciliation of the SAU's cash, the external control the business administrator described.

The board filled the office within the statutory window, on April 11, and Towle asked that reconciliation be set up so the backlog is cleared (0:48:40). How long the office was vacant, and whether a secretary was also chosen this year, is not on the tape; the minutes would show both. MEDIUM, as a control gap that the board corrected that night.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, I (1996, 298:3, never amended; SAU board organizes between April 1 and June 1 by choosing chair, secretary and treasurer)

OBSERVATION The rule against a substitute meal was described as a federal change; the rule on the books was New Hampshire's

Both the business administrator and the acting superintendent attributed the end of the "sun butter sandwich" substitute meal to a federal change (0:23:58, 0:24:48). From July 2, 2016, New Hampshire law required each district's meal-payment policy to ensure "no student will be subject to different treatment from the standard school lunch meal or school cafeteria procedures" (RSA 189:11-a, VIII, 2018 codification; statute's wording). The federal requirement was that school food authorities have a written meal-charge policy by July 1, 2017; USDA's guidance left the choice between a reimbursable and an alternate meal to local discretion. The practical point the speakers made, that the district must serve the standard meal regardless of balance, is correct; the source they named for it is not. The distinction matters to a reader trying to change the rule, since the State, not USDA, set it.

Sources: RSA 189:11-a, VIII (2018 codification; added 2016, 48:1, eff. July 2, 2016); USDA FNS memo SP 23-2017 (guidance; written meal-charge policy by July 1, 2017)
