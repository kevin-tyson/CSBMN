SAU 6 Board, May 11, 2023

Regular meeting of the joint SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity members) under chair Steven Horsky. HIGH The motion to enter nonpublic session named no exemption, on the agenda or on tape

RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires that "Any motion to enter nonpublic session shall state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II which is relied upon as foundation for the nonpublic session." (statute). The posted agenda lists the item as "Non- Public RSA 91-A:3,*" with a footnote reciting eight different paragraph II grounds, so it does not identify one. On tape the chair asks for "a motion to go into the nonpublic under RSA 91 a three", and the motion is "to move into nonpublic" (0:07:33, 0:07:47); no paragraph or subparagraph is spoken before the roll call. Only the minutes, drafted afterwards, head the item "RSA 91-A:3,II (a)", the ground for "The dismissal, promotion, compensation or disciplining of an employee" (agenda footnote). The board then appointed a business administrator immediately after the session; if that was the subject, II(b), "The hiring of any person as a public employee" (agenda footnote), would be the closer fit, but the record does not say what was discussed, and this page does not assume it. In mitigation, the vote was by roll call as I(b) also requires, and the minutes supply a ground after the fact; the statute asks for it on the face of the motion.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II (2022 codification; source note ends 2021, 172:1); May 11 agenda; draft minutes

MEDIUM The nonpublic minutes were sealed for ten years with no statutory finding stated, and the record disagrees on who seconded

RSA 91-A:3, III allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld only if, "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session, it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person other than a member of the public body itself, or render the proposed action ineffective" or would concern terrorism (statute). The vote here was taken in public session after the board came out of nonpublic, on a roll call answered Yes throughout (0:10:58), which satisfies the two-thirds and public-session conditions. Neither the motion, "to seal the minutes for ten years" (0:10:46), nor the minutes ("a motion to seal the non-public meeting minutes for 10 years" (minutes)) states which of the statutory determinations the board made, so the record does not show the finding on which a seal depends. The two records also differ on the seconder: on tape the chair says "Seconded by Kelly." (0:10:49); the minutes say Whitney Skillen. The ten-year period itself is not a defect: the text then in force set no term, and the ten-year review rule of RSA 91-A:3, IV did not take effect until October 3, 2023.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2022 codification); RSA 91-A:2, II (mover and seconder in the minutes); draft minutes

MEDIUM The SAU board had no secretary three weeks before the statutory organization window closed, and set no further meeting before it did

RSA 194-C:5, I provides that the SAU board "shall meet between April 1 and June 1 in each year" and "shall organize by choosing a chairperson, a secretary, and a treasurer." (statute). The masthead shows a chair and a vice chair, and the April 13 minutes in this packet record the election of a treasurer; the chair opens tonight with "Seeing that we don't have a secretary yet" (0:00:35), and the special education director calls the roll in the secretary's place. The board then said it did not expect to meet in June (0:34:37), and the minutes list no SAU 6 meeting before June 1. On this record the June 1 window closed with the secretary's office unfilled. The vice chair the board did elect is not one of the three statutory offices. The minutes are recorded by an SAU staff member, so the practical gap is the office itself rather than the record; the statute is the anchor for asking why the office stayed empty.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, I (2022 codification; source 1996, 298:3); April 13, 2023 draft minutes (treasurer election)
