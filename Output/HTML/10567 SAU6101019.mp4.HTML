MEDIUM No minutes of this meeting, or of the two sets it approved, are in any public share

This meeting approved the July 25 minutes eleven weeks after that meeting, and the September 5 minutes with corrections to the date and the chair's name (0:02:22 to 0:04:07). None of the three sets, and no agenda, is in any district share; the Drive archives begin in 2022. RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes open to public inspection within five business days, kept as permanent records, naming each motion's mover and seconder. Where a public body maintains a website, as SAU 6 did (the superintendent points viewers to “our website”, 0:09:04), ¶II-b requires it to post approved minutes or a notice of where they can be reviewed. A voice at 0:03:35 says “It's not online.”; the recording does not make clear what is meant. Graded MEDIUM because the record mitigates the absence: on December 5, 2019 the board took up the “minutes from October 10th, 2019”, moved by Lovett and seconded by Zullo, with Towle abstaining (Dialogue/10639 SAU6120519.mp4.CSV, about five minutes in).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II and II-b, 2019 codification (minutes within 5 business days; permanent records; mover and seconder; website posting or notice); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), 2019 codification (SAU board is a public body)

MEDIUM SAU 6 had no written policies of its own, and members say every audit cited missing procedures

Asked about SAU-specific policies, a member said “right now, we don't have any” (0:25:06), and an unidentified member who said the push “was me driving it” (0:25:30) said of the business office “We had nothing to protect us” and “All of the audits have talked to to the lack of procedures and policies” (0:26:11), down to “basic code of ethics things” (0:26:42). SAU 6 administers federal grants for both districts (it had just hired an accountant/grants manager). For a non-federal entity spending federal awards, the Uniform Guidance requires written procedures for payment and cash management and for determining the allowability of costs, and effective internal control over the awards. The audits themselves are not in the record, so this page cannot confirm what they said; Seaman said the auditors had not yet raised it with him (0:26:58). Graded MEDIUM as a probable control weakness, on members' own account. In mitigation, the board made SAU policy a continuing agenda item and Sprague undertook to obtain NHSBA models.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.302(b)(6)–(7), 2019 edition (written procedures for payment and for allowability of costs); 2 CFR 200.303(a), 2019 edition (effective internal control over federal awards)

MEDIUM FY2017 audits due at the end of October 2019, FY2018 by year end

After a correction from Seaman, the superintendent's schedule is the FY2017 audit “end of this month” and FY2018 by the end of the year (0:05:25 to 0:06:07). Fiscal 2017 ended June 30, 2017. Where a district spends $750,000 or more in federal awards, the single-audit reporting package is due nine months after year end, March 31, 2018 for FY2017 and March 31, 2019 for FY2018. The recording gives no federal-expenditure figure, so the threshold is not confirmed here and this stays MEDIUM. The same backlog is reported on the September 5 SAU 6 page and the October 2 Claremont page.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2019 edition (nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2019 edition ($750,000 threshold)
