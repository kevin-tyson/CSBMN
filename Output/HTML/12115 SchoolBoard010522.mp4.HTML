Claremont School Board, January 5, 2022

A two-and-a-half-hour regular meeting. The board ratified four collective bargaining agreements (secretaries, maintenance, paraprofessionals and administrators) and sent each to the ballot as a warrant article, moved a fiscal 2023 budget of $37,243,825 to its public hearing, approved the 2022-23 Stevens High School Program of Studies and accepted a $500 gift to Bluff School. Citizens' comments and a late-added discussion item were dominated by a COVID "cluster" ruling that shut down the middle school boys' basketball team. The page is built from the CCTV recording's dialogue transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes survive in the district's online shares. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on January 5, 2022, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies. No member is described as attending remotely, so the electronic-participation rules of RSA 91-A:2, III are not engaged on this recording.

HIGH The three-member budget committee met repeatedly, including a session that set a budget target, with no notice or minutes on record

The chair describes the board's budget committee (himself, Petrin and Stone) as having met for "I don't know how many collective hours, quite a few", and then "the three of us met, the Tuesday before Christmas maybe, or Tuesday after Christmas" to settle "suggestions for, areas to look at efficiencies and such with a number attached to that" (0:47:54, 0:48:19). That number shaped the $37,243,825 the board moved tonight. RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body" of a school board a public body, so each session was a meeting needing 24-hour notice and minutes open to inspection within five business days under RSA 91-A:2, II. The December 1 page records the same committee's December 13 and 14 sessions being scheduled. This page applies the HIGH limb of the project's severity rule: a public body met, no notice or minutes of any session exist in any district share, and nothing on the record explains the absence. The limit of the evidence: the district's online shares hold nothing from before mid-2022, so their silence does not prove the records were never made.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1); RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1)

The chair: "We don't have any meeting minutes tonight due to some technical difficulties." (0:02:02). The board's previous meeting in this corpus is December 1, 2021, five weeks earlier. OBSERVATION Contract terms were discussed with members one by one outside the meeting

On the administrators' agreement the chair said "I talked to a couple of board members ex parte, and that was important to them as well" (0:45:55), and on the paraprofessionals' starting rate the assistant superintendent for finance said "I have had conversations, I think with with most of the board members on this" (0:38:48). RSA 91-A:2-a, I requires public bodies to "deliberate on matters over which they have supervision, control, jurisdiction, or advisory power only in meetings", and II provides that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". Bargaining strategy itself is excluded from the definition of a meeting by RSA 91-A:2, I(a), which may cover some of these talks. Whether the conversations went beyond strategy into deciding positions cannot be told; the members voted on all four agreements in public with little discussion.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2008, 303:4; unamended); RSA 91-A:2, I(a) (2019 codification)

OBSERVATION A district employee described, in public, how the district had classified a specific student's COVID status

During citizens' comments a parent, who gave her name and ward, described her son's place in the basketball cluster count (0:07:10). The district's nurse coordinator, speaking "also as an employee" (0:10:58), answered by stating how the district had classified that student and why (0:11:46). This page does not repeat the details. Under FERPA, health information a school maintains about a student is part of the student's education records, and 34 CFR 99.30(a) requires a parent's signed and dated written consent before personally identifiable information from those records is disclosed, subject to exceptions in 99.31; 34 CFR 99.3 counts information "linked or linkable to a specific student" as personally identifiable. The parent had raised the matter herself in the same forum, which bears on how a reviewer weighs this, but her public statement is not written consent. Whether the nurse's statement drew on education records is the question that decides it. The superintendent later declined to discuss particulars because of "the confidentiality" (2:20:18).

Sources: 34 CFR 99.30(a) (2021 annual edition); 34 CFR 99.3 (2021 annual edition; education records, personally identifiable information)

OBSERVATION Catastrophic special education aid was described to the board in terms the statute does not use

The assistant superintendent for finance said: "once they go above $60,000 or something in that range, we the state, the state pays 90% of what's above 60,000" (1:09:06). RSA 186-C:18, III, as it read on this date (source note ending 2021, 209:2, eff. Oct. 9, 2021), sets the threshold at 3½ times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil, makes the state liable for 80 percent of the cost above it up to 10 times, and prorates the payment proportionally when the appropriation falls short. There is no 90 percent figure and no fixed $60,000 threshold in the text. The remark was offered with a hedge, and the budget's revenue line for catastrophic aid is not stated, so no effect on tonight's figures can be shown; the point is that the board and the public heard an inaccurate description of a revenue source.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III(a)-(b) (2022 codification; source note ends 2021, 209:2, eff. Oct. 9, 2021)

OBSERVATION Federal pandemic money pays contract stipends and other costs outside the appropriation voters will see

All four agreements carry a "disaster relief stipend" that "comes out of the CARES three money" (0:29:42, 0:44:33), and the budget excludes CARES funds on the advice of the auditor, with spending treated as "an approval done by the board as a part of accepting that grant" (0:58:20). RSA 198:20-b allows a board, where the district has adopted the enabling article, to accept and spend unanticipated money from a federal source, with a prior public hearing for amounts of $5,000 or more (III(a)). Whether the district holds that authority, and whether hearings preceded acceptance of these grants, cannot be checked from this recording. The amounts and the board's acceptance votes would show how much district spending runs outside the warrant.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1)
