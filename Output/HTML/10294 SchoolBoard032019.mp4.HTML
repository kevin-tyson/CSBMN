Claremont School Board, March 20, 2019

First meeting after the March 12, 2019 election. Heather Whitney, Carolyn Towle and Rob Lovett Jr. were sworn in; Frank Sprague was re-elected chair and Rebecca Zullo was elected vice chair over Jason Benware on a show of hands. The clerk read the election results, the board scheduled an April 3 hearing on $164,160 in unanticipated funds, adopted a bus lease resolution, and heard that the fiscal 2016 single audit was still unfinished. HIGH The fiscal 2016 single audit was still unfinished, and "up to date prior to June 30th" misses the federal deadline for fiscal 2018

The acting superintendent told the board that "there's actually two audits that happen in Claremont because we get such a large sum of grant funding, federal grant funding", and that for fiscal 2016 "the regular audit is done" while "the single audit, still needs to be complete" (2:07:14, 2:07:38). Fiscal 2017 fieldwork had begun and fiscal 2018 work had only started (2:07:38, 2:08:25). The goal was "to finish by the end of the school year and be up to date prior to June 30th" (2:08:50). Under 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) a single-audit reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period". For a fiscal year ending June 30 that is March 31 of the next year: March 31, 2017 for fiscal 2016, March 31, 2018 for fiscal 2017, and March 31, 2019 for fiscal 2018, eleven days after this meeting. On the stated plan, finishing fiscal 2018 by June 30, 2019 would itself be about three months late, so "up to date" is not the federal standard. The board heard the same backlog on February 20, 2019 (that page's flag 1). In mitigation, the auditors had committed a large team to the district and staff described the status openly.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2019 annual edition (due the earlier of 30 days after the auditor's report or nine months after period end); 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2019 edition ($750,000 threshold)

OBSERVATION The board was told a secret ballot was an option for electing its vice chair; the statute bars it

Asked how to hold the contested vice chair vote, the clerk suggested "Like ballots." (0:15:30) and the acting superintendent said "I think you have the option to do public or not." (0:15:32). RSA 91-A:2, II provides: "Except for town meetings, school district meetings, and elections, no vote while in open session may be taken by secret ballot." A school board choosing its own officers in open session is not a town meeting, a school district meeting or an election in that sense, so a ballot was not an option. The board voted by show of hands (0:15:40, 0:15:48), so the outcome complied; the advice did not. Graded an observation because no secret vote was taken.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II, 2019 codification (secret-ballot bar; source note ends 2018, 244:1)

POSITIVE Public hearings scheduled before accepting $164,160 in unanticipated funds, with the reason explained

For the Couch Family Foundation's $23,160 and the $141,000 in public school infrastructure funds, staff stopped the board from acting that night and set a public hearing for April 3 (0:22:45, 0:28:07), as RSA 198:20-b, III(a) requires for "unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more".
