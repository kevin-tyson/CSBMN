Claremont School Board, August 15, 2018

A regular meeting, the first with interim superintendent Keith Pfeifer, three weeks before school opened with 41 positions unfilled. OBSERVATION The syringe service program request came under a school-board exception that had taken effect four days earlier

LeClair presented the request for a syringe service program at Valley Regional Hospital, next to Disnard Elementary, as requiring "the board's permission in order to have that program in the safe school zone", adding "the law was just recently passed." (1:12:27). Ashley Greenfield of the Public Health Network and Laura Byrne of the HIV Resource Center described the program (1:13:25 to 1:27:27); the board tabled it until the hospital's CEO could attend in September, with one abstention (1:31:31, 1:31:41).

RSA 318-B:45 bars a syringe service program "within a drug-free school zone as defined in RSA 193-B:1, II", which reaches school property and land within 1,000 feet. Its exception, "Exceptions to this prohibition may be granted by the applicable district school board when a request is initiated by a syringe service program administrator", was added by 2018, 254:2, effective August 11, 2018. So the board did hold the authority LeClair described. For a reviewer: the statute ties the exception to a request "initiated by a syringe service program administrator", and the recording does not show who made the formal request. Tabling to hear the hospital's CEO, and inviting written questions seven days ahead (1:31:51, 1:32:08), left the decision for September. At that meeting the hospital's CEO described Valley Regional as host of a program run with the HIV Resource Center and the Public Health Network (September 5, 2018 page).

Sources: RSA 318-B:45 (2018 codification; 2018, 254:2, eff. Aug. 11, 2018); RSA 193-B:1, II (drug-free school zone; 1996, 290:1)

OBSERVATION A member proposed pulling students from Brattleboro Retreat placements over the contract dispute

After hearing that the Retreat had priced an in-district program up to its tuition (0:37:56), Horsky said he would "like to definitely explore options as a district to pull everything away from Brattleboro retreat, because if they want to play business like that, that's not the way Claremont conducts business." (0:40:09). Staff noted placements are contracted "on a kid by kid basis for one year" (0:39:41), and LeClair framed the next step as finding providers that meet "the needs of our individual students" (0:38:40).

Under the IDEA regulations in force in 2018, each child's placement "Is made by a group of persons, including the parents, and other persons knowledgeable about the child", "Is determined at least annually" and "Is based on the child's IEP" (34 CFR 300.116). A district can seek other providers, but moving a placed student is an IEP-team decision, not a board decision about a vendor. No action was taken.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.116(a)(1), (b)(1)-(2) (CFR annual edition revised July 1, 2018)

The legal risk is broader than a "board position": RSA 91-A:2-a requires public bodies to deliberate "only in meetings" and bars communications outside a meeting, "including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members", from being "used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". A four-member exchange on a public post is that kind of communication. Raising it in public session, with the reason, is good practice beyond what the law required of the chair.
