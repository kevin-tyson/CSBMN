SAU 6 Board, July 13, 2023

Regular meeting of the SAU 6 board (Claremont and Unity), all eleven members present. With the chair vacant after Steven Horsky's resignation from the Claremont board, vice chair Rocco Ruggeri opens the meeting; a proposed paper ballot is dropped after a point of order, and Arlene Hawkins is elected chair 8 to 3 by show of hands over Marjorie Erickson. The board adds and holds a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(a) and seals its minutes for five years, hears that the district audits will not be final until spring 2024 and that teachers' retirement deposits had lapsed for months, and adopts policy BCB on board-member conflicts of interest. MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for five years with no statutory determination on the record

RSA 91-A:3, III, as it read on this date, allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld beyond 72 hours only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" that determines divulgence "likely would affect adversely the reputation" of a non-member, would render a proposed action ineffective, or pertains to terrorism (statute). The vote was in public and by roll call, ten to one (0:13:16 to 0:13:39; minutes), which meets the two-thirds requirement. Neither the motion, "to seal the minutes for five years" (0:13:08), nor the minutes state any of the statutory determinations. The chair's question "And is there a motion about the minutes?" (0:12:58) and Whitney's "Yes we do. If we want them sealed." (0:13:08) show the seal treated as a choice rather than a finding. No decision from the session was announced. The ten-year review rule of RSA 91-A:3, IV did not take effect until October 3, 2023.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2022 codification; source note ends 2021, eff. Jan. 1, 2022); draft minutes, item IV

OBSERVATION The audits for Claremont, Unity and SAU 6 are put off to spring 2024, a year past the original target

The superintendent reports that the auditors expect "the final audits for us to be completely out into the spring of next year" for Unity, Claremont and the SAU (0:17:50), and that the work "was supposed to be this spring" but slipped "with the turnover in the payroll staff" (0:19:08). The minutes record: "An update on the audits and presentation will occur in the Spring of 2024." Federal rules require a single audit of any entity that "expends $750,000 or more during the non-Federal entity's fiscal year in Federal awards", submitted within "nine months after the end of the audit period" (2 CFR 200.501(a), 200.512(a)(1), 2023 edition), which for a June 30 year is March 31 of the next year. An audit that was due in spring 2023 and is now expected in spring 2024 would be about a year late for the year concerned. The record does not say which fiscal years are outstanding for which of the three entities, so this page records the point as an Observation; the audit reports and the Federal Audit Clearinghouse would settle it. The same delay was reported to the Claremont board on June 7.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2023 annual edition); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2023 annual edition); June 7, 2023 page, flag 3

POSITIVE A point of order kept the chair election out of a secret ballot

RSA 91-A:2, II: "Except for town meetings, school district meetings, and elections, no vote while in open session may be taken by secret ballot." (statute). A board electing its own officer is not one of the exceptions. Sprague proposed a paper ballot (0:01:40); Whitney objected that "everything that we do in public has to be in public" (0:02:18), the board searched, and the vote went to a show of hands (0:05:30) with the tally in the minutes. Hawkins's remark "that's how we did elect a chair" (0:02:33) refers to the March 30, 2023 SAU 6 chair election, which the project's records show was decided 6 to 5 by paper ballot, so no individual votes survive from it. A show of hands with a count, as here, still leaves no record of who voted which way; a roll call would have.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification)
