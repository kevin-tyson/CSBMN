MEDIUM The motion to enter nonpublic session, as transcribed, names no specific exemption, and the recording ends before the session

The presiding officer moved "that we go into a nonpublic session. Per RSA 91-A colon, three second" (0:23:58). RSA 91-A:3, I(b), as it read in 2018, required a motion to enter nonpublic session to state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II, and the decision to be made by roll call. The roll call was taken (0:24:28 to 0:24:44), but the motion as transcribed cites only the section; no lettered ground of paragraph II is heard, and the next word may be a second rather than a paragraph number. The superintendent had offered to give his report "later in nonpublic" (0:19:15), which suggests a personnel matter, but the record does not say so. The recording then ends, so nothing shows how long the session lasted, what was decided, or whether its minutes were disclosed within 72 hours or sealed under RSA 91-A:3, III. The December 13 correction to these minutes concerns the section headed as a request for a nonpublic session (show 10165, about two and a half minutes in), so the minutes may state the exemption; if they do not, this would be a HIGH item. The ASR may also have dropped a spoken letter, which only the audio can settle.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and III (2017 codification; source note ends 2016, 30:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017)

OBSERVATION The October 18 draft minutes omitted a member's nay vote and the reasons she asked to have recorded

Towle told the board that the draft minutes of October 18 reported the motion adding a food service compliance manager as accepted but left out her nay and the reasons she had asked "to be read into the record" (0:02:37); the board amended the minutes to include them (0:04:36 to 0:04:53). In 2018 RSA 91-A:2, II required minutes to give a brief description of the subject matter and the final decisions; the requirement to record who moved and seconded each motion began only on January 1, 2019, and neither version requires a roll of individual votes. The omission was therefore a quality gap rather than a statutory one, and it was caught and fixed in public. The October 18 meeting is on file as show 10059 (MAP.md section 84), where the original vote can be checked.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; the mover/seconder sentence was added by 2018, 244:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2019)

POSITIVE The SAU held a public hearing on its budget that the statute does not require, and adopted the budget well before the January 1 deadline

RSA 194-C:9, I, as it read in 2018, required the SAU board to adopt its budget at a meeting held before January 1 and to apportion it among the member districts half on average membership and half on equalized valuation; it sets no hearing or notice requirement. The board nonetheless opened a public hearing with a presentation (0:05:12 to 0:13:09), invited public comment, and adopted the budget on November 8 (0:18:10). The business administrator's explanation of why Unity's share rose while Claremont's fell (0:09:15, 0:09:37) matches the statutory formula, though he calls the property half "assessed valuation" where the statute says equalized valuation. The revised figure adopted was lower than the one presented at the hearing, which raises no issue: the only change was the actual health insurance rate (0:15:29).

Sources: RSA 194-C:9, I (2017 codification; source note 1996, 298:3; 2003, 279:1, eff. Sept. 16, 2003)
