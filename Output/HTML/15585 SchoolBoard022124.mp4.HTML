Claremont School Board, February 21, 2024

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, all seven members present, three weeks before the March 12, 2024 election. The board accepts a $10,000 Byrne Foundation gift for the eighth-grade Boston trip after a public hearing, hears the first monthly financial update (a projected balance of roughly $829,000 that the business administrator says will move as she reconciles ESSER III), reviews i-Ready benchmark and summer-program data, and raises substitute teacher pay to $125 a day, then defers its start to March 11. MEDIUM The board approved minutes of a January 29 working session that record no attendance, time or place

The consent agenda approved the "minutes of the working session on January 29th, 2024" without discussion (0:01:10). The approved document, Minutes from CSB working session 1.29.24 (1).pdf in this meeting's packet, runs three pages: it assigns slides of the deliberative-session presentation to Heather, Frank, Candy, Jenn and Bonnie, rewords the district's public case, reviews the business administrator's budget slides, and records the direction "Make sure to make a motion to restrict reconsideration of warrant articles at the very end". Five members named by first name is a quorum of seven, so the session was a meeting under RSA 91-A:2, I. RSA 91-A:2, II requires that minutes "shall include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions". The document has no attendance list, no call-to-order or adjournment time and no location; no notice for January 29 has been located in the district's Drive packet folders, and the session has no Cablecast recording.

Severity: the statute's attendance requirement is binding, but the minutes exist, five members can be identified from the slide assignments, and no vote is recorded. That places this at MEDIUM under the project's rule for records that exist but are deficient, rather than the HIGH reserved for a missing record with nothing explaining it. The defect belongs to the January 29 session; what this meeting added was approval without correction.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2023 codification; source note ends 2023, 188:1, eff. Oct. 3, 2023); Minutes from CSB working session 1.29.24 (1).pdf; agenda, consent item 2

MEDIUM The business administrator reports budget lines that do not reconcile and an ESSER III reconciliation that had reached only October or November

In the first monthly financial update (0:38:24 to 0:47:37), Mary Henry told the board that the spreadsheet figures "don't exactly line up within the actual budget itself"; that "a duplicate purchase order was entered for 90,000"; that a mental health counselor position was listed without a dollar amount; that $951,000 unspent in regular instruction against $955,000 over in special education suggests "things sitting in the wrong place"; and that she was reconciling ESSER III only "up to October or November" in late February, with salary charges still sitting in the general budget to be moved into ESSER. She also said she would not trace every misallocated salary line "Unless someone tells me I have to". For federal funds, the Uniform Guidance in force in 2024 required "Identification, in its accounts, of all Federal awards received and expended" and "Records that identify adequately the source and application of funds for federally-funded activities" (2 CFR 200.302(b)(1) and (3), 2023 edition), and effective internal control over federal awards (2 CFR 200.303). Mitigation on the record: Henry describes the work as under way and promises a fuller report the next month, and the board receives a written report for the first time.

Sprague's objection to "funding overages in other areas" from unfilled positions (0:47:10) concerns transfers between appropriations. RSA 32:10 lets the governing body transfer between appropriations within the total voted and requires records that let a citizen see what moved where. Whether the offsets Henry described were made as RSA 32:10 transfers is not stated in the record.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.302(b)(1), (3) (2023 annual edition); 2 CFR 200.303 (2023 annual edition; internal control); RSA 32:10 (transfers of appropriations; 2017, 127:4)

OBSERVATION The ESSER deadline was stated as September 1

Crawford said the ESSER funds run out "I think September 1st" and that the district must "spend it by the 15th in order to actually get it" (0:48:11); Koski's reply is transcribed only as 3030, which may be "September 30". American Rescue Plan ESSER funds were appropriated with an availability date that the Tydings Amendment extends by one additional fiscal year, which is why the U.S. Department of Education gave September 30, 2024 as the obligation deadline and a 120-day liquidation period after it. The board was not misled into an action, but a board planning to spend "every cent" was working from two different dates.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §1225(b) (Tydings Amendment: one additional fiscal year)

POSITIVE A public hearing for a gift the statute did not require one for

RSA 198:20-b, III(a) requires a prior public hearing only for unanticipated funds of $20,000 or more (the threshold since 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023). For smaller amounts, III(b) requires that the board "shall post notice of the funds in the agenda", include notice in the minutes, and accept the funds "in public session of any regular school board meeting". The board did all three for the $10,000 gift and also held a noticed public hearing on the agenda (0:01:57 to 0:03:54). Whether Claremont's acceptance rests on a district article adopted under RSA 198:20-b, I was not checked for this page.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III (source note ends 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023); agenda, item III
