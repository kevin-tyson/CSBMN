HIGH The FY2020 and FY2021 audits were only just completed in 2025, and FY2022 is still in fieldwork; federal single audits were due nine months after each year end

The business administrator, the chair and the auditor all describe an audit backlog measured in years. Mary Henry: "We're currently working on fiscal year 22." and "We hope to have all our audits completed by the October or November of this year, with the exception of 24" (0:22:03, 0:22:27). The chair summarizes that FY2020 and FY2021 "Those are the two audits that have been completed." (0:24:43). Michael Campo of Plodzik & Sanderson: "I've been working on this audit since 2016." and "because I'm in fiscal year 22" (0:31:25, 0:32:27); his target is "by the November deadline that we do have all the audits", with the FY2025 audit aimed at "the March 31st, headline for the federal clearinghouse" (0:32:55). He also says the board "were not getting audit reports on an annual basis" (0:36:09).

A non-federal entity that spends $750,000 or more of federal awards in a fiscal year must have a single audit for that year (2 CFR 200.501(a)), and the audit and reporting package are due "nine months after the end of the audit period" at the latest (2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2020 annual edition; the same nine-month rule applies in every later edition). The district's federal spending was well above $750,000 in these years: counsel describes ESSER awards that were "multi million dollars" (0:41:37), the chair says Claremont "got probably more than almost any district in the state" (0:45:49), and the superintendent puts the annual Title allocation "In the past has been over 2 million" (1:06:14). On a July to June year the FY2020 single audit was due March 31, 2021 and the FY2021 audit March 31, 2022. Both were finished three to four years after those dates, and FY2022 through FY2024 remain open. The auditor's own reference to a March 31 federal clearinghouse deadline confirms the rule is known.

Mitigation stated on the record: everyone presenting attributes the backlog to earlier administrations and lost records, the auditor reports regular status meetings with a board member, the superintendent and the business office, and he names a schedule. The page records the delay; it does not decide who is responsible for it.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a), 2020 annual edition ($750,000 single-audit threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2020 annual edition (due within nine months of year end)
