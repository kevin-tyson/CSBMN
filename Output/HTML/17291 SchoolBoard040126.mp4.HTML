Claremont School Board, Public Hearing & Meeting, April 1, 2026

A public hearing on a Kiwanis Club gift of up to $24,229.50 for two gymnasium scoreboards, followed by the board's regular meeting, the second under chair Candace Crawford. The board accepted the gift, approved a $70,353.08 school resource officer agreement, thanked Claremont Savings Bank after the interim business administrator reported the district had made it through March with a loan balance of zero, tabled by-law revisions pending a cost estimate from counsel, debated the scope of the ad hoc reconfiguration committee without a vote, authorized the chair to sign a SchoolCare pooled-risk resolution and adopted Policy BBBF on second read. These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each one names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on April 1, 2026, or is labeled an observation where no verified rule applies.

MEDIUM The chair told the board that remote attendance at subcommittee meetings is "entirely appropriate"; the statute sets conditions that do not depend on whether a vote is taken

Asked about joining a subcommittee meeting online, the chair said remote participation "for subcommittee meetings is entirely appropriate" and that the question arises only "when there's a vote" (2:36:43, 2:37:21). RSA 91-A:2, III applies to any public body, and a school board subcommittee is one (RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d)). Paragraph III(a) allows a member to participate other than in person "only when such attendance is not reasonably practical", and the statute adds that "Any reason that such attendance is not reasonably practical shall be stated in the minutes"; III(b) requires that, except in an emergency, a quorum be physically present. None of this turns on whether a vote is taken. The same evening the board approved the March 26 special-meeting minutes, which record "All present, Loren Howard virtually" with no reason stated. Mr. Rapp's remark about a physical quorum (2:37:10) states III(b) correctly. Graded MEDIUM as a probable process gap: the guidance given on the record is incomplete, and a minutes entry approved at this meeting omits a statutory element.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, III(a)-(b) (remote participation only when attendance is not reasonably practical; reason stated in the minutes; physical quorum); RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (subcommittees are public bodies); 3.26.26 Approved CSB Special Meeting Minutes

MEDIUM The approved minutes give the adjournment time but not the start time

Since August 22, 2025, RSA 91-A:2, II has required minutes to include "the start time and end time of the meeting, and name of the person who produced the minutes". The April 1 minutes name the clerk and record "Consent adjournment at 9:24pm", but they give only the agenda's scheduled times (6:30 and 7:00 p.m.) and no actual start time for the hearing or the meeting. The hearing ran into the regular meeting early, at 0:12:38, so the agenda's 7:00 p.m. is not the actual time either. The gap is small: the adjournment time less the 2:52:40 of recording puts the call to order at about 6:31 p.m., and the minutes document was created at 6:24 p.m. that evening. Graded MEDIUM as an omitted statutory element in otherwise thorough minutes.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (as amended 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025: start time, end time, name of the person who produced the minutes); 4.1.26 Approved minutes

OBSERVATION The Kiwanis gift was accepted after a hearing, but the newspaper notice cannot be confirmed and the minutes omit the amount

RSA 198:20-b, III(a) requires a prior public hearing before a school board accepts unanticipated funds of $20,000 or more, with notice of "the time, place, and subject of such hearing" published "in a newspaper of general circulation" at least seven days before. The board held the hearing, and the district's notice, created in Drive on March 24, eight days ahead, states the subject, date, time and place. Whether it ran in a newspaper is not shown in any district document reached here. Procedurally, the acceptance was moved at 0:09:37, before public comment opened, and the hearing was closed and the motion voted in one breath (0:11:59); comment was invited and heard in between, so nothing appears to have been cut off. The approved minutes record the motion without its figure; the motion on tape set a ceiling of $24,229.50, and that figure appears nowhere in the minutes. The statute's authority also depends on a district meeting having adopted an enabling article under ¶I, which this page did not check.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, I and III(a) (2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023: hearing for $20,000 or more; newspaper notice 7 days ahead); Public Notice 4.1.26; Nevco Quote.pdf

The resolution also says "the Claremont School District will assimilate SAU 6"; the only legal mechanism in the record is Unity's withdrawal from SAU 6, and this page does not adopt the resolution's characterization. No rule is shown to be broken; the record is thin for a benefits decision covering transferring employees and retirees.
