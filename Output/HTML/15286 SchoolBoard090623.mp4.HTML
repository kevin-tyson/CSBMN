HIGH No minutes exist for this meeting, and the recording stops before most of the agenda, including a nonpublic session

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes of every meeting, "including nonpublic sessions", with "a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", the names of movers and seconders, and public inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (2022 codification, in force on this date). No minutes of September 6, 2023 have been found: the Meeting Minutes share skips from August 16 to September 20, and the September 20 agenda and minutes carry "Minutes Approval- 9.6.23 (Postponed)" with no later approval found. The recording ends at 0:52:47 during item IV.2 of seven. Everything after that point has no public record of any kind: the board-effectiveness discussion, bargaining volunteers, subcommittee reports, the athletic-fields license, the policy action the district dates to this meeting (flag 2), and a nonpublic session noticed under RSA 91-A:3, II(c). For the nonpublic session, RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires a motion naming the exemption and a roll-call vote in public, and ¶III requires its minutes to be disclosed within 72 hours unless sealed "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session"; none of those steps can be checked. Severity limb under the project's rule for missing records: the meeting was noticed and packeted, which ordinarily points to MEDIUM, and the September 20 "(Postponed)" note is a mitigation. HIGH is applied because the minutes are not the only gap: the recording is also cut short, so for more than half the agenda neither record exists, the postponement was never followed by approval, and the statutory five days expired more than three years ago.

OBSERVATION A vendor's product grant of about $11,500 was reported to the board, with no board acceptance on the record

Pratt reported that after a Title grant application was refused, Yondr supplied the pouches and equipment because Stevens was the first New Hampshire school to adopt them, "a little over $11,000" (0:17:33); the packet answer gives "$11,500 worth of product". RSA 198:20-b, as amended by 2023, 38:1 (effective July 18, 2023, before this meeting), lets a school board accept and expend unanticipated money from a private source, requires a prior public hearing at $20,000 or more, and below that requires notice in the agenda and minutes and acceptance "in public session of any regular school board meeting." The statute speaks of money; whether it reaches an in-kind gift of product is not settled by its text, so this is not graded as a breach. No acceptance vote appears on the recording, and the meeting has no minutes. Pratt also says the first year came at "no cost to the taxpayers" (0:40:01) while next year's cost is still to be worked into the budget (0:39:34).

Sources: RSA 198:20-b (source note ends 2023, 38:1, eff. July 18, 2023); Questions for C. Pratt, Yondr answer
