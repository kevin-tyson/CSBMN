MEDIUM The May 17, 2023 minutes were not ready three weeks later

RSA 91-A:2, II requires that minutes "shall be promptly recorded and open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute). The agenda lists "(Meeting Minutes from 5/17/23 postponed*)", and the chair says "We were unable to have the minutes from the 517 meeting. Transcribed so that will be postponed until the next meeting." (0:01:22). June 7 is the fourteenth business day after May 17 (Memorial Day, May 29, was a state holiday). The project has located no May 17 minutes in any district share, and the district's own minutes numbering skips that meeting (this page's minutes are number 8, after number 7 for May 3). This page applies the MEDIUM limb of the project's severity rule because the record explains the delay on tape and promises the minutes; the defect belongs to the May 17 meeting, and it is recorded here because this is where the board acknowledged it.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2022 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2019); agenda, item II

OBSERVATION The interim business administrator reports the district's audits are behind, with no timeline

David Jack: "We're working with your auditors to catch up with some of the audience [audits], as you know, that have been behind." (0:12:12), with a timeline hoped for "perhaps by your next meeting" (0:12:40). He does not say which fiscal years are outstanding. The federal rule that most often sets the clock is the single audit: an entity that "expends $750,000 or more during the non-Federal entity's fiscal year in Federal awards must have a single or program-specific audit" (2 CFR 200.501(a), 2023 edition), submitted "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period" (2 CFR 200.512(a)(1)). For a June 30 fiscal year that is March 31 of the following year, so a FY2022 single audit was due March 31, 2023. In the same meeting Sprague describes $10 to $11 million of ESSER money and Jack nearly $3 million of reimbursements filed by June 2, which suggests the threshold is met. This page records the statement as an Observation because the record here does not identify which audit was late; the district's audit reports and the Federal Audit Clearinghouse filings would settle it.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a) (2023 annual edition; $750,000 threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1) (2023 annual edition; nine months)

OBSERVATION A member reports a $200,000 to $300,000 food-service balance that no one at the table could locate

Sprague says the former business administrator told him "either 2 or 300,000" had built up from pandemic-era lunch reimbursements (1:38:28) and asks "if we had that money, where did it go?" (1:39:38); the assistant superintendent: "I'm not aware of." (1:37:58). Federal school-lunch rules restrict that money: "Revenues received by the nonprofit school food service are to be used only for the operation or improvement of such food service", and the school food authority "shall limit its net cash resources to an amount that does not exceed 3 months average expenditures" (7 CFR 210.14(a) and (b), 2023 edition). No figure is in a document on this record, so this is an Observation. The minutes defer the question to an August meeting. The food-service fund balance in the FY2022 and FY2023 financial statements, and the NH Department of Education's administrative review of the program, would settle it.

Sources: 7 CFR 210.14(a), (b) (2023 annual edition); draft minutes, item VIII(d)
