HIGH The district's audits are about two and a half years behind: the FY2017 report had just arrived and FY2018 was only starting

Asked who checks the administration's financial information, the business administrator says the auditor works for the board and "We're up to 17, start 18. And so we're almost catching up." (1:53:51); a speaker adds "17 books." and the superintendent "We just came in yesterday." (1:54:09). On that account the audit of the year ended June 30, 2017 reached the district in January 2020, and the FY2018 audit had not begun. Under the Uniform Guidance a district that spends $750,000 or more in federal awards in a year must have a single audit, and the reporting package is due "within the earlier of 30 calendar days after receipt of the auditor's report(s), or nine months after the end of the audit period": March 31, 2018 for FY2017 and March 31, 2019 for FY2018. The same evening Seaman ties the district's policies to "title money, Ida money, some big pools of dollars" (0:59:08); the record does not give the FY2017 federal expenditure figure, and this page has not seen the audit itself, so the threshold is inferred from the scale of the federal programs named, not read from a document. Two further points for a reviewer: the board member's "eventually we'll actually do audits in the year the following year" (1:54:00) describes the backlog as normal practice, and the business administrator's offer to have the auditors answer questions "in executive session" names no ground in RSA 91-A:3, II that would cover an audit briefing. Mitigation on the record: the backlog predates the current business administrator, who began in October 2019, and he describes the district as catching up.

Sources: 2 CFR 200.501(a)-(b), 2019 edition ($750,000 single-audit threshold); 2 CFR 200.512(a)(1), 2019 edition (nine months after period end); RSA 91-A:3, II (2019 codification; nonpublic grounds)

RSA 273-A:3, II(b) provides that "Only cost items shall be submitted to the legislative body of the public employer for approval at the next annual meeting", and a cost item is "any benefit acquired through collective bargaining whose implementation requires an appropriation" (RSA 273-A:1, IV).
