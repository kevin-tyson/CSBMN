Claremont School Board, July 18, 2018

Short summer meeting of the Claremont board: it approved the Bridges program, two Brattleboro Retreat classrooms in Claremont schools projected by the business administrator to avoid about $385,000 in out-of-district costs, named the technical center director to the CCTV board of directors, gave the acting superintendent summer hiring authority, and approved Kathleen Bunnell as Maple Avenue principal. MEDIUM Summer authority for the acting superintendent to hire "without prior approval of the board"

The chair described the motion as letting "the superintendent or in this case, the acting superintendent to hire without prior approval of the board" during the summer, calling it "customary" (0:43:44, 0:44:21); it passed by voice vote with no stated limits, end date or reporting requirement (0:44:58). RSA 189:39, unchanged since 1971, provides: "Superintendents shall nominate and school boards elect all teachers employed in the schools in their school administrative unit". A delegation that lets teachers be hired with no board election is in tension with that text. The tension is cured if the summer hires are brought back for election, and the same meeting shows the board electing nominees in the ordinary way (0:48:14, 0:49:00), so the practice may have been a timing device rather than a transfer of the board's power. The recording does not say which. This is MEDIUM: a probable process gap that the board's August nominations, if they list the summer hires, would close.

Sources: RSA 189:39 (2019 codification; source note ends 1971, 371:2, so the text in force in 2018)

POSITIVE The board moved to educate students with significant emotional and behavioral needs in Claremont buildings rather than out of district

Bridges places level 2 and level 3 classrooms in district schools so that students who would otherwise ride to Brattleboro stay in Claremont; the business administrator said students in district are "much more likely to go back into the mainstream" (0:27:34). IDEA requires that, "To the maximum extent appropriate, children with disabilities ... are educated with children who are not disabled". A program that keeps students in their own district's buildings, with a planned transition back to regular classrooms, moves in that direction. Members also pressed on fairness, Horsky saying the district "can't just exclude" a family's child (0:42:04), and on whether district evaluators were being used (0:36:45). Whether any student's placement changes remains a decision for that student's IEP team, the point behind Horsky's "IEP question" (0:37:20). The presenter's own description of the program as "an out of district placement" located in Claremont (0:11:04) is a reminder that the classrooms are separate settings.

Sources: 20 U.S.C. §1412(a)(5)(A) (least restrictive environment; not amended after 2004)
