Claremont School Board, Deliberative Session, February 3, 2024

First session of the 2024 Claremont School District annual meeting under SB 2, at which voters discussed the five warrant articles (a $39,582,407 operating budget and three support-staff contracts), corrected one article and sent all five to the March 12 ballot. The body here is the voters, not the board. Built from the dialogue transcript, the agenda, the Rules of Procedure, the default-budget worksheet and the minutes. Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer's aid, not findings of violation and not legal advice. A deliberative session is governed by RSA chapter 40 as applied to school districts and by the district's own Rules of Procedure; board rules such as the public-comment period and nonpublic sessions do not apply. Each flag names the rule it rests on, as that rule read on February 3, 2024.

HIGH The session was run by an interim moderator, an office state law does not create, with no record of how she was chosen

The district moderator presides at a school district meeting with the powers of a town moderator (RSA 197:19). If the moderator is absent, the clerk acts as moderator “until a moderator pro tempore shall be chosen” (RSA 197:20, statute text), and if the office is vacant, “the school district clerk shall serve as moderator or shall appoint a moderator pro tempore” (RSA 671:33, III, statute text). The school board may fill vacancies in district offices “except that of moderator” (RSA 197:26, statute text). The agenda, the Rules of Procedure sheet and the minutes all style Charlene Lovett “Interim Moderator” (agenda), she introduces herself that way at 0:00:20, and the elected moderator is not named anywhere in the record. Nothing on the recording or in the minutes shows the clerk presiding first, the meeting choosing a moderator pro tempore, or the clerk appointing one; the title was printed on the agenda before the session opened. The session's actions (sending five articles to the ballot, amending Article V, restricting reconsideration) were all taken under her gavel. Mitigation: no voter objected, every article went to the ballot as RSA 40:13, VI requires regardless of floor action, and the one amendment corrected an evident typographical error. A clerk's appointment made off the record would cure the defect; none has been located.

Sources: RSA 197:19 (district moderator; in force since 1979); RSA 197:20 (clerk acts until a moderator pro tempore is chosen); RSA 671:33, III (vacancy in moderator's office); RSA 197:26 (board may not fill the moderator's office); agenda; Rules of Procedure

MEDIUM The Rules of Procedure read to voters misstate how a vote can be challenged

At 0:07:38 the presiding officer read the district's rule that “any voter who doubts the accuracy of any non-counted vote may require the Moderator to determine a vote by a counted show of hands or counted standing vote” (Rules of Procedure text). State law, which RSA 40:13, IV makes binding at a deliberative session, says otherwise: a declared vote questioned immediately by “7 or more of the voters present” must be retaken by secret yes-no ballot (RSA 40:4-b), and 5 voters may request a secret ballot in writing before a vote (RSA 40:4-a). The sheet sets the threshold at one voter and the remedy at a counted show of hands, and it says nothing of the secret ballot. The sheet also omits RSA 40:13, IV's limits on amendment (no amending articles whose wording is prescribed by law; no amendment eliminating an article's subject matter). Nobody invoked either rule on February 3, so no vote was affected; the defect is in what voters were told about their rights.

Sources: RSA 40:4-b (questioning a vote; 7 voters; secret ballot); RSA 40:4-a (secret ballot on written request of 5 voters); RSA 40:13, IV (first session governed by RSA 40:4, 40:4-a, 40:4-b; amendment limits; text unchanged since 2019); Rules of Procedure.docx (4).pdf

OBSERVATION The default-budget definition given to voters stops short of the eliminated-positions clause

RSA 40:13, IX(b) defines the default budget as the prior year's appropriations adjusted for debt service, contracts and obligations, “reduced by one-time expenditures contained in the operating budget and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget” (statute text). Henry's reading at 0:40:16 and the district's worksheet (dated 1/19/2024) both end the definition before the last clause, and the worksheet has no line for eliminated positions. RSA 40:13, XI(a)(4) requires the default budget form to show “Reductions for eliminated positions and benefit expenditures” (statute text). Whether any FY25 position counted as eliminated under the statute is not clear from the record: Henry said on January 17 that the middle school drops from 25 to 24 teaching positions through attrition, and here that “all Esser positions were removed” (0:48:07), but ESSER-paid positions were not in the FY24 operating budget. The worksheet also labels the default figure “Projected FY24 Default Budget” (worksheet text) where FY25 is meant. The default budget form filed with the Department of Revenue Administration would settle the point.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI(a) (default budget definition and form contents); Claremont Default Budget FY25 (1).pdf

The minutes are therefore the register of who spoke; their grouping by position, in speaking order within each group, fits every turn that can be checked on the recording, and this page names Article II's speakers from that order. The transcript has stretches with no words: about 104 and 111 seconds around 1:04:41, where the minutes place Tom Luther's process remarks, and about two minutes during the Article V debate, where the minutes list three speakers (Gallagher, Fontaine, Allen) the page cannot assign. These are gaps in the transcript, not necessarily in the video. The approved minutes also contain an error: under Article III they record a motion “to move Article II as written” (minutes text). The minutes themselves were posted promptly (flag 6). RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to name the members who moved and seconded each motion; at a deliberative session movers are voters, and the minutes name them.

Sources: approved minutes 2.3.24; RSA 91-A:2, II (contents of minutes)

POSITIVE A misprinted article was corrected on the floor before the ballot

Article V as printed gave its first-year cost as for 2015 rather than 2025. The error was caught when the article was read (1:18:41), corrected by a moved and seconded amendment, and adopted by the voters (1:20:55); the article went to the ballot as amended (1:31:21). RSA 40:13, IV allows amendment at the first session (“An amendment that changes the dollar amount of an appropriation in a warrant article shall not be deemed to violate this subparagraph”, statute text, and a date correction changes no amount), and RSA 40:13, VIII has the clerk print the ballot in substantially the amended wording. Fixing the error here, on the record, is the process working as designed. The presiding officer also caught and reversed her own attempt to put the amendment to a vote without a second (1:20:22).

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and VIII (amendment at the first session; ballot wording)
