Claremont School Board, Deliberative Session — February 3, 2024

The first of the two sessions of the annual Claremont School District meeting, held under the official-ballot (SB 2) procedure of RSA 40:13 and generated from the meeting recording, the nine-document posted packet, and both the draft and the approved minutes. This is not a school board meeting. The body in the room was the school district’s legislative body, the registered voters of Claremont, and the six board members who attended sat in it as voters; four of them spoke from the floor. Ninety-three minutes, five warrant articles, and the whole of the voters’ power exercised once: a correction of 2015 to 2025 in Article 5. The FY25 operating budget went to the March 12 ballot exactly as the board wrote it, at $39,582,407 against a default of $39,371,774. The presiding officer was not the district’s elected moderator, and no record in this meeting shows how she came to preside. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

Body
Claremont School District, legislative body (the voters), first session of the annual school district meeting under RSA 40:13. The packet’s Rules of Procedure open with it: “Claremont is an SB2 community, where determination of the final School Budget is conducted through two sessions.” A district moderator presides, not the board chair (RSA 197:19).
Date and statutory window
Saturday, February 3, 2024, 9:00 a.m. Snow date Saturday, February 10 (recorded in the board’s approved minutes of January 17, 2024, not in this packet). RSA 40:13, III requires the first session to fall “between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January, inclusive of those Saturdays”. The last Monday in January 2024 was the 29th, so the window ran February 3–10: the meeting sat on the first day of it and the snow date was the last (flag 12).
Start time
Advertised 9:00 a.m.; the recording opens before the gavel with the presiding officer explaining the delay: “We’re supposed to start at nine, but we’re going to give it a few more minutes because there’s a line of people trying to get in” (0:00:00), and she calls the meeting to order at 0:00:20. Both sets of minutes give 9:05 a.m. The session closes at 1:31:21 and adjourns at 1:32:30. No nonpublic session; a deliberative session cannot hold one.
Location
Stevens High School Auditorium. Agenda and both sets of minutes agree, and the presiding officer says so on the recording: “I notice that some people walked into the auditorium during the presentation” (0:50:05). The 2023 session’s records disagreed with themselves about auditorium versus gymnasium; this year’s do not.
Presiding officer
Charlene Lovett, styled “interim moderator” by the agenda, the Rules of Procedure and both sets of minutes. She self-identifies at 0:00:20: “My name is Charlene Lovett, and I am the interim moderator today.” The district’s elected moderator, Tracy Pope, is not mentioned anywhere: a scan of the dialogue file for Pope, Tracy, Tracey and Traci returns nothing, and neither set of minutes names her. Neither does either record show a moderator pro tempore being chosen, which is what RSA 197:20 and RSA 671:33, III provide for: flag 1.
Board composition
Seven seats, six members present. Present and introduced by the chair at 0:02:48: Heather Whitney (chair), Frank Sprague (vice chair), Candace Crawford, Jennifer Gallagher, Arlene Hawkins, Whitney Skillen. Bonnie Miles absent: “Bonnie Miles is unable to attend this morning” (0:03:43), and both sets of minutes record it. The board sat as voters; the minutes give four of them wards when they spoke from the floor.
The money
Article 2 as read and as balloted: general operating budget $36,117,407, plus grant and food-service funds $3,465,000, total $39,582,407. Default: general $35,906,774, total $39,371,774. Difference $210,633. Three cost-item articles followed: secretaries $102,860, maintenance $87,789, paraprofessionals $189,210, each for FY2024–25. Every figure here is in the business administrator’s deck and matches what was read aloud.

HIGH Nothing in this meeting’s record shows how the presiding officer came to preside

The elected moderator of the Claremont School District was Tracy Pope. She is not in this record. A scan of the dialogue file for Pope, Tracy, Tracey and Traci returns nothing; neither the agenda, the Rules of Procedure, the draft minutes nor the approved minutes mention her, her absence or any reason for it. What all four documents say instead is that Charlene Lovett was the “interim moderator”, and she says it herself at 0:00:20: “My name is Charlene Lovett, and I am the interim moderator today.”

“Interim moderator” is not an office New Hampshire law creates. A school district moderator has “all the statutory duties, powers and authority granted to town moderators” (RSA 197:19), and the law provides exactly two routes to somebody else in that chair:

RSA 197:20, where the moderator is absent: the district clerk “shall act as moderator of any meeting until a moderator pro tempore shall be chosen, if the moderator is absent or the office has become vacant”. The choosing is the meeting’s.

RSA 671:33, III, where the office is vacant: “Vacancies in the office of moderator shall be filled by vote at a school meeting or election, provided that, until a replacement is chosen, the school district clerk shall serve as moderator or shall appoint a moderator pro tempore.”

Both routes run through the district clerk. Claremont had one: Noelle Kronberg, appointed to the vacant clerk’s seat on 15 November 2023 under RSA 197:26 and present in this room writing the minutes; and if the signature on these minutes, “school board clerk”, is read strictly, then the district clerk’s office was empty and there was no one who could lawfully have appointed a moderator pro tempore at all. Either reading leaves the same gap. Neither route is used on this record. No moderator pro tempore is chosen by the meeting, none is appointed by the clerk, and the phrase pro tem does not occur anywhere in the transcript. The board could not have supplied the deficiency itself: RSA 197:26 lets a school board fill vacancies “on the board, and in other district offices, except that of moderator”.

What this flag does not say. It does not say the session was invalid, and nothing here suggests concealment: the arrangement was plainly made in advance (the posted agenda already names her “Charlene Lovett, Interim Moderator” and the packet’s Rules of Procedure are headed the same way) and the interim superintendent thanked her publicly at 0:02:18. It is possible the meeting acclaimed her before the recording’s first transcribed word, but the recording begins before the gavel, with her explaining the delay at the door, and runs continuously to the call to order at 0:00:20. It is possible the clerk appointed her under RSA 671:33, III, but that route presupposes a vacancy nothing in the record establishes, and no appointment is recorded. The point is that a citizen reading the district’s own record of its own annual meeting cannot find out on what authority the meeting was run.

MEDIUM The Rules of Procedure describe the voters’ amending power without any of the limits the statute puts on it, and without the one protection it gives them

At a deliberative session the voters cannot pass or defeat anything; the ballot does that five weeks later. Their entire power is the power to amend, and RSA 40:13, IV both grants it and bounds it: “Warrant articles may be amended at the first session, subject to the following limitations:” (a) “Warrant articles whose wording is prescribed by law shall not be amended.” (b) “Warrant articles that are amended shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote on the main motion, as amended.” (c) “No warrant article shall be amended to eliminate the subject matter of the article.” followed by the sentence that matters most to a budget meeting: “An amendment that changes the dollar amount of an appropriation in a warrant article shall not be deemed to violate this subparagraph.”

So the sheet omits (a), omits the subject-matter limit in (c), and omits the express permission in (c) that a change to a dollar amount is not an elimination of subject matter. MEDIUM The rule sheet’s account of what a voter may do about a doubted vote is not what the statute provides

Every substantive decision at this session was taken on an unrecorded voice vote: the 2023 minutes, the Article 5 amendment, the restriction on reconsideration, adjournment. In each the presiding officer declared the result and no count was taken. What a voter may do about that is therefore the whole of the check on the chair, and the district’s Rules of Procedure state it as follows, read aloud at 0:07:38: “The moderator may determine the method of voting. However, any voter who does the accuracy of any non county vote may require the moderator to determine a vote by counted show of hands or counted standing vote”; the packet sheet has the clean version, “any voter who doubts the accuracy of any non-counted vote”.

RSA 40:13, IV makes this session “governed by the provisions of RSA 40:4, 40:4-a, 40:4-b, 40:4-f, and 40:6-40:10”, and two of those sections are directly on the point:

RSA 40:4-a, I: “5 voters who are present may make a request in writing prior to a vote by voice vote or division vote that the vote be taken by secret written ballot”, and “Upon receiving such a request, the moderator shall conduct the vote by secret 'yes-no' ballot.” The same section gives a recount as of right on the request of 5 voters where the margin is within 10 per cent.

RSA 40:4-b: where a declared vote is “immediately and before any other business is begun, be questioned in writing or orally by 7 or more of the voters present”, the moderator “shall retake the vote by secret 'yes-no' ballot.”

Three differences. The statutory remedy is a secret written ballot, not a counted show of hands. The statutory threshold is 5 voters (in advance, in writing) or 7 (immediately after), not “any voter”. And the statutory right to a recount is not mentioned at all. The rule sheet is more generous on who may object and materially less generous on what they get, and a voter who followed it and asked for a counted show of hands would not have learned that seven of them could have compelled a secret ballot instead.

The district knows the mechanism. One year earlier its own deliberative session ran the $1,000,000 amendment to a secret ballot on exactly this footing: the 2023 minutes record that “Moderator Pope has received a written request for a secret ballot on Article 2. All citizens that requested a secret ballot are present”, and the result, 41 in favour to 27 opposed, is the only counted vote anywhere in this district’s two deliberative sessions. The same rule sheet, with the same sentence, was in use both years.

Recorded as MEDIUM, not higher, because nothing on this record turned on it: no vote was doubted, no objection was made, and there is no sign anybody wanted a count. The concern is that the document the district hands its voters describes their rights wrongly, and has done so for at least two annual meetings.
