Claremont School Board — January 21, 2020

Public hearing on the fiscal 2021 (2020–21) school budget followed by a regular board meeting, summarized from the recording; no agenda, packet or minutes are online. The superintendent announced that the proposed elementary restructuring was postponed a year, and most of the hearing's speakers addressed it. The board then sent a $38,202,661 operating budget and five warrant articles to the deliberative session, after a motion to cut the two new capital reserve articles to $350,000 and $150,000 failed 2–4. OBSERVATION No minutes are online, and the recording alone leaves the vote record incomplete

RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification, in force on this date) requires minutes that record the members present, the subject matter, final decisions, and the names of the mover and seconder of each motion, open to inspection within five business days. None for this meeting are in the district's public shares, which begin in 2022; that is a limit of what the district posts online, not evidence that minutes were never kept, and the board approved the previous week's minutes at this meeting (1:46:06). The gap matters here because the recording does not settle the vote record. The roll call names all seven members but the answers run together (1:45:33); the one tally announced, on Whitney's reduction, is "4 to 2" with "all the eyes were Heather and Rebecca. The nays were the other four of us" (2:12:23, 2:12:29), which accounts for six members; and the decisive vote sending the budget forward was a voice vote with no tally (2:13:22). Whether a member was out of the room, abstained or was simply not counted cannot be told from the tape. Graded an observation under the project's rule for missing records.

OBSERVATION The new business administrator could not explain several inherited budget lines and had no work papers for the prior budget

Three and a half months into the job, Richard Seaman told the hearing he "could never find substantiation" for a $65,000 transfer and dropped it (0:11:42); that "I don't know why 380 was put in there" for out-of-district special education, against $750,000 spent in fiscal 2019 (0:12:44); and, asked about bus maintenance and the Disnard line, "I didn't prepare the budget last year, and I don't have any work papers or anything that tells me how the budget last year was built" (0:59:17). In the regular meeting he also could not answer a resident's complaint that a snow-plowing contractor paid "$30,000 a year" was not doing the work while district trucks plowed at overtime, and promised to report back to the board (1:51:13, 1:51:20). He committed to be able to "speak backwards" on next year's budget (0:59:44). The admissions are candid; the underlying point, that the district's prior-year budget could not be reconstructed from its own records, is worth testing against the fiscal 2019 and 2020 audits. No verified rule is cited.

Sources: the recording at the times linked above. No verified rule applies.

OBSERVATION Two $500,000 reserve articles reached the board a week before it voted, with amounts the administrator called estimates and at least one member did not know the basis for

Carolyn Towle: "that was the first time I saw the Warren article last week" (1:56:34), and later "I did not know where those five and five figures came from. So that communication" (2:11:27). Seaman explained the figure as roughly $1 million of warrant articles over the last three years and said the $500,000 was one he "admittedly, I was estimating based on historic costs" (2:03:22, 2:03:45). Whitney, who sits on the capital improvement committee, feared "$1 million in Warren articles" would draw "a protest vote" (1:55:21, 2:02:50). The reduction failed and both articles went forward at $500,000. Nothing unlawful is suggested: setting warrant-article amounts is the board's call. It is recorded because the board's own members said on tape that they had not discussed the amounts before the vote.

Sources: the recording at the times linked above. No verified rule applies.

POSITIVE The budget hearing met the SB 2 deadline, disclosed the default budget, and the board finalized the budget only after testimony closed

RSA 40:13, II-a requires SB 2 budget hearings under RSA 32:5 to be held "on or before the third Tuesday in January"; January 21, 2020 was the third Tuesday, so the hearing met the deadline on its last day. RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires the default budget to be "disclosed and presented for questions and discussion at the first budget hearing"; the business administrator presented it at 0:36:16 and answered a question on it (0:55:13). RSA 32:5, I (2019 codification) provides that the governing body "after the conclusion of public testimony shall finalize the budget"; the chair closed the hearing (1:36:39) before the board took up the budget in its regular meeting. The reduction debate was held in public, a mislabeled amendment was caught and corrected (2:07:27), and the one split vote was announced with the names of the members in the minority (2:12:29).

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a and XI(a) (last amended 2019, 192:2; in force on this date); RSA 32:5, I (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 246:1).
