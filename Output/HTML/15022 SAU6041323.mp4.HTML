SAU 6 Board, April 13, 2023

Regular meeting of the SAU 6 board, the first under Chair Steven Horsky, with eight of twelve members present at the start. The board goes straight into a nonpublic session under RSA 91-A:3, II(c) and seals its minutes for fifty years, corrects the March 30 minutes, elects Arlene Hawkins SAU treasurer, reviews a budget report showing a projected surplus, hears about a forced accounting-software upgrade, adopts superintendent-evaluation policy CBI, sends exit-interview procedure GCQB-R to a third reading, approves the board calendar for the website and sets aside the policy subcommittee created two weeks earlier. MEDIUM Nonpublic minutes sealed for fifty years with no statutory determination stated

The entry was properly framed: the agenda printed "RSA 91-A:3, II (c )" with its text, the chair's motion recited the reputation ground (0:01:54), and the vote was a roll call, as RSA 91-A:3, I(b) requires. The seal was not. RSA 91-A:3, III, as codified for this date, allows nonpublic minutes to be withheld beyond 72 hours only "by recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" when "it is determined that divulgence of the information likely would affect adversely the reputation of any person" other than a member, render a proposed action ineffective, or pertain to terrorism (statute). The vote was recorded and unanimous (0:05:48). No determination was stated: the motion was "that the minutes of the closed session be sealed for 50 years" (0:05:29), and the minutes record only the motion and the roll call. The II(c) entry ground points toward the reputation finding, but the statute asks for the determination itself. Fifty years is ten times the five-year seal the Claremont board voted eight days earlier.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b), II(c) and III (2022 codification); draft minutes, item III.C; Claremont School Board, April 5, 2023, flag 1

RSA 91-A:2, II requires minutes to be "open to public inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting" (statute); for a Thursday, April 13 meeting that was Thursday, April 20. The only copy located was filed with the May 11 packet and created on Drive on Monday, May 8, 2023; the PDF's own modified time is the same morning, so nothing dates it earlier. The search was two-stage: this meeting's own folder, 6. SAU SB 4.13.23, holds no 4/13 minutes (its seven files are listed in the appendix), and a title search for 4.13.23 returned only that folder. Drive's created time shows when the public could see the document on the share, not whether a draft could be inspected at the office on request; nothing in the record says one could. Because the meeting was noticed and packeted and the minutes exist, this is graded MEDIUM under the project's severity rule, not HIGH.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (minutes within 5 business days); draft minutes (Drive metadata)

MEDIUM The board chose a treasurer but still no secretary

RSA 194-C:5, I requires the SAU board to meet between April 1 and June 1 and to "organize by choosing a chairperson, a secretary, and a treasurer" (statute). On March 30 the board chose a chair and a vice chair (an office the statute does not name). Tonight, inside the window, it chose Arlene Hawkins as treasurer (0:19:02), the chair stressing that the office is "not optional" (0:17:20). No secretary was nominated, and the agenda and minutes still carry a "Secretary Roll Call of Attendance" with no one in the office. The chair also announced there would be no June meeting unless needed (0:07:38), leaving one scheduled meeting, May 11, before the window closed. The project's later notes record the secretary's office still vacant in August 2023.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, I (1996, 298:3; unamended); draft minutes, items V and VI.2; SAU 6 Board, March 30, 2023, flag 2

OBSERVATION A budget report the public could not see, whose year the board could not agree on

The budget update was a report "right from Tyler" (0:24:52) that is not among the seven documents in the packet. Members disagreed about which fiscal year it showed (0:19:18 to 0:24:50), and the chair told viewers the year that began July 1, 2022 is "fiscal year 22" (0:21:18). The figures in the record do not match each other: the transcript has a surplus of "about 180,000" (0:20:06), the chair later says "if I see 118" (0:28:46), and the minutes record $118,000.00; the merit-pool balance is read as "36,190 and 41" (0:25:24) and recorded as $35,000. The chair says the board had eliminated merit pay, yet the line is still funded; his proposal to return it to taxpayers ended without a motion. No rule fixes how an SAU board reviews a mid-year report, so this is an observation; a monthly budget-to-actual report in the packet, which the board's own calendar now calls for, would let the public follow these numbers.
