This is a short introduction to a Right-to-Know request portal, built for the Claremont School District. It shows how a request would move through the portal. It also explains why the project is asking a company called Anthropic for support.

New Hampshire's Right-to-Know Law, R-S-A ninety-one A, gives every citizen the right to inspect public records. When a record is not immediately available, the District has five business days to produce it, deny the request in writing, or say in writing when it will answer.

In September of twenty twenty-five, the School Board adopted Policy E-H. It directs the Superintendent's procedures to include, in the policy's words, a method for logging and tracking both the requests and the responses. The policy also allows that log to be posted on the District's website.

The portal is one way to do that. A resident files a request online. The portal logs it, puts it in line, and prepares a draft response. District staff review and send every response. Then the request and the response appear on a public log.
