This video describes a proposal for the Claremont School District. The idea is to use Claude, an A-I model made by Anthropic, to help the district answer the people it serves, on time and within the law. It also explains why the project is asking Anthropic for support.

A school district owes information to many people, and the rules differ for each. Taxpayers and the public may inspect governmental records under New Hampshire's Right-to-Know Law. When a record is not immediately available, the district has five business days to respond.

Parents may see their own child's education records. Three laws set three deadlines for that: forty-five days under federal rule, fourteen days under one state law, and ten business days under a newer state law. A careful system follows the shortest.

Students take over those rights at eighteen. Former students can ask for their own records for as long as the district keeps them. Teachers and staff need the policies that govern their work, including the version that was in force on a given date.

The record is large. The School Board's policy index lists two hundred eighty-four policies, and one hundred twenty of them are dated before twenty fifteen. The archive of recorded meetings holds three hundred nineteen recordings. A resident with one free evening cannot read all of that.
