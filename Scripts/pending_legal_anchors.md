## CORRECTION to an already-catalogued anchor — act on this first

`RSA 186-C:18, III` in the catalog asserts a **"80%-of-entitlement floor"** on proration.
**That floor did not exist before 2025** — it was added by 2025 N.H. HB 2 §137. Citing the
catalogued holding against any pre-FY2026 meeting would wrongly imply the State broke the law
by funding catastrophic aid at 67.5%. Verified against the 2023 and 2024 Justia codifications.
Also confirmed: **no 90% figure appears anywhere in that section**, so the "supposed to fund up
to 90%" heard at the 11/19/24 finance meeting is a misstatement of the governing rate.

Roughly half the backlog predates the 2025 and 2026 session laws, so **holdings need an
"in force from" column** before the remaining 78 pages are built.

## Second vintage hazard — CORRECTED 2026-08-29, read this carefully

`RSA 198:40-a` (adequacy per-pupil amounts). **My earlier note here was wrong in a way that
over-corrects, and pages were told not to cite the statute at all. That is too strong.**

The current amounts — **$4,100 base / $2,300 F&R / $2,100 SPED / $800 ELL, third-grade
reading repealed** — were enacted by **2023, 79:150, effective July 1, 2023, "for the
biennium beginning July 1, 2023."** So:

- **Meetings from July 2023 onward: the current statutory text IS the right one to quote.**
- **Meetings before July 2023:** the figures in force were **$3,866.18 / $1,933.08 /
  $2,079.89 / $756.43**, plus $756.43 per third grader below proficient. Those are the
  **FY2024 ESTIMATE** rates from NHDOE's **15 November 2022** explainer, computed under the
  pre-amendment formula — not "FY2024–25 actuals" as I first wrote. Cite the NHDOE year
  explainer, not the statute, for any pre-July-2023 meeting.

Corroborated three ways: gc.nh.gov's text and source note; the 8/2/23 packet's Exhibit H
side-by-side "FY24 Estimated / FY24 Estimated Actual" tables; and Mary Henry on tape at
that meeting — "base adequacy aid went from 3866 to 4100."

## Third: Title IX rule vintage

## verify_quotes.py — a known limitation agents must work around

Its stutter collapse is `\b(\w+)( \1\b)+`, which handles single-word repeats only. It
**cannot** collapse:
- repeats containing an apostrophe — `we've we've`
- phrase repeats — `we want to we want to`, `at the at the`, `this is this is`

So those will always show as unmatched. **Preserve them verbatim in the quotation** rather
than tidying them to satisfy the tool. A quotation over **600 characters** also exceeds the
matcher's window and silently swallows the quotes that follow it — split long ones.
