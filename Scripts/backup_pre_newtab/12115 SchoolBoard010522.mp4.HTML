Claremont School Board — January 5, 2022

Regular meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The board ratified four collective bargaining agreements (secretaries, maintenance, paraprofessionals and administrators) and sent each to the ballot as a warrant article, approved a $37,243,825 fiscal year 2023 budget for the January 18 public hearing, accepted a $500 gift to Bluff School and the 2022-23 Stevens High School Program of Studies, and discussed lockdown drills. A dispute over a middle school basketball cluster ran from the citizens' comment period, where the district's nurse coordinator answered two parents from the podium, into an added Covid discussion item on which no vote was taken. OBSERVATION A district nurse used the citizens' comment period to answer two named critics and described a student's quarantine classification in public

Two speakers, the middle school basketball coach and a parent, criticized how a team cluster had been counted and how their calls to the nurse coordinator had gone (0:04:22, 0:07:10). The parent herself told the board that her husband had tested positive and that her son was unvaccinated. The nurse coordinator then came to the podium "not only as a citizen but also as an employee" (0:10:58), said the coach "started screaming at me" and that the parent had called her a liar, and stated: "Nobody classified her son as being positive. He's been classified as a household exposure because he has a family member who tested positive." She also mentioned another, unnamed student "who plays on that same team who was sent home sick today". Later, when the parent returned to the podium, the nurse said "I don't want to talk" (2:17:40) and the chair asked both to stop.

Two points for a reviewer. First, FERPA's consent rule, 34 CFR 99.30(a) as it stood in 2022, bars a school from disclosing "personally identifiable information from the student's education records" without written parental consent, subject to listed exceptions; a school nurse's record of a student's exposure status is the kind of information the rule concerns. The parent had already made the family's circumstances public, and the nurse's statement largely corrected how the parent had characterized them, so whether anything was disclosed in the regulatory sense is doubtful; this page does not reach that question. Second, the superintendent said at 2:20:18: "There is a degree to which we can never speak about the confidentiality." The better practice his remark describes, answering process questions in general terms and handling a family's case privately, was not followed at the podium. The chair did cut the exchange short at 0:14:28.

Sources: 34 CFR 99.30(a) (2021 annual edition, in force January 2022; last amended 69 FR 21671, 2004): written consent before disclosing personally identifiable information from education records.

OBSERVATION The chair said he had discussed a contract term with a couple of board members outside the meeting

Explaining the administrators' agreement at 0:45:55, the chair said: "I talked to a couple of board members ex parte, and that was important to them as well." RSA 91-A:2-a, I, in force on this date, requires a public body to "deliberate on matters over which they have supervision, control, jurisdiction, or advisory power only in meetings", and ¶II bars using "communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members" to "circumvent the spirit and purpose" of the chapter. The chair plus "a couple" of members is three of seven, short of a quorum, so no meeting occurred, and he disclosed the conversations on the record himself. The point to note is that the term in question, a superintendent's discretion to add up to $5,000 to a principal's salary, was settled in part outside the meeting before the public discussion of it. It carried by voice vote without further discussion.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2022 codification; source 2008, 303:4): deliberation only in meetings; outside communications not to circumvent the chapter.

OBSERVATION The finance officer described catastrophic special education aid as 90% of costs above about $60,000; the statute then in force set an 80% share, subject to proration

At 1:09:06 the assistant superintendent for finance said: "I believe once they go above $60,000 or something in that range, we the state, the state pays 90% of what's above 60,000." RSA 186-C:18, III, as it read in January 2022, made a district eligible when a pupil's costs exceed 3 1/2 times the state average expenditure per pupil, and left the district "liable for 3½ times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil ... plus 20 percent of the additional cost, up to 10 times" that average, so the state's share in that band was 80 percent, not 90. The statute also provided that an insufficient appropriation "shall be prorated proportionally based on entitlement", so actual reimbursement could be lower still. The $60,000 figure is a fair approximation of the 3 1/2-times threshold; the percentage is not. He framed it as his belief, and catastrophic aid is a revenue estimate rather than an appropriation voters approve, so this is recorded as an observation about the accuracy of information given to the public.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III(a) and (b) (2022 codification; source note ends 2021, 209:2, eff. Oct. 9, 2021): threshold of 3 1/2 times the state average, district pays 20 percent above it up to 10 times, pro rata reduction when underfunded. The 80 percent proration floor in today's text was added in 2025 and does not apply to this meeting.

OBSERVATION A policy barred citizens' comments on agenda items, and was invoked while a speaker asked for budget detail

At 0:17:43 a member reminded the chair "that we've made a policy where you cannot speak on agenda items", after Patrick Adrian had asked that the budget's position cuts be named before the public hearing (0:16:34). The chair let the speakers finish and the Covid questions were taken up under the added item. The policy text is not in any available record. In January 2022 no statute set a minimum school board comment period; RSA 189:74 took effect September 6, 2022. The rule was therefore within the board's discretion, but its effect on this night was that the budget, the contracts and the Program of Studies could not be addressed by the public at this meeting, and the recording shows no public comment taken during those items. The budget itself went to a public hearing on January 18.

Sources: RSA 189:74 (source: 2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022): school board public comment period, not in force on this date; district citizens' comment policy (not located).

POSITIVE Each labor agreement's cost was stated year by year in public before ratification, and the cost items went to the voters as separate warrant articles

For all four agreements the finance officer read out the changes and "the financial impact" for each fiscal year, separating local costs from the one-time stipends paid "out of the CARES three money" (0:28:03 to 0:46:19), and the board negotiator for each unit spoke to it. Each ratification motion sent the agreement to the ballot as its own warrant article. That is what RSA 273-A:3, II(b) requires of a public employer: "Only cost items shall be submitted to the legislative body" for approval, and the agreement's costs are voted on separately from the operating budget. Reading the per-year cost of each unit into the record before the vote gave the public the figures the warrant articles would carry.

Sources: RSA 273-A:3, II(b) and RSA 273-A:1, IV (cost items; 2013, 244:1 and 2014, 13:1, both in force on this date), RSA chapter 273-A.
