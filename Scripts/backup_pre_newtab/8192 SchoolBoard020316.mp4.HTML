Claremont School Board — February 3, 2016

Regular meeting held after the district's deliberative session the same evening, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. Police Chief Alex Scott briefed the board on pre-employment drug screening, the superintendent proposed revisiting class rank, candidates were invited to address the next meeting, and the board voted to place Articles 1 through 9 on the March 8 ballot. OBSERVATION A parent described a guidance counselor offering to adjust her daughter's class standing for a scholarship; the board did not take it up on the record

Speaking on the class-rank item at 0:40:06, a parent recounted calling the high school to ask where her daughter stood: "The guidance counselor said, where does she need to be?" and, after she answered, "Call me back a few minutes. Yeah, we can make that work. Are you serious?" This is one speaker's account of a past conversation, with no counselor named and no year given, and nothing on the recording tests it. It matters because the board was weighing whether to keep class rank at all, and an account that a ranking could be adjusted on request goes to its integrity. The chair's reply (0:40:29) addressed the piecemeal history of the grading change, not the account itself. No follow-up is on the record.

Sources: No verified rule; speaker's characterization only. See the timeline at 0:38:49.

OBSERVATION The board voted to place Articles 1 through 9 on the ballot, a step the SB 2 statute already made automatic

At 0:53:09 the clerk said that the motion on each article usually states it is being placed on the warrant for a given date, "And that wasn't done tonight", and the board then voted on Brent Ferland's motion to place Articles 1 through 9 "as written and voted upon in the public. Deliberative session" on the March 8 ballot. RSA 40:13, VI, as it read in 2016, provided that "All warrant articles shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote, including warrant articles as amended by the first session." The board's vote therefore added nothing legally, and its absence would have taken nothing away; no article had been amended at the deliberative session, so there was no wording question for the board to settle. The practice is harmless, but it reflects a belief that the ballot depended on this vote.

Sources: RSA 40:13, VI (2017 codification; source note ends 2014, so in force on February 3, 2016); the deliberative session page.

OBSERVATION Members asked the administration to "market and publicize" the budget before the vote; the electioneering statute later enacted was not yet in force

At 0:50:52 Irish asked for a larger effort than usual to "market and publicize" the budget and "get the realities of the tax impact out there", with the annual report posted online and district email outreach; a parent later suggested the superintendent write a newspaper article (0:56:26). Explaining the budget and its tax effect is ordinary public information. The line between information and advocacy by staff became a statutory question in New Hampshire only with RSA 659:44-a, which bars a public employee from electioneering "while in the performance of his or her official duties" and took effect January 1, 2017, eleven months after this meeting. It does not apply here, and nothing on the recording shows material that was produced. Recorded so that a reader applying the later statute does not apply it to 2016.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a (source note 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017): not in force on February 3, 2016.
