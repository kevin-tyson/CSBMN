Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — May 6, 2019

Joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The two bodies heard updates on the city manager search and the new superintendent's contract, a state foster-care presentation, an update on the syringe service program at Valley Regional Hospital, and discussions of school funding, youth mental health, election turnout, a state diversity report, energy initiatives and shared city-school services, and set a quarterly meeting schedule. Only three members of the nine-member council were present. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION Only three of the City Council's nine members attended, so the joint meeting had a quorum on the school side only

The clerk's roll (0:01:12, 0:01:25) names three council members: Councilor Jonathan Stone, Assistant Mayor Allen Damren and Mayor Charlene Lovett. The city's charter provides for "a city council of nine (9) councilors" and says "A majority of the council shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business", and the city's own council page lists nine seats. The school board, by contrast, had six of seven members. A joint meeting is two bodies, and each body's quorum is counted against its own membership, so the council side could not transact business that evening. It did not try to: the schedule, the next agenda and the follow-ups were settled by consensus, and the only vote was the adjournment. The council's own notice and minutes for this date are kept by the city clerk and were not inspected; this page makes no finding about them. Recorded as an observation because nothing was decided on the council's behalf.

Sources: Claremont City Charter, sections 16 (nine councilors) and 21 (majority quorum), in a copy hosted by the Town of Newmarket; the revision date of this copy was not established; City of Claremont, City Council page (nine members), read 2026-09-26.

OBSERVATION Three board members built the budget with administrators over "30, 35 hours"; whether that group was a noticed committee is not shown

Answering a complaint about low turnout, the chair described the FY2020 budget process: "Mike, Jason and I met with Corey and Keith and every single administrator" and "We probably spent 30, 35 hours in the process" (1:19:16). Three members of a seven-member board are not a quorum, so these sessions were not meetings of the full board under RSA 91-A:2, I. But RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d), in force throughout this period, makes "any committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto" a public body in its own right. If the three met as a budget committee of the board, those sessions needed notice and minutes. The recording does not say how the group was constituted, and no committee records from 2019 are online to check. The chair's point was that the process was open ("We were talking about it all the time at our board meetings"), and that is fairly recorded here too.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (2019 codification; source note ends 2008, 354:1): committees and subcommittees are public bodies; RSA 91-A:2, I and II (2019 codification): meeting defined by a quorum; notice and minutes.
