Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — March 30, 2017

Summary of a joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The new city manager introduced himself; the two bodies, two state representatives, Valley Regional Hospital and Southwestern Community Services discussed childhood lead screening and abatement; and the district's lawsuit counsel, the superintendent and members discussed state education funding, the phase-out of the district's stabilization grant and the voucher bill SB 193. No votes were taken. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION A member said the stabilization grant phase-out came with no change in the law; the statute then in force wrote the 4 percent phase-out in

Adrian asked why the phase-out of the district's stabilization grant was not grounds for a lawsuit, saying "there wasn't any any legislative decision or any change in the law to my understanding" (1:52:05, 1:52:50). The mayor had just described the district as "still losing 4%" (1:35:06), and the superintendent put the district's grant at $5.8 million, "going to be reduced to $0" (1:30:03). The adequacy-aid statute as codified in 2016 did provide for this: for fiscal year 2017 a municipality received 96 percent of its fiscal year 2012 stabilization grant, "reduced by 4 percent of the amount of the 2012 education grant for each fiscal year thereafter". The superintendent's link of the change to the Dover case and a September 2016 Supreme Court decision (2:07:06, 2:07:37) is his characterization; this page did not verify it. Whether the phase-out was constitutional is a legal question the page does not answer; the point recorded is only that the reduction was statutory, not an administrative decision.

Sources: RSA 198:41, IV(a) and IV(d) (2016 codification; source note ends 2015, 276:139 and 2016, 8:4, 5 and 85:9): stabilization grant "96 percent for fiscal year 2017", then reduced by 4 percent each fiscal year.

POSITIVE Lead screening was coordinated in public across the district, the city, the hospital and a community agency, ahead of any state mandate

The district's policy asks for lead testing on entry to pre-kindergarten or kindergarten (0:06:39); the superintendent said the state called Claremont "the first district in New Hampshire" with such a policy (0:07:37), a claim this page did not verify. The policy had been drafted with the school nurses and the hospital's medical staff (0:06:09), and the city described a parallel abatement grant effort (0:10:26). SB 247, the state bill on universal testing, was still in the Senate that evening (0:38:31, 2:20:21). No statute required a school district to adopt a lead screening policy in March 2017, so the board acted ahead of the law, in a televised joint session. Graded POSITIVE on that basis, with the caution that the recording shows plans, not results.
