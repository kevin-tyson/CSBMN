MEDIUM No record says whether a quorum of the board attended, and the answer decides whether notice and minutes were owed

RSA 91-A:2, I defines a meeting as the convening of a quorum of a public body, able to communicate contemporaneously, to discuss or act on a matter within its jurisdiction, and excludes only collective-bargaining strategy, consultation with counsel, a party caucus, and the circulation of draft documents. A candidate forum is not excluded, and the caucus exclusion is unavailable to a school board elected on a non-partisan ballot (RSA 671:30). Choosing a superintendent is within the board’s jurisdiction, so the question is the quorum. The recording establishes two members (the chair, and Madden at 1:09:03); Howard said on April 1 he would film it. The only evidence of more is the candidate’s own hedged impression at 0:56:09, “I think are in the room right now.” If four or more members were present, 24 hours’ posted notice and minutes within five business days naming members and persons appearing were required (RSA 91-A:2, II); none exist. If fewer were present, neither was owed.

Points on each side. The chair behaved as a moderator: “it's so hard to sit here and not say anything” (0:36:57) and “No comment.” (0:57:19). But one employment term was discussed in the room, at 1:02:37, and the approved April 15 minutes say “The Board as a whole interviewed candidates”, which places the board’s own characterisation of its interviews in the record. Graded MEDIUM on the mitigated limb: the forum was announced with date, hours and room at the noticed April 1 meeting and listed in its approved minutes, and it was recorded and published. A roll call at the start would have settled the question.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I and II (meeting defined; notice; minutes; start/end-time and producer clauses from 2025, 112:1, eff. Aug. 22, 2025); RSA 671:30 (non-partisan ballot; 1979, 321:1); 4.1.26 Approved CSB Public Hearing & Meeting Minutes; 4.15.26 Approved CSB Meeting Minutes.

OBSERVATION Which board hires, and why the post can carry another title

RSA 194-C:5, III gives the SAU board the power to fix the salaries of all SAU personnel and to remove superintendents; chapter 194-C does not say who appoints one. On April 6 the SAU 6 board was the joint Claremont and Unity board, whose April 9 agenda lists twelve members and contains no superintendent item. Unity leaves the unit on July 1, 2026, after which the SAU board is the Claremont board (the chair of the SAU board says so on the April 9 recording, and the April 15 minutes record that “it will only be Claremont in the SAU as of July 1, 2026”). The Claremont board hiring the person who will lead the unit it is about to constitute is neither authorised nor forbidden in terms by the statute. The two titles in use (the chair on April 1: “our next superintendent or executive director”, show 17291, not deep-linked here; the candidate at 0:06:01) are lawful: under RSA 194-C:5, II(a) “School districts shall not be required to have a superintendent”; the superintendent services of RSA 194-C:4 are what is compulsory.

Sources: RSA 194-C:5, II(a) and III (1996, 298:3; never amended); RSA 194-C:4; 4.9.26 SAU6 Board Agenda; SAU 6 Board, April 9, 2026.
