Claremont School Board & City Council, Joint Meeting — June 21, 2018

Joint meeting of the Claremont School Board and the Claremont City Council, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares, and the city's records were not inspected. The city honored departing Superintendent Middleton McGoodwin, the new SAU 6 chair answered a citizen's call to open the superintendent search to the community, the two bodies agreed to hold a separate August 9 meeting on a shared public information officer, and they named education funding as their top 2019 legislative priority with two state representatives present. OBSERVATION Two public bodies met; neither body's quorum is fully established from the recording, and the district posts no record of the meeting

A joint meeting is two bodies, each with its own quorum and its own duty to keep minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II as it read in 2018 (minutes naming members and "persons appearing before the public bodies", open to inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting"). The school board's roll is audible in part (0:00:53); the City Council's is not, beyond Councilor Stone, the mayor and Councilor Pope, so the council's quorum cannot be confirmed from the tape. The district's Drive shares hold nothing older than 2022 (MAP.md section 72); the city's notice, agenda and minutes live with the city clerk and were not inspected, so this page makes no city-side negative. Minutes of the previous joint meeting were approved here two months after it (0:01:16), and the mayor asked for this meeting's minutes "sometime in July" to prepare for August (1:46:27); draft minutes may have been available sooner, and nothing on the recording shows a lapse. The project's missing-records rule is applied on its online-availability limb.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, I-II (2017 codification, in force throughout 2018).

OBSERVATION The board chair's account of shrinking catastrophic special education aid describes proration the statute then allowed

Sprague told the legislators that the state was "only paying 60% of above 55,000" and that the district must "pay the first 50,000" (1:09:52, 1:10:24); the two figures are his and do not agree with each other. As RSA 186-C:18, III read in 2018 (source note ending with 2017 amendments effective July 1, 2017), the state was liable for 80 percent of a pupil's special education costs above 3 1/2 times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil, and when the appropriation fell short payments were "prorated proportionally", with no floor (the 80-percent-of-entitlement floor dates only from 2025). A share below 80% therefore reflects proration within the statute, not a breach of it; the chair's point that costs were being shifted to the district is a policy argument, recorded here as his characterization.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III(a)-(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2017, 156:96-100, eff. July 1, 2017, so the text in force in June 2018).

OBSERVATION The SAU 6 chair promised community members a place in the superintendent search; a week later the interim search committee was formed of officers and staff only

A Ward 2 speaker asked that the selection process be opened to "concerned community members" (0:12:15). The new SAU 6 chair answered from the floor that the board had "not formed a committee" and "will be forming committees that that are not just the school board but are made up of the greater community" (0:13:03, 0:13:56). On June 28 the SAU 6 board formed the interim search committee from the board chairs and three administrators (show 9876), and on July 12 the chair distinguished the interim search from the permanent one, where, she said, the community would be involved far more broadly (show 9906). No statute governs who sits on a search committee. The observation is that the promise, as made here, was fulfilled if at all only for the permanent search, which is outside this page's record.

Sources: the recording at the times cited; the SAU 6 recordings of June 28 and July 12, 2018 (shows 9876 and 9906).
