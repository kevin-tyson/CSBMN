Claremont School Board (part 1 of 2) — December 6, 2017

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, summarized from part 1 of the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. Nine members of the public spoke in a 36-minute comment period dominated by the proposed budget cuts, a Bluff School lunch-drink complaint and a classroom head-lice spray; City Manager Ryan McNutt discussed the city budget, tax rate and property valuations; the board accepted a $1,600 donation for the One for All space; and administrators walked the board line by line through the list of cuts that would meet its directive of a FY19 budget $1.2 million below FY18, with their own recommended restorations. This file stops mid-discussion before the budget review ends. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION The superintendent briefed the board against a pending bill (SB 193), urged lobbying and proposed posting an advocacy analysis on the district website

In his report the superintendent summarized a Reaching Higher New Hampshire analysis of Senate Bill 193, called the program potentially "very detrimental for the communities like Claremont" (0:23:03), said it was expected to pass "Unless there is quite a bit of lobbying", and proposed to "put this document on our website just to inform our community" (0:23:32). Board member Sprague asked the audience to "make sure that, we are putting pressure on our representatives" (0:27:17). In fairness, the superintendent also said "I believe that parents have the right and should have the right to decide how their child will be educated" (0:27:31), and the figures he gave are his characterization of a bill still in the Legislature, not verified here. No New Hampshire rule verified for this page bars a school official from commenting on pending legislation: RSA 659:44-a, in force from January 1, 2017, forbids a public employee to electioneer on duty, defined as acting to influence "the vote of a voter on any question or office" (statute text), which reaches elections, not bills in the Legislature. Recorded as an observation so readers can weigh whether district channels (the website, a board meeting) should carry one side of a policy debate.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a, I and III (source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; same text in force in December 2017).
