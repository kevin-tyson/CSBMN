Claremont School Board — June 20, 2018

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. Six days after the SAU 6 board parted ways with Superintendent Middleton McGoodwin, three citizens thanked him and one criticized the board; a departing Stevens English teacher explained why she was leaving. The business administrator projected a FY18 fund balance of about $275,000 and described catastrophic-aid proration; the board heard the extended learning opportunities and high school scheduling committee updates, approved the middle school and preschool handbooks and four staff nominations, and heard that 59 names were on the resignation list. OBSERVATION The board was told the State pays back "50%" above the catastrophic-aid threshold; the statute then said 80 percent

Asked by the chair to explain catastrophic aid, the business administrator said that above three and a half times the state average cost "we're going to give you back 50%" and 100% above ten times (0:32:16), then that the State prorates, so "We get two thirds of that" and the rate has been "about 66 to 68%" (0:32:32, 0:32:47). RSA 186-C:18, III, as amended effective July 1, 2017, made the department "liable for 80 percent of the cost above" the 3 1/2 times threshold and for all costs above 10 times. The 50% figure understates the statutory share. The rest of the explanation fits the statute: it provides that an insufficient appropriation "shall be prorated proportionally based on entitlement", with no floor in 2018, which is also why "even though it's statutorily required that they pay us that" (0:32:47) overstates the entitlement. Benware's reading at 0:33:03 is close to the statutory position. Recorded as an observation: a misstatement in a briefing, not a decision.

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2017 codification; source 2017, 156:96-100, eff. July 1, 2017): 80 percent above 3 1/2 times; proportional proration.

OBSERVATION Grant signing authority was delegated at a meeting nobody could date, and the general assurances were signed without a vote

The superintendent asked the chair to sign the Department of Education general-assurances certification, a new requirement ("In the past, it was only the superintendent", 1:36:48). No rule was identified that requires a vote before the chair signs an assurance page. The point is traceability: with no minutes online for 2018, a citizen cannot find the meeting at which the delegation was made or its terms.

Sources: the recording at 1:36:48 to 1:37:54. No statute relied on.

OBSERVATION A departing teacher said she was never sent an exit survey; the superintendent said every leaver is; 59 names were on the list

Karen Collison said "I have not been sent a survey asking why I was leaving" (0:14:05) and gave her reasons in public instead. The superintendent answered that "every time a person resigns, they are sent an electronic exit interview" and that he would check (1:39:19). The two accounts of the exit process conflict on the record, and the check the superintendent promised falls after his departure. No legal rule is engaged; this is recorded because the board asked for staff-departure information at its June 6 meeting (June 6 page) and exit data is how it would get the reasons.

Sources: the recording at 0:14:05, 1:39:19, 1:47:24. No statute relied on.

OBSERVATION No agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted online

MAP.md section 71 records that neither district Drive share holds anything older than 2022 (checked 9/26/26), so this page rests on the recording alone. That is a limit of what the district posts online, not by itself evidence that records were never made: the board approved and corrected the June 6 minutes at this meeting (0:04:30 to 0:05:57), and members worked from a packet. RSA 91-A:2, II, as it read in 2018, required minutes to "include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions" and to be open to inspection "not more than 5 business days after the meeting". The requirement to name the mover and seconder of each motion was added later (2018, 244:1, effective January 1, 2019) and is not applied here. The project's missing-records rule is applied on its online-availability limb.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification; source note ends 2017, 165:1 and 234:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2018); Input/SupportingDocuments/MAP.md section 71.

The superintendent brought four nominations to the board, added to the agenda because they arrived that day (0:01:31), and explained the distinction a member asked about: the board has "no control over a person leaving", "But with nominations, we do need your approval" (1:43:25 to 1:43:41).
