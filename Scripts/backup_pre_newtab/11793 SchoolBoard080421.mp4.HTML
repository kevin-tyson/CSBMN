Claremont School Board — August 4, 2021

Regular meeting summary generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. OBSERVATION The reopening plan was adopted by voice vote after the chair declined a roll call, and the tally cannot be recovered from the recording

The board's main decision of the night, reopening schools under the administration's matrix with masks optional except on buses, was put to a voice vote at 0:42:11: "I don't see any need for a roll call vote." The chair's count implies at least one no: he asks 5 to 1, your neighbor? and says "So 3 to 214."; neither the count nor the names of the dissenters can be recovered. Horsky had argued against masking visitors at the minimal level, which makes him a plausible no, but the recording does not show it.

No statute required a roll call at an in-person meeting in 2021. RSA 91-A:2, II then required that minutes "shall include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions", and "The names of the members who made or seconded each motion". The mover (Petrin) and seconder (Whitney) are clear here. How each member voted is recorded only if the minutes chose to record it; the minutes are not available online. Two weeks later the board used roll calls for its matrix votes (August 18).

Sources: RSA 91-A:2 (2022 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1, so the text in force in 2021), paragraph II.

OBSERVATION Out-of-district pay-to-play was approved in one vote as a "placeholder" ahead of any policy reading

The athletic directors reported that out-of-district players had been admitted informally in the past with "no formal policy on it" (1:43:54). At 2:01:04 Horsky moved to "adopt the policy that's been presented" and send it to the policy committee; the chair called it "a placeholder policy for now because we have to go through first read second or third read", and Horsky then said he "wouldn't even call it a policy". The motion, amended to non-cut sports, passed by voice vote with one abstention (2:07:25).

Treating the vote as an interim practice rather than an adopted policy is consistent with what the chair said about readings. Whether the district's own policy-adoption rules allowed an interim rule of this kind cannot be checked, because the board's policies for 2021 are not available online, and no statute verified for this page governs it. The practical risk is the one the members named: a practice that governs which children may play, set without a written policy. At the end of the meeting ({T(564)}) Horsky noted the policy committee was a member short.

POSITIVE Rather than limit attendance at a community forum to fewer than a quorum, the board chose to post notice so every member could attend

Discussing the administration's Monday community forum on elementary restructuring, the chair first suggested that three members could go (0:34:41), fewer than the four needed for a quorum of seven. The superintendent proposed instead to post notice of the forum and have members attend without deliberating, and the chair agreed: "that way everybody wants to go. Can go".

Under RSA 91-A:2, I as it read in 2021, a meeting is "the convening of a quorum of the membership of a public body" to discuss or act on matters within its jurisdiction. A forum on restructuring the district's schools is squarely such a matter, so a quorum present there risks being a meeting. Posting notice in advance protects the public's right to know either way. The label non meeting used on tape is not a statutory category, and the protection holds only if members in fact refrained from deliberating; the forum itself is not in this project's record.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2 (2022 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1, so the text in force in 2021), paragraphs I and II.

POSITIVE The chair publicly corrected his own earlier statements about education freedom accounts, and the enacted statute bears out the correction

At 2:07:44 the chair said he had emailed members and wanted to correct on the record statements he had made about the state budget. His corrected account: an education freedom account carries only the state adequacy money, not a local share; eligibility is capped at "300% of, of poverty"; and there is no exemption for schools above a performance percentile.

The enacted law agrees on the two points this page could verify. RSA 194-F:1 defines an eligible student by household income "less than or equal to 300 percent of the federal poverty guidelines", and RSA 194-F:2 has the commissioner transfer "the per pupil adequate education grant amount under RSA 198:40-a, plus any differentiated aid", and the section names no local share. The chapter was enacted by 2021 N.H. Laws ch. 91, §431, effective August 24, 2021, three weeks after this meeting. The percentile point and the account of how the bill changed are the chair's characterization and were not verified. A public official correcting his own record, unprompted, is a practice worth noting.

Sources: RSA 194-F:1 (eligible student; 2021, 91:431, eff. Aug. 24, 2021); RSA 194-F:2 (funds transferred; 2021, 91:431, eff. Aug. 24, 2021).
