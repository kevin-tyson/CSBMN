Claremont School Board — January 31, 2018

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. OBSERVATION The board replaced its chair and vice chair in a vote that is not on the recording, and no minutes are online to show it

The recording opens at 0:00:08 with Sprague presiding and no roll call; the only on-tape trace of the change is Herzog's thanks to "Chris and Brian" for their service as "assistant chair and chair" (1:58:58). The Valley News reported that the board "voted, 5-2, on Wednesday night" to make Frank Sprague chair (and Rebecca Zullo vice chair, on Irish's motion), "just before the board entered a non-public session", and that the vote "was not recorded on public access television"; it names Rapp and Herzog as opposed. No law required the meeting to be televised. The vote had to be recorded in the minutes under RSA 91-A:2, II as it read in 2018 (members present and "final decisions"), and the nonpublic session required a motion stating its exemption and a roll call under RSA 91-A:3, I(b). None of this can be checked: the minutes exist only offline (neither district Drive share reaches back before 2022, MAP.md section 58), and the recording starts after both events. Graded as an observation on the project's online-availability limb; the account of the vote is the newspaper's, not the district's.

Sources: Valley News, February 2, 2018, Patrick O'Grady (reporting, not law); RSA 91-A:2, II (2017 codification, in force for all of 2018); RSA 91-A:3, I(b) (2017 codification).

OBSERVATION The moderator told the board the clerk appoints a moderator pro tem; the statute says only that one "shall be chosen"

Pope said the lawyers at her training advised that "there be a moderator pro tem appointed by the school board clerk", to step in if she could not attend or had to recuse herself on a teacher-contract article because she is married to a teacher (0:24:34, 0:32:28). RSA 197:20, unchanged since 1964, provides that the district clerk "shall act as moderator of any meeting until a moderator pro tempore shall be chosen, if the moderator is absent or the office has become vacant"; it does not itself say who chooses. The clerk-appointment rule the moderator described may come from another provision or from the training materials; this page has not verified it for 2018. The planning itself, naming a stand-in in advance and disclosing a possible conflict, is sound. Recorded because later meetings in this corpus turn on who may preside at a deliberative session.

Sources: RSA 197:20 (source note ends 1963, 120:5; stable throughout the corpus).

OBSERVATION A member's doubt about audio recording on buses was left open; state law then expressly allowed it with board authorization

Rapp said "it's against state law to record audio without permission" and the superintendent undertook to check (1:55:35, 1:56:02); the policy went to second reading with the question open. RSA 570-A:2, II(k), in force from January 1, 2017, provides that "It shall not be unlawful under this chapter for" a school bus owner or operator to make "an audio recording in conjunction with a video recording of the interior of the school bus" when the school board authorizes it, and forbids keeping the recording "longer than 10 school days" unless it is relevant to a disciplinary proceeding or a court orders otherwise. So the concern was fair, the answer is in the statute, and adopting the policy is the authorization the statute contemplates. The superintendent's statement that for gifts "The statute says 500" (1:57:12) was not verified for this page.

Sources: RSA 570-A:2, II(k) (source note ends 2016, 169:1 and 322:2, eff. Jan. 1, 2017; the 2017 codification agrees).
