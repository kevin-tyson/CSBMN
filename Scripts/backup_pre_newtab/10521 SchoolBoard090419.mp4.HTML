Claremont School Board — September 4, 2019

Regular meeting of the Claremont School Board, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The board approved all school handbooks, reappointed Pauline Pelletier to the CCTV board, heard the superintendent's opening-of-school report and State Representative Gary Merchant on the vetoed state budget, adopted the student discipline and due process policy on second reading, put a $20,000 Byrne Foundation gift over to a public hearing, set a budget retreat for October 20, and reorganized members' seats on the negotiating committees. OBSERVATION A state representative presented one side of the state budget impasse during the superintendent's report, and viewers were urged to call the governor

The chair brought Rep. Gary Merchant forward "to kind of explain to the public and to us" the difference between the legislature's budget and the governor's counterproposal (0:17:39). His figures were his own estimates ("The best I can calculate.", 0:18:58): about $4.5 million to Claremont over two years under the legislature's budget, of which Claremont "would probably lose about 3 million". He framed the choice as "Wall Street or ... Main Street" and asked viewers to "Call the governor's office" (0:24:17); the chair closed the topic with the same request (0:59:52). No one presented the governor's position. The figures matter to the district's budget, and the board was entitled to hear its representative. No rule identified restricts a school board from hearing legislators or taking a position on state funding. Recorded as an observation so a reader knows the dollar figures on this recording are one legislator's estimate during a live dispute, not a state calculation.

Sources: the recording as cited. No statutory rule identified.

POSITIVE The board held a $20,000 gift until it could hold the public hearing the statute requires

Presented with a $20,000 Byrne Foundation donation to the tech center, the chair asked whether it was "above the threshold" for a public hearing (0:30:53); members confirmed the $5,000 figure and the board deferred acceptance to a hearing at the start of the September 18 meeting instead of voting that night (0:31:08). RSA 198:20-b, III(a), as it read in 2019, provided: "For unanticipated funds in the amount of $5,000 or more, the school board shall hold a prior public hearing on the action to be taken", with notice of the time, place and subject published in a newspaper at least 7 days before. The chair also laid out the sequence correctly: hearing first, acceptance afterward on the agenda. (The threshold rose to $20,000 only in 2023; the $5,000 figure the board used was the law on this date.) Whether the hearing was noticed as required is for the September 18 page.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1): prior public hearing for unanticipated funds of $5,000 or more.

POSITIVE The student discipline policy was adopted on a second reading after committee review, to bring it in line with state law

On August 21 the administration told the board that its existing discipline policy gave less than state law provides and said nothing about due process (see the August 21 page). The policy committee reviewed it between readings, working on "time frames more than verbiage" (0:28:26), and the board adopted it on second and final reading (0:29:39). RSA 193:13, I, as it read in 2019, authorized the superintendent or a written designee to suspend "for a period not to exceed 10 school days", and the school board or its written designee, "following a hearing", to continue a suspension beyond 10 days. The adopted text is not online and was not reviewed for this page, so this flag credits the process (two readings, committee review, a stated aim of conforming to the statute) rather than the wording.

Sources: RSA 193:13, I(a)-(b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2017, 12:1): suspension up to 10 days; continuation by the board or its designee after a hearing.
