SAU 6 Board — September 20, 2021

Summary of a community forum on restructuring Claremont's elementary schools, generated from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript. The project's map files it under the SAU 6 Board and Cablecast titles it an SAU 6 meeting, but on the recording it is an administration-led forum: there is no call to order, roll call, motion or vote, and no board member can be identified taking part. In an hour the superintendent presented the case for grade-level schools with universal pre-K, the draft class-size configuration and the timeline, and took questions from a former board member, parents, a teacher, a former student and online viewers. Timestamps link to the same moment in the Cablecast recording.

OBSERVATION Catalogued as an SAU 6 Board meeting, this recording is an administration-led forum with no board business

MAP.md section 152 heads this recording "SAU 6 Board" and the Cablecast title calls it an "SAU6 Meeting". On the tape there is no call to order, roll call, motion or vote, the superintendent runs the whole session, and no board member can be identified among the speakers; the only former member heard is Patrick Adrian, speaking as a resident. The attribution report reaches the same conclusion ("Not a board business meeting"). Whether any board members attended in the audience cannot be determined from the recording. A reader looking for board action on restructuring should look to the board meetings of September 15 and October 20, not here. The label is a cataloguing issue in the project's map and the gallery title, not a defect of the district's.

Sources: Input/SupportingDocuments/MAP.md section 152; Scripts/attribution_reports_2015_2023/11933 SAU6092021.mp4.md.

OBSERVATION The survey offered as early community input could not separate groups, allowed duplicate responses and, by the superintendent's account, was incomplete

The survey results shown at 0:04:47 rest on about 75 to 80 responses. Asked what they would be used for, the superintendent said "that data is incredibly incomplete and we know that" and that respondents were not identified or disaggregated (0:30:19). A teacher said she could have answered twice, once through her school email (0:50:41), and a parent said the questions invited only "important and very important" answers and did not ask how restructuring, as opposed to its goals, would produce the gains (0:40:58); the superintendent agreed the survey could not "differentiate between the two structures". A month later the October 20 board meeting heard that of 202 responses "60% or so" were not Claremont parents. None of this breaks a rule: no statute governs a district's opinion surveys. It is recorded so that a reader weighing later references to survey support for restructuring knows the limits stated at the time by the district itself.

Sources: No statute applies; see the October 20, 2021 page for the later survey figures.

POSITIVE A major change to the elementary schools was opened to the public early, in person, remotely and on television, before any board vote

The forum was announced at the September 15 board meeting, noticed a week ahead by the superintendent's account, televised by CCTV, and run in hybrid form with online questions read aloud (0:00:15). The superintendent set out the concerns as well as the benefits, including "The whole idea of the loss of the neighborhood school", stated on the record that the plan "is supposed to be neutral or less money", invited people to join a steering committee, and promised a second forum on October 25. No statute required a forum before a board decision of this kind; the practice exceeds the minimum. The limits recorded in flag 2 apply to the survey, not to the forum itself.
