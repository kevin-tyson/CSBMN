Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the meeting against the law in force on October 19, 2016. RSA 91-A:2 and 91-A:3 were amended effective January 1, 2017, and each flag cites the text as it then read. They are not findings of violation, and nothing here is legal advice. Where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Seven flags follow, ordered by severity.

MEDIUM The superintendent told the board that a subcommittee with fewer board members than a board quorum need not be noticed

Asked by Patrick Adrian whether committees with board members are subject to the open meeting rules, the chair gave the statutory answer at 1:17:24: "A quorum of your committee is a quorum for purposes of of open meeting." The superintendent then stated a different rule at 1:17:37: "If we have a if we have a committee, a subcommittee that has a quorum that has so many board members, we have to post that, we have to warn it, if you will. But if we don't have a quorum, we have two board members on a subcommittee, and there are seven of you. We don't have to warn that."

In 2016, as now, RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) made a school board's "committee, subcommittee, or subordinate body thereof, or advisory committee thereto" a public body in its own right, so a quorum of the committee triggers notice and minutes whatever share of the full board it represents. The superintendent's rule would leave a two-member subcommittee unnoticed. The mitigation is on the record: the chair stated the correct rule first, and the assistant superintendent said the district had in fact noticed the committee in question ("Did, just to make sure we were okay."). MEDIUM because the misstatement describes the administration's working understanding of notice practice, not a single recorded lapse.

Sources: RSA 91-A:1-a, VI(d) (source note ends 2008, 354:1, so the same text governed in 2016): committees and subcommittees of a school board are public bodies; RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification, in force through December 31, 2016): notice and minutes.

MEDIUM Two members described emailing the full board outside a meeting; the policy's disclosure step is not shown on the recording

The superintendent put the board's electronic-communication policy on the agenda because "Patrick, you had done some emailing" (1:09:23), and read its remedy: "the contents of the electric communication will be publicly disclosed and included in the minutes of the next regularly scheduled board meeting." Adrian explained that he wrote to the chair and then sent the same note to other members individually, rather than by reply-all, so they would know what he had asked for; he accepted that doing so with a quorum was "a violation policy" (1:13:45). Frank Sprague then said he had "circulated it back out to everyone again" when a board applicant's email went unanswered (1:15:27); the chair said that "probably isn't the best way to do it either."

RSA 91-A:2-a, as in force in 2016, required the board to deliberate "only in meetings" and provided that "Communications outside a meeting, including, but not limited to, sequential communications among members of a public body, shall not be used to circumvent the spirit and purpose of this chapter". Nothing on the recording shows either email carried deliberation, and both members described their intent as openness; Sprague's was logistical. The gap a reviewer can check is the policy's own remedy: the contents of Adrian's emails are not read into the recording, and the October 19 and November 2 minutes that would show whether they were "included in the minutes" are not available online. The policy text itself is not posted, and the transcript reads its code as "BHP". The meeting deserves credit for airing the matter in public at length.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, I and II (2016 codification; source note 2008, 303:4): deliberation only in meetings; outside communications not to circumvent; district electronic-communication policy (read aloud in part; text not located).

OBSERVATION The board did not know whether it could deliberate on the vacancy appointment in nonpublic session

Setting up the November 2 interviews, the chair said at 0:18:33: "we can find more out about this, about deliberations whether to have those in nonpublic or if those are are required to be in public. We'll find out." No member answered, and no legal advice was reported back on this recording. In 2016 RSA 91-A:3, I(b) required any motion to enter nonpublic session to "state on its face the specific exemption under paragraph II"; the paragraph II grounds then in force included "The hiring of any person as a public employee" (II(b)) and matters likely to affect adversely the reputation of a person "other than a member of the public body itself" (II(c)). None names the appointment of a member to fill a board vacancy, and whether an elected-office appointee is a "public employee" under II(b) is a legal question this page does not resolve.

What happened two weeks later is on the November 2, 2016 page, which flags a nonpublic motion that named no exemption and minutes sealed by voice vote. This meeting's own conduct raises no issue: the open question was stated in public, and the interview format itself was settled in public (flag 6).

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, I(b) and II(b)-(c) (2015 and 2016 codifications identical on these passages): motion must state the exemption; hiring and reputation grounds.

OBSERVATION A member said he had never seen nonpublic minutes; this meeting's own minutes, and any nonpublic minutes of the period, are not online

At 1:41:00 Adrian, reading the statute, said "I just have never seen the nonpublic minutes other." The chair's answer was accurate for 2016: sealing requires a vote "in public session" (1:42:03). RSA 91-A:3, III then required nonpublic minutes and decisions to be "publicly disclosed within 72 hours" unless withheld by a "recorded vote of 2/3 of the members present taken in public session" with one of the statutory determinations, and permitted withholding only "until, in the opinion of a majority of members, the aforesaid circumstances no longer apply". The chair's gloss that sealing is "typically" "until a certain date" is his description of practice, not the statutory test. The superintendent added that the public minutes record the votes to enter nonpublic session.

The record limit is the project's usual one for this period: MAP.md records that neither district Drive share holds anything before July 2022 (checked 9/26/26), so no agenda, packet, public minutes or nonpublic minutes for October 2016 could be inspected. That is a limit of what the district posts online, not evidence that minutes were never kept: the board approved the October 5 minutes on this recording at 0:04:15. Under the project's missing-records rule this is recorded as an online-availability observation, because the recording itself shows minutes being kept and approved and shows no nonpublic session at this meeting.

Sources: RSA 91-A:3, III (2015 and 2016 codifications): 72-hour disclosure unless withheld by recorded 2/3 vote with a determination; RSA 91-A:2, II (2015 codification): minutes open to inspection not more than 5 business days after the meeting; Input/SupportingDocuments/MAP.md section 16.

OBSERVATION The chair's account of Right-to-Know penalties is partly right, but its date and dollar figure are not supported

At 1:14:22 the chair cited "a recent case with sandwich" [Sandwich, N.H.] and said "I think in 2015 they beefed up the 91 A where the board is liable to pay", adding "I think it was $207,000 in particular. The board then can hold the person responsible for paying that, that fine." RSA 91-A:8 as it read in 2016 did make a public body liable for reasonable attorney's fees and costs where a lawsuit was necessary to enforce compliance (¶I), set a civil penalty of "not less than $250 and not more than $2,000" for an officer who violated the chapter in bad faith (¶IV), and allowed that such a person "may also be required to reimburse the public body" for fees it paid. But the section's source note ends "2012, 206:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2013"; there was no 2015 amendment. The Sandwich case and the $207,000 figure were not verified and are the chair's characterization. The practical point he was making, that personal exposure exists for bad-faith violations, was correct.

Sources: RSA 91-A:8, I and IV (2016 codification; source note ends 2012, 206:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2013): fees against the body; bad-faith penalty and reimbursement.

POSITIVE The vacancy interview format was set in public, and members' questions were routed to avoid a board email exchange

Rather than agreeing on a process by email, the board settled the November 2 format in open session (0:14:05 to 0:22:59): each candidate makes an opening statement, then all are asked the same prepared questions. When Adrian proposed sending questions in, the superintendent cautioned at 0:15:40 against "any kind of business between board members" outside a meeting, and the members agreed each would send questions to the superintendent alone and not reply to all. That is the hub-and-spoke risk RSA 91-A:2-a, II describes, handled by keeping the substance (the questions) with staff and the decisions on the record. The effect is visible on the November 2 recording, where the vacancy was filled through public interviews.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2-a, II (2016 codification): communications outside a meeting not to circumvent the chapter; November 2, 2016 page.

POSITIVE The assistant superintendent declined to display a named student report on television

Asked for skill-level detail during the i-Ready presentation, Cory LeClair said at 0:56:32: "I only have live data with actual student names on it to show that report. And so I can't do that publicly", and offered a redacted example instead. The board saw only grade-level aggregates. Displaying named assessment results at a televised meeting would have been a disclosure of personally identifiable information from education records, which FERPA bars without written parental consent unless an exception applies.

Sources: 34 CFR 99.30(a) (last amended 2004, so the same text governed in 2016): signed, dated written consent before disclosing personally identifiable information from education records.
