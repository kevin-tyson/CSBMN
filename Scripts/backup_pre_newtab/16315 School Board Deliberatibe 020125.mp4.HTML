The first session of the Claremont School District’s 2025 annual meeting under RSA 40:13 (the SB 2 deliberative session). Items flagged for review

These flags are a reviewer’s aid. They are not findings of violation and not legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; an Observation rests on no confirmed rule. A deliberative session is a meeting of the voters, so the school-board rules (public comment under RSA 189:74, nonpublic sessions, roll calls) do not apply to it.

MEDIUM Voters were told that questions belonged at “an earlier public meeting”

RSA 40:13, IV says the first session “shall consist of explanation, discussion, and debate of each warrant article, including warrant articles pertaining to the operating budget and the default budget.” Explanation is the first of the three. Immediately before Article 1 the moderator told the room that “the discussion should be limited to comments about the articles and not questions which should have been asked at an earlier public meeting” (0:54:35). Her own Rules of Procedure say the moderator “can also field questions from the voters and is the facilitator of debate.” A voter who took the instruction at its word would have stayed seated with a question the statute makes this session’s business. Mitigation: it was a reminder, not a ruling on anyone; she invited speakers for and against every article; the presenters had just explained the budget for about 44 minutes; and no voter sought recognition, so no question was refused. The flag records the instruction, not a denial.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV (first session for “explanation, discussion, and debate”; last amended 2019, 192:2, eff. July 10, 2019; text read 2026-09-25); Rules of Procedure.docx (1).pdf.

MEDIUM The default budget was defined to the voters without its eliminated-positions clause, in a budget that eliminates positions, and no default budget form is in the packet

The statutory definition of the default budget, RSA 40:13, IX(b), ends by requiring the prior-year appropriation to be “reduced by one-time expenditures contained in the operating budget and by salaries and benefits of positions that have been eliminated in the proposed budget,” excluding “vacant positions under recruitment or positions redefined in the proposed operating budget.” The business administrator read the definition to the voters at 0:24:39–0:25:09 and stopped after the one-time-expenditure clause; the eliminated-positions clause is not on the recording. The same presentation described five teaching positions cut (“roughly 334,000”), another 4.35 positions (“185,000”), a middle-school guidance counselor and the preschool program, “So that total budget adjustment is 438,000” (0:32:19–0:33:15), and put the $160,786 gap between the proposed and default budgets mainly down to out-of-district placements (0:25:49). Whether the default was reduced for the eliminated positions cannot be checked from the public record. RSA 40:13, XI(a) requires a default budget form showing, at a minimum, changes “by account code, and the reasons for each change” and “Reductions for eliminated positions and benefit expenditures”. The deliberative packet holds no such form, and none of the four documents in the January 15, 2025 hearing folder is titled as one. Some of the positions may be vacancies under recruitment, which the statute excludes; the record does not say. The same truncation appears on the project’s February 3, 2024 deliberative session page and December 18, 2024 board page.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IX(b) and XI(a) (2019, 192:2); Claremont Budget Summary (1).pdf; 12. CSB Budget Public Hearing 1.15.25 (folder).

MEDIUM Catastrophic special-education aid was described to the voters with a “94%” the law then in force does not contain

At 0:20:05–0:20:27 the business administrator said the State “did not give us the 94% that they’re supposed to do” and paid “67%” because it lacked the money. RSA 186-C:18, III as in force in February 2025 (2023, 79:141, 142, eff. July 1, 2023) makes the department liable for “80 percent of the cost above the 3½ times the estimated state average expenditure per pupil” and for all costs above ten times it, with an insufficient appropriation prorated proportionally on entitlement. It sets no 94 percent figure and no minimum payment. The 67 percent is consistent with the FY2025 rate of 67.5 percent reported by the New Hampshire Bulletin on November 15, 2024. No breach by the State is implied: the 80-percent-of-entitlement floor in today’s text was added by 2025 legislation (HB 2, §137) and did not exist at this session. The defect is in the information given to voters about what the State owed. Mitigation: the point she was making, that the district carries mandated costs the State does not fully fund, is accurate, and Sprague put it correctly as a reduction “due to budget shortfalls at the state level” (0:37:33).

Sources: RSA 186-C:18, III (2024 codification, in force at this session; text read 2026-09-25); New Hampshire Bulletin, November 15, 2024 (reporting, not law); companion page Finance Subcommittee, December 13, 2024.

MEDIUM The Rules of Procedure misstate what a voter can do about a doubted vote, and omit the limits on amendment

The sheet handed to voters ends: “any voter who doubts the accuracy of any non-counted vote may require the Moderator to determine a vote by a counted show of hands.” The statutes that govern this session say otherwise. RSA 40:13, IV makes the first session “governed by the provisions of RSA 40:4, 40:4-a, 40:4-b, 40:4-f, and 40:6-40:10.” Under RSA 40:4-a, five voters may ask in writing before a vote that it be taken by secret ballot, and may obtain a recount of a close result; under RSA 40:4-b, a declared vote questioned immediately by seven or more voters must be retaken by secret “yes-no” ballot. The sheet’s remedy is a counted show of hands, available to one voter; the statute’s is a secret ballot on the demand of five or seven. The sheet also describes amendment without the statutory limits: it omits RSA 40:13, IV(a) (“Warrant articles whose wording is prescribed by law shall not be amended”), IV(c) (no amendment may “eliminate the subject matter of the article”, and “An amendment that changes the dollar amount of an appropriation in a warrant article shall not be deemed to violate this subparagraph”), and XI(b) on the default budget (“This amount shall not be amended by the legislative body”). The same sheet was used in 2023 and 2024. Mitigation: every vote at this session was procedural and none was close or questioned, so no voter lost a remedy on February 1.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and XI(b); RSA 40:4-a (secret ballot on request of 5 voters; 2006, 117:1); RSA 40:4-b (vote questioned by 7 or more voters retaken by secret ballot; 1971, 524:1); Rules of Procedure.docx (1).pdf.

OBSERVATION No amendment, no voter at the podium, and reconsideration restricted on all four articles

Amendment is the voters’ only power at a first session; RSA 40:13, VI puts every article on the ballot in any case. No amendment was moved to any article, no voter spoke for or against any of them, and each went to the March 11 ballot as printed. The only votes were procedural: the 2024 minutes, and two motions to restrict reconsideration (1:01:28–1:02:51 and 1:11:35–1:12:17). The second mover cited a provision the transcript renders “RSA 4413 Roman numeral for”, most likely RSA 40:13, IV, which incorporates RSA 40:10; the operative section is RSA 40:10, which lets a meeting restrict reconsideration of votes already taken, lasting “until final adjournment of the meeting”. On February 1 that was minutes away, so the restriction had little practical effect. The first mover’s words, “Pursuant to, say, 22”, cannot be matched to a provision. No petitioned article appears on the 2025 warrant.

Sources: RSA 40:13, IV and VI; RSA 40:10, I, III and V (1996, 64:1); 2025 Warrant.

OBSERVATION No capital-reserve or trust-fund article, and the expendable trusts were left unfunded

The warrant carries four articles and nothing to raise money for, or withdraw it from, any capital reserve or expendable trust fund. Both presenters said why. Crawford (0:48:07): the Capital Improvement subcommittee recommended, and the board agreed, to “put off having a warrant article to add capital funds because the warrant articles addressing bargaining units were the priority.” Henry (0:35:02): the usual route of depositing year-end surplus was not used, “We did not do that this year given the budget.” Capital reserve money is held by the trustees “until such time as the town, district or county shall have voted to withdraw funds,” so both deposits and withdrawals run through the voters. The effect is that the Tech Center capital plan Crawford said would reach the board “this spring” (0:47:32) has no funding article behind it before the 2026 meeting. The voters were told this, which is the point of the session.

Sources: RSA 35:15, I (capital reserve held until the district votes to withdraw; last amended 2021, 105:2); 2025 Warrant.

OBSERVATION The preschool program was closed with an assurance about special education and no detail on where eligible three- and four-year-olds will be served

Whitney told the voters the State does not require a district preschool and that the district “will continue as before to provide special education services” in accordance with each student’s IEP (0:42:56, 0:44:19); the minutes say the same. The statement about general preschool is not contradicted by anything the project has located. The special-education duty is federal: a free appropriate public education must be available to “all children residing in the State between the ages of 3 and 21, inclusive,” and each child’s placement is decided by a group including the parents, in the least restrictive environment. Closing the program removes the classroom in which many preschool IEPs were delivered alongside typically developing peers; the record gives no account of the replacement settings. The proposal first surfaced at the December 13, 2024 finance session. Part of the presentation falls in a stretch the transcript barely captures (0:40:50), so the page may miss detail that was said.

Sources: 34 CFR 300.101 (FAPE, ages 3 through 21); 34 CFR 300.116 (placements).

POSITIVE Every date in the SB 2 calendar was met, and the session sat on the first day of its window

RSA 40:13, II-a applies “Notwithstanding any other provision of law” and sets the schedule for official-ballot districts: budget hearings “on or before the third Tuesday in January” (January 21, 2025) and warrants and budgets “posted and copies available to the general public on or before the last Monday in January” (January 27). Claremont held its hearing on January 15, and the warrant, generated by the Department of Revenue Administration on January 22, carries the board’s certification that it was posted “on or before January 27,2025” at the place of meeting and on the SAU 6 website. RSA 40:13, III puts the first session “between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January”, February 1 to 8; the session sat on February 1. The copy in the district’s Drive archive is dated January 28, a day after the posting deadline; the certification covers the website and the meeting place, not the archive. An earlier version of this page left open whether RSA 32:5, I’s 25-day hearing rule was met (January 15 to February 1 is 17 days); II-a’s notwithstanding clause and its own January schedule answer that question for an SB 2 district. The elected moderator presided, which was not the case in 2024.

Sources: RSA 40:13, II-a and III (2019, 192:2); RSA 32:5, I and VII(a); 2025 Warrant (certification page); project page for the January 15, 2025 budget hearing.

POSITIVE Documents in hand, by QR code and online; the session televised; the minutes public on the next business day

The warrant, budget summary, agenda and rules were in the district’s Drive folder by January 28, handed out at the session, and offered by QR code (0:00:05); the moderator reviewed the rules aloud (0:06:20–0:08:26). The session was carried live on CCTV Channel 8 and the recording is public. The draft minutes PDF was created in the public minutes folder at 9:07 a.m. on Monday, February 3, the first business day after the session, within the five business days RSA 91-A:2, II allows, and names a mover and seconder for every motion.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2023 codification); RSA 32:5, VII(a) (certified budget posted with the warrant); Drive creation dates of the packet and minutes files, read 2026-09-25.
