Claremont School Board — March 4, 2020

Public hearing on donations and a regular meeting, the last in-person meeting in this series before the COVID-19 closure, summarized from the Claremont Community Television recording and its speaker-attributed transcript; no agenda, packet or minutes for this meeting are posted in the district's online shares. The board heard the early childhood program, the superintendent's entry-plan findings and a first coronavirus briefing, a Medicaid-to-schools update, and the 2020-21 program of studies, which it approved provisionally; it tabled its February 23 retreat minutes as incomplete. Six days before the town election, the chair, a member and the business administrator urged a yes vote on the budget and contract articles. Items flagged for review

Flags are a reviewer's aid generated by comparing the meeting against the law in force on March 4, 2020. They are not findings of violation, and nothing here is legal advice. Each flag cites the rule it rests on; where no verified rule applies, the flag says so and is recorded as an observation. Six flags follow, ordered by severity.

OBSERVATION Six days before the town election, the chair, a member and the business administrator spoke for the ballot articles at a televised meeting

At 1:48:45 the chair turned to "the vote on Tuesday", described the main budget and the three negotiated agreements, rejected a newspaper's description of "a spending spree", and closed: "Support your schools. Support our kids." (1:51:50). Benware followed at 1:52:11, answering a letter to the editor and urging viewers not to "take the word of somebody" in a newspaper. The business administrator then added that the district was "still looking at a 56% reduction in tax rate" and called it "an important budget that will materially change the future of the financial makeup of Claremont School District" (1:56:57, 1:57:32). The chair was himself one of two candidates for the two open seats (1:47:47).

RSA 659:44-a, as in force in 2020, barred a "public employee, as defined in RSA 273-A:1, IX" from electioneering "while in the performance of his or her official duties", and defined electioneering as acting "in any way specifically designed to influence the vote of a voter on any question or office". RSA 273-A:1, IX excludes "Persons elected by popular vote", so the statute does not reach board members speaking for their own budget. Whether it reaches the business administrator depends on whether his post falls within the statute's exclusions (for example, duties implying "a confidential relationship to the public employer") and on whether a tax-rate projection with that closing remark was "specifically designed to influence the vote"; neither question is settled by the record. His figures are largely factual. The flag is recorded as an observation for a reader weighing how the district presented its budget; the chair's "56% increase" remark in reply appears to be a slip or a transcription error, since both men were describing a reduction.

Sources: RSA 659:44-a (2019 codification; source note ends 2016, 176:1, eff. Jan. 1, 2017); RSA 273-A:1, IX (2019 codification): exclusions from "public employee".

OBSERVATION The board's own member said the February 23 retreat minutes left out "a lot of conversations"

The February 23 goal-setting retreat was held on a Sunday and has no recording in the project's corpus; the business administrator refers to "our meeting we had on Sunday two weeks ago" (0:57:59). At 0:02:50 Carolyn Towle objected to its minutes: "There was a lot of things missing. A lot of conversations weren't included." The clerk asked her to send "what I missed" and whether she had "notes written down". The minutes were tabled here, tabled again on May 6, and approved on June 3. RSA 91-A:2, II, as in force in 2020, required the minutes of every meeting to "include the names of members, persons appearing before the public bodies, and a brief description of the subject matter discussed and final decisions". A brief description is the legal floor, so omitted conversations are not necessarily a defect; the point is that a retreat without a recording depends entirely on its minutes, and a member present found them incomplete. What the final version says is not available here.

Sources: RSA 91-A:2, II (2019 codification; source note ends 2018, 244:1, so the text in force in 2020): minutes contents; related pages: May 6, 2020 and June 3, 2020.

POSITIVE A public hearing was held before the board accepted the donations

The meeting opened with a public hearing on "A total of $11,947.39, specifically for Chromebooks" raised by Sue Taylor and $3,000 from an anonymous donor for the Bluff fourth-grade trip (0:00:12); the chair invited public comment (0:00:51), and the board accepted both by vote in public session later in the meeting (1:45:25). RSA 198:20-b, III, in its 2019 codification, required a public hearing, with newspaper notice at least 7 days ahead, before a board accepts unanticipated money of $5,000 or more; for smaller sums it required notice on the agenda and in the minutes, with acceptance in public session. The larger gift crossed the threshold, and the board held a hearing on both. Whether the newspaper notice ran cannot be tested here.

Sources: RSA 198:20-b, III(a) and (b) (2019 codification; source note ends 2005, 188:1, so in force until 2023, 38:1).

POSITIVE Citizens' comments were called although no statute yet required it, and budget material was posted for voters

At 0:23:22 the chair opened "the floor for citizens comments" and invited "any of our local people" to address the board; no one came forward. RSA 189:74, which now requires a public comment period at school board meetings, was enacted in 2022, so the practice here went beyond the law of the time. The chair and a member also pointed voters to the SAU website, where "all the budgeting documents are there" and the warrant articles were posted (1:54:06).

Sources: RSA 189:74 (2022, 333:1, eff. Sept. 6, 2022): not in force in 2020.
